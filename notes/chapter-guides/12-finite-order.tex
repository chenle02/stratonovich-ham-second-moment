Before trusting an asymptotic argument, compute something by hand. What do the
first terms of the second moment look like? This appendix works them out in
one space dimension with initial data $(1,0)$. Figure~\ref{Fig:white-strip}
is the picture to keep in mind.

Already at small order the Stratonovich expansion shows its three
features: pairings inside one chain, pairings between the two chains,
and products of different orders. Starting from Proposition~\ref{P:mixed},
Proposition~\ref{P:white-four} computes every term of total order four for
spatial white noise, and Proposition~\ref{P:riesz-small} gives the order-two
coefficient and an integral formula for every order-four Riesz term. Here
the total order is the sum of the two Stratonovich orders; it is not a
Wiener-chaos order.

These formulas test the scaling, the pairing counts and the static
limit. Finitely many coefficients cannot determine the exponent or the
constant, and the main argument does not rely on this appendix. A reader
who wants examples first can start here after the chain notation of
Chapter~\ref{S:G0}. Episode~\VideoRef{9}{eLz9Hd20ZT0} of the video series
covers this appendix.
