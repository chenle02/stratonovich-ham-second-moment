A singular Gaussian potential cannot simply be treated as a bounded
multiplication operator. To construct its effect on a wave, we need estimates
that combine the covariance with wave propagation and spatial localization.
Moreover, controlling an operator and its adjoint requires two variance
estimates; one direction alone does not supply the later Gaussian bounds.

The assumptions are those of Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}. A physical mask means a
smooth function of compact support multiplying the potential in space.
Section~\ref{S:masked-covariance} establishes covariance bounds for such masks
and explains why multiplying a test by a mask requires care in the covariance
completion. Section~\ref{S:wave-variance} applies these bounds to the full wave
phase. Section~\ref{S:compact-gaussian} then combines finite Gaussian matrix
estimates with compactness supplied by spatial cutoffs. These results prepare
the operator-path construction in Chapter~\ref{S:book-operator-paths}. At this
stage the Gaussian sums are finite, and no infinite random path or uniform
large-radius estimate is being asserted.
