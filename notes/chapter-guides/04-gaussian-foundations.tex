A rough Gaussian potential is not a bounded multiplication operator. So what
does it do to a wave? We need estimates that hold three things together:
the covariance, wave propagation and spatial localization. There is one
trap. An operator and its adjoint need two variance estimates, and one
direction alone does not give the later Gaussian bounds.

This chapter has no figure. Picture a window on the potential: a physical
mask is a smooth function of compact support that multiplies it in
space. Section~\ref{S:masked-covariance} bounds the covariance behind
it. Section~\ref{S:wave-variance} carries the bounds to the full wave
phase. Section~\ref{S:compact-gaussian} adds finite Gaussian matrix estimates
and the compactness from spatial cutoffs.

The assumptions are those of Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}. On a
first reading, keep Proposition~\ref{P:wave-two-variances}
and Corollary~\ref{C:finite-gaussian-operators} and let the
proofs wait. The Gaussian operator-series estimates here concern
finite noise sums; fixed-vector Gaussian column limits are also
constructed. Radius-explicit operator-path moment bounds come in
Chapter~\ref{S:book-growing-radius}. Episode~\VideoRef{5}{FGR9ShF2F0w}
of the video series covers this chapter and the next two.
