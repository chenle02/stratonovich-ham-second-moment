The complete pairing expansion gives a finite second moment, but estimating
every pairing by the same quantity loses too much information. We now seek an
upper bound at the correct time scale without first knowing the limiting
constant. The wave equation introduces an additional difficulty: evolution
across a time interval must retain both displacement and velocity.

The starting points are Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} and the fixed-order
construction in Chapter~\ref{S:G0}. Section~\ref{S:static-phase} first examines
the variance contribution for arbitrary deterministic initial phase. This
calculation motivates the all-order static estimates that follow, but is not
itself an all-order bound. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper} then represents the
temporal comparison through Gaussian fields supported on intervals and controls
their combined contribution. The resulting Theorem~\ref{T:full-upper} gives a
finite upper rate for the full admissible covariance class. Its constant is not
claimed to be optimal. This independent finiteness estimate is available before
the variational identification in Chapter~\ref{S:book-constant}.
