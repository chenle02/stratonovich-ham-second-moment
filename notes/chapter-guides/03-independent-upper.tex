Chapter~\ref{S:G0} shows that the second moment is finite. How fast can
it grow? This chapter gives a first answer, before the exact constant is
known: no faster than $\exp(CT^\kappa)$, up to a polynomial factor. This is
Theorem~\ref{T:full-upper}, and it proves Theorem~\ref{T:intro-upper}. Its
constant is not claimed to be optimal.

The natural plan is to cut time into blocks and multiply the bounds. A wave
does not allow this easily. Each block must hand on both displacement
and velocity (No-go~\ref{P:nogo-14}), and the noise in one block is
correlated with the noise in the others. Sections~\ref{S:static-phase}
and~\ref{S:static-all-orders} show how far independent blocks
go. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper} then replaces the blocks by Gaussian fields
on overlapping intervals; Figure~\ref{Fig:dyadic-cover} is the picture to
keep in mind.

The model prerequisites are Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} and the
construction of Chapter~\ref{S:G0}. On a first reading, skip the proofs of
Propositions~\ref{P:static-phase-variance} and~\ref{P:static-phase-all-orders}
and of Corollary~\ref{C:static-interaction}: Theorem~\ref{T:full-upper}
uses only the lemmas of those sections and one calculation, the free-phase
energy bound at the end of the proof of
Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-variance}.
