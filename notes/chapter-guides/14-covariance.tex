Suppose a bound is known for simple covariances, such as products of
one-dimensional ones. Can it be carried over to a general homogeneous
covariance in dimension two or three? Sometimes. A pointwise factorization is
not enough: each factor must itself be a covariance, and the comparison must
keep the amplitudes and the scaling. This matters most for measure-valued
and anisotropic covariances. This appendix has no figure.

We keep Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} and the mixed pairing formula
of Chapter~\ref{S:G0}. Section~\ref{S:planar-holder}
builds planar endpoints and proves the transfer
(Proposition~\ref{P:planar-upper}). Section~\ref{S:three-endpoints}
gives product constructions in dimension three and a conditional transfer
(Proposition~\ref{P:three-transfer}). It does not claim a factorization
for every three-dimensional covariance. Section~\ref{S:energy-fidelity}
identifies the physical and spectral forms of the spatial energy for measure
covariances; this identity does not remove the assumptions of the transfers.

The upper bound of Chapter~\ref{S:book-independent-upper} and the
constant of Chapter~\ref{S:book-constant} do not rely on these
comparisons. On a first reading, the two transfer statements are
enough. Episode~\VideoRef{9}{I-1qNZWs770} of the video series covers this
appendix.
