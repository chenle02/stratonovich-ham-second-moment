A lower moment bound can be obtained by shifting the Gaussian noise toward a
deterministic potential that makes the wave grow. The gain from this growth must
be compared with the cost of the shift. Temporal correlation means that we must
charge the cost of one control in the original covariance space, rather than add
costs as though successive time intervals were independent.

We begin with the canonical solution of Chapter~\ref{S:G0} and the phase and
ground-state constructions of Chapter~\ref{S:book-wave-evolution}.
Section~\ref{S:exact-canonical-scaling} converts long time into an equivalent
small-noise problem by equality in law. Section~\ref{S:actual-raw-block-lower}
then follows the full wave phase over time blocks and proves a pointwise lower
bound for each fixed nonnegative smooth control. Finally,
Section~\ref{S:unmasked-variational} removes the spatial mask and optimizes over
controls, including those that do not separate into time and space factors. The
result is a variational lower value. Its equality with the upper value is proved
only in Chapter~\ref{S:book-constant}.
