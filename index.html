A book in stochastic partial differential equations · Le Chen, Auburn University

Stratonovich Waves Driven by Space-Time Gaussian Noise

Second Moment and Exact Asymptotics

To make new mathematics found with AI as trustworthy as mathematics proved by hand: every step open, every check recorded, every contributor credited.

We study the hyperbolic Anderson model, the stochastic wave equation ∂t2u = Δu + u Ẇ in dimensions one to three, with initial data (1, 0), in the Stratonovich sense. The Gaussian noise has temporal covariance |s − t|−α0 and a homogeneous spatial covariance of order −α. The book identifies the large-time behaviour of the second moment at the scale tκ, κ = (4 − α − α0)/(3 − α), with an intrinsic constant given by an explicit variational formula.

The work was developed by Le Chen with Amy, his AI research system, and is published with its filtered development history, so that mathematicians can study how the work was actually done, check the results, and help interpret them.

What is established, and what is not

Please read this before citing anything.

  • The proofs are written out in full. Part I (the reading edition) has not yet been checked line by line by a human reader.
  • The technical notes have been reviewed by AI systems from several model families. The latest whole-paper review returned pass with notes at medium confidence and is not accepted under the project's own standard, which requires high confidence.
  • A Lean companion formalizes some results in full under the manuscript's own hypotheses. For others it proves every conclusion, or checks steps, with the analytic inputs entering as explicit hypotheses. Of the four main theorems, two are outside Lean; for the other two, Lean proves a narrower conclusion, for one of them with analytic inputs supplied. The correspondence between Lean and the manuscript was reviewed by AI, not by a human reader.
  • No AI review grade is a proof. Human verification is recorded, result by result, in VERIFICATION.md.

Le Chen made the series with Amy. She drafted the scripts and the Manim animations from the book; every displayed formula is copied from the statement carrying its label and checked by script, the narration was checked to be exactly the script, and each script was reviewed against the manuscript by a model family different from the one that drafted it. Episodes 1–10 follow the draft at version 38d67ec and episode 11 (Chapter 12) the draft at 0f553e08, so theorem numbers in them may differ from later versions. The videos explain the argument; they are not part of it or of its review.

How you can help

  • Check a result. Pick a row in VERIFICATION.md, check it, and open a pull request that changes that row and says exactly what you checked.
  • Report a gap or an error. Open an issue with the result's label.
  • Discuss interpretation. Use GitHub Discussions.
  • Formalize. See the Lean companion's contributor guide and its work packages.

Credit is set out in advance in CONTRIBUTING.md: every merged contribution is acknowledged by name, and substantial contributions lead to coauthorship.

How this was built

HOW-THIS-WAS-BUILT.md is a guided tour of the development history: the workflow, the reviewer model families, the routes that failed, and where to look in the record. Some material was removed from the published history (other authors' copyrighted texts, private infrastructure details, collaborators' unpublished work, and private planning notes); REDACTIONS.md lists every class of removal with its reason and counts.

The book's Appendix E, "The AI work in numbers", counts the work from this record: tokens and sessions by model family, the recorded review runs and their outcomes, the failed approaches, and a day-by-day timeline with 15 milestones. Every number is rebuilt by the scripts in scripts/ai-statistics/. The milestones are tagged in the repository (m01-project-set-up … m15-final-pdf-on-master), so git checkout m05-goal-revised shows the project as it stood that day.

AI use

Amy, Le Chen's AI research system, developed the mathematics: the models working within her drafted the proofs, the LaTeX source and the bookkeeping. Le Chen's role was to drive the work: choosing the problem, setting and revising the goals, deciding which approaches to pursue or abandon, choosing the reviewers and deciding which results to keep.

Amy is model-agnostic: a research operating system (workflows and standing rules, plus a research knowledge base of bibliography, reference library, curated arXiv sources and an SPDE wiki) within which any capable language model can work. Here models from several families worked within her: Anthropic Claude, OpenAI GPT through Codex, DeepSeek and Kimi. Reviews are always recorded with the reviewer's model family, because a review counts only when that family differs from the author's.

Amy is not an author, because she cannot be held responsible for what she produces; that responsibility rests with Le Chen, who set up and ran the work and is responsible for what it reports. The technical chapters have not yet been read in full by a human; the preface explains this, and the AI-use statement gives details.
