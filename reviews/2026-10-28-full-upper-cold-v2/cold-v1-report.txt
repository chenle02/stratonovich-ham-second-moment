I have read the manuscript through at referee pace. This is an unusually careful and self-aware "working notes" text, and in several places I spot-checked the algebra (e.g., the white-noise order-two computation in Lemma L:white-test, the constants $Z$, $c_0$, the ratio $2(3-\alpha_0)$ in Remark R:d1-upper-checks, the first-moment calibration in Corollary C:d1-first-calibration, the resolvent/`$R_\alpha$` constant, and the inventory lemma) and found it consistent. Nonetheless, a number of things caught my attention that would make me hesitate to recommend acceptance. I list them below.

[held back until the source author has been told]

**2. The linchpin identification in the proof of Theorem T:full-upper is asserted, not shown.**
The comparison step says: "Expanding a finite pair of its Duhamel terms and applying Wick's formula therefore gives exactly the two terminal wave chains with one factor $K_J(s_j,s_l)\Gamma_\epsilon(y_j-y_l)$ per pair." This is the exact point on which the whole comparison $\E|U_N^{\epsilon,\delta}|^2 \le \lim_J\E|V_{N,J}^\epsilon|^2$ turns, and it is compressed into one paragraph. What needs to be explicit — and is not — is a written-out statement of the *terminal-chain* kernels on both sides, with their integration domains ($[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^n_<$) and the exact combinatorial factors, so one can see that the time-ordered Duhamel expansion of the interval model and the symmetric Stratonovich expansion of the source produce identical pairing sums up to the covariance factor. The surrounding remark that "$K_J$ need not be stationary in time" and "the comparison is made before any time reversal" does not substitute for that verification. I believe the identification is likely correct, but as written it is a statement, not an argument.

**3. Proposition P:interval-coefficients is the heart of the main theorem and is very hard to referee.**
The proof runs through a "table of methods" keyed to $(r_i,q_i)$-splits ("mean term on one copy," "one insertion on each copy," "Cauchy on each copy where used"), Cauchy circles with radii $\varrho_i$, per-segment complex-phase bounds with changing weights $\Lambda(t)$, a "metric" factor $D_{\mathcal A}$, and finally a reduction to Lemma L:inventory-product. There are many places where a small mistake (in the split count $3^k$, in the metric factor being charged once or twice, in the "at most $2k+1$ segments" claim, or in the passage from $h_i \le LT/i$ to the $v_i$, $b_{i,l}$ inventory) would change the factorial power $\nu = 3-\alpha$. The individual lemmas (L:interval-contractions, L:interval-complex, L:inventory-product) check out, and I spot-verified the inventory lemma's exponents, but the assembly in P:interval-coefficients is the kind of compressed combinatorial argument where an error is easy to hide and hard to find.

**4. The "G0" section claims a comparison to Chen's first-moment factorial power that I cannot verify from the packet.**
Remark R:G0-verdict asserts that the uniform pairing estimate "also loses the factorial power $\alpha_0$ relative to Chen's Theorem 1.2," i.e., $(k!)^{3-\alpha-\alpha_0}$ versus $(k!)^{3-\alpha}$. Since the proof of Chen's Theorem 1.2 is not in the supplied packet (only the statement is quoted in Theorem T:first), I cannot check this correspondence. It is presented as a diagnosis rather than an input, but it is a substantive claim about the source and should be verifiable.

**5. Notation is overloaded in ways that genuinely obstruct reading.**
The manuscript itself flags some of this ("$\delta$ is an exponent" vs. "$\delta$ is a cutoff width"; "$b=2-\alpha$" vs. coordinate powers "$b_j$"), but the collisions run deeper than the flag suggests. In Section S:d1-upper, "$y_l$" is an *increment* and "$x_l$" a *position*, while in E:mixed-diagram "$y_l$" is a *position* — the switch is only noted in a parenthetical. "$\beta$" is simultaneously the coefficient power $4-\alpha-\alpha_0$ (Sections S:G0, S:d1-upper, S:interval-upper) and appears as the exponent of $t$ in several places; "$\nu$" is introduced as $3-\alpha$ only in S:interval-upper while the same quantity is "$q$" in S:d1-upper and S:higher-alpha; "$\Gamma$" is both the Euler gamma function and the spatial covariance measure. The careful reader can disambiguate, but it is a real readability cost in a paper whose proof already carries a heavy notation load.

**6. A large fraction of the manuscript is explicitly not part of the proof of the main result.**
The abstract and the "Main result and dependencies" paragraph state that Theorem T:full-upper does not use the arguments in Sections S:higher-alpha–S:energy-fidelity (interpolation bounds, planar/three-dimensional endpoint constructions, energy fidelity), and Sections S:static-phase–S:static-all-orders contribute only Lemma L:phase-contractions and Lemma L:complex-phase (the variance estimates P:static-phase-variance and P:static-phase-all-orders are explicitly disclaimed: "they do not yet establish Goal G:upper"). Combined with the "Working notes" subtitle and the route section, this reads as a snapshot of a project rather than a finished contribution. For a journal submission, the amount of non-contributing material (several complete sections of conditional bounds under hypotheses like H:spatial-maximum or H:three-endpoints, which are then superseded) would need to be cut or clearly separated.

**7. The lower bound is far from the target and the sharp constant is open.**
The only lower bound is the Jensen comparison (Proposition P:Jensen), giving $\liminf t^{-\kappa}\log\E u^2 \ge 2L_*$ conditionally. The conjecture (Conjecture allp, and Goal G:sharp) predicts $2^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}L^*$, which is strictly larger than $2L^*$; and the upper bound actually proved (Theorem T:full-upper) has constant $CR_\alpha^{1/(3-\alpha)}$, which is not the conjectured $2^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}L^*$. All of this is acknowledged, so it is not an error — but it means the paper's novel mathematical content is "an upper bound at the right scale," with the two-sided asymptotics and sharp constant still open.

**8. Minor citation/verification points.**
- Crouzeix–Palencia is cited to "author preprint v1" (arXiv:1702.00668v1), not the published version, with the note that the numbering refers to that version. This is acceptable but worth flagging, since the constant $(1+\sqrt2)$ in E:cp-input is the published bound.
- Balan–Conus is invoked only for the *exponent* comparison, not as an input, and the paper is careful to say so; this is fine, but a referee should confirm the exponent $(2H+2-a)/(3-a)$ with $H=1-\alpha_0/2$, $a=\alpha$ genuinely matches $\kappa$ (it does).
- The Remark R:source-uniformity observation (that a claimed uniform-in-$t$ bound $\E X_n^2 \le C^n t^{2n}/n!$ contradicts the white-noise test at $n=1$, since $\E X_1^2 = c_0 t^{3-\alpha_0}$) is correct as far as I can tell; it is, however, a diagnosis about the source's intermediate estimate, and the paper's own Lemma L:crude-corrected recovers only the *scale* $e^{Ct^2}$ (in fact $e^{2\theta_0t+2Ct^2}$), not the source's printed constant. The word "recovers" in the abstract should be read as "recovers the scale," which is fine but should be made explicit to avoid overstating.

**9. A few localized things that struck me as terse or needing unpacking (not necessarily wrong):**
- In Lemma L:white-test, the phrase "the integral of the first Green factor is $r$, whereas the second Green factor at zero is $1/2$" is opaque about which "$r$" is meant; the final value $c_0t^{3-\alpha_0}/2$ is right, but the sentence should identify the gap variable.
- In Proposition P:mixed, the reversal step refers to "$J_{m,n,D^{\rm rev}}$" and says "$D\mapsto D^{\rm rev}$ is a bijection of pairings" without spelling out how the reversal acts on the pairing data; a precise definition would help.
- In Theorem T:full-upper, the three-grid nesting claim ("the difference between the parent and child shifts in child units is an integer, since $3t\in\mathbb Z$") and the "at least one grid interval contains both points" argument are correct but compressed to the point where a reader must reconstruct the arithmetic.
- The claim that the final constant $C=C(\alpha,\alpha_0)$ is independent of the covariance, with all covariance dependence entering only through $R_\alpha=\alpha\pi\mu(B_1)/(2\sin(\pi\alpha/2))$, is a strong structural statement. The proof appears to support it (Lemma L:interval-contractions uses only radial ball masses $\mu(B_r)=r^\alpha\mu(B_1)$ and no angular information), but since it is a surprising amount of generality, it deserves an explicit sentence acknowledging that no angular/spectral-distribution dependence has been silently absorbed.

Overall: I did not find a demonstrably false step in the parts I could verify, and the paper is commendably honest about its own limitations and about the defects in the source it builds on. My hesitation is not "this step is wrong" but rather (i) the verification burden is concentrated in one unpublished, self-admittedly flawed source, (ii) the central comparison step and the central combinatorial proposition are asserted/compressed to a degree that is hard to referee, and (iii) the manuscript's scope and notation are not yet in publishable form.
