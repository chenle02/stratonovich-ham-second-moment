\section{Spatial white noise: energy and explicit moment bounds}
\label{S:intrinsic-white-evaluation}

Take $d = \alpha = 1$ and $\Gamma = c\delta_0$, $c \ge 0$, with temporal kernel
$|s-t|^{-a}$, $0 < a < 1$, and data $(1,0)$. The parameter $c$ is covariance
mass; the coefficient of unit noise would be $\sqrt c$. No additional temporal
normalization is inserted. The wave kinetic coefficient is one. For real unit $f
\in H^1(\R)$ put $A = \int_\R f^4$ and $b = \int_\R(f')^2$. The physical
covariance is $A_{\mathrm{sp}}(f) = cA$.

The next calculation recovers the sharp one-dimensional interpolation
inequality recorded by Sz.-Nagy~\cite{sz.nagy:source1941:integralungleichungen}*{(2),
p.~64, and (4), p.~65}, now commonly called a Gagliardo--Nirenberg inequality,
and its hyperbolic-secant optimizer. We give the proof to fix the normalization
of the spatial energy.

\begin{lemma}[A square identity for the distribution function]\label{L:white-cdf-square}
  Every such $f$ has $A < \infty$. With $\mathsf{F}(x) =
  \int_{-\infty}^xf(y)^2\ud y$, for every real $z$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:white-cdf-square}
    0\le\int_\R[f'-zf(1-2\mathsf{F})]^2
    =b-2zA+z^2/3 .
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  On a bounded interval the weak derivative $f'$ is $L^1$ by Cauchy--Schwarz.
  Its integral primitive has weak derivative $f'$ by Fubini. Subtracting it from
  $f$ gives a function of weak derivative zero, hence constant almost
  everywhere: test against derivatives of compact smooth primitives of all
  zero-integral test functions. The resulting locally absolutely continuous
  representatives patch on overlaps, giving one continuous representative. Thus,
  $\rho = f^2$ is locally absolutely continuous with $\rho' = 2ff' \in L^1(\R)$,
  while $\rho \ge 0$ and $\int\rho = 1$. Integrability of $\rho'$ makes $\rho$
  Cauchy on each infinite tail, so it has finite limits at both ends. Its $L^1$
  integrability forces both limits to be zero. It is therefore bounded, and $A =
  \int\rho^2 \le \|\rho\|_\infty\int\rho < \infty$.

  Now $0 \le \mathsf{F} \le 1$, $\mathsf{F}' = \rho$ continuously, and its
  limits are zero and one. The derivative of $\rho(1-2\mathsf{F})$ is
  $\rho'(1-2\mathsf{F})-2\rho^2 \in L^1$. Its endpoint limits vanish.
  Integrating on $[-R,R]$ and sending $R \to \infty$ gives
  \[
    \int_\R f'f(1-2\mathsf{F})=A .
  \]
  The polynomial $P(y) = y-2y^2+(4/3)y^3$ has derivative $(1-2y)^2$. The chain
  rule and the endpoints of $\mathsf{F}$ give
  \[
    \int_\R f^2(1-2\mathsf{F})^2=P(1)-P(0)=1/3 .
  \]
  Absolute integrability holds since this integrand is bounded by $f^2$. For
  each real $z$, both terms in the square in~\eqref{E:white-cdf-square} are in
  $L^2$, and their cross product is absolutely integrable. Expansion and the two
  preceding identities prove the formula. Neither positivity of $f$ nor strict
  increase of its CDF is required. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:white-cdf-square}.
\end{proof}

One choice of $z$ in Lemma~\ref{L:white-cdf-square} gives an inequality
between $A$ and $b$. We use it to evaluate the spatial ratio $R_{\mathrm{sp}}$
and the energy $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ of
Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-energy-map} for white noise.

\LeanMarker{P:white-spatial-energy}
\begin{proposition}[Exact white spatial energy]\label{P:white-spatial-energy}
  For $c > 0$,
  \[
    R_{\mathrm{sp}}=c/\sqrt3,\qquad
    \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}=c^2/6.
  \]
  Both values are zero at $c = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Substitution $z = 3A$ into~\eqref{E:white-cdf-square} gives $3A^2 \le b$. This
  is the exact premise and conclusion of the existing conditional WhiteCDF
  square deduction. Here $A > 0$, since $A = 0$ would imply $f = 0$ a.e. Also $b
  > 0$: zero derivative makes its continuous representative constant and hence
  zero in $L^2$, contradicting unit mass. Thus, $A/\sqrt b \le 1/\sqrt3$.

  For $\ell > 0$ use the real smooth $H^1$ profile $f_\ell(x) =
  \sqrt{\ell/2}\operatorname{sech}(\ell x)$, with $f_\ell' = -\ell\tanh(\ell
  x)f_\ell$. Set $t = \tanh(\ell x)$, so $\ud t = \ell\operatorname{sech}^2(\ell
  x)\ud x$. Direct integration gives
  \[
    \int f_\ell^2=\frac12\int_{-1}^1\ud t=1,\quad
    \int f_\ell^4=\frac\ell4\int_{-1}^1(1-t^2)\ud t=\ell/3,\quad
    \int(f_\ell')^2=\frac{\ell^2}2\int_{-1}^1t^2\ud t=\ell^2/3.
  \]
  The ratio is attained, proving $R_{\mathrm{sp}} = c/\sqrt3$. The physical
  Dirac measure introduces no Fourier constant or nonintegrable kernel
  $|x|^{-1}$. The existing conditional energy deduction applies to $3A^2 \le b$:
  \[
    cA-b/2\le cA-\tfrac32A^2
    =c^2/6-\tfrac32(A-c/3)^2\le c^2/6 .
  \]
  At $\ell = c > 0$ the profile attains $c^2/6$. These are full $H^1$ energy
  profiles; compact approximation equality is
  Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-four-linear}, not a claim that the noncompact
  profile itself is a compact stochastic control. At $c = 0$ the ratio is zero
  and the energy is $\sup(-b/2) = 0$, approached by $f_\ell$ as
  $\ell\downarrow0$. No zero-scale profile is used as a unit vector. The CDF,
  integral and attainment arguments remain analytic; the two finite conditional
  deductions do not formalize them. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:white-spatial-energy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit white moment bounds]\label{T:white-moment-bounds}
  For the same original point limit,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:white-moment-bounds}
    \frac23\sqrt{\frac{c}{M_a}}\le C_2(c,a)
    \le\frac23\sqrt{cK_0(a)},\qquad
    K_0(a)=\frac{2^a}{1-a},\quad
    M_a=\frac{\Gamma_E((1+a)/2)}
    {\Gamma_E(1+a)\Gamma_E((1-a)/2)} .
  \end{equation}
  Its time exponent is $(3-a)/2$. These are bounds, not a numerical evaluation
  of the intrinsic supremum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Here $q = 3,k = 2,m = 3/2$. Formula~\eqref{E:spatial-static-energy-score}
  gives
  \[
    F_0(z)=2\left[\frac{(2z)^{1/2}}{8\,3^{3/2}}\right]^{1/2}.
  \]
  For $c > 0$, $\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}} = c/\sqrt3$, hence
  $F_0(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}) = \sqrt c/(3\sqrt2)$ and
  $2^mF_0(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}) = (2/3)\sqrt c$. Substitution into
  Corollary~\ref{C:intrinsic-spatial-bracket}
  proves~\eqref{E:white-moment-bounds} in the original units. All Gamma factors
  are positive finite for $0 < a < 1$. At $c = 0$, $u = 1$ and $C_2 = 0$ by
  Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}; both displayed bounds vanish. This
  proves Theorem~\ref{T:white-moment-bounds}.
\end{proof}

For $c>0$, both formulas in~\eqref{E:white-moment-bounds} are multiples of
$\sqrt c$. Their ratio depends only on $a$.

\LeanMarker{C:white-relative-width}
\begin{corollary}[Uniform relative width of the bound formulas]
  \label{C:white-relative-width}
  For $c > 0$, the ratio of the upper and lower formulas is
  \[
    \sqrt{K_0(a)M_a}
    \le\left[\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\Gamma_E(5/4)^2}\right]^{1/2},
  \]
  with equality at $a = 1/2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Duplication and recurrence from Lemma~\ref{L:equilibrium-gamma-beta} give
  \[
    \Gamma_E(1+a)
    =\frac{2^a\Gamma_E((1+a)/2)\Gamma_E(1+a/2)}{\sqrt\pi},
    \qquad
    (1-a)\Gamma_E((1-a)/2)=2\Gamma_E((3-a)/2).
  \]
  Therefore,
  \[
    K_0(a)M_a
    =\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\Gamma_E(1+a/2)\Gamma_E((3-a)/2)} .
  \]
  Put $x = 1+a/2$, $y = (3-a)/2$, so $x,y > 0$ and $x+y = 5/2$. In Euler's Gamma
  integral the $L^2(0,\infty)$ functions $t^{(x-1)/2}e^{-t/2}$ and
  $t^{(y-1)/2}e^{-t/2}$ have squared norms $\Gamma_E(x),\Gamma_E(y)$ and inner
  product $\Gamma_E(5/4)$. Cauchy--Schwarz, positivity, inversion and the
  increasing square root prove the bound. At $a = 1/2$, $x = y = 5/4$, giving
  equality. Replacing $a$ by $1-a$ exchanges these two arguments, so only this
  ratio of bound formulas is symmetric, not the moment constant or exponent.
  This proves Corollary~\ref{C:white-relative-width}.
\end{proof}

  proves Theorem~\ref{T:white-moment-bounds}.
\end{proof}

For $c>0$, both formulas in~\eqref{E:white-moment-bounds} are multiples of
$\sqrt c$. Their ratio depends only on $a$.

\LeanMarker{C:white-relative-width}
\begin{corollary}[Uniform relative width of the bound formulas]
  \label{C:white-relative-width}
  For $c > 0$, the ratio of the upper and lower formulas is
  \[
    \sqrt{K_0(a)M_a}
    \le\left[\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\Gamma_E(5/4)^2}\right]^{1/2},
  \]
  with equality at $a = 1/2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Duplication and recurrence from Lemma~\ref{L:equilibrium-gamma-beta} give
  \[
    \Gamma_E(1+a)
    =\frac{2^a\Gamma_E((1+a)/2)\Gamma_E(1+a/2)}{\sqrt\pi},
    \qquad
    (1-a)\Gamma_E((1-a)/2)=2\Gamma_E((3-a)/2).
  \]
  Therefore,
  \[
    K_0(a)M_a
    =\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\Gamma_E(1+a/2)\Gamma_E((3-a)/2)} .
  \]
  Put $x = 1+a/2$, $y = (3-a)/2$, so $x,y > 0$ and $x+y = 5/2$. In Euler's Gamma
  integral the $L^2(0,\infty)$ functions $t^{(x-1)/2}e^{-t/2}$ and
  $t^{(y-1)/2}e^{-t/2}$ have squared norms $\Gamma_E(x),\Gamma_E(y)$ and inner
  product $\Gamma_E(5/4)$. Cauchy--Schwarz, positivity, inversion and the
  increasing square root prove the bound. At $a = 1/2$, $x = y = 5/4$, giving
  equality. Replacing $a$ by $1-a$ exchanges these two arguments, so only this
  ratio of bound formulas is symmetric, not the moment constant or exponent.
  This proves Corollary~\ref{C:white-relative-width}.
\end{proof}

This example retains the all-model theorem rather than replacing its scope. The
formal static exponent is $3/2$ as $a\downarrow0$; no interchange of long-time
and temporal-endpoint limits is asserted. Jensen holds for the same canonical
solution, while $S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}$ remains auxiliary. The read-only Skorohod
comparison at strict spatial Riesz exponent below one does not cover this
spatial-white endpoint. A separate white-time problem cannot be substituted for
$0 < a < 1$. No new Skorohod result, source correction or certified decimal
enclosure is claimed.

\section{Model and standing assumptions}\label{S:model}

We study the hyperbolic Anderson model
\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(1.1)}
\begin{equation}\label{E:HAM}
  \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\W(t,x)u(t,x),
  \qquad u(0,x)=1,\quad \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=0,
\end{equation}
for $(t,x) \in \R_+\times\R^d$, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, interpreted in the
Stratonovich sense (limit of classical wave equations driven by smoothed noises
$\W_{\epsilon,\delta}$). The noise is centered Gaussian with
\begin{equation}\label{E:cov}
  \operatorname{Cov}\big(\W(s,x),\W(t,y)\big)=|s-t|^{-\alpha_0}\gamma(x-y),
  \qquad 0<\alpha_0<1,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma \ge 0$ is nonnegative definite with spectral measure $\mu$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Homogeneous covariance]\label{H:homog}
  Let $d \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$. The spatial covariance is a
  nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon measure $\Gamma$ on
  $\R^d$, with positive even tempered Radon spectral measure $\mu$ in the
  convention
  \[
    \Gamma=\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu(\ud\xi)
    \quad\text{in the sense of tempered distributions}.
  \]
  The symbol $\gamma$ in~\eqref{E:cov} denotes this covariance as a function
  when one exists, and as a distribution otherwise. For every Borel set $A$ and
  $c > 0$, $\Gamma(cA) = c^{d-\alpha}\Gamma(A)$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:threshold}
    \alpha_0+\alpha<2,
  \end{equation}
  with $0 < \alpha < d$, or $\alpha = d = 1$. In particular, the last case
  includes spatial white noise $\Gamma = \delta_0$.
\end{hypothesis}

\paragraph{Zero covariance.}
Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} allows $\Gamma = 0$ by design. In this case the
centered Gaussian noise is $\W = 0$, and the solution with initial data
$(1,0)$ is $u(t,x) = 1$. Thus $\E|u(t,x)|^p = 1$ for every $p > 0$, and
all normalized logarithmic moment rates are zero. Results asserting strict
positivity of covariance-dependent quantities impose $\Gamma \ne 0$ locally.

\section{The temporal equilibrium density}
\label{S:temporal-equilibrium}

The density in Lemma~\ref{L:temporal-equilibrium} is the classical interval
Riesz equilibrium density. Its probability-normalized form on $[-1,1]$ is
recalled by Calef and Hardin~\cite{calef.hardin:source2008:riesz}*{p.~1}.
We prove the potential identity with the normalization used here.

We use Euler's function $\Gamma_E(x) = \int_0^\infty u^{x-1}e^{-u}\ud u$, $x >
0$, and $\operatorname{Beta}(x,y) = \int_0^1t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}\ud t$, $x,y > 0$.
Both integrals are positive and finite.

\LeanMarker{L:equilibrium-gamma-beta}
\begin{lemma}[Gamma and Beta identities]\label{L:equilibrium-gamma-beta}
  For positive arguments,
  \begin{align*}
    \Gamma_E(x+1)&=x\Gamma_E(x),\\
    \operatorname{Beta}(x,y)
    &=\frac{\Gamma_E(x)\Gamma_E(y)}{\Gamma_E(x+y)},\\
    \Gamma_E(2x)
    &=\frac{2^{2x-1}\Gamma_E(x)\Gamma_E(x+1/2)}{\sqrt\pi}.
  \end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Integration by parts gives the recurrence, with vanishing boundary terms. In
  the positive product Gamma integral, use $(u,v) = (Rt,R(1-t))$, with Jacobian
  $R$; Tonelli gives the Beta--Gamma identity. For the last identity, symmetry,
  then $t = (1+v)/2$ and $v^2 = w$, give $\operatorname{Beta}(x,x) =
  2^{1-2x}\operatorname{Beta}(1/2,x)$. Also $\Gamma_E(1/2) = \int_\R e^{-z^2}\ud
  z = \sqrt\pi$: the substitution $u = z^2$ gives the first equality, and
  squaring the positive Gaussian integral and using polar coordinates gives
  $2\pi\int_0^\infty re^{-r^2}\ud r = \pi$. Substituting these into the
  Beta--Gamma identity proves duplication. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:equilibrium-gamma-beta}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:temporal-equilibrium}
\begin{lemma}[Exact potential and mass]\label{L:temporal-equilibrium}
  For $0 < a < 1$ put $r = (1-a)/2$ and, for $0 < t < 1$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:temporal-equilibrium-density}
    f_a(t)=\frac{[t(1-t)]^{-r}}{\Gamma_E(r)\Gamma_E(1-r)}.
  \end{equation}
  This positive function belongs to $L^1(0,1)\cap L^2(0,1)$, and
  \begin{align}
    \int_0^1|s-t|^{-a}f_a(t)\ud t&=1
    &&\text{for every }s\in[0,1],\label{E:temporal-equilibrium-potential}\\
    M_a:=\int_0^1f_a(t)\ud t
    &=\frac{\Gamma_E((1+a)/2)}
    {\Gamma_E(1+a)\Gamma_E((1-a)/2)}>0.
    \label{E:temporal-equilibrium-mass}
  \end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The positive binomial series $(1-z)^{-p} = \sum_{n\ge0}(p)_nz^n/n!$, $p > 0$,
  $0 \le z < 1$, follows directly as follows. Its coefficient ratio gives
  uniform convergence on compact subintervals, including for its derivative.
  Termwise differentiation gives $(1-z)y' = py$, $y(0) = 1$; hence $(1-z)^py$ is
  constant one. Here $(p)_0 = 1$, $(p)_n = p(p+1)\cdots(p+n-1)$. All terms used
  below are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies.

  For $0 < s < 1$ split the integral of $|s-t|^{2r-1}[t(1-t)]^{-r}$ into left
  and right parts. In the left part, $t = su$ gives
  \begin{align*}
    L(s)
    &=s^r\int_0^1(1-u)^{2r-1}u^{-r}(1-su)^{-r}\ud u\\
    &=\operatorname{Beta}(1-r,2r)s^r
    \sum_{n\ge0}
    \frac{(r)_n(1-r)_ns^n}{(1+r)_n n!}.
  \end{align*}
  The Beta ratio follows from the Gamma recurrence. Since $(r)_n/(1+r)_n =
  r/(r+n)$, expanding the incomplete Beta integral gives
  \[
    L(s)=r\operatorname{Beta}(1-r,2r)B_s(r,r),\qquad
    B_s(r,r)=\int_0^s u^{r-1}(1-u)^{r-1}\ud u.
  \]
  Reflection makes the right part $L(1-s)$, while symmetry gives
  $B_s(r,r)+B_{1-s}(r,r) = \operatorname{Beta}(r,r)$. The full integral is
  therefore $r\operatorname{Beta}(1-r,2r)\operatorname{Beta}(r,r) =
  \Gamma_E(r)\Gamma_E(1-r)$. All integrals are finite because $-r > -1$ and
  $2r-1 > -1$. At $s = 0$ the integral is directly $\operatorname{Beta}(r,1-r) =
  \Gamma_E(r)\Gamma_E(1-r)$; $s = 1$ follows by reflection. This proves
  \eqref{E:temporal-equilibrium-potential}, including the endpoints without a
  limiting argument there.

  Integrability and square integrability follow from $-r > -1$ and $-2r = a-1 >
  -1$. Finally,
  \[
    M_a=
    \frac{\operatorname{Beta}(1-r,1-r)}
    {\Gamma_E(r)\Gamma_E(1-r)}
    =\frac{\Gamma_E(1-r)}{\Gamma_E(2-2r)\Gamma_E(r)},
  \]
  which is~\eqref{E:temporal-equilibrium-mass}. Its time mass has not been
  renormalized. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:temporal-equilibrium}.
\end{proof}

\section{Physical spatial pairings and the full occupation class}
\label{S:spatial-occupation-foundation}

Retain a nonzero original covariance from Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} with its
amplitude. Set $a = \alpha_0$, $k = 3-\alpha > 1$, $q = 4-\alpha = k+1$ and $D =
q-2a > 0$. The scalar $D$ is fixed. All profiles are real. The original positive
temporal exponent and threshold imply $\alpha < 2$; this is the strict
inequality used in the scalar powers and concavity. The finite variational value
over nonnegative controls $S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}$ is that of
Proposition~\ref{P:variational-positive-scores}.

\LeanMarker{L:spatial-physical-pair}
\begin{lemma}[The physical two-point integral]\label{L:spatial-physical-pair}
  The positive measure $\nu(\ud x,\ud y) = \ud x\,\Gamma(\ud y)$ is
  sigma-finite. For nonnegative functions define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-physical-pair}
    B_\Gamma(f,h)=\int f(x)h(x-y)\nu(\ud x,\ud y)
  \end{equation}
  initially as an extended nonnegative integral, using finite Borel
  representatives of Lebesgue classes. Its value is representative independent.
  Lebesgue-a.e. convergence transfers to $\nu$-a.e. convergence at both
  positions $x$ and $x-y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Local finiteness of $\Gamma$ gives sigma-finiteness on exhausting balls, and
  Lebesgue measure is sigma-finite. If $N$ is Borel and Lebesgue null, Tonelli
  applied first in $x$ gives $\nu\{x\in N\} = 0$ and $\nu\{x-y\in N\} = 0$,
  because every fixed translate of $N$ is null. An arbitrary Lebesgue null set
  has a Borel null superset. Thus, arbitrary versions are measurable in the
  $\nu$ completion with the same value, and the same observation applies to the
  exceptional sets of a.e. convergence. Signed pairings will be used only after
  absolute convergence. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-physical-pair}.
\end{proof}

Put $A_{\mathrm{sp}}(g) = B_\Gamma(g^2,g^2)$ and $b(g) = \|\nabla g\|_2^2$ on
$H^1$; initially $A_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is used only for smooth compact profiles
before the extension below. For smooth compact $z$, write $U_z = \Gamma*z$ in
its usual pointwise sense.

\LeanMarker{L:spatial-static-controls}
\begin{lemma}[Smooth temporal controls]\label{L:spatial-static-controls}
  For every nonnegative nonzero $z \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$, let
  \[
    \lambda_z(c)^2=
    \sup_{\substack{g\in H^1(\R^d;\R)\\\|g\|_2=1}}
    \left(c\int U_zg^2-b(g)\right),\qquad c>0.
  \]
  This is positive and finite, and $\lambda_z$ is Lipschitz on every compact
  positive $c$ interval. With the exact $M_a$
  in~\eqref{E:temporal-equilibrium-mass},
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-static-score}
    S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}\ge
    \lambda_z(1)-\frac{M_a}{2}B_\Gamma(z,z).
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Choose nonnegative nonzero $f \in C_c^\infty(0,1)$ and $s_0 = 1/2$. Let
  $k_f(s_0) = \int_0^1|s_0-t|^{-a}f(t)\ud t > 0$. The smooth test $\Psi(t,x) =
  cf(t)z(x)/k_f(s_0)$ has mean $cU_z$ at $s_0$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:variational-unmasked-rate} gives boundedness of this
  potential, the displayed real Rayleigh formula, and a positive rate for every
  $c > 0$. Comparing identical unit tests makes $\lambda_z(c)^2$ Lipschitz with
  constant $\|U_z\|_\infty$. On a positive compact interval its rate has a
  positive floor by monotonicity, giving the square-root Lipschitz bound.

  Use the nonincreasing smooth cutoff $\chi$ constructed in the proof of
  Proposition~\ref{P:variational-remove-mask}. For integers $n \ge 4$, set on
  $(0,1)$
  \[
    f_n(t)=f_a(t)\chi(1/(nt))\chi(1/(n(1-t))),\qquad
    k_n(s)=\int_0^1|s-t|^{-a}f_n(t)\ud t.
  \]
  Extend $f_n$ by zero. It is smooth compact, vanishes near both endpoints, is
  nonzero, and increases to $f_a$. Thus, $\Psi_n = f_n\otimes z$ is a positive
  smooth control. Monotone convergence
  and~\eqref{E:temporal-equilibrium-potential} give $k_n(s)\uparrow1$ for every
  $s \in [0,1]$. Since the kernel is at least one on the unit square off its
  null diagonal, $0 < c_0: = \int_0^1f_4 \le k_n(s) \le 1$. The mean is
  $k_n(s)U_z$; hence its rate is $\lambda_z(k_n(s))$, converging to
  $\lambda_z(1)$ and bounded by that finite number. Dominated convergence gives
  $I_{\Psi_n} \to \lambda_z(1)$.

  The Gaussian cost is exactly the nonnegative Tonelli pairing
  \[
    v_{\Psi_n}=B_\Gamma(z,z)\int_0^1f_n(s)k_n(s)\ud s.
  \]
  Its time integrand increases to $f_a$, so the cost tends to
  $M_aB_\Gamma(z,z)$. The spatial factor is finite and positive: its smooth
  compact autocorrelation is positive near zero, and homogeneity scales any
  origin-centered ball of positive $\Gamma$ mass to every other positive radius.
  Such a ball exists by exhaustion. Take the limit of the individual
  inequalities $S_1^{\mathrm{pos}} \ge I_{\Psi_n}-v_{\Psi_n}/2$. The variational
  expression differences need not increase. No control depends on original large
  time and no rough stochastic evolution has been used. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-static-controls}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:spatial-fourier-bound}
\begin{lemma}[Deterministic Fourier bound for the spatial energy]
  \label{L:spatial-fourier-bound}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, allowing $\Gamma=0$, let
  $g\in C_c^\infty(\R^d;\R)$ satisfy $\|g\|_2=1$ and put
  $b=\|\nabla g\|_2^2$. Then $b>0$ and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-fourier-bound}
    0\le A_{\mathrm{sp}}(g)
    \le 2^{1+\alpha}R_\alpha b^{\alpha/2},
  \end{equation}
  where $R_\alpha$ is defined in~\eqref{E:intro-spectral-mass}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  If $b=0$, smoothness makes $\nabla g=0$ everywhere. Thus, $g$ is constant,
  and compact support forces $g=0$, contrary to $\|g\|_2=1$. Put $Q=g^2$ and
  use the unnormalized Fourier transform
  \[
    \mathcal{F}_0Q(\xi)=\int_{\R^d}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}Q(x)\ud x.
  \]
  In the unitary convention of Section~\ref{S:masked-covariance}, this is
  $c_d^{-1}\widehat Q$, with $c_d=(2\pi)^{-d/2}$.
  The autocorrelation
  \[
    c_Q(y)=\int_{\R^d}Q(x)Q(x-y)\ud x
  \]
  is smooth and compactly supported. Apply the distributional spectral
  identity~\eqref{E:masked-spectral-convention} to this Schwartz function.
  Its inner Fourier integral is $|\mathcal{F}_0Q(\xi)|^2$, since $Q$ is real.
  Hence
  \[
    A_{\mathrm{sp}}(g)=\int c_Q(y)\Gamma(\ud y)
    =\int|\mathcal{F}_0Q(\xi)|^2\mu(\ud\xi).
  \]
  Both integrals are finite: $c_Q$ is compactly supported and $\Gamma$ is
  Radon, while $|\mathcal{F}_0Q|^2$ is Schwartz and $\mu$ is tempered.
  No density of either measure is required.

  Since $\|Q\|_1=1$, we have $|\mathcal{F}_0Q|\le1$. Integration by parts
  and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality give
  \[
    |\xi|\,|\mathcal{F}_0Q(\xi)|
    \le\|\nabla Q\|_1
    \le2\|g\|_2\|\nabla g\|_2=2\sqrt b.
  \]
  Splitting into $|\xi|^2\le4b$ and $|\xi|^2>4b$ therefore yields
  \[
    |\mathcal{F}_0Q(\xi)|^2\le\frac{8b}{4b+|\xi|^2}.
  \]
  The deterministic resolvent calculation in the proof of
  Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}, together
  with~\eqref{E:intro-spectral-mass}, gives, for $q>0$,
  \[
    \mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q):=\int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{q^2+|\xi|^2}
    =q^{\alpha-2}R_\alpha<\infty.
  \]
  Consequently,
  \[
    A_{\mathrm{sp}}(g)\le8b\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(2\sqrt b)
    =2^{1+\alpha}R_\alpha b^{\alpha/2}.
  \]
  Nonnegativity follows from the spectral identity. If $\Gamma=0$, then
  $\mu=0$ and both sides of~\eqref{E:spatial-fourier-bound} vanish. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-fourier-bound}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-static-controls} gives a lower bound for each nonzero,
nonnegative smooth spatial control of compact support. We now scale and
dilate a single profile and optimize. The resulting bound depends on the
profile only through the ratio of $A_{\mathrm{sp}}$ to a power of $b$.

\LeanMarker{P:spatial-smooth-ratio}
\begin{proposition}[The finite smooth spatial ratio]
  \label{P:spatial-smooth-ratio}
  For real unit $g \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$ put $R(g) =
  A_{\mathrm{sp}}(g)/b(g)^{\alpha/2}$. Its supremum $R_0$ over these profiles is
  a positive finite real number, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-smooth-score}
    S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}\ge
    k\left[
    \frac{R(g)\alpha^{\alpha/2}}{8M_aq^{q/2}}
    \right]^{1/k}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The gradient energy $b(g)$ is positive: a nonzero $L^2$ function with zero
  weak gradient would have Fourier transform supported only at zero, and hence
  would vanish. Its square is nonnegative nonzero smooth compact, so the
  autocorrelation argument in the proof of
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-static-controls} gives
  $0 < A:=A_{\mathrm{sp}}(g)<\infty$. Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-fourier-bound} gives
  $R(g)\le2^{1+\alpha}R_\alpha$ for every such profile. A normalized compact
  bump supplies a positive ratio. Thus, $0<R_0<\infty$, independently of the
  finiteness of $S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}$. It remains to prove the score inequality.
  With $U_\ell g(x)=\ell^{d/2}g(\ell x)$, the physical changes of variables give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-dilation}
    A_{\mathrm{sp}}(U_\ell g)=\ell^\alpha A,\qquad
    b(U_\ell g)=\ell^2b(g).
  \end{equation}
  The factors in the first identity are $\ell^{2d}$ from the two squares,
  $\ell^{-d}$ from $\ud x$, and $\ell^{\alpha-d}$ from the Radon pushforward.

  For $\theta,\ell > 0$ choose the smooth control $z = \theta(U_\ell g)^2$. The
  Rayleigh test $U_\ell g$ gives $\lambda_z(1)^2 \ge \theta\ell^\alpha
  A-\ell^2b$, while $B_\Gamma(z,z) = \theta^2\ell^\alpha A$. For any $L > 0$,
  put $u = \alpha/q > 0$ and choose
  \[
    \ell^2=uL^2/b,\qquad
    \theta=(1+u)L^2/(\ell^\alpha A).
  \]
  The radicand is $L^2$. Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-static-controls} consequently
  gives
  \[
    S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}\ge L-CL^q,\qquad
    C=\frac{M_a(1+u)^2}{2R(g)u^{\alpha/2}}>0.
  \]
  Its maximum occurs at $L^k = 1/(qC)$ and is $(k/q)(qC)^{-1/k}$. Since $1+u =
  4/q$, $1/(q^qC) = R(g)\alpha^{\alpha/2}/(8M_aq^{q/2})$,
  proving~\eqref{E:spatial-smooth-score}. This argument retains the finite
  real value $S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}$ fixed at the start of the section; only the
  proof of $R_0<\infty$ is independent of that input. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-smooth-ratio}.
\end{proof}

So far $A_{\mathrm{sp}}$ has been used only for smooth compact profiles.
Proposition~\ref{P:occupation-physical-class} needs it and the square pairings
for real $H^1$ profiles, so we extend them with the same constant $R_0$.

\LeanMarker{P:spatial-four-linear}
\begin{proposition}[The physical four-linear extension]
  \label{P:spatial-four-linear}
  For real $f \in H^1(\R^d)$ define $N(f) = \|f\|_2^{1-\alpha/4}\|\nabla
  f\|_2^{\alpha/4}$. Every physical square pairing is finite and
  \begin{align}
    A_{\mathrm{sp}}(f)&\le R_0N(f)^4,\label{E:spatial-homogeneous-bound}\\
    0\le B_\Gamma(f^2,h^2)
    &\le\sqrt{A_{\mathrm{sp}}(f)A_{\mathrm{sp}}(h)}.
    \label{E:spatial-square-CS}
  \end{align}
  The signed integral $C_4(f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4) = B_\Gamma(f_1f_2,f_3f_4)$ is
  absolutely convergent and jointly continuous on $(H^1)^4$, with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-four-linear}
    \int|f_1(x)f_2(x)f_3(x-y)f_4(x-y)|\ud\nu
    \le R_0\prod_{j=1}^4N(f_j).
  \end{equation}
  The ratio supremum $R_{\mathrm{sp}}$ over all real unit $H^1$ profiles equals
  $R_0$. Every nonzero $H^1$ profile has $A_{\mathrm{sp}}(f) > 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For smooth compact $f$, normalization in $R_0$ proves
  \eqref{E:spatial-homogeneous-bound}, including $f = 0$. All exponents in $N$
  are positive and $N(f) \le \|f\|_{H^1}$. For smooth compact signed $Q,P$, the
  absolute correlation is compactly supported and bounded by $\|Q\|_2\|P\|_2$.
  Local finiteness of $\Gamma$ therefore makes its physical integral finite.
  Smooth signed covariance is positive: with the ordinary transform
  $\widehat{Q}_{\mathrm{ord}}(\xi) = \int e^{i\xi\cdot x}Q(x)\ud x$, the model
  convention gives
  \[
    B_\Gamma(Q,Q)=\int|\widehat{Q}_{\mathrm{ord}}(\xi)|^2\mu(\ud\xi)
    \ge0,\qquad Q\in C_c^\infty(\R^d;\R).
  \]
  Pair $\Gamma$ with the smooth compact correlation to obtain this identity;
  rapid Fourier decay and tempered $\mu$ give finiteness. Evenness of $\Gamma$
  and $(x,y)\mapsto(x-y,-y)$ give symmetry. The quadratic polynomial for $Q+tP$
  gives covariance Cauchy--Schwarz. In particular, for smooth real $f,h$,
  \[
    0\le B_\Gamma(f^2,h^2)
    \le R_0N(f)^2N(h)^2.
  \]
  The squares of signed smooth profiles are already smooth and nonnegative.

  Smooth compact functions are dense in $H^1$. Cutoffs equal to one on the
  radius-$R$ ball, vanishing beyond $2R$ and with gradient at most $C/R$,
  converge by the function and gradient tails and the bound $C\|f\|_2/R$ for the
  extra gradient term. Mollification then converges in $H^1$ by $L^2$
  translation continuity of the function and its weak gradients. The space is
  separable through its closed isometric image $f\mapsto(f,\nabla f)$ in
  $L^2(\R^d)^{d+1}$.

  For arbitrary $f,h \in H^1$ take smooth approximants converging in $H^1$ and
  along a common subsequence Lebesgue-a.e. Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-physical-pair}
  transfers this convergence at both positions to $\nu$-a.e. convergence. Fatou
  and continuity of $N$ give $B_\Gamma(f^2,h^2) \le R_0N(f)^2N(h)^2 < \infty$,
  including the diagonal bound. At this stage Fatou supplies an inequality, not
  continuity.

  Apply ordinary Cauchy--Schwarz under the positive measure $\nu$ to
  $f_1(x)f_3(x-y)$ and $f_2(x)f_4(x-y)$. The two squared integrals are finite by
  the last bound; their square root is at most $R_0\prod_jN(f_j)$. This
  proves~\eqref{E:spatial-four-linear}, absolute convergence and multilinearity.
  Telescoping a difference one argument at a time and using $N(f) \le
  \|f\|_{H^1}$ proves joint continuity. Thus, $A_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and the square
  pairings are continuous. PSD for smooth compact profiles passes to finite
  linear combinations of $H^1$ squares by this continuity, and its two-profile
  Cauchy--Schwarz gives \eqref{E:spatial-square-CS}. This completes the physical
  pairing argument beyond Fatou.

  Every real unit $H^1$ profile has positive gradient energy by the Fourier
  argument above. The homogeneous bound gives $R_{\mathrm{sp}} \le R_0$, and
  smooth inclusion gives the reverse. Normalized smooth approximations preserve
  the ratio by continuity. Finally, for nonzero $f$, choose a bounded set $E$ of
  finite positive measure where $|f| \ge c > 0$. The correlation of $1_E$ is
  continuous and positive near zero by $L^2$ translation continuity. An
  origin-centered ball there has positive $\Gamma$ mass, so $A_{\mathrm{sp}}(f)
  \ge c^4B_\Gamma(1_E,1_E) > 0$. This uses a nonnegative integral, not an $H^1$
  claim for $1_E$. Finiteness was already proved. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-four-linear}.
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-four-linear}, the ratio supremum is the same
over smooth and over $H^1$ profiles. We now express it through an energy and
restate the score bound in those terms.

\LeanMarker{P:spatial-energy-map}
\begin{proposition}[The spatial energy and static lower variational expression]
  \label{P:spatial-energy-map}
  Over all real unit $H^1$ profiles define the energy
  \[
    \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}
    =\sup_{\|g\|_2=1}
    \left(A_{\mathrm{sp}}(g)-\frac{b(g)}2\right).
  \]
  Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-energy-map}
    \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}
    =\frac{2-\alpha}{2}\alpha^{\alpha/(2-\alpha)}
    R_{\mathrm{sp}}^{2/(2-\alpha)}
    \in(0,\infty).
  \end{equation}
  In particular,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-static-energy-score}
    S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}\ge M_a^{-1/k}F_0(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}),
    \qquad
    F_0(z)=k\left[
    \frac{(2z/(2-\alpha))^{(2-\alpha)/2}}{8q^{q/2}}
    \right]^{1/k},\quad z>0.
  \end{equation}
  The subscript in $F_0$ labels this scalar map; the covariance retains its
  original temporal exponent $a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Physical change of variables, or smooth approximation and
  Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-four-linear}, extends \eqref{E:spatial-dilation} to
  real $H^1$ profiles. For fixed unit $g$, with $A,b > 0$, the maximum of
  $\ell^\alpha A-\ell^2b/2$ occurs at $\ell^{2-\alpha} = \alpha A/b$ and equals
  \[
    \frac{2-\alpha}{2}\alpha^{\alpha/(2-\alpha)}
    R(g)^{2/(2-\alpha)}.
  \]
  Dilation preserves the whole unit class and includes $\ell = 1$. Taking both
  numerical suprema, then profiles with ratio tending to $R_{\mathrm{sp}}$,
  proves~\eqref{E:spatial-energy-map}. No optimizer is assumed. Smooth ratios
  also approach $R_{\mathrm{sp}}$ by Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-four-linear}.
  Pass them through~\eqref{E:spatial-smooth-score} and substitute
  \[
    R_{\mathrm{sp}}\alpha^{\alpha/2}
    =\left(\frac{2\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}}{2-\alpha}
    \right)^{(2-\alpha)/2}.
  \]
  This gives~\eqref{E:spatial-static-energy-score}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-energy-map}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The kernel row and full occupation energy}

Define, for $u \ne 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{E:occupation-damped-kernel}
  K_\star(u)=\frac1{\Gamma_E(a)}
  \int_0^\infty
  e^{-(\lambda+\lambda^2/(2D))|u|}
  \lambda^{a-1}\ud\lambda,
\end{equation}
and set $K_\star(0) = 0$ for time integration. The spatial estimates above
concern individual $H^1$ profiles. To apply them to time-dependent families,
we first compute the exact row bound for this time kernel.

\LeanMarker{L:occupation-kernel-row}
\begin{lemma}[Exact row maximum]\label{L:occupation-kernel-row}
  The kernel is even, positive off zero, locally integrable, decreasing on
  $(0,\infty)$, and $K_\star(u) \le |u|^{-a}$ off zero. Its exact row maximum on
  $[0,1]$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:occupation-kernel-row}
    \max_{s\in[0,1]}\int_0^1K_\star(s-t)\ud t
    =R_\star:=2\int_0^{1/2}K_\star(u)\ud u\in(0,\infty).
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Dropping the positive quadratic damping bounds the mixture by the Gamma
  integral $|u|^{-a}$. Positivity and monotonicity follow directly from the
  integrand, and $a < 1$ gives local integrability. Put $\Phi(t) =
  \int_0^tK_\star(u)\ud u$. It is concave because its integrand is decreasing on
  positive arguments. The row is $\Phi(s)+\Phi(1-s) \le 2\Phi(1/2)$, with
  equality at $s = 1/2$. The diagonal value changes no time integral. This
  proves Lemma~\ref{L:occupation-kernel-row}.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the full class of strongly $H^1(\R^d;\R)$-measurable fields
$s\mapsto g_s$ on $(0,1)$ with $\|g_s\|_2 = 1$ a.e. and $T(g) =
\int_0^1b(g_s)\ud s < \infty$. No time smoothness, common compact support or
optimizer condition is imposed.

\LeanMarker{P:occupation-physical-class}
\begin{proposition}[The full physical occupation class]
  \label{P:occupation-physical-class}
  The pairings $(s,t)\mapsto B_\Gamma(g_s^2,g_t^2)$ are measurable,
  representative independent and equal to their physical integrals. With
  \[
    J(g)=\int_0^1\int_0^1
    K_\star(s-t)B_\Gamma(g_s^2,g_t^2)\ud s\ud t,
  \]
  every $g \in \mathcal{O}$ satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:occupation-physical-bound}
    0\le J(g)\le R_\star R_{\mathrm{sp}}T(g)^{\alpha/2}<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  The energy over exactly this full class satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:occupation-own-energy}
    0<\mathcal{E}_\star^{\mathcal{O}}
    :=\sup_{g\in\mathcal{O}}\big(J(g)-T(g)/2\big)
    \le R_\star^{2/(2-\alpha)}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}<\infty.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The map $s\mapsto b(g_s)$ is measurable by continuity of the squared gradient
  norm. Also $\int_0^1\|g_s\|_{H^1}^2\ud s = 1+T(g)$. Use a separably valued
  strong representative and replace exceptional null times by one fixed real
  unit compact bump. Joint measurable spatial representatives exist: approximate
  this Bochner $L^2$ field by simple $H^1$-valued functions with summable
  $L^2(0,1;H^1)$ errors, and choose measurable spatial versions for their
  countably many values. A subsequence has a measurable spacetime $L^2$ limit,
  and for a.e. $s$ its spatial $L^2$ class is $g_s$.
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-physical-pair} makes every pairing independent of the
  spatial versions. The continuous pairing map on the separable space $H^1\times
  H^1$ from Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-four-linear} consequently gives a
  measurable function of $(s,t)$ agreeing a.e. with the nonnegative physical
  integral.

  Set $F(s) = b(g_s)^{\alpha/4}$. Equations~\eqref{E:spatial-homogeneous-bound}
  and~\eqref{E:spatial-square-CS} give $B_\Gamma(g_s^2,g_t^2) \le
  R_{\mathrm{sp}}F(s)F(t)$. Since $0 < \alpha/2 < 1$, concavity on the unit time
  interval yields $\int_0^1F^2 = \int_0^1b(g_s)^{\alpha/2}\ud s \le
  T(g)^{\alpha/2}$. Now use $2F(s)F(t) \le F(s)^2+F(t)^2$ and the exact row
  bound \eqref{E:occupation-kernel-row}, whose column bound is the same because
  $K_\star$ is even, to obtain \eqref{E:occupation-physical-bound}. Positive
  Tonelli is valid before finiteness is known. Null-time modifications affect
  only a product-null set, so they cannot change $J$.

  The scalar maximum of $R_\star R_{\mathrm{sp}}T^{\alpha/2}-T/2$ over $T \ge 0$
  is $R_\star^{2/(2-\alpha)}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}$, by the same
  differentiation as~\eqref{E:spatial-energy-map}. This bounds the occupation
  supremum from above. For positivity take a constant-in-time unit compact
  profile and its spatial dilation $U_\ell$. Its $J$ is $J_0\ell^\alpha
  A_{\mathrm{sp}}(g)$, where $J_0 = \int_0^1\int_0^1K_\star(s-t)\ud s\ud t \in
  (0,\infty)$, while $T = \ell^2b(g)$. For sufficiently small $\ell > 0$,
  $\alpha < 2$ makes $J-T/2 > 0$. Thus, the nonempty supremum is a positive
  finite real number. This is a comparison bound; no attaining profile or
  equality in it has been asserted. No spatial point trace or rough stochastic
  evolution was used. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:occupation-physical-class}.
\end{proof}

For zero covariance all spatial pairings vanish and set $R_{\mathrm{sp}} = 0$.
The spatial and occupation energies are zero suprema of $-b/2$ and $-T/2$: small
spatial dilations of one fixed unit compact profile make these energies tend to
zero, with the profile constant in time for the latter. No zero-gradient unit
state is used. The temporal identities remain valid, and the moment model is the
free case already treated.

\section{Occupation bounds for the intrinsic constant}
\label{S:intrinsic-occupation-ceiling}

Retain all original models and amplitudes, put $a = \alpha_0$, $q = 4-\alpha >
2$, $k = 3-\alpha = q-1 > 1$ and $m = q/k$. The temporal kernel is $K(s,t) =
|s-t|^{-a}$ on $I = (0,1)$; its diagonal value may be set to zero. Let
$\mathcal{O}$ be the full strongly $H^1$-measurable unit-profile class defined
before Proposition~\ref{P:occupation-physical-class}. Here we use the
\emph{original} kernel, defining
\begin{equation}\label{E:raw-occupation-definitions}
  \begin{aligned}
    A(g)&=\int_{I^2}K(s,t)B_\Gamma(g_s^2,g_t^2)\ud s\ud t,\\
    C_{\rm occ}&=\sup_{g\in\mathcal{O}}
    \frac{A(g)}{T(g)^{\alpha/2}},&
    \mathcal{E}_\zeta^{\mathcal{O}}
    &=\sup_{g\in\mathcal{O}}\{A(g)-\zeta T(g)\},\qquad \zeta>0.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The scalar penalty $\zeta$ is not a spatial mask. These objects differ from the
earlier damped-kernel energy. Assume $\Gamma \ne 0$ until the direct zero case
below.

\LeanMarker{L:occupation-raw-square}
\begin{lemma}[Full-profile squares in the original space]
  \label{L:occupation-raw-square}
  There is a continuous map $Q:H^1(\R^d;\R) \to H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ with
  \[
    \langle Q(f),Q(h)\rangle_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}
    =B_\Gamma(f^2,h^2),\qquad
    \|Q(f)\|^2=A_{\mathrm{sp}}(f).
  \]
  For $g \in \mathcal{O}$, the element $\rho_g = J_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}(s\mapsto
  Q(g_s))$ belongs to the original $H_I$ and satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:occupation-raw-identities}
    \|\rho_g\|_{H_I}^2=A(g),\qquad
    \langle\Psi,\rho_g\rangle_{H_I}
    =\int_I\int_{\R^d}h_{\Psi,s}(x)g_s(x)^2\ud x\ud s
  \end{equation}
  for every $\Psi \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$. In particular,
  \[
    \langle\Psi,\rho_g\rangle_{H_I}\le\sqrt{v_\Psi A(g)}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Approximate $f$ in $H^1$ by real compact smooth $f_n$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-four-linear} gives
  \[
    \|f_n^2-f_l^2\|_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}^2
    =C_4(f_n-f_l,f_n+f_l,f_n-f_l,f_n+f_l)
    \le R_{\mathrm{sp}}N(f_n-f_l)^2N(f_n+f_l)^2\to0.
  \]
  Here $N$ is continuous on $H^1$ and $N(f) \le \|f\|_{H^1}$. Completeness
  defines $Q(f)$; interlacing two approximating sequences proves independence.
  Passing the physical four-linear continuity and Hilbert pairings to limits
  proves the first identities. The same difference bound, first for smooth
  approximants and then by passage to the limit, proves continuity of $Q$.

  Thus, $F_g(s) = Q(g_s)$ is strongly measurable. Writing $b_s = \|\nabla
  g_s\|_2^2$, the unit norm and $0 < \alpha/2 < 1$ give
  \[
    \int_I\|F_g(s)\|^2\ud s
    \le R_{\mathrm{sp}}\int_I b_s^{\alpha/2}\ud s
    \le R_{\mathrm{sp}}T(g)^{\alpha/2}<\infty .
  \]
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-raw-embedding} now constructs $\rho_g$ and identifies
  its squared norm with the physical $A(g)$. Joint measurability and
  representative independence follow from the continuous physical pairing on
  $(H^1)^2$. Absolute time integrability follows from the same Schur bound.

  Choose one compact smooth $\chi$ equal to one on all spatial supports of
  $\Psi_t$. For real $f \in H^1$, smooth approximation gives
  \[
    \langle\Psi_t,Q(f)\rangle_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}
    =\lim_n B_\Gamma(\Psi_t,f_n^2)
    =C_4(\chi,\Psi_t,f,f)=B_\Gamma(\Psi_t,f^2).
  \]
  Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-four-linear} justifies these absolute integrals and
  limits. Polarizing~\eqref{E:positive-bochner-inclusion} gives
  \[
    \langle\Psi,\rho_g\rangle
    =\int_{I^2}K(s,t)\langle\Psi_t,Q(g_s)\rangle_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}
    \ud t\ud s .
  \]
  The absolute integrand is bounded by the product of two $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$
  norms against $K$, integrable by Schur and $L^2$ Cauchy--Schwarz. Evenness of
  $\Gamma$ and Tonelli for the nonnegative physical integrands
  yield~\eqref{E:occupation-raw-identities}. Hilbert Cauchy--Schwarz with
  $\|\Psi\|^2 = v_\Psi$ proves the bound. No rough occupation is inserted into a
  stochastic flow. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:occupation-raw-square}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{P:raw-occupation-energy}
\begin{proposition}[Original occupation energy and dilation]
  \label{P:raw-occupation-energy}
  With $K_0 = 2^a/(1-a)$,
  \[
    0<C_{\rm occ}\le K_0R_{\mathrm{sp}}<\infty .
  \]
  Moreover,
  \begin{align}
    \mathcal{E}_\zeta^{\mathcal{O}}
    &=D_\zeta C_{\rm occ}^{2/(2-\alpha)},&
    D_\zeta&=\frac{2-\alpha}{2}
    \left(\frac{\alpha}{2\zeta}\right)^{\alpha/(2-\alpha)},
    \label{E:raw-occupation-energy}\\
    C_{\rm occ}
    &=\left(\frac{2\mathcal{E}_\zeta^{\mathcal{O}}}{2-\alpha}\right)^{(2-\alpha)/2}
    \left(\frac{2\zeta}{\alpha}\right)^{\alpha/2}.
    \label{E:raw-occupation-ratio}
  \end{align}
  In particular, $\mathcal{E}_\zeta^{\mathcal{O}} > 0$ is finite and equals
  $\zeta^{-\alpha/(2-\alpha)}\mathcal{E}_1^{\mathcal{O}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  A unit $H^1$ profile has positive gradient energy: a zero weak gradient makes
  it constant, and an $L^2$ constant on $\R^d$ is zero. Hence, $0 < T(g) <
  \infty$. Spatial covariance Cauchy--Schwarz gives $B_\Gamma(g_s^2,g_t^2) \le
  R_{\mathrm{sp}}b_s^{\alpha/4}b_t^{\alpha/4}$. Schur with row bound $K_0$, then
  concavity, proves
  \begin{equation}\label{E:raw-occupation-bound}
    0\le A(g)\le K_0R_{\mathrm{sp}}T(g)^{\alpha/2}.
  \end{equation}
  A fixed compact smooth unit profile, constant in time, belongs to
  $\mathcal{O}$ and has positive $A$ by Proposition~\ref{P:spatial-four-linear}.
  This proves positivity and finiteness of $C_{\rm occ}$.

  The map $(U_\ell g)_s(x) = \ell^{d/2}g_s(\ell x)$ is an $H^1$ isomorphism
  preserving unit mass and strong measurability. It has inverse $U_{1/\ell}$ and
  is a bijection on $\mathcal{O}$. The physical Radon change of variables gives
  \[
    A(U_\ell g)=\ell^\alpha A(g),\qquad
    T(U_\ell g)=\ell^2T(g).
  \]
  Indeed, the two densities contribute $\ell^{2d}$, $\ud x$ contributes
  $\ell^{-d}$, and the pushforward of $\Gamma$ contributes $\ell^{\alpha-d}$.
  Approximation extends the identity to full $H^1$. Taking the union over
  $g,\ell$ leaves the class unchanged. For fixed $A,T > 0$, the derivative of
  $A\ell^\alpha-\zeta T\ell^2$ has the sign of $\alpha A-2\zeta
  T\ell^{2-\alpha}$. Its global maximum occurs at $\ell = (\alpha A/(2\zeta
  T))^{1/(2-\alpha)}$ and equals $D_\zeta[A/T^{\alpha/2}]^{2/(2-\alpha)}$. For
  $A = 0$ the supremum is zero as $\ell\downarrow0$. Bounding every ratio by
  $C_{\rm occ}$ gives one inequality in~\eqref{E:raw-occupation-energy}. Fixed
  ratios approaching its positive supremum give the other by continuity of the
  positive power. Algebra gives~\eqref{E:raw-occupation-ratio} and the penalty
  scaling. No optimizer or minimax interchange is assumed. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:raw-occupation-energy}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:occupation-rayleigh-selection}
\begin{lemma}[Countable Rayleigh near-occupations]
  \label{L:occupation-rayleigh-selection}
  For fixed $\Psi \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$
  there is $g_\varepsilon \in \mathcal{O}$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{E:occupation-rayleigh-selection}
    I_\Psi^2+T(g_\varepsilon)
    \le\sqrt{v_\Psi A(g_\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon .
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The real $H^1$ space is separable, for example by its isometric embedding
  $f\mapsto(f,\nabla f)$ into a finite product of $L^2$ spaces. Compact smooth
  density therefore supplies a countable real family. Discard zero and normalize
  each member in $L^2$ to obtain $f_j$ dense in the $L^2$-unit $H^1$ class.
  Normalization preserves convergence because the approximating $L^2$ norms tend
  to one. Put $b_j = \|\nabla f_j\|_2^2$ and $R_j(s) = \int
  h_{\Psi,s}f_j^2-b_j$. Bounded-potential form continuity and
  Proposition~\ref{P:variational-unmasked-rate} give $\lambda_{\Psi,s}^2 =
  \sup_jR_j(s)$. The canonical functions are measurable, indeed continuous. Let
  $U_j = \{s:R_j(s)>\lambda_{\Psi,s}^2-\varepsilon\}$, $A_1 = U_1$ and $A_j =
  U_j\setminus\bigcup_{i<j}U_i$. These first-index cells are Borel and cover
  $I$. Put $g_\varepsilon(s) = f_j$ on $A_j$. Finite-valued truncations using
  $f_1$ on the remaining cells converge pointwise in $H^1$, proving strong
  measurability. A null-set representative may be filled by $f_1$.

  With $H_\Psi = \sup_{s,x}h_{\Psi,s}(x) < \infty$, the selection implies
  \[
    b(g_\varepsilon(s))
    <\int h_{\Psi,s}g_\varepsilon(s)^2-\lambda_{\Psi,s}^2+\varepsilon
    \le H_\Psi+\varepsilon .
  \]
  Thus, it belongs to the $\mathcal{O}$. Integrating the near-Rayleigh
  inequality, using Lemma~\ref{L:occupation-raw-square}, gives
  \[
    \int_I\lambda_{\Psi,s}^2\ud s+T(g_\varepsilon)
    \le\langle\Psi,\rho_{g_\varepsilon}\rangle+\varepsilon
    \le\sqrt{v_\Psi A(g_\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon .
  \]
  Time Cauchy--Schwarz on the unit interval gives $I_\Psi^2 \le
  \int_I\lambda_{\Psi,s}^2\ud s$. This
  proves~\eqref{E:occupation-rayleigh-selection}. No eigenvector, continuum
  selector or limiting occupation is required. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:occupation-rayleigh-selection}.
\end{proof}

For $0 \le b < q/2$ define $F_b(0) = 0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{E:occupation-scalar-map}
  F_b(z)=k\left[
  \frac{(q-2b)^{(q-2b)/2}}{8(q-b)^{q-b}}
  \left(\frac{2z}{2-\alpha}\right)^{(2-\alpha)/2}
  \right]^{1/k},\qquad z>0.
\end{equation}
At $b = 0$ this is exactly the map in~\eqref{E:spatial-static-energy-score}. The
display parameter $b$ does not change the temporal exponent $a$.

\LeanMarker{T:intrinsic-occupation-ceiling}
\begin{theorem}[The computed occupation ceiling]\label{T:intrinsic-occupation-ceiling}
  For every variational value over nonnegative controls coefficient
  $\mathfrak{c}$,
  \begin{align}
    S_{\mathfrak{c}}^{\mathrm{pos}}
    &\le U_{\mathfrak{c}}
    :=\mathfrak{c}^m k
    \left[\frac{\alpha^{\alpha/2}C_{\rm occ}}{8q^{q/2}}\right]^{1/k}
    \label{E:occupation-score-ceiling}\\
    &=\mathfrak{c}^m(2\zeta)^{\alpha/(2k)}
    F_0(\mathcal{E}_\zeta^{\mathcal{O}}),\qquad \zeta>0.\notag
  \end{align}
  In particular, the original moment constant satisfies $C_2(\Gamma,a) \le U_2$
  for every original model. This ceiling is not asserted to be attained or equal
  to $C_2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Fix $\Psi$ and write $v = v_\Psi$, $C = C_{\rm occ}$ and $\beta = \alpha/4$.
  Lemma~\ref{L:occupation-rayleigh-selection} gives $I_\Psi^2 \le
  \sqrt{vC}T^\beta-T+\varepsilon$ for some $T > 0$. Bound it by the supremum
  over all $T \ge 0$ before sending $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. If $v = 0$, that
  supremum is zero. If $v > 0$, the derivative $\beta\sqrt{vC}T^{\beta-1}-1$
  strictly decreases from infinity to $-1$. Its unique positive zero is $T_* =
  (\beta\sqrt{vC})^{1/(1-\beta)}$. The global maximum equals
  $(q/4)(\alpha/4)^{\alpha/q}(vC)^{2/q}$. In both cases,
  \[
    I_\Psi\le Dv^{1/q},\qquad
    D=\sqrt{q/4}(\alpha/4)^{\alpha/(2q)}C^{1/q}.
  \]
  The scalar function $\mathfrak{c} Dv^{1/q}-v/2$ vanishes at zero, tends to
  minus infinity, and has strictly decreasing derivative. Its unique positive
  maximum is at $v_* = (2\mathfrak{c} D/q)^{q/k}$, with value $kv_*/2$. Direct
  positive-power algebra gives
  \[
    (2D/q)^q=q^{-q/2}(\alpha/4)^{\alpha/2}C .
  \]
  Since $k+\alpha = 3$, the maximum is precisely $U_{\mathfrak{c}}$
  in~\eqref{E:occupation-score-ceiling}, with denominator eight. Substitution
  of~\eqref{E:raw-occupation-ratio} gives the second form, retaining the factor
  $(2\zeta)^{\alpha/(2k)}$. Take the supremum over the smooth positive-control
  class. Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment} identifies $C_2 =
  S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$, proving the moment ceiling. The penalty factor cancels
  its energy scaling, so the ceiling is independent of $\zeta$. This proves
  Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-occupation-ceiling}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{C:intrinsic-spatial-bracket}
\begin{corollary}[A common spatial-energy bracket]
  \label{C:intrinsic-spatial-bracket}
  With the same original covariance and $M_a$
  of~\eqref{E:temporal-equilibrium-mass},
  \begin{equation}\label{E:intrinsic-spatial-bracket}
    2^m M_a^{-1/k}F_0(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}})
    \le C_2(\Gamma,a)
    \le2^m K_0^{1/k}F_0(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}}).
  \end{equation}
  For $\Gamma \ne 0$ there is also the comparison of the energies defined here
  \[
    C_2\le2^mF_0(\mathcal{E}_{1/2}^{\mathcal{O}})
    <2^mF_a(\mathcal{E}_{1/2}^{\mathcal{O}}).
  \]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Insert $C_{\rm occ} \le K_0R_{\mathrm{sp}}$ into
  \eqref{E:occupation-score-ceiling} and use \eqref{E:spatial-energy-map}. This
  gives the upper bracket; the lower is \eqref{E:spatial-static-energy-score}
  multiplied by $2^m$, using Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}. Both
  concern one common spatial energy, not an assumed first moment.

  For completeness put
  \[
    \Delta=\tfrac12[q\log q+(q-2a)\log(q-2a)]
    -(q-a)\log(q-a)>0.
  \]
  Here $a > 0$ and $q-2a > 2-a > 1$. The scalar inequality for the difference of
  logarithms applies at exactly these conditions, without $q \ge 3$.
  Equivalently the strict midpoint convexity of $x\log x$ on positive arguments,
  whose second derivative is $1/x$, gives the sign. Subtracting logarithms
  in~\eqref{E:occupation-scalar-map} gives
  \[
    \log(F_a(z)/F_0(z))=\Delta/k,\qquad
    \frac{\mathfrak{c}^mF_a(\mathcal{E}_\zeta^{\mathcal{O}})}{U_{\mathfrak{c}}}
    =\exp\!\left(\frac{\Delta-(\alpha/2)\log(2\zeta)}{k}\right).
  \]
  At $\zeta = 1/2$ this and the ceiling prove the strict comparison. At $\zeta =
  1$ the comparison is not universally favorable. For $\alpha = 5/4,a = 1/2,d =
  2$,
  \[
    8[\Delta-(\alpha/2)\log2]
    =\log\frac{11^{11}7^7}{2^5 9^{18}}<0,
  \]
  since $11^{11}7^7 = 234966429149994773$ and $2^5 9^{18} =
  4803028329503971872$. This contrasts computed bounds; it refutes no moment
  inequality. This proves Corollary~\ref{C:intrinsic-spatial-bracket}.
\end{proof}

The kinetic normalization matters in this comparison.
No-go~\ref{P:nogo-28} shows that changing it can reverse an ordering
between the computed ceiling and a comparison value. That example makes
no claim about the ordering of the actual moment constant.


For $\Gamma = 0$, all original means and variational values vanish and $C_2 =
0$. Here $Q = 0$, $A = 0$ and $C_{\rm occ} = 0$. The supremum of $-\zeta T(g)$
is zero: dilate a fixed unit compact profile by $\ell^{d/2}f(\ell x)$ and let
$\ell\downarrow0$. The same argument gives $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sp}} = 0$ and
the earlier damped energy zero, without using a zero vector as a unit profile.
With $F_b(0) = 0$, every non-strict bound above is the zero identity. These
energies are not identified with Chen's printed energies.

\section{A variational formula for the second-moment limit}
\label{S:intrinsic-second-moment}

Retain every original covariance and amplitude. Write $a = \alpha_0$, $I =
(0,1)$, $K(s,t) = |s-t|^{-a}$ and $H_I = H_t\otimes H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ for the
original covariance. Keep the uniform $E$ mean $\mathcal{T}_R = T_R(C_t\otimes
I)$ and $C_{\rm mean} = M_0\|C_t\|$ of
Section~\ref{S:uniform-physical-products}, with exactly the fixed $r$ chosen
there. In particular, $2 < r < 2/a$. To distinguish it from the map below, write
$J_R = T_R$. For a bounded multiplier $V:H^{1/2} \to H^{-1/2}$ write
$B_E(V)(u,v) = (0,Vu)$, the $E$ operator of Lemma~\ref{L:uniform-E-lift}. The
physical mask is $m_R = \eta(\cdot/R)^2$, used once. Let
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$ be the full nonseparable class
in~\eqref{E:variational-positive-class}. For $\Psi$ in that class, retain its
unmasked mean $h_{\Psi,s}$, original variance $v_\Psi$, integrated full-gradient
rate $I_\Psi$ and variational values for nonnegative controls
$S_c^{\mathrm{pos}}$ from Section~\ref{S:unmasked-variational}. The value with
coefficient one is not identified with a first-moment limit or a constant in the
cited source. We assume $\Gamma \ne 0$ through the following comparison lemmas.
The theorem treats zero covariance directly.

\LeanMarker{L:positive-raw-embedding}
\begin{lemma}[Inclusion into the covariance space and the smooth mean]
  \label{L:positive-raw-embedding}
  For any real Hilbert space $G$ there is a bounded map $J_G:L^2(I;G) \to
  H_t\otimes G$ with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-bochner-inclusion}
    \|J_GF\|^2
    =\int_{I^2}K(s,t)\langle F(s),F(t)\rangle_G\ud s\ud t
    \le K_0\|F\|_{L^2(I;G)}^2,\qquad K_0=\frac{2^a}{1-a}.
  \end{equation}
  The natural inclusion puts every $\Psi \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$ in
  $H_I$, with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-raw-mean}
    \|\Psi\|_{H_I}^2=v_\Psi,\qquad
    \mathcal{T}_R\Psi(s)=B_E(m_Rh_{\Psi,s})\quad\hbox{a.e. }s.
  \end{equation}
  For each fixed smooth $\Psi$ the right side gives a canonical version for
  every $R \ge 1$ and every $s \in I$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Direct integration and concavity of $x^{1-a}$ give
  \[
    \int_I K(s,t)\ud t
    =\frac{s^{1-a}+(1-s)^{1-a}}{1-a}\le K_0 .
  \]
  For scalar $f \ge 0$, Cauchy--Schwarz against $K(s,t)\ud t$ and then Tonelli
  give $\|Kf\|_2 \le K_0\|f\|_2$. The same estimate for absolute values bounds
  the bilinear integral by $K_0\|f\|_2\|g\|_2$. Approximate scalar $L^2(I)$
  functions by real $C_c^\infty(I)$ functions. This bound makes their original
  test-null classes Cauchy in $H_t$, defines the bounded inclusion, and
  identifies its pairing with the integral. Finite $G$-valued tensors then give
  \eqref{E:positive-bochner-inclusion}. Simple fields are dense in Bochner
  $L^2(I;G)$; the same bound on differences extends $J_G$. Absolute bilinear
  continuity extends the integral identity as well.

  For $G = H_{\mathrm{sp}}$, the Bochner integral $\int_IK(s,t)F(t)\ud t$ exists
  almost everywhere: its norm is bounded by $K\|F\|(s)$, which belongs to $L^2$.
  Simple-field approximation and the Schur estimate give a strongly measurable
  $L^2$ version and a bounded map of norm at most $K_0$. On smooth finite
  tensors it agrees with $(C_t\otimes I)J_G$ by the covariance definition.
  Approximation by such tensors converges in $L^2(I;G)$. The integral converges
  there by Schur; the completed tensor map converges in $L^r(I;G)$ by
  Lemma~\ref{L:uniform-mean-tensor} and~\eqref{E:positive-bochner-inclusion},
  hence also in $L^2$. Uniqueness of the $L^2$ limit identifies these versions.
  In particular, the integral belongs to $L^r$.

  Choose a closed ball $B$ containing all spatial supports of $\Psi$. For real
  smooth $q$ supported there, the physical covariance gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-local-spatial-bound}
    \|q\|_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}^2
    \le |B|\,\Gamma(B-B)\|q\|_\infty^2.
  \end{equation}
  Indeed, in $\int\ud x\int\Gamma(\ud y)q(x)q(x-y)$ only $x \in B,y \in B-B$
  contribute. Absolute values and local finiteness prove the bound, including
  white and singular Radon covariances. Thus, $t\mapsto\Psi_t$ is continuous,
  bounded and compactly supported as an $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$-valued field, and
  belongs to $L^2$. Its image under $J_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}$ is the original test
  class of $\Psi$. Indeed, the separated compact smooth approximants in
  Lemma~\ref{L:cameron-supported-tensor} converge uniformly on one common
  compact support. The local bound above and the temporal Schur estimate give
  convergence both in $L^2(I;H_{\mathrm{sp}})$ and in the original covariance
  norm. Their finite tensor identifications therefore have this same limit.
  Formula~\eqref{E:positive-bochner-inclusion} and absolute Fubini give
  \[
    \|\Psi\|_{H_I}^2
    =\int_{I^2}K(s,t)\langle\Psi_s,\Psi_t\rangle_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}
    \ud s\ud t
    =\int_{I\times\R^d}\Psi(s,x)h_{\Psi,s}(x)\ud x\ud s=v_\Psi.
  \]
  The compact spatial supports and~\eqref{E:positive-local-spatial-bound} make
  all these absolute integrals finite.

  Test the spatial row~\eqref{E:uniform-mean-row} first with displacement $f$
  and dual second component $\Lambda g$, with $f,g$ compact smooth. The row
  product is $m_Rfg$, so the preceding temporal identity gives
  \[
    \left\langle\int_IK(s,t)\Psi_t\ud t,m_Rfg\right\rangle_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}
    =\int m_Rh_{\Psi,s}fg .
  \]
  This Fubini step has two fixed compact supports and integrable time kernel.
  Both resulting operators are bounded on $E$: the left by the mean theorem, the
  right by the bounded smooth $h_{\Psi,s}$ and compact smooth mask. Dense states
  extend the identity to $E$. For this fixed $\Psi$ the temporal integral exists
  at every $s$, with norm at most $K_0\sup_t\|\Psi_t\|_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}$.
  Applying $J_R$ gives the explicit version in~\eqref{E:positive-raw-mean} for
  every $R,s$. For each $R$ it agrees with the completed $L^r$ mean class. This
  makes no simultaneous-version assertion over all rough controls. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-raw-embedding}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{P:original-signed-point-upper} bounds the original point
limsup by a limsup of the signed values $\mathcal{S}_R$.
Proposition~\ref{P:variational-positive-scores} bounds the original point
liminf below by $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$. To close this gap in
Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment} we compare $\mathcal{S}_R$ with
$S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$. We start with finite Gram matrices, with and without the
mask.

\LeanMarker{L:positive-physical-gram}
\begin{lemma}[Finite covariance vectors and entrywise order]
  \label{L:positive-physical-gram}
  Take a finite partition of $I$ with nonempty cells $I_i$, lengths $\ell_i > 0$
  and $\sum_i\ell_i = 1$, and real $f_i \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$ with $\|f_i\|_2 =
  1$. Put
  \[
    h_i=\mathbf1_{I_i}/\ell_i,\quad b_i=\|\nabla f_i\|_2^2,\quad
    k_i^R=h_i\otimes(m_Rf_i^2),\quad
    k_i^\infty=h_i\otimes f_i^2 .
  \]
  These are elements of $H_I$. If $v_L$ is the multiplier form
  of~\eqref{E:uniform-E-pairing}, then for every $\Phi \in H_I$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-label-row}
    \langle\Phi,k_i^R\rangle_{H_I}
    =\ell_i^{-1}\int_{I_i}v_{\mathcal{T}_R\Phi(s)}(f_i,f_i)\ud s .
  \end{equation}
  The Grams $C^R_{ij} = \langle k_i^R,k_j^R\rangle$ and $D_{ij} = \langle
  k_i^\infty,k_j^\infty\rangle$ satisfy $0 \le C^R_{ij} \le D_{ij}$ entrywise.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-raw-embedding} puts $h_i$ in $H_t$. The compact smooth
  spatial factors lie in $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ by its test definition
  and~\eqref{E:positive-local-spatial-bound}. On a smooth finite tensor,
  use~\eqref{E:uniform-mean-row} with $z = (f_i,0)$ and $w = (0,\Lambda f_i)$.
  Its row is $(\eta_Rf_i)(\eta_R\Lambda^{-1}\Lambda f_i) = m_Rf_i^2$. Temporal
  integration gives the pairing with $h_i$. This
  proves~\eqref{E:positive-label-row} on dense tensors. Its right side is a
  bounded original functional, with bound $\ell_i^{-1/r}C_{\rm
  mean}\|\Phi\|\|f_i\|_{H^{1/2}}^2$ by Hölder
  and~\eqref{E:uniform-completed-mean}. The left side is bounded by Hilbert
  Cauchy--Schwarz. Thus, the identity extends to the completed $H_I$. The form
  is the displacement form by Lemma~\ref{L:uniform-E-lift}.

  Each Gram is symmetric positive semidefinite. Its entries factor into the
  temporal pairing of nonnegative $h_i,h_j$ and the physical spatial covariance
  pairing. The kernels $K,\Gamma$ and $f_i^2,f_j^2$ are nonnegative; $0 \le m_R
  \le 1$. Tonelli therefore gives the entrywise order. The physical pairing
  already contains $\Gamma$, and the row cancellation introduces no additional
  Fourier factor. Zero labels are permitted. All hypotheses of
  Proposition~\ref{P:gram-entrywise-comparison} are met, without assuming
  $D-C^R$ is positive semidefinite. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-physical-gram}.
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{P:gram-positive-optimizer}, the unmasked finite problem has
a maximizer built from the $k_i^\infty$. Their time factors $h_i$ are scaled
indicators, so this maximizer is in general not a test in
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$. We also need a bound on the controls that holds
uniformly in $R$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform optimizer norm and positive smoothing]
  \label{L:positive-uniform-optimizer}
  Put $M = 4C_{\rm mean}+4$. The supremum defining $\mathcal{S}_R$ may be
  restricted to $\|\Phi\| \le M$, uniformly in $R$. For every finite trial
  family in Lemma~\ref{L:positive-physical-gram}, the unmasked finite
  variational expression has a maximizer $\Phi_D$ with $\|\Phi_D\| \le M$. It is
  zero or is a positive conic combination of the $k_i^\infty$. In the latter
  case it is approximable in $H_I$ by tests in $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Put $C = C_{\rm mean}$ and $q = \|\Phi\|$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-rate-averaging} gives
  \[
    2\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Phi)-q^2/2
    \le2+2Cq-q^2/2<2-2q<0\quad(q>M).
  \]
  The zero control gives zero, proving the restriction.

  Denote the finite unmasked objective by $\mathfrak{F}_D$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:gram-positive-optimizer} supplies an attained maximum at
  zero or a positive conic sum $\Phi_D$. Choose a finite $R' \ge 1$ whose
  plateau contains all supports of $f_i$. Then $k_i^{R'} = k_i^\infty$. Write
  $W_i = \ell_i^{-1}\int_{I_i}\mathcal{T}_{R'}\Phi(s)\ud s$. The row identity
  and real Rayleigh formula imply, for every $\Phi$,
  \begin{align*}
    \mathfrak{F}_D(\Phi)
    &\le2\sum_i\ell_i\lambda(W_i)-q^2/2\\
    &\le2+2\sum_i\ell_i\|W_i\|-q^2/2
    \le2+2Cq-q^2/2 .
  \end{align*}
  For fixed $f_i$, the map $L\mapsto v_L(f_i,f_i)$ is bounded linear; hence the
  form of the Bochner average is the average form. Here $\lambda(L) \le
  \max(1,\|L\|) \le 1+\|L\|$, the norm of an average is bounded by the average
  norm, and the $L^1$ mean norm is at most its $L^r$ norm. No time-averaging
  error or concavity of the positive-part square root enters this estimate.
  Since the maximum is nonnegative, its optimizer lies in the same ball of
  radius $M$, independently of the partition, profiles and $R'$.

  On a nonzero branch write $\Phi_D = \sum_{i\in A}w_i h_i\otimes f_i^2$, with
  $w_i > 0$. Extend $h_i$ by zero, convolve with a nonnegative compact smooth
  approximate identity, and multiply by smooth cutoffs $0 \le \chi_j \le 1$
  compactly supported in $I$, tending pointwise to one. The resulting
  nonnegative $h_{i,j} \in C_c^\infty(I)$ converge to $h_i$ in $L^2$: the error
  is bounded by the convolution error plus $\|(\chi_j-1)h_i\|_2$, which tends to
  zero by domination. Set $\Psi_j = \sum_{i\in A}w_i h_{i,j}f_i^2$. Finite
  tensor estimates and~\eqref{E:positive-bochner-inclusion} give $\Psi_j \to
  \Phi_D$ in $H_I$. The positive conic sum has positive squared norm, since at
  least one eligible diagonal is positive and all Gram entries are nonnegative.
  Thus, $\Psi_j \ne 0$ eventually, so $\Psi_j \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$.
  Costs converge. The integrated rate continuity proved in
  Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-rate-averaging} gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-rate-continuity}
    |\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Psi_j)-\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Phi_D)|
    \le\sqrt{3+C\|\Psi_j\|+C\|\Phi_D\|}
    \sqrt{C\|\Psi_j-\Phi_D\|}\longrightarrow0 .
  \end{equation}
  This estimate is uniform in $R'$. It asserts no uniform derivative bound on
  the smooth approximants. The zero branch will use $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}} \ge 0$
  directly. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:positive-uniform-optimizer}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:positive-physical-gram} orders the masked and unmasked Grams
entrywise. Lemma~\ref{L:positive-uniform-optimizer} keeps the unmasked optimizer
in a fixed ball and, when it is nonzero, approximates it by nonnegative tests.
The time averaging of Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-rate-averaging} links these
finite problems to the rate.

\begin{proposition}[Signed variational values bounded by the variational value over nonnegative controls]
  \label{P:positive-signed-comparison}
  For every $R \ge 1$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-signed-comparison}
    \mathcal{S}_R\le S_2^{\mathrm{pos}} .
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Use uniform partitions with $n \ge 2$ cells of length $1/n$. They are the $B =
  1,\delta = 1/n$ partitions in Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-rate-averaging}, with
  the empty last cell omitted. That proposition gives $e_n \to 0$, independent
  of $R$, such that
  \[
    \left|\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Phi)
    -\sum_i\ell_i\lambda\left(\ell_i^{-1}
    \int_{I_i}\mathcal{T}_R\Phi(s)\ud s\right)\right|\le e_n
    \quad(\|\Phi\|\le M).
  \]
  Fix $R,\Phi,n$, with $\|\Phi\| \le M$, and denote these averages by $W_i$.
  Real compact smooth unit trials are dense in the real $H^1$ unit sphere:
  truncate by cutoffs tending to one, whose gradient errors tend to zero, then
  mollify in $H^1$ and normalize in $L^2$. Continuity of the multiplier form in
  $H^{1/2}$ and hence $H^1$ proves
  \[
    \lambda(W_i)=
    \sup_{\substack{f\in C_c^\infty(\R^d;\R)\\\|f\|_2=1}}
    \sqrt{(v_{W_i}(f,f)-\|\nabla f\|_2^2)_+}.
  \]
  The real Rayleigh supremum is nonnegative by
  Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-E-spectral}, so the positive part does not change
  its supremum. For $\varepsilon > 0$ choose one near-maximizing $f_i$ per cell
  with error at most $\varepsilon$ in the square root. This is a finite choice,
  not a measurable continuum optimizer. Since $\sum_i\ell_i = 1$, the weighted
  trial error contributes $2\varepsilon$ to the next inequality.
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-physical-gram} gives
  \[
    2\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Phi)-\|\Phi\|^2/2
    \le\mathfrak{F}_{C^R}(\Phi)+2e_n+2\varepsilon .
  \]
  Optimize this finite objective and use entrywise Gram comparison:
  \[
    \mathfrak{F}_{C^R}(\Phi)
    \le\mathscr{S}(C^R)\le\mathscr{S}(D)=\mathfrak{F}_D(\Phi_D).
  \]
  This compares optimized values, not the same individual control.
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-uniform-optimizer} proves $\|\Phi_D\| \le M$ before time
  averaging is used again. Choose $R'$ covering the finite supports as in that
  lemma. The label row, Rayleigh bound and the same uniform averaging estimate
  give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-finite-optimizer-bound}
    \mathfrak{F}_D(\Phi_D)
    \le2\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Phi_D)-\|\Phi_D\|^2/2+2e_n .
  \end{equation}

  If $\Phi_D = 0$, its finite variational expression is zero. Otherwise use its
  positive smooth approximants $\Psi_j$. Lemma~\ref{L:positive-raw-embedding}
  identifies their mean with $B_E(m_{R'}h_{\Psi_j,s})$ and their Gaussian cost
  with $v_{\Psi_j}$. Since $h_{\Psi_j,s} \ge 0$ and $0 \le m_{R'} \le 1$, real
  $H^1$ form order gives $\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Psi_j) \le I_{\Psi_j}$.
  Thus, $2\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Psi_j)-\|\Psi_j\|^2/2 \le
  S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$. Rate and cost convergence pass this inequality to
  $\Phi_D$. Together with~\eqref{E:positive-finite-optimizer-bound} and the
  separate zero branch this yields
  \[
    2\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Phi)-\|\Phi\|^2/2
    \le S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}+4e_n+2\varepsilon .
  \]
  All finite choices were made after fixing $n,R,\Phi$. The right side is
  independent of those choices. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, take the supremum
  over the original ball, use Lemma~\ref{L:positive-uniform-optimizer}, and then
  let $n \to \infty$. This proves~\eqref{E:positive-signed-comparison}.
  Finiteness of the variational value over nonnegative controls was already
  proved from the independent canonical upper bound in
  Proposition~\ref{P:variational-positive-scores}, not from this conclusion.
  This proves Proposition~\ref{P:positive-signed-comparison}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{P:positive-signed-comparison} bounds $\mathcal{S}_R$ above for
every $R$. For the limit we still need a lower bound as the mask is removed.

\begin{proposition}[Cutoff removal for the optimized variational expression]
  \label{P:positive-score-cutoff}
  The possibly nonmonotone mask family satisfies
  \[
    \lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{S}_R=S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}.
  \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Fix $\Psi \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$ before taking $R$ or moment limits.
  Use the explicit mean version of Lemma~\ref{L:positive-raw-embedding}. Its
  rate $\lambda_R(s)$ obeys $0 \le \lambda_R(s) \le \lambda_{\Psi,s}$ by real
  form order. For each real compact smooth unit $f$, $m_R = 1$ on its support
  for every sufficiently large $R$. The Rayleigh formula therefore gives
  \[
    \liminf_{R\to\infty}\lambda_R(s)^2
    \ge\int h_{\Psi,s}f^2-\|\nabla f\|_2^2.
  \]
  Compact trial density and~\eqref{E:variational-real-rayleigh} identify the
  supremum of the right side as $\lambda_{\Psi,s}^2$. Hence, $\lambda_R(s) \to
  \lambda_{\Psi,s}$ for these versions. The global smooth-source bound $H_\Psi =
  \sup_{s,x}h_{\Psi,s}(x) < \infty$ gives $\lambda_R(s) \le \sqrt{H_\Psi}$.
  Dominated convergence yields $\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Psi) \to I_\Psi$. Neither
  monotonicity of $m_R$ nor an unmasked ground eigenfunction was used.

  The original representative and cost $\|\Psi\|^2 = v_\Psi$ are independent of
  $R$. Thus, $\liminf_R\mathcal{S}_R \ge 2I_\Psi-v_\Psi/2$ for each fixed test.
  Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose one fixed test with variational expression
  greater than $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}-\varepsilon$; the finite supremum admits such
  a test. Take the liminf and then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:positive-signed-comparison} supplies the reverse limsup.
  This proves Proposition~\ref{P:positive-score-cutoff}.
\end{proof}

This passage from cutoff scores to their suprema needs the comparison
just proved. No-go~\ref{P:nogo-30} shows why pointwise convergence and
bounded control costs alone would not justify it.


\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic original second-moment constant]
  \label{T:intrinsic-second-moment}
  For every original admissible model with data $(1,0)$ and fixed $x$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:intrinsic-second-moment-limit}
    \lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-(4-\alpha-a)/(3-\alpha)}
    \log\E|u_\Gamma(T,x)|^2
    =C_2(\Gamma,a)=S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}
    =2^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}.
  \end{equation}
  Its complete variational formula over nonnegative controls is
  \begin{align}
    C_2(\Gamma,a)
    &=\sup_{\Psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}}
    \left\{2\int_I
    \sqrt{\sup_{\substack{f\in H^1(\R^d;\R)\\\|f\|_2=1}}
    \left(\int h_{\Psi,s}f^2-\|\nabla f\|_2^2\right)}
    \ud s-\frac{v_\Psi}{2}\right\},
    \label{E:intrinsic-positive-formula}\\
    h_{\Psi,s}(x)
    &=\int_I|s-t|^{-a}(\Gamma*\Psi_t)(x)\ud t,\qquad
    v_\Psi=\int_{I\times\R^d}\Psi(s,x)h_{\Psi,s}(x)\ud x\ud s.
    \notag
  \end{align}
  Here $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$ consists of all nonzero nonnegative real
  $C_c^\infty(I\times\R^d)$ tests, including nonseparable ones. The covariance
  retains its amplitude, the kinetic term has coefficient one, and the Gaussian
  action is $v_\Psi/2$. For nonzero covariance $0 < C_2 < \infty$. For $\Gamma =
  0$ the constant and moment rate are zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Assume first $\Gamma \ne 0$. Proposition~\ref{P:original-signed-point-upper},
  followed by Proposition~\ref{P:positive-score-cutoff} with $R =
  \delta^{-1}+2$, bounds the original point limsup by $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:variational-positive-scores} bounds the original point
  liminf below by this same value. Both are for the same canonical Stratonovich
  solution, exponent, original covariance and variance-half action. Their with
  the regularization removed first identifications retain all mixed, unequal,
  self and empty chains and both phase components with initial data $(1,0)$. The
  original spatial stationarity proved in
  Proposition~\ref{P:raw-block-original-lower} extends both bounds to every
  fixed $x$. Thus, the limit exists and equals $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$. Its
  homogeneity, positivity and finiteness are
  Proposition~\ref{P:variational-positive-scores}.
  Formula~\eqref{E:variational-real-rayleigh} and the definition of the original
  variance give~\eqref{E:intrinsic-positive-formula}.

  If $\Gamma = 0$, $H_I = \{0\}$, all means, variances and rates vanish,
  $\mathcal{S}_R = S_c^{\mathrm{pos}} = 0$, and $u = 1$. Both formulas are
  direct zero identities. This proves Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.
\end{proof}

The variational value with coefficient one is auxiliary; the theorem does not
identify it with a first-moment limit or with Chen's printed constants. It
neither repairs a source energy normalization nor asserts a Jensen improvement.
The exponent formally tends to $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$ as $a\downarrow0$; no
interchange with the static moment limit is claimed. The explicit bounds and
full calibration are separate from this identification.
