I have read the manuscript and cross-checked its source citations against the frozen texts (Chen's current author manuscript, Chen–Hu 2025, Chen 2023, Crouzeix–Palencia v1, Balan–Conus, and the arXiv v1). Below are the things that caught my attention as a referee, in free-form prose. I have not attempted a line-by-line verification of the main proof; these are reactions at reading pace.

## Genre and presentation

**1. The document reads as working notes, not a paper.** The subtitle is "Working notes"; the abstract ends by listing "the planned route"; Section "Established steps and open questions" is a plan/status list; and large portions of the text are meta-commentary about another manuscript ("Which source statements are inputs", "Cutoff dependence and the order of limits", "This is a comparison of scales, not a coefficient estimate asserted by the source's Theorem 1.2"). A very large fraction of the paper is devoted to auditing Chen's 2026 manuscript rather than to proving the paper's own theorem. For a journal this is a serious presentation concern: it is hard to tell what the citable contribution is, and the main theorem is buried under several alternative arguments that are explicitly "retained to record their separate hypotheses and limitations" and "not needed" for the main result (Sections higher-alpha, static-holder, planar-holder, three-endpoints, energy-fidelity, and the whole of G0 are all declared non-inputs to Theorem T:full-upper).

**2. Massive symbol overloading, acknowledged but pervasive.** The paper itself lists $\delta$ (an exponent in one section, a cutoff width in another), $p$ (moment order vs. inventory exponent), $\Gamma$ (covariance measure vs. Euler Gamma function, only mitigated by $\Gamma_{\rm E}$), $b$ (local exponent vs. coordinate powers), and $\chi$ vs. $\alpha$ as distinct spatial degrees. Even with the warnings, a referee (and any reader) must carry a load of locally-redefined notation across ~60 pages.

**3. The "main result" is a bound, not a theorem about the model's sharp constant.** Goal G:sharp (the two-sided second-moment constant) is explicitly open, and the main theorem T:full-upper gives only a nonsharp upper growth rate with a constant $C=C(\alpha,\alpha_0)$ that is "finite" but not numerically optimized. The abstract and title foreground the second-moment problem, but the actual headline result is an upper bound at the correct *scale*, not the constant. This is defensible, but the framing (title, abstract's item (ii), Goal G:sharp) promises more than the theorem delivers.

## Dependence on the source and its normalization

**4. The paper builds its "sharp constant" target on constants it declines to define.** Theorem T:first quotes the source's $L(\mathcal{E}^\sharp)$ formula verbatim (I checked it against the source's (1.10)–(1.13): the transcription matches), but then repeatedly asserts that the printed normalization "requires clarification" and that "no correction is adopted." Yet Goal G:sharp and the calibrations in Corollary C:d1-first-calibration, Remark R:d1-comparisons, R:static-holder-scope, R:planar-scope, and R:three-endpoint-scope all use $L_*$ and $L^*$ (conditionally). A paper whose stated target is literally "$2^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}L_* \le \cdots \le 2^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}L^*$" while refusing to state what $L_*$, $L^*$ unambiguously are is awkward. The author is careful to say these are "not inputs," but the headline open problem is then not a well-posed target.

## Points in the mathematics that I would want to scrutinize

**6. The main theorem's proof is an extremely long dependency chain with a delicate combinatorics core.** Theorem T:full-upper rests on: Lemma L:joint-fixed-order (fixed-order construction), the operator estimates of Sections static-phase/static-all-orders (Lemma L:complex-phase via Crouzeix–Palencia, Lemma L:fock-form, Lemma L:finite-word-cutoff), and the interval-field machinery of Section interval-upper (Lemma L:interval-contractions, L:interval-complex, L:inventory-product, Lemma L:fixed-split, Proposition P:interval-coefficients). The single most delicate step is Lemma L:fixed-split, whose statement (E:fixed-split-bound) alone is hard to parse (background sets $\mathcal A_r,\mathcal A_q$, "cross-color subspaces," "insertion cube," radii $\varrho_i$, metric factors $\ell_i$). Its proof runs to several pages and is where any hidden error would live. I could not convince myself at referee pace that the factor accounting (the $(2k+1)\le 3^k$ split count, the $100^{3k+1}e^{4k}$ phase product, the radius-to-factorial conversion at (E:split-radius-factorial)) is airtight; this is flagged as the part of the paper that most needs independent checking, not a verified flaw.

**7. The "entire-series majorant" and the order of limits are delicate, and the author knows it.** Proposition P:interval-coefficients uses a majorant depending on $v_{\max}$ and the finite family for *identification only*, while the uniform coefficient bound is claimed independent of them; the enumeration at the end of Theorem T:full-upper (items (i)–(v)) is essentially a map of where a limit interchange could fail. The paper is unusually careful here, but that very carefulness signals how fragile the structure is. In particular, the step $\E|U_N^{\epsilon,\delta}|^2 \le \lim_J \E|V_{N,J}^\epsilon|^2 \le \sup_J \E|u_{J,\epsilon}|^2$ relies on entrywise (nonnegative) domination of Wick integrals via $K_J \uparrow K_\infty \ge \gamma_\delta^0$, and the diagonal $s=r$ of $K_\infty$ is infinite (the sum $\sum_j |h_j|^{-\alpha_0}$ diverges). The paper says "endpoint values do not affect the time integrals," which is right off-diagonal, but a referee will want to be sure the diagonal-in-$s,r$ singularity of the dominating covariance never enters any of the $K_J$-model moment estimates before the $J\to\infty$ limit.

**8. The time-covariance domination constant.** At (E:dyadic-time-majorant) the paper asserts $|s-r|^{-\alpha_0}\le 6^{\alpha_0}\sum_{\text{grids},|I|\le H_0}|I|^{-\alpha_0}\mathbf 1_I(s)\mathbf 1_I(r)$, with $6T\le H_0<12T$. The three-grid, mutual-spacing-$h_j/3$ construction is plausible and the "off-diagonal sum is $O(|s-r|^{-\alpha_0})$" remark is stated without detail; a referee should check that a single interval $I$ with $3|s-r|<|I|\le6|s-r|$ containing both points always exists for $s,r\in[0,T]$ and that the cumulative-count bound $h_i \le 78T/i$ (and hence $L=78$) is correct. These are the kinds of constant bookkeeping that decide whether the "$e(T+2)^d$" prefactor and the final $C$ are as claimed.

**9. The claimed full covariance generality via a single scalar $R_\alpha$.** Lemma L:interval-contractions claims all estimates depend on the covariance only through $R_\alpha=\alpha\pi\mu(B_1)/(2\sin(\pi\alpha/2))$, so the final constant is uniform over all homogeneous covariances with the same $\mu(B_1)$. This is a strong and appealing claim; it is exactly what makes the theorem cover "every admissible dimension $d=1,2,3$" including $d=3$ sphere kernels and $d=\alpha=1$ white noise, without isotropy or a spectral density. The uniformity over covariance *shape* (through only the unit-ball spectral mass) is the kind of statement a referee would stress-test, since the interval-field and terminal-chain arguments involve only radial spectral masses.

**10. Homogeneity/scaling spot-checks I did (these were fine).** I checked the damped-norm scalings in Lemma L:damped-homogeneous-norms ($N_0=\theta^{-2}$, $N_1=\theta^{\alpha+\alpha_0-2}$, $N_2^2=\theta^{-\beta}$), the white test (Lemma L:white-test), the spatial Wick-measure homogeneity exponent $(4-\alpha)k$ in Lemma L:spatial-wick-measures (the time covariance is *not* in $\rho_D$, so no $\alpha_0$ appears — the claim is right), the AM-GM constant $K_{\rm sp}$ in Lemma L:d1-spatial (the $(1-\alpha)^{(1-\alpha)k}$ factor is correct via the optimized split $S=2t/(2-\alpha)$), and the calibration ratio $2Z/(2c_0)=2(3-\alpha_0)$ in Remark R:d1-upper-checks. These all check out, which raises my confidence in the routine parts but does not shorten the critical path in item 6.

## Citation accuracy

**11. Most pinpoints check out.** I verified: Theorem 3.1/(3.3)–(3.6) of the source (pp. 14–15); the source's (5.3)–(5.4) and Remark 5.1/(5.8); Theorem 6.1 and Remark 6.3 (single-chain monotone order, "cannot work for $p>1$"); Crouzeix–Palencia (3) on p. 1 and Theorem 6 on pp. 5–6 of arXiv v1 (the quoted $\|F(T)\|\le(1+\sqrt2)\sup_{W(T)}|F|$ is accurate); Chen–Hu Theorem 1.2/(1.9) p. 2147, (1.11) p. 2147, Theorem 3.8(i) p. 2165, (2.17)–(2.19), (2.22) pp. 2152–2153, (5.1) p. 2173, (5.15) p. 2180; and Chen 2023 Theorem 1.1's closing finiteness sentence (the variation in (1.5)). The Balan–Conus exponent $\rho_w=(2H+2-a)/(3-a)$ and the $\alpha_H=H(2H-1)$ normalization are also accurate, and $H=1-\alpha_0/2$, $a=\alpha$ indeed recovers $\kappa$.

**12. Two citation caveats.** (a) The Balan–Conus pinpoint "(8), (10), p. 1492" is cited to *published* equation numbers, but the only frozen text I have is the local source TeX, where the bound is `\eqref{wave-Lp-bound}` (Theorem 2.1(b)); I could not confirm the published numbering "(8), (10)" or the page 1492 from the packet. This is minor but should be re-checked against the published PDF. (b) The paper leans on the identification "$2^{\chi/(4-\chi)}\mathcal{M}$" on Chen–Hu p. 2175; the source text there gives "$=2^{\alpha/(4-\alpha)}\mathcal{M}$" for the generic degree $\alpha$, so the paraphrase (with $\chi$ substituted for the endpoint degree) is faithful in form, but a reader cannot verify the exact "$p=2$" specialization of (5.15) without the published page layout.

## Smaller items

**13. "No uniqueness assertion is made" (Prop. P:mild-construction, and again in T:full-upper).** The theorem bounds the *canonical expansion* solution, not necessarily the unique solution; this is disclosed but means the result is conditional on the reader accepting the Stratonovich expansion as *the* solution.

**14. The Jensen proposition (P:Jensen) and its forward references.** It uses Lemma L:vertex-crude (proved much later) to justify $\sum_n\|X_n\|_2<\infty$ and then the mean interchange; the paper notes the non-circularity, but the logical order (Section 2 depending on Section G0) is awkward.

**15. Volume-factor prefactor.** The final bound (E:full-upper-bound) carries $e(T+2)^d$, arising from the $|B_{T+2}|/|B_1|$ ratio and the localization to $B_1$; at scale $t^\kappa$ this is sub-exponential, so it is harmless for the growth rate, but it is a slightly distracting artifact that the theorem statement does not explain.
