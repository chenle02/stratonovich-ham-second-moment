I've read the manuscript in full and checked the load-bearing citations against the frozen source texts in the packet (Chen's current author manuscript, Chen–Hu, Chen 2023, Crouzeix–Palencia, Balan–Conus). Here is what would give me pause as a referee, in roughly decreasing order of seriousness.

---

**1. The actual contribution is hard to locate, and much of the paper is declared non-load-bearing.**
The title and abstract promise "the second moment," but the only standing theorem (Theorem T:full-upper) is an *upper* bound; the sharp constant (Goal G:sharp) is explicitly open, and the abstract itself says the notes "record exactly what is known … state the targets, and list the planned route." Moreover, the dependency statement (and the abstract) say that the entire blocks S:higher-alpha, S:static-holder, S:planar-holder, S:three-endpoints, S:energy-fidelity, plus S:G0, S:G1, and even most of S:static-phase/S:static-all-orders, are **not** inputs to the main theorem. That is well over half the paper standing outside the proof of its one main result. Several of those sections are conditional transfers under hypotheses (H:spatial-maximum, H:three-endpoints) that are only discharged for restricted covariance families, and several are explicitly described as "diagnosis" (S:G0). A referee would want this either collapsed to an appendix/second note or clearly relabeled, because as submitted it reads like a lab notebook rather than an article.

**2. The linchpin lemma is not verifiable at referee pace.**
Everything reduces to Lemma L:fixed-split inside Proposition P:interval-coefficients: the Dyson-expansion coefficient extraction with "background evolution" $P_z$, Cauchy circles, the cross-insertion cube over $\prod_{i\in\mathcal C}y_i$, the metric budget $D_{\mathcal A}$, and the $\ell_i$ factors. I could verify the *downstream* bookkeeping (the weights table in P:interval-coefficients, the mapping of those weights onto $v_i$ and $b_{i,l}$, and the inventory product Lemma L:inventory-product all close correctly), and the exponents ($\beta,\nu,p$, $\beta-\nu=1-\alpha_0$, $\nu+\alpha-2=1$) are internally consistent. But I could not verify Lemma L:fixed-split itself from what is written. The statement has many interacting parameters ($\mathcal C$ vs $\mathcal B$, $\ell_i$, $\lambda=k/T$, $k^2C_{\mathrm{split}}^k$, the cross factor $\sigma_i^2h_i^2\lambda^{\alpha-2}$), and the proof is a compressed series of qualitative claims ("each metric change still occurs just once along the word," "the fixed segment constants … are absorbed into $C_{\mathrm{split}}^k$"). This is exactly where a missing factor of $k!$ or a miscounted symmetry would hide and destroy the $(k!)^{3-\alpha}$ denominator. I would ask for a substantially expanded derivation, or would want to verify it symbolically.

**3. Heavy black-box imports of the static coefficient rate.**
Sections S:static-holder, S:planar-holder, and S:three-endpoints all import the *quantitative static coefficient rate* of Chen–Hu (5.1)+(5.15) at $p=2$ to control $B_k(t)\le C_{\rm st}^k t^{(4-\chi)k}/(k!)^{3-\chi}$. I checked the pinpoint and the paper's transcription is faithful — including the subtle factor in (E:three-variational-upper), where $2^{-Q}2^{\nu_{\rm st}}$ with $\nu_{\rm st}-Q=1$ correctly reproduces Chen–Hu's (5.15) at $p=2$ — and the affine identities ($2\vartheta+(1-\vartheta)(3-\chi)=3-\alpha$, etc.) check out. Two residual worries: (i) the paper imports Chen–Hu's theorem, which is proved there via the Brownian intersection-local-time representation, and then *asserts* it extends to the weighted endpoint measures $\Gamma_{\rm st}=|x|^{\alpha-\chi}\Gamma(\ud x)$ it constructs; the justification is a pointer to Chen 2023 Theorem 1.1's finiteness (a single-variational statement), not a coefficient-rate theorem for such measures. That gap is papered over by "These are the source's general homogeneous-covariance statements, not a Riesz specialization." (ii) Since these imports are *not* used in the main theorem (per the dependency statement), their correctness is not essential to the headline result — but then their presence invites scrutiny that isn't repaid.

**4. Fragile dependence on an unpublished, pinned manuscript, with an unresolved normalization issue.**
The entire paper is keyed to a SHA-256-pinned 64-page author manuscript "submitted to Ann. Appl. Probab.," and the paper repeatedly states that its printed first-moment normalization "requires clarification" but "no correction is adopted" (Theorem T:first's comment box, Remark R:three-endpoint-scope, the abstract). The paper is scrupulous about quarantining this (the upper-bound proof claims not to use the first-moment constant), but the two-sided targets (Goal G:sharp) and every numerical $L_*$ calibration are conditional on a source that is itself unsettled. This is a fragile foundation for a journal submission, independent of the mathematics.

**5. Notation overloads.** The paper flags two of them ($\delta$ as exponent vs. cutoff width; $b$ vs. $b_j$), but two more are unacknowledged:
- **$\Gamma$** denotes the spatial covariance measure throughout S:planar-holder, S:three-endpoints, and S:interval-upper, *and* the Euler gamma function $\Gamma(\alpha_0)$ in the temporal regularization $\gamma_\delta^0(u)=\Gamma(\alpha_0)^{-1}\int_0^{1/\delta}\dots$ within the very same sections (e.g., in the proof of T:full-upper and in S:three-endpoints). It is disambiguated once by "Here $\Gamma(\alpha_0)$ is the Euler Gamma function," but the collision is real and confusing.
- **$p$** is the moment order in Conjecture Conj:allp and the discussion of Theorem T:first, and simultaneously $p=2-\alpha_0$ in S:interval-upper. Not flagged.

**6. Proposition P:mixed.** Its proof extends the source's Theorem 4.1 — which I verified only treats two chains of the *same* length — to unequal chain lengths via the "independent copy per pair + coloring + reversal" argument. The source's Lemma 4.4/Remark 4.5 (which the paper imports for the joint limit) is likewise for equal lengths only; the unequal-length extension is the paper's own, and while plausible, it is written very densely (the objects $Y_{r,a}$, $\Pi_r(c)$, the involution $\rho(j)=m+1-j$, $D^{\rm rev}$, and the nonnegativity claim are all compressed into one proof). The proof also invokes "Lemma 2.5 and (2.18)" of the source for the product identity $X_mX_n=S_{m+n}(g_m\otimes g_n)$ while immediately disclaiming that "that product identity is not needed" — a referee would want it stated unambiguously which of these auxiliary facts the proposition actually rests on.

**8. Completeness of the main theorem's statement.** T:full-upper is only for $T\ge1$ with prefactor $e(T+2)^d$; the goal is a limsup, so this suffices, but there is no $t<1$ statement and no numerically optimized constant (the paper concedes this). The limit passages in its proof (mode/band cutoffs, Green width $\eta$, noise widths $\epsilon,\delta$, interval depth $J$) are asserted to be uniform in several places without being traced through every step; the paper leans on the amplitude-scaling trick (multiply the noise by $a=2$) plus Lemma L:uniform-series-tail to get the geometric tail, which I found to be a correct and clean device — but the "uniform in $\epsilon,\delta,J$" claims above it are the kind of thing a referee must re-derive.

**9. Minor:** the red framed editorial comments (`\myCmts` boxes in Theorem T:first and around Goal G:sharp) appear in the body and signal a not-final manuscript; the title still carries "Working notes."

**What I checked and found consistent** (for calibration of the above): the reproduction of the source's Theorem 1.2/(1.10)–(1.13), the conjecture (1.22) with constant $(3-\alpha)p^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}$, Theorem 3.1/(3.3)–(3.6), Lemma A.1/(A.1)–(A.3), Lemma 4.4/Remark 4.5, the regularization (2.23)–(2.24) and (2.24)'s $\gamma_\delta^0(0)=\delta^{-\alpha_0}/(\alpha_0\Gamma(\alpha_0))$; Chen–Hu's (1.9)/(1.11), Theorem 3.8(i), (5.1)/(5.15), and the $2^{\alpha/(4-\alpha)}\mathcal M$ identification on p.2175–2176; Chen 2023 Theorem 1.1 and its closing finiteness sentence (p. 2); Crouzeix–Palencia (3) and Theorem 6; and Balan–Conus Theorem 2.1(b) with $\rho_w=(2H+2-a)/(3-a)$ and normalization $\alpha_H=H(2H-1)$. All of these match how the paper invokes them. The finite-order calibrations (Lemma L:white-test, Lemma L:static-four's $11/540$, the ratio $2(3-\alpha_0)$ and the $1/2$ vs $11/540$ order-four check in Remark R:d1-upper-checks) are internally consistent.

The single biggest thing I would need before recommending acceptance is a transparent, independently checkable proof of Lemma L:fixed-split (item 2) — it is the pivot on which the one new theorem rests, and as written it is asserted rather than shown.
