# 005 — Removal induction, corrected adjacent-gap bound, and two-path absorption

All objects are as in `L:spatial-wick-measures`. Fix a smoothed covariance `γ_b = p_b ∗ Γ`, and use the heat bound

\[
0\le (p_t*\gamma_b)(x)\le A\,t^{-\alpha/2},\qquad
A=\alpha\,V\int_0^\infty u^{\alpha-1}e^{-u^2/2}\ud u,\quad V=\mu(B_1).
\]

## A1. The exact remaining-pair invariant

Fix an ordered time list \(0<s_1<\cdots<s_{2j}\le T\) and a pairing \(D\). The induction invariant on the current remaining label set \(R\) is:

1. \(R\) is a union of whole pairs of \(D\), and the retained matching is \(D|_R\);
2. the position variables \((B_{s_l})_{l\in R}\) carry the ordinary Brownian marginal law at exactly those retained times;
3. removed steps have contributed only deterministic factors \(A(s_v-s_{v-1})^{-\alpha/2}\), one per selected right endpoint \(v\), with the selected labels distinct.

For nonempty \(R\), put \(v=\max R\), \(p=D(v)\), and \(q=\max(R\setminus\{v\})\). Then \(s_p\le s_q<s_v\). Condition on all retained position variables except \(B_{s_v}\). The Brownian increment \(B_{s_v}-B_{s_q}\) is independent of the conditioning and Gaussian with variance \(s_v-s_q\), so

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\big[\gamma_b(B_{s_v}-B_{s_p})\,\big|\,
(B_{s_l})_{l\in R\setminus\{v\}}\big]
&=\int p_{s_v-s_q}(y)\,\gamma_b(B_{s_q}+y-B_{s_p})\ud y\\
&=(p_{s_v-s_q}*\gamma_b)(B_{s_q}-B_{s_p})
\le A(s_v-s_q)^{-\alpha/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The key correction: the anchor and gap are \(B_{s_q}\) and \(s_v-s_q\), **not** \(B_{s_p}\) and \(s_v-s_p\). The remaining partnership only fixes the other endpoint \(p\); the conditioning set supplies the last retained predecessor \(q\).

Since \(q\le v-1\), we have \(s_q\le s_{v-1}\), hence

\[
s_v-s_q\ge s_v-s_{v-1},
\qquad
(s_v-s_q)^{-\alpha/2}\le(s_v-s_{v-1})^{-\alpha/2}.
\]

Therefore the conditional mean is bounded by \(A\) times the **original adjacent gap at the selected right endpoint \(v\)**,

\[
\mathbb E\big[\gamma_b(B_{s_v}-B_{s_p})\,|\,\cdot\big]
\le A(s_v-s_{v-1})^{-\alpha/2}.
\]

All other remaining covariance factors are nonnegative and measurable with respect to the conditioning \(\sigma\)-algebra, so multiplying by them is legitimate. Remove \(v\) and \(p\). If \(p<q\), the free unused vertex \(p\) is integrated away and the marginal at the new set \(R\setminus\{v,p\}\) is again Brownian; if \(p=q\), both \(v\) and \(q\) are removed together. The selected right-end label is always the current maximum, which is strictly decreasing, so the \(j\) selected labels are distinct and the corresponding original adjacent gaps \(s_v-s_{v-1}\) are distinct. This closes the induction.

## A2. The countercheck against the false paired-gap formulation

The diagnosis formulation used \(s_v-s_p\). That is false when \(p<q\). Take \(d=\alpha=1\), \(\Gamma=\delta_0\), hence \(\gamma_b(y)=p_b(y)\), and take the four original times

\[
s_1=1,\quad s_2=1.5,\quad s_3=2,\quad s_4=2+\delta,
\qquad
D=\{\{1,4\},\{2,3\}\}.
\]

For the pair \(\{1,4\}\) we have \(p=1, q=3, v=4\), with \(s_v-s_q=\delta\) and \(s_v-s_p=1+\delta\). Conditioning on \(B_{s_3},B_{s_2},B_{s_1}\),

\[
\mathbb E[\gamma_b(B_{s_4}-B_{s_1})\,|\,\cdot]
=p_{b+\delta}(B_{s_3}-B_{s_1}).
\]

At \(B_{s_3}=B_{s_1}\) this equals

\[
p_{b+\delta}(0)=(2\pi(b+\delta))^{-1/2}.
\]

The heat bound here is \(A=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\). Therefore

\[
p_{b+\delta}(0)=A(b+\delta)^{-1/2}
>A(1+\delta)^{-1/2}=A(s_v-s_p)^{-1/2}
\]

for every \(0<b<1\). On an open neighborhood of \(B_{s_3}=B_{s_1}\), \(p_{b+\delta}(\cdot)\) stays above the threshold \(A(1+\delta)^{-1/2}\), and that neighborhood has positive Brownian probability. Hence the paired-gap bound fails. The correct last-remaining-predecessor bound is \(A\delta^{-1/2}=A(s_v-s_q)^{-1/2}\), which does dominate \(p_{b+\delta}(0)\) since \(b+\delta\ge\delta\). The source's \((s_v-s_q)\mapsto(s_v-s_{v-1})\) comparison is therefore the only valid one.

## A3. Dirichlet integral and the \((2j)!\) count

After \(j\) removals on a fixed ordered simplex, the bounding factor is \(A^j\) times a product of \(j\) distinct adjacent gaps to the power \(-\alpha/2\). The ordered simplex of \(2j\) points in \([0,T]\) has \(2j+1\) increments: \(j\) selected ones with Dirichlet parameter \(1-\alpha/2\), and \(j+1\) remaining ones with parameter \(1\). Hence

\[
\int_{\text{ordered simplex}}
\prod_{\text{selected }v}(s_v-s_{v-1})^{-\alpha/2}\ud s
=\frac{\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)^j}
{\Gamma((2-\alpha/2)j+1)}\,T^{(2-\alpha/2)j},
\]

all parameters positive since \(\alpha<2\). There are \((2j)!\) possible orders, giving

\[
\mathbb E_B S_b(T)^j
\le\frac{(2j)!\,[A\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)]^j}
{\Gamma((2-\alpha/2)j+1)}\,T^{(2-\alpha/2)j},
\]

which is exactly `E:construction-brownian-moments`. This is the finite moment majorant used later.

## A4. Pathwise absorption over all \((v_1,v_2,w)\)

On fixed independent Brownian paths, after extending the ordered chain domains to their full time cubes, each time integral collapses to one occupation integral per pair:

- a self-pair on chain \(1\): \(S_1\);
- a self-pair on chain \(2\): \(S_2\);
- a cross-pair: \(A_{12}\), with spectral Cauchy–Schwarz
  \[
  0\le A_{12}\le \sqrt{S_1S_2}.
  \]

For a pairing with \(v_1\) self-pairs in chain \(1\), \(v_2\) self-pairs in chain \(2\), and \(w\) cross-pairs, with \(v_1+v_2+w=k\), the aggregate factor is at most

\[
S_1^{v_1+w/2}S_2^{v_2+w/2}
\le(1+S_1)^k(1+S_2)^k.
\]

Both exponents \(v_i+w/2\) lie in \([0,k]\), and for \(S\ge0,\ 0\le r\le k\), \(S^r\le(1+S)^k\) (check \(S<1\) and \(S\ge1\) separately). This holds **for each fixed split \((v_1,v_2,w)\) and fixed pairing**, with coefficient exactly one. If finitely many pairings or splits are subsequently summed, their finite cardinalities are kept explicitly; there is no hidden combinatorial sum inside the displayed absorption.

## A5. Exponential horizon majorant

Expanding \((1+S)^k=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{k}{j}S^j\) and using the moment bound gives

\[
M_k(a)
:=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{k}{j}
\frac{(2j)!\,[A\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)]^j}
{\Gamma((2-\alpha/2)j+1)}\,a^{(2-\alpha/2)j}
\]

as a finite majorant for each \(\mathbb E[(1+S(\cdot))^k]\), independently of the smoothing width \(b\). Each exponential horizon integral is finite:

\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-a/2}a^{(2-\alpha/2)j}\ud a
=2^{(2-\alpha/2)j+1}\Gamma((2-\alpha/2)j+1).
\]

Substituting, every term is finite, and independence of the two full paths gives

\[
\sup_{\epsilon>0}L_{\epsilon,D}(0)
\le2^{-2k-2}
\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-a/2}M_k(a)\ud a\right)^2<\infty.
\]

The factor \(2^{-2k}\cdot 1/4\) comes from the wave-resolvent-to-heat identity plus the two inserted terminal horizon integrals. An empty chain has \(S=0\) and horizon zero; the auxiliary exponential integral retains its stated value.

## A6. Separation from temporal weights

This is purely a spatial lemma: no temporal singular factor \(|s-r|^{-\alpha_0}\) appears anywhere in `L:spatial-wick-measures`. The later joint diagram limit in `L:joint-fixed-order` handles temporal cutoffs and marked temporal-tail convergence separately through the pairing-product envelope and the damped tail norms. Nothing in the spatial induction depends on those later temporal weights.

## B2. The p=2 coefficient is the candidate's \(B_k(1)\)

At \(p=2\),

\[
\sum_{l_1+l_2=2n}\mathbb E S_{l_1}S_{l_2}
=\sum_{m=0}^{2n}\mathbb E\big[U_m(1,x)U_{2n-m}(1,x)\big]
=B_n(1).
\]

Both empty-chain orientations are present and distinct (\(m=0\) and \(m=2n\)), and Wick's formula `(2.17)/(2.19)-(2.22)` includes every pairing, including self-pairings and cross-pairings. The candidate's static coefficient is exactly this source object, not a different quantity.

## B3. Exact factor computation

Multiplying the `(5.13)` bound by \((n!)^{Q}\), \(Q=3-\chi\), and taking \(n\)-th roots:

\[
\binom{n+p-1}{p-1}^{2/n}\to1,
\]

and by Stirling,

\[
\Bigl(\frac{n!^{(Q+1)}}{\Gamma(1+\nu n)}\Bigr)^{1/n}
=\Bigl(\frac{(n!)^\nu}{\Gamma(1+\nu n)}\Bigr)^{1/n}
\to \nu^{-\nu},
\]

since \(Q+1=3-\chi+1=4-\chi=\nu\). Therefore the rate is

\[
p^{\nu}\mathcal M^{\nu/2}\frac{2}{\nu^{\nu}}
=2\,p^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal M}}{\nu}\Bigr)^{\nu}.
\]

On the other hand the `(5.15)` product is

\[
2^{-Q}p^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{2\sqrt{\mathcal M}}{\nu}\Bigr)^{\nu}
=2^{-Q}p^{\nu}2^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal M}}{\nu}\Bigr)^{\nu}
=2^{\nu-Q}p^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal M}}{\nu}\Bigr)^{\nu}.
\]

Since \(\nu-Q=(4-\chi)-(3-\chi)=1\), both forms equal

\[
2p^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal M}}{\nu}\Bigr)^{\nu}.
\]

At \(p=2\),

\[
2\cdot2^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal M}}{\nu}\Bigr)^{\nu}
=2\Bigl(\frac{2\sqrt{\mathcal M}}{\nu}\Bigr)^{\nu}
=2\Bigl(\frac{2\sqrt{\mathcal M}}{4-\chi}\Bigr)^{4-\chi}.
\]

This is precisely the candidate's

\[
\Lambda_{\rm st}
=2\Bigl(\frac{2\sqrt{\mathcal M_{\rm st}}}{4-\chi}\Bigr)^{4-\chi}.
\]

No extra factorial, chain-copy, or orientation factor appears. The candidate's \(r>0\) cutoff then takes, for every \(r>0\), an \(N_r\) with

\[
B_k(1)\le\frac{(\Lambda_{\rm st}+r)^k}{(k!)^{3-\chi}},\qquad k\ge N_r,
\]

and keeps \(1\le k<N_r\) as exact finite coefficients, so the asymptotic rate is never substituted as the all-time coefficient constant. The zero static-rate branch \((\Xi=0)\) is direct: \(B_k=0\) for \(k\ge1\), \(C_{\rm st}=1\), with no division by a static rate.
