I've read the manuscript and cross-checked the key imported inputs against the frozen source texts (Chen 2026 author version, Chen–Hu 2025, Chen 2023, Crouzeix–Palencia v1, Balan–Conus). Below are the things that caught my attention as I read, in free-form prose, with labels. I am not grading; these are reactions at referee pace.

## Overarching status and structure

1. **The document is explicitly "Working notes," and its real deliverable is one theorem.** The title, abstract, and the paragraph "Main result and dependencies" make clear that the only new result is Theorem T:full-upper (a finite, nonsharp upper growth rate). Goal G:sharp (the sharp second-moment constant) is openly left open. A very large fraction of the paper — Sections S:higher-alpha, S:static-holder, S:planar-holder, S:three-endpoints, S:energy-fidelity, S:static-phase, S:static-all-orders — is explicitly *not* an input to the main theorem ("retained to record their separate hypotheses and limitations"). As a referee I would want the author to consider whether this material belongs in the same document; several of these sections import further heavy machinery (Chen–Hu variational constants, endpoint-covariance constructions, the energy-fidelity identification) and each is a potential independent source of error that does not bear on the one theorem being claimed. This is not a correctness issue by itself, but it materially affects the burden of verification.

2. **Heavy, careful dependence on a source that the paper itself flags as having an error and an unclear normalization.** The main proof imports Chen (2026) Theorem 4.1 (fixed-order $L^2$ convergence), Lemma 4.4/Remark 4.5 (equal-length joint limit), and the mild-solution identification (Theorem 1.1). Separately, the paper identifies (Remark R:source-uniformity) a real time-uniformity defect in the source's (5.4), and repeatedly qualifies the source's printed variational normalization in Theorem 1.2 without specifying a correction. I checked the source text: the (5.4) uniformity claim ("$C$ independent of $n$ and $t$") is indeed contradicted at $n=1$ by the exact computation in Lemma L:white-test, so the paper's diagnosis is correct. What I could not fully ascertain is whether the imported items (Theorem 4.1, Lemma 4.4) are cleanly independent of the flagged defect: Lemma 4.4 is proved in Section 4 of the source using Theorem 3.1 and Lemma A.1, while the uniformity defect occurs in Section 5's (5.3)–(5.4). This looks safe, but a referee should confirm that Theorem 4.1's proof does not inherit the uniformity loss that the paper correctly identifies in Section 5.

## Specific proof points

3. **Proposition P:mixed (unequal-length extension of Lemma 4.4).** The source's Lemma 4.4 is stated only for two chains of the *same* length. The paper extends it to arbitrary $(m,n)$ with $m+n=2k$ via a "finite coloring" argument that assigns an independent noise copy to each pair and reduces to the equal-length import. This is a genuine new step, not a direct citation. The Wick-reduction ($\Pi_r(c)$ = equal-color pairings) is correct as far as I checked, and the reversal maps each terminal-chain integral to the source's two-chain form. But the claim that each $B_{D'}$ "is a diagram in the imported joint-limit statement" deserves scrutiny: in $B_{D'}$ a single color can occur on four vertices (self-paired color), so the covariance factors are products of *several* factors of the same covariance function, whereas Lemma 4.4's integrand is written for one field with per-vertex widths. This is plausibly absorbed because Lemma 4.4 allows arbitrary vertexwise widths, but the reduction is not spelled out in enough detail that I could confirm it without re-deriving. I'd flag this as the single most delicate logical step in Section G0.

4. **Forward reference in Proposition P:mixed.** Its proof invokes Lemma L:crude-corrected ("proved independently in Lemma L:crude-corrected below") for the summability of the solution series, but Lemma L:crude-corrected is stated in the following subsection. There is no circularity (L:crude-corrected does not use P:mixed), but the logical order is awkward for a reader trying to certify the mixed expansion.

5. **Lemma L:complex-phase: a small exposition gap in the real-part bound.** The proof states "Optimizing (E:abstract-sandwich) at $\rho=s$ gives $|\langle Bu,u\rangle|\le 2ds^a$," and then immediately concludes $x+\lambda^2\ge(1-\delta)(s^2+\lambda^2)$. As written, that conclusion does *not* follow from $|\langle Bu,u\rangle|\le 2ds^a$ alone (for small $s/\lambda$ the bound $2\delta\lambda^{2-a}s^a\le\delta(s^2+\lambda^2)$ fails). It does follow cleanly by evaluating the same sandwich at $\rho=\lambda$, giving $|\langle Bu,u\rangle|\le d\lambda^{a-2}(s^2+\lambda^2)$ and hence $|z\langle Bu,u\rangle|\le\delta(s^2+\lambda^2)$. The proof is correct, but the stated justification is not the one actually used; a referee will notice this and the exposition should be fixed.

6. **Lemma L:interval-complex (non-selfadjoint extension).** This extends Lemma L:complex-phase to a bounded, not-necessarily-selfadjoint $V$ via the adjoint sandwich. The substitution "Selfadjointness of $V$ was not used; the adjoint estimate replaced it" is terse. The block formulas and the numerical-range parabola I checked appear to go through without self-adjointness (only the sandwich bound and its adjoint are used), but this is a point where a reader cannot verify without redoing the resolvent algebra; it deserves a sentence or two more rather than a one-line assertion.

7. **The metric-factor substitution in Proposition P:interval-coefficients is doing a lot of work in one sentence.** The fixed-split weight is $h_i^{\beta l}(1+l/(\lambda h_i))^2$, and the paper says "These weights increase with $h_i$" and then substitutes $h_i\le LT/i$. I checked this: the factor is increasing in $h_i$ (expanded powers $\beta l, \beta l-1, \beta l-2$ are all positive), so evaluating at the upper bound $h_i=LT/i$ does give $(1+li/(kL))^2\le(1+li/k)^2$, matching $b_{i,l}$ in Lemma L:inventory-product. So the argument is correct, but the direction of the monotonicity is the entire crux, and it is compressed into a single clause; a referee will want this spelled out, since the naive direction (upper bound on $h_i$ → upper bound on $1/(\lambda h_i)$) is the wrong one.

8. **Lemma L:fixed-split and the operator accounting.** This is the technical heart of Section S:interval-upper. I could verify the surrounding algebra (the complex-phase block norms, the root-comparison factors $\le2$, the partition count $M\le3k+1$, the metric product $D_{\mathcal A}$ with one ending charge per interval, the radius-factorial bound $\varrho_i^{-2k_i}$, the constant $C_{\rm split}=100^8e^8\max\{1,12\delta_{\alpha/2}^{-2}\}$), and each step I checked is consistent. However, the treatment of the cross-color (vacuum-column) insertions and the claim that the Hermite cutoff $N_i=2k_i$ "preserves each selected coefficient exactly" is the kind of argument that is easy to state and hard to certify; I could not fully verify it at referee pace. This is where I would concentrate a second reading.

9. **The interval inventory and dyadic majorant are sound.** I verified the nesting condition ($3t\in\mathbb Z$ ⇒ parent endpoints are child endpoints), the boundary spacing $h_j/3$, the "at least one grid interval contains both points" step, the cumulative count $6T/h_j+6(j+1)\le78T/h_j$ (so $L=78$), and the off-diagonal bound $K_\infty\le C|s-r|^{-\alpha_0}$. The monotone comparison $K_J\uparrow K_\infty$ against the source covariance $\gamma_\delta^0\le|\cdot|^{-\alpha_0}$ is also consistent, with entrywise Wick monotonicity supplied by Lemma L:terminal-wick. No concern here.

10. **Amplitude-scaling step (E:regularized-scaled-moment).** I checked that scaling the source noise by $a$ turns $\Gamma$ into $a^2\Gamma$, hence $R_\alpha\mapsto a^2R_\alpha$ with the parameter-only constant $C$ unchanged (the dyadic-majorant constant $c=6^{\alpha_0}$ is not rescaled, because the scaling is absorbed into the field covariance, not the weights). This is correct and is needed for the tail bound $B(T;2)$. Good.

11. **Finite-order calibrations (Lemmas L:white-test, L:static-four, L:riesz-small) check out.** I reproduced $\E X_1^2=c_0t^{3-\alpha_0}$, $\E X_2=\tfrac12c_0t^{3-\alpha_0}$, the order-two total $2c_0t^{3-\alpha_0}$, the static order-two value $t^3/3$, and the static order-four total $11t^6/540$ (the three-row sector table sums to $704/34560=11/540$). The white-space order-four table and the static sector values are internally consistent (I spot-checked the $(2,2)$-B and $(2,2)$-A entries). The bound/exact ratio $2(3-\alpha_0)$ in Remark R:d1-upper-checks is also correct. No concern.

12. **Citation accuracy.** I spot-checked the external citations against the frozen texts: Crouzeix–Palencia (3) p.1 and Theorem 6 p.5–6 ($\|F(T)\|\le(1+\sqrt2)\sup_{W(T)}|F|$) is accurate; Chen 2023 Theorem 1.1 (finiteness under Dalang) and Corollary 1.2 (the quartic bound used in Lemma L:physical-spectral-energy) are accurate; Chen–Hu 2025 (5.1) p.2173 and (5.15) p.2180 give exactly the homogeneity $t^{(4-\chi)k}$ and the coefficient rate $\limsup_k\frac1k\log((k!)^{3-\chi}B_k(1))<\infty$ that Lemma L:static-holder-input/L:planar-static-input import; Balan–Conus $\rho_w=(2H+2-a)/(3-a)$ with the $H(2H-1)$ normalization is accurate, and setting $H=1-\alpha_0/2$, $a=\alpha$ does recover $\kappa$. No mismatched citation found.

## Smaller items and notation

13. **Notation overloads.** The paper flags them itself ($\Gamma$ the measure vs Euler $\Gamma(q)$; $p=2-\alpha_0$ vs the moment order $p$; $\delta=\alpha-1$ vs the cutoff width; $b=2-\alpha$ vs coordinate powers $b_j$; $\alpha$ vs $\chi$). This is handled, but the Euler-$\Gamma$/measure-$\Gamma$ overload in the *same section* (S:interval-upper and the Theorem T:full-upper proof use "$\Gamma(\alpha_0)$ is the Euler Gamma function" while $\Gamma$ is also the spatial covariance) is genuinely confusing in print and worth renaming.

14. **"No correction adopted" hedging.** The paper repeatedly declines to adopt a correction of the source's printed first-moment normalization while still using $L_*$ in calibrations (Proposition P:Jensen, Remark R:d1-upper-checks, Remark R:higher-calibrations, R:static-holder-scope, R:planar-scope, R:three-endpoint-scope). Each use is explicitly conditional ("if the intended first-moment lower bound holds"), so nothing downstream depends on a disputed constant. But the volume of conditional statements makes it harder than necessary to see what is unconditional. The paper is careful; a referee would still want a single consolidated statement of exactly which printed formulas are trusted.

15. **Abstract phrase.** "the correct scale $t^{(4-\alpha-\alpha_0)/(3-\alpha)}$" in goal (i) — this is $\kappa$, consistent with the body. No issue, but the abstract's $\rho=(4-\alpha-\alpha_0)/(3-\alpha-\alpha_0)$ and "$\kappa<\rho<2$" both check out.

In summary: I did not find a clear, demonstrable error in the main line of proof, and the exponent and the external imports all check out. My hesitations concentrate on (a) the unequal-length extension in Proposition P:mixed (the coloring reduction to the equal-length import), (b) the highly compressed non-selfadjoint and metric-monotonicity steps in Lemmas L:interval-complex and the P:interval-coefficients substitution, (c) the exposition gap in Lemma L:complex-phase's real-part bound, and (d) the sheer volume of non-essential alternative-argument material whose verification is independent of — and adds risk beyond — the one theorem claimed. These are the points I would want the author to address or, at minimum, expand before I could recommend acceptance.
