## Referee reaction (free-form)

**1. The manuscript is working notes, not a paper.** This is the first and most persistent thing that would make me hesitate. The title itself is "Working notes (2026-10-27)"; the body contains red-boxed editorial comments (`\myCmts`) such as "Reviewed partial result: … These partial results have passed independent review", "The convolution assumption below is additional to homogeneity", and "The exponents in Goal G:sharp are the conjectured ones. If the analysis produces a different p=2 constant, that is a finding…". There are "Gates" (G0, G1), a "Planned route" section, "falsification first" language, a SHA-256 hash of the cited preprint embedded in Section 2, and forward-looking scaffolding ("Spatial subclass insertion marker"). None of this belongs in a submission, and it means the document is a status report, not a self-contained article. I would not be able to recommend acceptance of the document in this form regardless of the mathematics.

**2. The stated goals exceed what is proved.** Goal G:upper is posed under the full Hypothesis H:homog (0 < α < d), but the proved result (Proposition P:d1-upper and Proposition P:low-alpha-upper) covers only 0 < α ≤ 1. The α > 1 cases in d = 2, 3 are handled only under an *additional* hypothesis (H:spatial-maximum) that is explicitly not implied by homogeneity, and Proposition P:higher-obstructions shows that hypothesis *fails* for radial Riesz covariances in d = 2 with 3/2 ≤ α < 2 and for certain non-radial homogeneous covariances for every 1 < α < 2. Goal G:sharp (the sharp second-moment constant, i.e. the actual "second moment" of the title) is entirely open — it is only conjectured. The abstract is honest about this, but the title and the framing promise a second-moment result that is not delivered.

**4. The second-chain convention in the two-chain integrals is delicate and should be checked against Theorem 4.1.** Equation E:Sn2 quotes the source's Theorem 4.1 (4.3) with the notation "s_l − s_{l−1}, x_l − x_{l−1}" over the product domain [0,t]^n_< × [0,t]^n_<, which is internally ambiguous: the consecutive index l−1 suggests chain 2 attaches to the last vertex of chain 1, while the product domain requires two independently ordered chains. Section 3 then reinterprets this via a "preceding vertex in its own chain" p(l), explicitly stating "the second chain has its own root, rather than the last vertex of the first chain." I could not verify that Theorem 4.1 in the source uses this two-root convention (as opposed to a single ordered chain of 2n vertices, or chains rooted at the terminal point (t,x)). The reversal argument in the proof of Proposition P:mixed is asserted to reconcile these, but the reader is being asked to take on faith that the two conventions coincide. This is exactly the kind of convention mismatch that silently changes the mixed-term expansion (E:full-second), which the entire upper-bound argument builds on.

**5. Pointwise nonnegativity of γ is assumed and doing real work, but it sits outside the hypothesis the Goals cite.** The covariance is specified in E:cov as "γ ≥ 0 … nonnegative definite," but Hypothesis H:homog — under which every Goal and Theorem is stated — does not repeat pointwise nonnegativity. The proof of Proposition P:mixed needs the integrands J_{m,n,D} to be nonnegative (to justify the increasing-limit/series-regrouping step), and the "0 ≤" halves of Lemmas L:low-alpha-convolution and L:alpha-one-convolution need γ_ε ≥ 0. Nonnegative-definiteness alone does not give pointwise nonnegativity (a positive-definite function may be sign-changing), so the general result of Section 5.3 (Proposition P:low-alpha-upper) is stated for pointwise-nonnegative homogeneous covariances. It is never made explicit whether this is the entire class allowed by H:homog at 0 < α ≤ 1, or a genuine restriction; if there are homogeneous, nonnegative-definite, but non-pointwise-nonnegative covariances in this range, they are silently outside the theorem. This should be clarified, even if the intended applications (Riesz, white) are unaffected.

**6. Notation collisions that obscure the argument.** Several are acknowledged but still tax the reader:
- In E:mixed-diagram, y_l is a *position*; in Section 5 (before Lemma L:d1-spatial), y_l is an *increment* while x_l is a position. The paper flags this, but the switch is precisely where the main spatial estimate is set up, and the terminal point of u(t,x) is also called x.
- δ denotes the noise-smoothing parameter (X_n^{ε,δ}) in Section 5 and the exponent α − 1 in Section 6 (flagged there, but the same letter in adjacent sections).
- M is the convolution constant with at least three different meanings (V/(1−α) in Lemma L:low-alpha-convolution, the constant in Hypothesis H:spatial-maximum, and the M_d of Lemma L:higher-riesz).
- b is the parameter 2 − α in Section 5 and a generic constant in Proposition P:envelope.

**7. Two different constants for the same d = 1 Riesz case.** Proposition P:d1-upper produces the constant K_sp (with c_α), while Proposition P:low-alpha-upper, specialized to d = 1 Riesz, produces K_sp^gen (with V = μ(B₁)). These do not generally agree for Riesz covariance — the paper only checks the white endpoint, where πV = c_δ recovers K_sp — and there is no remark on which bound is sharper or whether the spectral constant is larger than the direct one. Not an error, but it should be acknowledged, since a reader computing the d = 1 Riesz constant will get different numbers from the two propositions.

**8. The d = 3 limiting passage is compressed and appears twice verbatim.** In the proofs of Proposition P:low-alpha-upper and Proposition P:higher-transfer, the removal of the Green smoothing η → 0 in d = 3 rests on weak convergence of products of the finite Green measures plus dominated convergence with a "constant times Π h_l" bound. This is plausible, but the argument is sketched ("These products of measures converge weakly … a constant times Π_l h_l dominates on each bounded time simplex"), and it is exactly the step where the measure-valued Green kernel meets the pairing integrands. A referee will want this written as a proper lemma with the tightness/domination verified, especially because the same sketch is reused for the more delicate Section 6 transfer where the spatial bound is M h^{−δ}.

**9. The L⁴ convergence invoked for the product rule.** In Proposition P:mixed, the product rule S_{m+n}(g_m ⊗ g_n) = X_m X_n is justified by "the smoothed factors converge in L⁴, so their products converge in L² by Hölder." This needs the source to actually establish L⁴ (or uniformly-bounded-in-L⁴) convergence of the smoothed Stratonovich chaoses, not merely L². If the source only proves L² convergence, the L⁴ step is an unstated strengthening. This is a citation-fidelity item I could not check.

**10. "After the chain reversal in P:mixed, its spatial factor is the one in L:d1-spatial."** The phrasing in the proof of Proposition P:d1-upper is confusing: the spatial estimate in Lemma L:d1-spatial is set up in the roots-at-zero orientation (two separate roots, increments h_l), which is the *post*-reversal form of E:mixed-diagram; the "reversal" being referenced is the one already performed inside Proposition P:mixed, not an extra step. It is not wrong, but the sentence reads as if another transformation is being applied at that point.

**11. A small imprecision in the source-audit remark.** Remark R:source-uniformity says the asserted uniformity is contradicted "at n = 1," but the contradiction is a *large-t* one (t^{3−α₀} dominates t² for large t, since 3 − α₀ > 2), not a small-t one. The remark is phrased as "Taken for all t > 0," which is technically the correct statement, but a reader could easily take it to mean a small-time contradiction. Worth one sentence of clarification.

**12. Bookkeeping the reader must take on trust in Section 6.** The Hölder/r/r′ exponents, the Gamma lower bound Γ(1+ak) ≥ e^{−1}(a/e)^{ak}(k!)^a, and the assembly of K = 12M[Γ(1−δr)D_a]^{1/r} Z_θ^{1/r′} are correct as far as I could verify by following the algebra, but they are intricate, and the constants are described only as "explicit, nonsharp." A referee will have to redo this end-to-end; the intermediate steps (the Dirichlet integral normalization, the 2k+1 ≤ 3^k split count, the factorial-power balance 1 + (2−δr)/r + 2/r′ − 1 = 2 − δ) are asserted rather than displayed in full.

**13. On the positive side (to make clear this is not a blanket reaction).** I re-derived several of the finite-order computations independently and they held together: Lemma L:white-test (both E X₁² = c₀t^{3−α₀} and E X₂ = ½c₀t^{3−α₀}, and the total-order-two sum 2c₀t^{3−α₀}); the area formula F(a,b,c); the white-noise order-four table entries I sampled, including the static (0,4) and (2,2) coefficients 1/2880 and 1/144 and the aggregate 11/540; the two Riesz order-two coefficients in Proposition P:riesz-small; the AM–GM step producing K_sp with the (1−α)^{(1−α)k} factor; the symmetry integral in E:d1-time-symmetry; and the final coefficient count giving A_k ≤ (2ZK_sp)^k t^{βk}/(k! k^{bk}). The first-moment calibration in Corollary C:d1-first-calibration is also internally consistent, including the ratio C_{2,*} = 2^{(4−α)/(3−α)} C_{1,*}. So my hesitation is concentrated in the form, the scope, and the citation/convention fidelity, not in the core coefficient algebra, which appears sound where I tested it.

**14. Last, a standing-instruction observation rather than a mathematical one.** The paper repeatedly imports facts from the cited author version (Theorem 4.1, Theorem 3.1, Lemma A.1, the product rule at (2.18), the L⁴ convergence) as "From [Chen, Theorem 3.1]" while simultaneously publishing a pointed correction of that same source's Remark 5.1. That is defensible, but it means acceptance would depend entirely on the reader trusting a set of pinpoints into a 64-page PDF that the manuscript itself warns has a garbled text layer — and that several of those pinpoints (Section 5.3's Fourier/mass formulas (2.3)–(2.5), the regularization scheme (2.9)/(2.11)/(2.23)–(2.24), the pp. 13–14 d = 3 convention) are stated without the reader being able to see them.

If I had to compress this to the single thing that matters most: the mathematics of the *proved* upper bound at 0 < α ≤ 1 looks correct on close inspection, but the document is working notes with process metadata embedded in it, the title/goals promise results (sharp constant; full α range) that are not proved, and the paper's correctness turns on a cluster of source pinpoints and a chain-root convention that I could not verify from the packet and that the paper itself flags as contested in one case.
