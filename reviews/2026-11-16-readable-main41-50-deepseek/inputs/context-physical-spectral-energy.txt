\section{Physical and spectral energy for measure covariances}
\label{S:energy-fidelity}

This section justifies the positive-measure energy used
in~\eqref{E:three-static-variational}. It does not assume
Hypothesis~\ref{H:three-endpoints} or the conditional moment bound. The imported
analytic input is the finiteness of the variation on the right of (1.5), stated
in the closing sentence of Chen~\cite{chen:23:exponential}*{Theorem~1.1, p.~2}.

This identification of variational functionals does not remove the additional
hypotheses of the separate moment argument. It does not resolve general
covariance factorization.

\begin{lemma}[Sobolev energy identification]\label{L:physical-spectral-energy}
  Let $\Xi$ be a nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon covariance
  on $\R^3$, with $\Xi(rA) = r^{3-\chi}\Xi(A)$ for $0 < \chi < 2$. Let $\mu_\Xi$
  be its positive even spectral measure in the convention $\Xi = \int
  e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi)$, understood distributionally, and use the
  Fourier transform $\widehat{h}(\xi) = \int e^{-i\xi\cdot x}h(x)\ud x$. For
  every real $g \in W^{1,2}(\R^3)$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:physical-spectral-energy}
    \mathcal{Q}_\Xi(g)
    :=\int_{\R^3}\int_{\R^3}g(y+z)^2g(y)^2\ud y\,\Xi(\ud z)
    =\int_{\R^3}|\widehat{g^2}(\xi)|^2\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi)<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  The physical expression is independent of the Borel representative of $g$.
  There is a finite $C_0 \ge 0$, depending on $\Xi$, such that
  \begin{equation}\label{E:energy-quartic-bound}
    \mathcal{Q}_\Xi(g)
    \le\frac12\|g\|_2^2\|\nabla g\|_2^2+C_0\|g\|_2^4.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Fourier uniqueness gives $\mu_\Xi(rA) = r^\chi\mu_\Xi(A)$. Since its unit-ball
  mass is finite, the dyadic estimate with $\sum_{j\ge0}2^{j(\chi-2)}$ verifies
  Dalang's condition. For a real Schwartz function $h$ put
  \[
    \mathscr{E}(h)=\int|\widehat{h}(\xi)|^2\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi),
    \qquad Q(f)=\mathscr{E}(f^2).
  \]
  Schwartz Fourier pairing identifies $Q(f)$ with its ordinary positive-measure
  integral. From the finiteness assertion in
  Chen~\cite{chen:23:exponential}*{Theorem~1.1, closing sentence for the
  variation in (1.5), p.~2}, restricted to real unit-$L^2$ Schwartz profiles,
  there is $C_0 \ge 0$ such that $Q(g) \le \frac12\|\nabla g\|_2^2+C_0$ for
  $\|g\|_2 = 1$. For $f \ne 0$, quartic homogeneity gives
  \[
    Q(f)=\|f\|_2^4Q(f/\|f\|_2)
    \le\frac12\|f\|_2^2\|\nabla f\|_2^2+C_0\|f\|_2^4.
  \]
  The same inequality is immediate for $f = 0$. Only Schwartz pairings have been
  used at this point, so this import presupposes no energy identification for
  nonsmooth profiles. If $N(f)^2 = \|f\|_2^2+\|\nabla f\|_2^2$, it follows that
  $Q(f)^{1/2} \le D N(f)^2$ for some finite $D$.

  We need two different Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities. For real Schwartz $f,h$,
  spectral Cauchy--Schwarz gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      0\le P(f,h)
      &:=\int\Xi(\ud z)\int f(y+z)^2h(y)^2\ud y\\
      &=\int\widehat{f^2}(\xi)
      \overline{\widehat{h^2}(\xi)}\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi)
      \le Q(f)^{1/2}Q(h)^{1/2}.
    \end{split}
  \]
  Evenness makes the Fourier sign convention in this real pairing immaterial. On
  the other hand, Cauchy--Schwarz on the positive measure $\ud y\,\Xi(\ud z)$
  gives
  \[
    \mathscr{E}(fh)
    =\int\Xi(\ud z)\int
    f(y+z)f(y)h(y+z)h(y)\ud y
    \le Q(f)^{1/2}Q(h)^{1/2}.
  \]
  All these are absolutely convergent Schwartz pairings. In particular,
  $\mathscr{E}(fh)$ is the quadratic energy of $fh$, not the quartic expression
  $Q(fh)$.

  Let $\mathfrak{m}(\ud y,\ud z) = \ud y\,\Xi(\ud z)$ and define
  \[
    A_f(y,z)=f(y+z)f(y),\qquad B_f(\xi)=\widehat{f^2}(\xi).
  \]
  The measure $\mathfrak{m}$ is positive and sigma-finite. The decomposition
  \[
    A_f-A_h=(f-h)(y+z)f(y)+h(y+z)(f-h)(y)
  \]
  and the bound for $P$ imply
  \[
    \|A_f-A_h\|_{L^2(\mathfrak{m})}
    \le D\bigl(N(f)+N(h)\bigr)N(f-h).
  \]
  Since $f^2-h^2 = (f-h)(f+h)$, the bound for $\mathscr{E}$ gives
  \[
    \|B_f-B_h\|_{L^2(\mu_\Xi)}
    \le D N(f-h)N(f+h)
    \le D\bigl(N(f)+N(h)\bigr)N(f-h).
  \]

  Choose real Schwartz $f_n \to g$ in $W^{1,2}$. These estimates make both
  $A_{f_n}$ and $B_{f_n}$ Cauchy in their respective $L^2$ spaces. Pass to a
  subsequence with $f_n \to g$ Lebesgue-almost everywhere. Translation
  invariance of Lebesgue measure and sigma-finite Fubini give
  \[
    f_n(y+z)f_n(y)\longrightarrow g(y+z)g(y)
    \quad\text{for }\mathfrak{m}\text{-almost every }(y,z).
  \]
  Thus, the strong $L^2(\mathfrak{m})$ limit is $A_g$. The same Fubini argument
  shows that changing the Borel representative of $g$ does not change this
  limit.

  Also
  \[
    \|f_n^2-g^2\|_1
    \le\|f_n-g\|_2(\|f_n\|_2+\|g\|_2)\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  Hence, $\widehat{f_n^2} \to \widehat{g^2}$ uniformly on all frequencies. A
  subsequence of its strong $L^2(\mu_\Xi)$ limit converges $\mu_\Xi$-almost
  everywhere, and must agree with this uniform limit. Consequently, that strong
  limit is $B_g$. For every $n$, $\|A_{f_n}\|_{L^2(\mathfrak{m})}^2 = Q(f_n) =
  \|B_{f_n}\|_{L^2(\mu_\Xi)}^2$. Passing to the strong limits proves
  \eqref{E:physical-spectral-energy}, including finiteness. Passing the Schwartz
  quartic bound to the same limit proves \eqref{E:energy-quartic-bound}. This
  proves Lemma~\ref{L:physical-spectral-energy}.
\end{proof}

In particular, for $\|g\|_2 = 1$ the energy is at most $\|\nabla g\|_2^2/2+C_0$.
Therefore, the supremum of $\mathcal{Q}_\Xi(g)^{1/2}-\|\nabla g\|_2^2$ is
finite. Applied to $\Xi = \Gamma_{\rm st}$, the profile-by-profile identity
justifies the measure-form functional in~\eqref{E:three-static-variational}
without assigning a density to the covariance or borrowing a disputed
special-case constant.
