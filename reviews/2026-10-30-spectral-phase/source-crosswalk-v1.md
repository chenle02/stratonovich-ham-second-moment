## Exact normalization map

Simon(A1),p448 uses H=-Delta/2+V. For our H_b=-Delta-V_b take
H=H_b/2 and V=-V_b/2. If our eigenvalue is -lambda_b^2, his is
E=-lambda_b^2/2. TheoremC.3.5,p498 permits exponential exponent beta with
beta^2/2 < inf sigma_ess(H)-E. Once essential threshold0 is independently
established, this becomes0<beta<lambda_b. No factor sqrt2 may be imported
from comparing different kinetic normalizations.

For the present bounded smooth potentials, the Kato-class hypotheses must
be checked from Simon's definitions before invocation. C.3.5 is a theorem
about decay of an EXISTING below-essential eigenfunction. It does not prove
a negative eigenvalue exists, essential spectrum starts at0, or constants
are uniform over b. Overviewp449 reports ground-state uniqueness if it
exists; this is not an existence proof for our family. C.3.4 supplies a
weaker positive decay exponent for isolated eigenvalues. Neither result is
a fractional-Sobolev wave or uniformly-local complementary propagator bound.

## Prospective local-phase transfer mechanism, UNPROVED

A possible deterministic bridge is a two-region finite-propagation cutoff,
using a cutoff radius proportional to the wave time with sufficiently large
fixed proportionality constant. Near the origin, use the global zero-ground-
projection condition to express the cutoff projection as an omitted tail.
Far from the origin, the cutoff itself is far from the decaying ground state.
A positive exponential margin could dominate the growing wave factor,
while the global E norm of localized F data costs only a polynomial.

Every part requires proof: weighted ground-state derivative/duality tails,
uniform constants and family continuity, the global E complementary wave
bound, and actual local cutoff estimates at growing radii. The finite-speed
statement must apply to the physical potential flow and both phase entries.
This is a possible route, not a lemma or a substitute for S1. Riesz tails
remain present and cannot be dropped to use a compact-potential argument.
