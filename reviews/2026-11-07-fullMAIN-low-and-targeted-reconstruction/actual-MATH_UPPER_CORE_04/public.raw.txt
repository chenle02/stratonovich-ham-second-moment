# Part I. The six operator-core lemmas

## 1. PROOF037 — translated spectral resolvent and physical Green/covariance trace

### Setting and two distinct masses

Let \(\Gamma\) be nonnegative, even, positive-definite, tempered Radon on \(\R^d\), \(d\in\{1,2,3\}\), homogeneous in the physical sense

\[
\Gamma(cA)=c^{d-\alpha}\Gamma(A),\qquad 0<\alpha<2,
\]

and with spectral measure \(\mu\) in the convention

\[
\Gamma=\int_{\R^d}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu(\mathrm d\xi).
\]

Fourier uniqueness transfers homogeneity:

\[
\mu(cA)=c^{\alpha}\mu(A).
\]

We emphasize the **two different finite masses**:

\[
V:=\mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le1\}),\qquad M:=\Gamma(\{x:|x|\le1\}).
\]

For nonzero \(\Gamma\), both are finite and positive. If \(\Gamma=0\), then \(\mu=0\), \(V=M=0\); all bounds below are trivial and are handled separately.

Define

\[
R_\alpha=\frac{\alpha\pi V}{2\sin(\pi\alpha/2)},\qquad 0<\alpha<2.
\]

The point \(q=0\) integral uses the elementary identity

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{r^{\alpha-1}}{1+r^2}\,\mathrm dr=\frac{\pi}{2\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}.
\]

### (a) The resolvent contraction — all \(d\in\{1,2,3\}\)

For \(\lambda>0\), \(q\in\R^d\), and \(s>0\),

\[
\int_{\R^d}e^{-s|q+\xi|^2}\mu(\mathrm d\xi)
=\int_{\R^d}p_{2s}(x)e^{iq\cdot x}\Gamma(\mathrm dx),
\]

which is a direct Fourier-pairing identity (the \(p_{2s}\) Fourier factor is \(e^{-s|q+\xi|^2}\) after the evenness shift \(\xi\mapsto-\xi\)). Since \(\Gamma\) is a nonnegative measure,

\[
\left|\int_{\R^d}p_{2s}(x)e^{iq\cdot x}\Gamma(\mathrm dx)\right|
\le\int_{\R^d}p_{2s}(x)\Gamma(\mathrm dx)
=\int_{\R^d}e^{-s|\xi|^2}\mu(\mathrm d\xi).
\]

This is an estimate of a **real physical integral by its total mass**; it uses no pointwise ordering of arbitrary phases or covariance values. Multiplying by \(e^{-\lambda^2s}\) and integrating over \(s>0\) gives

\[
\int_{\R^d}\frac{\mu(\mathrm d\xi)}{\lambda^2+|q+\xi|^2}
\le\int_{\R^d}\frac{\mu(\mathrm d\xi)}{\lambda^2+|\xi|^2}.
\]

Because \(\mu(B_r)=Vr^\alpha\),

\[
\int_{\R^d}\frac{\mu(\mathrm d\xi)}{\lambda^2+|\xi|^2}
=\alpha V\int_0^\infty\frac{r^{\alpha-1}}{\lambda^2+r^2}\,\mathrm dr
=R_\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\sup_{q\in\R^d}\int_{\R^d}\frac{\mu(\mathrm d\xi)}{\lambda^2+|q+\xi|^2}
\le R_\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-2}.}
\]

This is dimension-free and covers anisotropic, white, and singular covariances: only \(\mu(B_r)=Vr^\alpha\) and physical nonnegativity enter.

### (b) The physical trace estimate — the \(d=3\) wave measure

The second bound in `L:phase-contractions` is dimension-three specific, because \(G_a\) there is the normalized sphere wave measure. The Fourier kernel is

\[
\widehat G_a(\xi)=\int_{\R^3}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}G_a(\mathrm dx)
=\frac{\sin(a|\xi|)}{|\xi|}.
\]

For \(\Gamma_\varepsilon=p_{2\varepsilon}*\Gamma\), with spectral multiplier \(e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^2}\),

\[
T_\varepsilon(a,q)
=\int_{\R^3}e^{iq\cdot x}\Gamma_\varepsilon(x)G_a(\mathrm dx)
=\int_{\R^3}\widehat G_a(q+\xi)e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^2}\mu(\mathrm d\xi).
\]

The measure \(\Gamma_\varepsilon(x)G_a(\mathrm dx)\) is finite and nonnegative, so

\[
|T_\varepsilon(a,q)|\le T_\varepsilon(a,0),
\qquad T_\varepsilon(a,0)=\int_{\R^3}\Gamma_\varepsilon(x)G_a(\mathrm dx)\ge0.
\]

From \(\Gamma(B_r)=Mr^{3-\alpha}\) one obtains the radial distributional law

\[
\int_{\R^3}F(x)\Gamma(\mathrm dx)
=(3-\alpha)M\int_0^\infty F(r)r^{2-\alpha}\,\mathrm dr
=c_\Gamma\int_{\R^3}F(x)|x|^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm dx,
\qquad c_\Gamma=\frac{(3-\alpha)M}{4\pi},
\]

for every nonnegative radial Schwartz \(F\). Taking \(F=G_a*p_{2\varepsilon}\),

\[
T_\varepsilon(a,0)
=c_\Gamma[p_{2\varepsilon}*(G_a*|\cdot|^{-\alpha})](0)
\le c_\Gamma\sup_{z\in\R^3}(G_a*|\cdot|^{-\alpha})(z).
\]

With \(r=|z|\), the sphere convolution is

\[
(G_a*|\cdot|^{-\alpha})(z)
=\frac{(a+r)^{2-\alpha}-|a-r|^{2-\alpha}}{2r(2-\alpha)}.
\]

The three-region bounds

\[
r<a/2:\ \le2^{\alpha}a^{1-\alpha},
\qquad
r>2a:\ \le a^{1-\alpha},
\qquad
a/2\le r\le2a:\ \le\frac{3^{2-\alpha}a^{1-\alpha}}{2-\alpha}
\]

give

\[
\boxed{|T_\varepsilon(a,q)|\le D_\Gamma a^{1-\alpha},
\qquad D_\Gamma=c_\Gamma\left(2^{\alpha}+\frac{3^{2-\alpha}}{2-\alpha}\right).}
\]

No isotropic or density hypothesis is used. For \(d=1,2\), the wave measure is not the sphere measure, so the analogous trace bounds require separate region computations; the declared lemma is the \(d=3\) statement.

## 2. PROOF039 — complex phase bound with genuinely noncommuting \(A_0,B\)

### Falsification-first framing

The central algebraic identity is **ordered**:

\[
A_\lambda^{1/2}\big[(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}-A_\kappa^{-1}\big]
=A_\lambda^{1/2}A_\kappa^{-1/2}(1-zK_\kappa)^{-1}zK_\kappa A_\kappa^{-1/2}.
\]

Here \(A_\kappa\) commutes with \(A_\lambda\), but \(L_\lambda=A_\lambda-zB\) does **not** commute with \(A_\lambda\) when \(A_0\) and \(B\) do not commute. The numerical \(2\times2\) test in the supplied data is only evidence; the reconstruction below is the actual proof.

### Setup

\(A_0\ge0\) bounded selfadjoint, \(B\) bounded selfadjoint, \(A_\rho=\rho^2+A_0\), and

\[
\|A_\rho^{-1/2}BA_\rho^{-1/2}\|\le d\rho^{a-2},\qquad 0<a<1.
\]

Because \(A_0\) is bounded and \(A_\rho\ge\rho^2 I\), the operators \(A_\rho^{1/2},A_\rho^{-1/2}\) are bounded invertible square roots of positive selfadjoint operators.

### Resolvent factorization

For \(t\ge0\), set \(\kappa=(\lambda^2+t)^{1/2}\), \(A_\kappa=A_\lambda+t\). Then

\[
L_\lambda+t=A_\lambda+t-zB
=A_\kappa^{1/2}(1-zK_\kappa)A_\kappa^{1/2},
\qquad
K_\kappa:=A_\kappa^{-1/2}BA_\kappa^{-1/2}.
\]

The sandwich hypothesis gives \(\|K_\kappa\|\le d\kappa^{a-2}\). Since \(a-2<0\) and \(\kappa\ge\lambda\),

\[
|z|\le\frac{\delta_a\lambda^{2-a}}{d}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\|zK_\kappa\|\le\delta_a<1.
\]

Therefore \(1-zK_\kappa\) is invertible, hence \(L_\lambda+t\) is invertible for every \(t\ge0\). Moreover

\[
(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}
=A_\kappa^{-1/2}(1-zK_\kappa)^{-1}A_\kappa^{-1/2},
\]

and

\[
\|A_\kappa^{1/2}(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}\|
=\|(1-zK_\kappa)^{-1}A_\kappa^{-1/2}\|
\le\frac1{(1-\delta_a)\kappa}.
\]

### The four ordered inverse-root comparisons

Use the scalar identity, valid on the right half-plane,

\[
w^{-1/2}=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}(w+t)^{-1}\,\mathrm dt,
\]

together with the fact that the principal square roots of \(L_\lambda\) and \(L_\lambda^*\) are available because both have positive-definite real part (proved below). For

\[
Q_z:=A_\lambda^{1/2}L_\lambda^{-1/2},
\]

one obtains

\[
Q_z-1
=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2}
\big[(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}-A_\kappa^{-1}\big]\,\mathrm dt.
\]

The ordered factorization above gives the integrand bound, without the factor \(t^{-1/2}/\pi\),

\[
\|A_\lambda^{1/2}[(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}-A_\kappa^{-1}]\|
\le\frac{|z|d}{1-\delta_a}\kappa^{a-3}.
\]

The four factors have norms

\[
\|A_\lambda^{1/2}A_\kappa^{-1/2}\|\le1,
\qquad
\|(1-zK_\kappa)^{-1}\|\le(1-\delta_a)^{-1},
\qquad
\|zK_\kappa\|\le|z|d\kappa^{a-2},
\qquad
\|A_\kappa^{-1/2}\|\le\kappa^{-1}.
\]

The first uses the operator inequality \(A_\lambda\le A_\kappa\). Therefore

\[
\|Q_z-1\|
\le\frac{|z|d\,\lambda^{a-2}I_a}{\pi(1-\delta_a)}
\le\frac{\delta_a I_a}{\pi(1-\delta_a)}
\le\frac16,
\]

because

\[
I_a=\int_0^\infty s^{-1/2}(1+s)^{(a-3)/2}\,\mathrm ds<\infty
\qquad(0<a<1),
\]

and the substitution \(t=\lambda^2 s\) produces the \(\lambda^{a-2}I_a\) factor.

Consequently

\[
\|Q_z\|\le2,\qquad\|Q_z^{-1}\|\le2.
\]

Apply the identical argument to \(L^*=A_0-\overline zB\) and take adjoints. This yields all four ordered comparisons

\[
\|A_\lambda^{1/2}L_\lambda^{-1/2}\|\le2,
\qquad
\|L_\lambda^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2}\|\le2,
\qquad
\|L_\lambda^{1/2}A_\lambda^{-1/2}\|\le2,
\qquad
\|A_\lambda^{-1/2}L_\lambda^{1/2}\|\le2.
\]

Only functions of \(L\) are allowed to commute with each other; \(L\) is never commuted with \(A_\lambda\).

### Numerical range containment

For \(\|u\|=1\), put \(s:=\|A_0^{1/2}u\|\). For every \(\rho>0\),

\[
|\langle Bu,u\rangle|
\le\|A_\rho^{-1/2}BA_\rho^{-1/2}\|\,\|A_\rho^{1/2}u\|^2
\le d\rho^{a-2}(s^2+\rho^2).
\]

Evaluating at \(\rho=s\) (with the \(s=0\) case by \(\rho\downarrow0\)) gives

\[
|\langle Bu,u\rangle|\le2ds^{\,a}.
\]

Evaluating at \(\rho=\lambda\) gives

\[
|z\langle Bu,u\rangle|
\le\delta_a(s^2+\lambda^2).
\]

Writing \(w=x+iy=\langle L_\lambda u,u\rangle\) (equivalently \(L=\lambda^2+A_0-zB\) shifted), one gets

\[
x\ge(1-\delta_a)(s^2+\lambda^2),
\qquad
|y|\le2\delta_a\lambda^{2-a}s^a.
\]

Using \(s^{2a}\lambda^{2-2a}\le s^2+\lambda^2\),

\[
y^2\le\frac{4\delta_a^2}{1-\delta_a}\lambda^2x
\le\frac13\lambda^2 x,
\qquad
x\ge\frac34\lambda^2,
\]

so the closure of \(W(L)\) lies in the parabolic region

\[
\{x+iy:x\ge-\lambda^2/4,\ y^2\le\tfrac13\lambda^2(x+\lambda^2)\}.
\]

This region implies

\[
|\operatorname{Im}\sqrt w|\le\lambda,\qquad |w|\le x+2\lambda^2,
\]

for every \(w\in\overline{W(L)}\).

### Holomorphic functions and the imported bounded Crouzeix–Palencia theorem

The functions

\[
C_h(w)=\cos(h\sqrt w),
\qquad
F_h(w)=\sqrt{w+\lambda^2}\,\frac{\sin(h\sqrt w)}{\sqrt w},
\qquad
H_h(w)=\frac{w}{\sqrt{w+\lambda^2}}\,\frac{\sin(h\sqrt w)}{\sqrt w}
\]

are holomorphic on a neighborhood of \(\overline{W(L)}\). On that region the bounds

\[
|C_h(w)|\le e^{\lambda h},
\qquad
|F_h(w)|\le2(1+\lambda h)e^{\lambda h},
\qquad
|H_h(w)|\le2e^{\lambda h}
\]

follow from \(|\operatorname{Im}\sqrt w|\le\lambda\), \(\sin z/z=\int_0^h\cos(r\sqrt w)\,\mathrm dr\), and the split \(|w|\le\lambda^2/2\) versus \(|w|>\lambda^2/2\).

The imported bounded-operator theorem from the packet (Theorem 6, p. 5) states that for a bounded operator \(T\) and \(f\) holomorphic on a neighborhood of \(\overline{W(T)}\),

\[
\|f(T)\|\le(1+\sqrt2)\sup_{w\in W(T)}|f(w)|.
\]

It is applied only to the **bounded** operator \(L\) at finite cutoffs; no unbounded square-root domain theorem or commutation is imported. Since \(1+\sqrt2<3\), one gets

\[
\|C_h(L)\|\le3e^{\lambda h},
\qquad
\|F_h(L)\|\le6(1+\lambda h)e^{\lambda h},
\qquad
\|H_h(L)\|\le6e^{\lambda h}.
\]

### The phase propagator block norm

Under the isometry \((u,v)\mapsto(u,A_\lambda^{-1/2}v)\), the propagator of the generator with rows \((0,I)\), \((-L,0)\) has ordered blocks

\[
C_h(L),
\qquad
F_h(L)L_\lambda^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2},
\]

\[
-A_\lambda^{-1/2}L_\lambda^{1/2}H_h(L),
\qquad
A_\lambda^{-1/2}L_\lambda^{1/2}C_h(L)L_\lambda^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2}.
\]

These are exactly the standard blocks \((C_h,\sin)/(\cdot)\) weighted by the two root comparisons. Their norms are at most

\[
3e^{\lambda h},\qquad12(1+\lambda h)e^{\lambda h},
\qquad12e^{\lambda h},\qquad12e^{\lambda h}.
\]

By the bound on a block operator as the sum of its block norms,

\[
\|\mathcal M_z(h)\|_{X_\lambda\to X_\lambda}
\le(39+12\lambda h)e^{\lambda h}
\le100e^{2\lambda h}.
\]

This completes the uniform bound, independent of \(H,\|A_0\|,\|B\|\). The abstract exponent \(a\) here will later be specialized to \(a=\alpha/2\); it is distinct from the temporal \(\alpha_0\).

### Remaining incoming hypothesis of 039

The Crouzeix–Palencia theorem itself is the only imported theorem. The remaining hypothesis is the abstract sandwich

\[
\|A_\rho^{-1/2}BA_\rho^{-1/2}\|\le d\rho^{a-2}.
\]

The initialization \(\delta_a<1\) requires \(0<a<1\).

## 3. PROOF040 — Fock multiplication with correct number-operator reading

The target is the uniform bound

\[
\|A_\rho^{-1/2}B_{N,m,b}A_\rho^{-1/2}\|
\le d_N\rho^{\alpha/2-2},
\qquad
d_N=2\sqrt{N+1}\,\sqrt{R_\alpha},
\]

with

\[
B_{N,m,b}=P_NS_b\Big(\sum_{i=1}^m g_i M_{e_i}\Big)S_bP_N,
\qquad
A_\rho=\rho^2-\Delta.
\]

### Spatial column bound

From the representation \(W_\varepsilon(x)=\sum_i g_i e_i(x)\), the functions satisfy

\[
\sum_i e_i(x)e_i(y)=\Gamma_\varepsilon(x-y),\qquad
\sum_i e_i(x)^2=\Gamma_\varepsilon(0):=v_\varepsilon<\infty.
\]

For \(H_i:=A_\rho^{-1/2}M_{e_i}A_\rho^{-1/2}\), Plancherel and PROOF037 give

\[
\sum_i\|H_i f\|_2^2
=\frac1{(2\pi)^d}\int_{\R^d}
\frac{|\widehat f(\eta)|^2}{\rho^2+|\eta|^2}
\left(\int_{\R^d}\frac{e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^2}\mu(\mathrm d\xi)}
{\rho^2+|\eta+\xi|^2}\right)\mathrm d\eta
\le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\|A_\rho^{-1/2}f\|_2^2.
\]

Using \(\|A_\rho^{-1/2}\|\le\rho^{-1}\),

\[
\sum_i\|H_i f\|_2^2
\le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-4}\|f\|_2^2,
\]

equivalently, in quadratic forms,

\[
\sum_i H_i^2\le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-4}I.
\]

For the band projection, \(S_b\) commutes with \(A_\rho\) and, since each \(S_bH_iS_b\) is selfadjoint,

\[
(S_bH_iS_b)^2\le S_bH_i^2S_b.
\]

Thus the same column bound holds for the band-compressed operators \(H_i^b:=S_bH_iS_b\).

### Creation/annihilation norm on the correct degree

Write multiplication by the normalized Gaussian coordinate \(g_i\) as

\[
M_{g_i}=a_i+a_i^*,
\qquad
g_ih_{\mathbf a}
=\sqrt{a_i+1}\,h_{\mathbf a+e_i}
+\sqrt{a_i}\,h_{\mathbf a-e_i}.
\]

The full multiplication operator on degree \(\le N\) is the sum of a creation part and an annihilation part. The precise statement avoiding both errors the task flags is as follows.

Let

\[
R:\ell^2\otimes\mathcal H\to\mathcal H,\qquad (y_i)_i\mapsto\sum_i a_i^*y_i.
\]

Its adjoint is

\[
R^*:\mathcal H\to\ell^2\otimes\mathcal H,\qquad F\mapsto(a_iF)_i.
\]

Hence

\[
RR^*=\sum_i a_i^*a_i=N_{\rm tot},
\]

the total number operator. On the subspace of total degree \(k+1\), \(N_{\rm tot}=(k+1)I\). Therefore the restriction of \(R\) from inputs of degree \(k\) to outputs of degree \(k+1\) has norm

\[
\|R\|=\sqrt{k+1}.
\]

This is the **correct** operator-norm computation: the product is \(RR^*=N_{\rm tot}\), not \((k+1)I\) acting on the degree-\(k\) input, and there is no identity-per-mode term \(N+M\). The adjoint product \(R^*R\) on the sequence side is not diagonal; it is not used.

Now define the spatial column

\[
C:\mathcal H\to\ell^2\otimes\mathcal H,\qquad f\mapsto(H_i^b f)_i.
\]

Since \(\sum_i(H_i^b)^2\le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-4}I\),

\[
\|C\|\le\sqrt{R_\alpha}\,\rho^{\alpha/2-2}.
\]

The creation part is

\[
W_c=P_NS_b\Big(\sum_i a_i^*M_{e_i}\Big)S_bP_N
=R(I\otimes C),
\]

and the annihilation part is

\[
W_a=P_NS_b\Big(\sum_i a_iM_{e_i}\Big)S_bP_N
=(I\otimes C^*)R^*.
\]

On degrees at most \(N\), each part has norm at most

\[
\sqrt{N+1}\sqrt{R_\alpha}\,\rho^{\alpha/2-2}
\]

(because \(\|C\|=\|C^*\|\) for the selfadjoint \(H_i^b\)). Adding the two parts proves

\[
\boxed{\|A_\rho^{-1/2}B_{N,m,b}A_\rho^{-1/2}\|
\le2\sqrt{N+1}\,\sqrt{R_\alpha}\,\rho^{\alpha/2-2}.}
\]

This is uniform in \(\varepsilon,m,b\), with no mode-count factor. Every statement tensors with an arbitrary additional Hilbert factor by inserting identities there; the Gaussian and spatial operators act only on their respective factors.

### Remaining hypotheses of 040

Boundedness of \(B_{N,m,b}\) requires the band projection \(S_b\), so \(-\Delta\) is bounded on the band. The bound is only at finite \(N,m,b\); no limit is taken here. The proof requires the strengthened spatial column bound \(\sum_iH_i^2\le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-4}I\), not only the weaker \(\rho^{\alpha-2}\|f\|^2\) interval bound.

## 4. PROOF041 — exactness of selected finite words

The proof is the color-degree induction stated in the source.

For a fixed word \(i_1,\dots,i_n\), multiplication by \(g_{ij}\) changes **only** the total degree of color \(i\) by \(+1\) (creation) or \(-1\) (annihilation), and leaves every other color degree unchanged. The free operators \(I\otimes F_l\) leave all Gaussian degrees unchanged.

Before insertion \(l\), the uncompressed word has color-\(i\) degree at most

\[
r_i(l)=|\{j\le l:i_j=i\}|.
\]

Therefore, for the selected word only,

\[
r_i(l)\le r_i\le N_i.
\]

Consequently \(P=\bigotimes_i P_{N_i}\) is the identity on every intermediate vector along that word:

\[
(I\otimes F_n)(PJ_{i_n}P)\cdots(PJ_{i_1}P)(I\otimes F_0)(1\otimes v)
=(I\otimes F_n)J_{i_n}\cdots J_{i_1}(I\otimes F_0)(1\otimes v).
\]

The annihilation (lowering) term \(\sqrt{a_i}\,h_{\mathbf a-e_i}\) is retained for all \(a_i\ge1\), so **both creation and later annihilation of the same mode are preserved**, including every self-contraction within the word.

**What stabilizes:** The total color degrees along the one selected word never exceed \(N_i=r_i\), so the projection inserted between consecutive word factors coincides with the identity there.

**What does not follow:** This provides no identity between the full cutoff propagator and the uncompressed propagator. Different words with different intermediate degrees, words that would exceed \(N_i\), and all non-selected contractions are not covered. In particular, this is not a fresh-vacuum bound for already-occupied colors.

The Bochner-integral extension is valid because the equality is equality of the vector-valued integrand at each fixed parameter.

## 5. PROOF044 — interval contractions, tensoring earlier dependence

The dimension-free proof uses the same Gaussian-comparison mechanism as PROOF037. Since \(W(p_\varepsilon(x-\cdot))\) has covariance \(\Gamma_\varepsilon=p_{2\varepsilon}*\Gamma\),

\[
W_\varepsilon(x)=\sum_m g_me_m(x),
\qquad
\sum_me_m e_m=\Gamma_\varepsilon.
\]

The one explicit dimension-independent inequality is

\[
\int e^{-s|\eta+\xi|^2}\mu(\mathrm d\xi)
=\int p_{2s}^{(d)}(x)e^{i\eta\cdot x}\Gamma(\mathrm dx)
\le\int e^{-s|\xi|^2}\mu(\mathrm d\xi).
\]

Integrating against \(e^{-\rho^2s}\,\mathrm ds\) and using \(\mu(B_r)=Vr^\alpha\) gives

\[
\sup_{\eta\in\R^d}\int\frac{\mu(\mathrm d\xi)}{\rho^2+|\eta+\xi|^2}
\le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}.
\]

Plancherel then gives the column bound

\[
\sum_m\|A_\rho^{-1/2}M_{e_m}f\|_2^2\le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\|f\|_2^2.
\]

The phase-insertion bound on a fresh vacuum is immediate because on the vacuum only creation contributes and its norm is exactly the left side of the column bound.

Substituting \(A_\rho^{-1/2}f\) gives the selfadjoint sandwiches

\[
H_i:=A_\rho^{-1/2}M_{e_i}A_\rho^{-1/2},
\qquad
\sum_iH_i^2\le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-4}I,
\]

and the Fock sandwich follows from PROOF040's number-operator argument. Tensoring with any other Hilbert factor only inserts an identity in that factor and preserves all bounds.

No angular density, no origin-atom exclusion, no density of \(\mu\), and no isotropic hypothesis is used. The only covariance quantity entering is \(R_\alpha\), which depends on the spectral ball mass \(V=\mu(B_1)\).

## 6. PROOF045 — nonsymmetric complex sums and endpoint metric

### Nonsymmetric sandwich and adjoint

For bounded \(V\), not necessarily selfadjoint, the hypothesis is

\[
\|A_\rho^{-1/2}VA_\rho^{-1/2}\|\le D\rho^{a-2},\qquad
D\le\delta_a\Lambda^{2-a}.
\]

Since the adjoint of \(A_\rho^{-1/2}VA_\rho^{-1/2}\) is \(A_\rho^{-1/2}V^*A_\rho^{-1/2}\), the same sandwich holds for \(V^*\).

For \(L=A_0-V\), the factorization

\[
L_\Lambda+t=A_\kappa^{1/2}(1-K_\kappa)A_\kappa^{1/2},
\qquad
K_\kappa=A_\kappa^{-1/2}VA_\kappa^{-1/2}
\]

uses complex but bounded \(K_\kappa\). The resolvent difference is exactly

\[
(L_\Lambda+t)^{-1}-A_\kappa^{-1}

### Metric change and endpoint charges

For a common phase vector,

\[
\|z\|_{\rho_{\rm new}}
\le\max\{1,\rho_{\rm old}/\rho_{\rm new}\}\,\|z\|_{\rho_{\rm old}}.
\]

At a jump time \(\tau\), denote by \(E\) the **set** of fields ending at \(\tau\), by \(S\) the set starting at \(\tau\), and by \(R\) the set remaining. The ratio is at most

\[
\frac{\lambda+\sum_R\omega_i+\sum_E\omega_i}
{\lambda+\sum_R\omega_i+\sum_S\omega_i}
\le1+\frac{\sum_E\omega_i}{\lambda}
\le\prod_{E}\left(1+\frac{\omega_i}{\lambda}\right).
\]

An initial increase of the weight (a start) creates no loss. The final return to \(X_\lambda\) is charged as an ending at \(T\).

**Endpoints are bundled as sets.** If several connected intervals share a fixed endpoint at a grid boundary, they all belong to the same \(E\) at that jump and are charged simultaneously; they are not charged sequentially or repeatedly. Consequently their end charges multiply only once:

\[
\text{total loss}\le\prod_i\left(1+\frac{\omega_i}{\lambda}\right).
\]

Only insertion-time coincidences between variable insertion times and each other or with fixed cut points are Lebesgue-null and may be discarded. Fixed interval endpoints are not null sets.
