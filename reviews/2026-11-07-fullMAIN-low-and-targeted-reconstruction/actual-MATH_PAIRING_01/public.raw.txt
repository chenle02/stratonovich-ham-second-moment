## 1. PROOF003: the white‑space order‑two calibration (falsification attempted, statement verified)

Fix \(d=\alpha=1\), \(\Gamma=\delta_0\), write \(a=\alpha_0\in(0,1)\). Work in the initial‑chain convention: each chain has its own root \((0,0)\), the first Green factor is \(G(s,x)\) at the first vertex, and all chain time variables are strictly ordered. For \(d=1\),

\[
G(s,x)=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{|x|<s\}},\qquad \int_\mathbb R G(s,x)G(r,x)\,\mathrm dx=\frac{\min(s,r)}2 .
\]

### 1.1 The \((1,1)\) cross‑term, i.e. \(\mathbb E X_1(t,x)^2\)

Two chains of length one, vertices \((s,x_1)\), \((r,x_2)\). There is one pairing \(D=\{\{1,2\}\}\), so

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E X_1(t,x)^2
&=\int_0^t\int_0^t\int_{\mathbb R^2}
G(s,x_1)G(r,x_2)\,\delta_0(x_1-x_2)|s-r|^{-a}\,
\mathrm dx_1\,\mathrm dx_2\,\mathrm ds\,\mathrm dr\\
&=\int_0^t\int_0^t\frac{\min(s,r)}2\,|s-r|^{-a}\,\mathrm ds\,\mathrm dr .
\end{aligned}
\]

By symmetry,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E X_1(t,x)^2
&=\int_0^t\int_0^s r\,(s-r)^{-a}\,\mathrm dr\,\mathrm ds\\
&=\int_0^t\frac{s^{2-a}}{(1-a)(2-a)}\,\mathrm ds\\
&=\frac{t^{3-a}}{(1-a)(2-a)(3-a)}
=c_0\,t^{3-a}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The \(1/2\) from \(G(s)G(r)\) swallowed the factor \(2\) from the symmetric decomposition. This is exactly the statement \(c_0t^{3-a}\).

### 1.2 The single‑chain \((0,2)\) term, i.e. \(\mathbb E X_2(t,x)\)

One chain of length two, vertices \(0<s_1<s_2<t\). The only pairing joins the two vertices, and \(\delta_0\) forces \(x_1=x_2\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E X_2(t,x)
&=\int_{0<s_1<s_2<t}\int_{\mathbb R^2}
G(s_1,x_1)\,G(s_2-s_1,x_2-x_1)\,
\delta_0(x_1-x_2)(s_2-s_1)^{-a}\,
\mathrm dx_1\,\mathrm dx_2\,\mathrm ds_1\,\mathrm ds_2\\
&=\frac12\int_{0<s_1<s_2<t}s_1(s_2-s_1)^{-a}\,\mathrm ds_1\,\mathrm ds_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(\int_\mathbb R G(s_1,x_1)\,\mathrm dx_1=s_1\), and \(G(0)=1/2\) at the second vertex after collision. Put \(v=s_2-s_1\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E X_2(t,x)
&=\frac12\int_{0}^{t}s_1\int_{0}^{t-s_1}v^{-a}\,\mathrm dv\,\mathrm ds_1\\
&=\frac1{2(1-a)}\int_0^t s_1(t-s_1)^{1-a}\,\mathrm ds_1\\
&=\frac1{2(1-a)}
\frac{t^{3-a}}{(2-a)(3-a)}
=\frac12 c_0 t^{3-a}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The \((2,0)\) term is identical.

### 1.3 Total order‑two contribution, including both unequal splits

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_1(t)
&=\mathbb E[X_0X_2]+\mathbb E[X_1X_1]+\mathbb E[X_2X_0]\\
&=2\,\mathbb E X_2(t,x)+\mathbb E X_1(t,x)^2\\
&=2\cdot\left(\frac12c_0t^{3-a}\right)+c_0t^{3-a}
=2c_0t^{3-a}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The empty‑chain factor \(X_0=1\) and the equality \(\mathbb E[X_0X_2]=\mathbb E X_2\) give the factor \(2\) in front of \(\mathbb E X_2\); the middle term \(\mathbb E[X_1X_1]=\mathbb E X_1(t,x)^2\) is the cross‑term computed above.

Calibration at the static boundary: \(c_0|_{a=0}=1/6\), so \(\mathbb E X_1^2=t^3/6\), \(\mathbb E X_2=t^3/12\), and \(A_1(t)=t^3/3\). This matches the static value recorded elsewhere.

**Conclusion for PROOF003:** no falsification. The computation is exact on its ordered two‑chain/one‑chain domains.

## 2. PROOF214: coefficient‑one translated covariance Cauchy–Schwarz

The lemma uses the Laplace–mixture feature

\[
h_\lambda(t,r)=\sqrt{2\lambda}\,e^{-\lambda(t-r)}\mathbf 1_{\{r\le t\}},
\]

which satisfies

\[
\int_\mathbb R h_\lambda(t,r)h_\lambda(s,r)\,\mathrm dr=e^{-\lambda|t-s|},
\qquad
\int_\mathbb R h_\lambda(t,r)^2\,\mathrm dr=1 .
\]

Define the capped stationary covariance

\[
C_L((t,x),(s,y))
=\kappa_L(t-s)\gamma(x-y),
\qquad
\kappa_L(t)=\int_{(0,L]}e^{-\lambda|t|}\nu(\mathrm d\lambda).
\]

On

\[
\mathcal H_L=L^2((0,L]\times\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R,\nu\otimes\mu\otimes\mathrm dr)
\]

set

\[
\Phi_L(t,x)(\lambda,\xi,r)
=h_\lambda(t,r)e^{i\xi\cdot x}.
\]

Then

\[
\|\Phi_L(t,x)\|^2=\nu((0,L])\mu(\mathbb R^d),
\qquad
\langle \Phi_L(t,x),\Phi_L(s,y)\rangle
=C_L((t,x),(s,y)).
\]

Thus

\[
Q_L(f,h)=\langle F_L(f),F_L(h)\rangle_{\mathcal H_L},
\qquad
F_L(f)=\int f(t,x)\Phi_L(t,x)\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm dx,
\]

and Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
|Q_L(f,h)|\le Q_L(f,f)^{1/2}Q_L(h,h)^{1/2}.
\]

Two distinct translations are handled by using the original stationary covariance and the equal‑translation invariance

\[
Q_L(T_{A,a}f,T_{A,a}h)=Q_L(f,h).
\]

This yields the coefficient‑one bound

\[
0\le Q_L(T_{A,a}G_1^0,T_{B,b}G_2^0)
\le N_{2,L}(G_1)N_{2,L}(G_2),
\]

where the left nonnegativity is plain nonnegativity of \(G_i\) and \(C_L\ge0\), and \(N_{2,L}\) is the capped Hilbert norm. The predecessors \(A,B\) are not discarded: the time argument retains \(s-t+A-B\); invariance is applied separately to each self pairing.

The passage to unbounded \(G_i\) uses the truncations \(G_{i,M}=\min(G_i,M)\mathbf1_{\{0<t\le M,|x|\le M\}}\) and monotone convergence since \(C_L\ge0\). Finally \(L\to\infty\) is monotone by positivity of \(\nu\) and nonnegativity of \(\gamma\). Zero measures and zero norms cause no division by a norm.

**Verification:** the normalization is consistent. For \(f=h=G_i^0\), \(\langle F_L,F_L\rangle=Q_L(G_i^0,G_i^0)\), which increases to \(N_C(G_i)^2\). The coefficient one is independent of origin, dimension, cap, and covariance mass. PROOF214 is sound.

## 3. EXTERNAL_CANDIDATE_OWN215: the pairing‑product envelope

### 3.1 The precise input

For each edge \(e\in D\), \(\mu_e\) is finite and positive with

\[
\gamma_e(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu_e(\mathrm d\xi)\ge0,
\]

and for \(0<a<1\),

\[
\nu_e(\mathrm d\lambda)=\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(a)^{-1}\mathbf 1_{A_e}(\lambda)\lambda^{a-1}\mathrm d\lambda,
\qquad
\kappa_e(t)=\int e^{-\lambda|t|}\nu_e(\mathrm d\lambda),
\]

with \(A_e\subset(0,\infty)\) measurable. The norms are

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_0(G)&=\int G,\\
N_{1,e}(G)&=\int\left|\int e^{-\lambda t+i\xi\cdot x}G(t,x)\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm dx\right|
\nu_e(\mathrm d\lambda)\mu_e(\mathrm d\xi),\\
N_{2,e}(G)^2&=\int G(s,x)G(t,y)\kappa_e(s-t)\gamma_e(x-y)
\,\mathrm ds\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm dy .
\end{aligned}
\]

The diagram \(I_D(\mathbf G)\) and the admissible products \(\mathcal P_{Q,\iota}\) are as defined in the source body.

### 3.2 The three convolution laws

Put \(F(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb R^d}G(r,y)H(t-r,x-y)\,\mathrm dy\,\mathrm dr\). By Tonelli,

\[
N_0(F)=N_0(G)N_0(H),
\qquad
\mathcal L_F(\lambda,\xi)=\mathcal L_G(\lambda,\xi)\mathcal L_H(\lambda,\xi).
\]

Since

\[
|\mathcal L_G(\lambda,\xi)|\le\int e^{-\lambda t}G(t,x)\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm dx\le N_0(G),
\qquad \lambda\ge0,
\]

we get

\[
N_{1,e}(F)\le N_0(G)N_{1,e}(H).
\]

For \(N_{2,e}\), zero‑extend \(H\) in time and write \(F\) as the Bochner integral of the translates \(H(\cdot-r,\cdot-y)\) against the nonnegative density \(G(r,y)\). Translation preserves each second norm, so Minkowski gives

\[
N_{2,e}(F)\le N_0(G)N_{2,e}(H).
\]

Thus a fused kernel is admissible at every remaining edge and in every remaining slot type.

### 3.3 Base cases

- \(n=0\): both sides equal \(1\).
- One vertex on each chain: the cross covariance is

\[
\int G_1(s,x)G_2(t,y)\kappa_e(s-t)\gamma_e(x-y)
=Q(G_1^0,G_2^0)\le N_{2,e}(G_1)N_{2,e}(G_2),
\]

so \(Q_2=\{1,2\}\), \(Q_0=Q_1=\varnothing\).

- Two vertices on one chain, the other chain empty: with \(G_{\rm first}\), \(G_{\rm last}\), the integral factors as

\[
\int N_0(G_{\rm first})\int G_{\rm last}(t,x)\kappa_e(t)\gamma_e(x)\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm dx
\le N_0(G_{\rm first})N_{1,e}(G_{\rm last})
=\frac12N_0(G_{\rm first})\bigl[2N_{1,e}(G_{\rm last})\bigr].
\]

This is exactly the chronological base; it produces the factor \(2\) on the first norm and the domestic half‑bound.

### 3.4 Regional half‑bound

Let \(v\) be a chain endpoint, \(p\) its predecessor, \(a\) the partner of \(v\), \(e_0=\{a,v\}\), and suppose a region \(R\) satisfies \(s_a\le s_p\) with \(R\) independent of \((s_v,x_v)\). Since \(\kappa_{e_0}\) is decreasing on positive arguments,

\[
\kappa_{e_0}(s_v-s_a)\le\kappa_{e_0}(s_v-s_p).
\]

After the change \(u=s_v-s_p,\ z=x_v-x_p\), the endpoint integral has spectral phase \(e^{i\xi\cdot(x_p-x_a)}\) of modulus one, so it is bounded by

\[
\int|\mathcal L G_v(\lambda,\xi)|\,\nu_{e_0}(\mathrm d\lambda)\mu_{e_0}(\mathrm d\xi)
=N_{1,e_0}(G_v).
\]

Dropping \(R\) by positivity, removing \(v\), and either integrating \(a\) to \(N_0(G_a)\) or fusing \(G_a G_b\) into \(F=G_a*G_b\) gives a reduced diagram with \(n-1\) edges. Induction supplies a full admissible product \(P_{\rm rest}\).

The three possible slots for \(F\) are now exactly as in the source body.

- \(i=0\), \(F\) in a mass slot: mass multiplicativity gives

\[
I_R\le \frac12\,N_0(G_a)N_0(G_b)\bigl[2N_{1,e_0}(G_v)\bigr]B_{\rm rest}.
\]

- \(i=1\), \(F\) in a first slot with inherited edge \(e_1\): the retained factor \(2\) gives

\[
N_{1,e_0}(G_v)\,[2N_{1,e_1}(F)]B_{\rm rest}
\le\frac12 N_0(G_a)\,[2N_{1,e_1}(G_b)]\,[2N_{1,e_0}(G_v)]B_{\rm rest}.
\]

This is the nontrivial repair: the first‑slot factor \(2\) is passed through, not absorbed into an \(N_{2}N_{2}\) comparison.

- \(i=2\), \(F\) in a second slot with inherited edge \(e_1\): by \(N_{2,e}(F)\le N_0(G)N_{2,e}(H)\),

\[
I_R\le\frac12 N_0(G_a)\,[2N_{1,e_0}(G_v)]\,N_{2,e_1}(G_b)B_{\rm rest}.
\]

The cardinality identities

\[
|Q_0|=|Q_1|,\qquad |Q_2|\text{ even}
\]

are preserved in all three cases because the reduced products already obey them.

### 3.5 Global cases

- **Domestic endpoint**: has its partner at or before its predecessor, so the half‑bound applies on the full domain.
- **Case 1**: one domestic endpoint exists; the half‑bound proves the full bound.
- **Case 2**: \(u=n_1\) and \(v=2n\) paired together. Conditioning on predecessors and keeping \(A-B\), PROOF214 supplies

\[
\le N_{2,e}(G_u)N_{2,e}(G_v);
\]

then induction and adding \(u,v\) to \(Q_2\) prove the full bound.
- **Case 3**: both endpoint pairs cross and distinct. Split at \(\{s_a\le s_q\}\). On each piece the half‑bound produces possibly different admissible products, so

\[
I_D\le\frac{P+P'}{2}\le\max(P,P').
\]

### 3.6 Cap removal and zero branch

The proof is first done at \(A_e\cap(0,L]\). Letting \(L\to\infty\) is monotone, and since the admissible set is finite, the maximum converges to the maximum of limits. Zero kernels, zero norms, and zero measures are allowed because no division by a norm occurs.

**Mathematical conclusion:** the statement of OWN215 is proved. The bound is a per‑family existential maximum; it does not assert a single \(Q\) valid for all kernel families, and it does not require a measurable maximizing selector.

## 4. PROOF005 audit: positive spatial Wick‑measure limit

**Parent trace.** PROOF005 does **not** consume OWN215 or PROOF214. It uses

- the heat bound (E:construction‑heat‑bound),
- the iterated Brownian moment bound (E:construction‑brownian‑moments),
- the wave‑to‑heat identity,
- spectral Cauchy–Schwarz,
- standard monotone/Dirichlet/Laplace moment arguments.

### 4.1 The transform formula

The measure is \(\nu_{\varepsilon,D}=e^{-\sum r_l}\rho_{\varepsilon,D}\). Its Laplace transform is

\[
L_{\varepsilon,D}(\lambda)
=\int e^{-\sum \lambda_l r_l}\mathrm d\nu_{\varepsilon,D}
=\int\exp\left(-\frac12\sum_{e=\{j,h\}}(\epsilon_j+\epsilon_h)|\xi_e|^2\right)
\prod_{l=1}^{2k}\frac1{(1+\lambda_l)^2+|\eta_l(\xi)|^2}
\prod_{e\in D}\mu(\mathrm d\xi_e).
\]

Here the denominator uses damping \(1+\lambda_l\), exactly because \(\nu\) already carries the factor \(e^{-\sum r_l}\). Since \(\int_0^\infty e^{-(1+\lambda)r}G_r(\mathrm dz)\) has Fourier transform \(((1+\lambda)^2+|\xi|^2)^{-1}\), the identity is consistent.

The integrand is nonnegative and increases when the spatial widths decrease. The bound by the all‑\(\epsilon_\ast\) transform is legitimate because replacing \((\epsilon_j+\epsilon_h)/2\) by the smaller \(\epsilon_\ast\) increases each Gaussian multiplier.

### 4.2 The Brownian moment bound

For one path,

\[
\mathbb E_B S_b(T)^j\le
\frac{(2j)![A\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1-\alpha/2)]^j}
{\Gamma_{\mathrm E}((2-\alpha/2)j+1)}
T^{(2-\alpha/2)j}.
\]

The exponent is consistent at \(j=1\): ordering the two times gives \(T^{2-\alpha/2}\), and the Dirichlet factor is

\[
\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1-\alpha/2)\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1)}{\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(3-\alpha/2)}.
\]

Multiplication by \(2!\) and the prefactor \(A\) reproduces

\[
2A\int_{0<s<t<t?} (t-s)^{-\alpha/2}\,\mathrm ds\,\mathrm dt
=\frac{2A T^{2-\alpha/2}}{(1-\alpha/2)(2-\alpha/2)}.
\]

For general \(j\), the iterated deletion selects \(j\) distinct adjacent gaps among the \(2j\) gaps (including the terminal gap). The Dirichlet denominator is \(\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(j(1-\alpha/2)+j+1)=\Gamma_{\mathrm E}((2-\alpha/2)j+1)\), and the exponent \(T^{(2-\alpha/2)j}\) includes the \(j\) unselected gaps integrated with \(\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1)=1\). This verifies the stated formula.

### 4.3 Common‑width total‑mass bound

The wave‑to‑heat identity

\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-at}G_t(\mathrm dx)\,\mathrm dt
=\frac12\int_0^\infty e^{-a^2u/2}p_u(x)\,\mathrm du\,\mathrm dx
\]

converts the common‑width transform to \(2^{-2k}\) times a two‑Brownian‑chain integral with gap weights \(e^{-\sum u_l/2}\). The quarter‑fraction insertion

\[
1=\frac14\int_{\tau_1}^{\infty}\int_{\tau_2}^{\infty}
e^{-\frac12[(a-\tau_1)+(b-\tau_2)]}\,\mathrm da\,\mathrm db
\]

allows the ordered chain times to be extended to full cubes. At fixed paths, each pair contributes one occupation integral; cross‑pairs are bounded by

\[
A_{12}\le\sqrt{S_{2\epsilon_\ast}^{(1)}(a)S_{2\epsilon_\ast}^{(2)}(b)}.
\]

If there are \(v_1,v_2\) self‑pairs and \(w\) cross‑pairs with \(v_1+v_2+w=k\), the occupation product is

\[
S_1^{v_1+w/2}S_2^{v_2+w/2}\le(1+S_1)^k(1+S_2)^k.
\]

The moment bound and independence of the two Brownian chains give the uniform bound

\[
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}L_{\varepsilon,D}(0)\le
2^{-2k-2}\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-a/2}M_k(a)\,\mathrm da\right)^2
<\infty.
\]

### 4.4 Weak limit and face masses

Monotone convergence gives the limiting Laplace transforms \(L_D(\lambda)\) for \(\lambda\ge0\). On \([0,1]^{2k}\) via \(y_l=e^{-r_l}\), the bounded masses have weak cluster limits. The integer monomial moments are the prescribed \(L_D\) values, so polynomial density identifies the limit. Since \(\int\prod y_l^c\) is \(L_D(c,\dots,c)\) and \(c\downarrow0\) gives no mass on any \(y_l=0\) face, closed neighborhoods of these faces have vanishing limiting mass. The portmanteau inequality then gives weak convergence on \([0,\infty)^{2k}\), independent of the relative width rates.

Coordinate‑zero faces have zero mass because \(\lambda_l\to\infty\) in the limiting integral gives zero, and this equals \(\nu_D\{r_l=0\}\). The homogeneity

\[
\rho_{\varepsilon,D}(cA)=c^{(4-\alpha)k}\rho_{\epsilon/c^2,D}(A)
\]

uses \(G_{cr}(c\,\mathrm dz)=cG_r(\mathrm dz)\), \(\gamma_b(cx)=c^{-\alpha}\gamma_{b/c^2}(x)\), and the \(2k\) time Jacobians. The exponent is \(2k\) from Green factors, \(-k\alpha\) from covariances, and \(2k\) from \(\mathrm dr\)-Jacobians: \(2k-\alpha k+2k=(4-\alpha)k\). Horizon faces have zero mass by the usual disjoint‑dilate/local finiteness argument.

**Conclusion:** no specific failed inequality was located in PROOF005. The four load steps (transform, mass bound, weak cluster, homogeneity) are all independently checkable as above.
