## 0. Falsification first

### 0.1 Zero-frequency temporal increment variance

For an interval of length \(h>0\), the form \(\|1_{[0,h]}\|_a^2=\int_0^h\int_0^h|t-s|^{-a}\,dt\,ds\) is exactly

\[
2\int_0^h\int_0^s(s-t)^{-a}\,dt\,ds
=2\int_0^h\frac{s^{1-a}}{1-a}\,ds
=\frac{2h^{2-a}}{(1-a)(2-a)}.
\]

At \(a=1/2\): \(\frac{2h^{3/2}}{(1/2)(3/2)}=\frac{8}{3}h^{3/2}\). This is the scalar kernel test confirming the \(h^{2-a}\) scale used throughout 066–069; it uses a constant vector \(A\equiv1,\omega=0\) only as a self-consistent check that the modulation bound of `E:masked-temporal-modulation` is not tight but valid, since \(2/(2-a)\le2<16\).

### 0.2 Rank-one families: why BOTH variances are needed

Use \(H=\ell^2(\mathbb N)\), a fixed unit vector \(e_0\), and an orthonormal tail \(\{e_j\}_{j=1}^N\), with

\[
T_j=e_0\otimes e_j\quad(j=1,\dots,N),
\qquad T_jT_j^*=e_0\otimes e_0,\qquad T_j^*T_j=e_j\otimes e_j.
\]

Then

\[
V_{\rm out}=\sum_jT_jT_j^*=N\,e_0\otimes e_0,
\qquad
V_{\rm in}=\sum_jT_j^*T_j=\sum_{j\le N}e_j\otimes e_j=P_N,
\]

so \(\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}=N\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}=N\). For the Gaussian \(X=\sum_jg_jT_j\),

\[
XX^*=\left(\sum_jg_j^2\right)e_0\otimes e_0
\Longrightarrow
\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_4}^4=\mathbb E\left(\sum_jg_j^2\right)^{\!2}=N(N+2).
\]

But \(\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^2=N^2\) while \(\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^2=N\). Controlling only \(V_{\rm in}\) (whose trace square is \(N\)) cannot bound the \(S_4\) moment (\(N(N+2)\)), and its transpose family \(T_j'=e_j\otimes e_0\) reverses the roles: \(V_{\rm in}'=N e_0\otimes e_0\), \(V_{\rm out}'=P_N\). Hence a Gaussian matrix bound must keep the **average of the trace powers of BOTH variances**,

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^m+\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^m}{2},
\]

exactly as the Hermitian-dilation/IBP argument produces later in 077. This is a scalar-operator check, not a surrogate for the wave estimates.

## 1. OWN061 — translated spectral mass

From `E:masked-spectral-convention`, the single tempered pairing applied to the Schwartz test \(e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{-iy\cdot x}\) gives

\[
\int e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{-iy\cdot x}\Gamma(\mathrm dx)
=\int\left(\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{-iy\cdot x}\,\mathrm dx\right)\mu(\mathrm d\xi).
\]

The frequency-side Gaussian is, coordinate by coordinate from \(J(b)=\sqrt{\pi/A}e^{-b^2/(4A)}\),

\[
\int e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{i(\xi-y)\cdot x}\mathrm dx
=\left(\frac{2\sqrt\pi}{r}\right)^d e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\int e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}\mu(\mathrm d\xi)
=\left(\frac r{2\sqrt\pi}\right)^d\int e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{-iy\cdot x}\Gamma(\mathrm dx).
\]

Since \(\Gamma\ge0\), the absolute value removes the phase, and homogeneity \(x\mapsto rx\) pushes \(\Gamma\) to \(r^{\alpha-d}\Gamma\):

\[
\int e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}\Gamma(\mathrm dx)
=r^{\alpha-d}M_\Gamma.
\]

Hence

\[
0\le\int e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}\mu(\mathrm d\xi)\le\frac{r^\alpha M_\Gamma}{(2\sqrt\pi)^d}.
\]

On \(B(y,r)\) the integrand is \(\ge e^{-1}\), giving

\[
\mu(B(y,r))\le C_\Gamma r^\alpha,\qquad C_\Gamma=\frac{eM_\Gamma}{(2\sqrt\pi)^d}.
\]

Positivity \(0<M_\Gamma<\infty\): finiteness is temperedness; strict positivity uses \(\Gamma\ne0\) and the everywhere-positive Gaussian. The closed-ball estimate follows by applying the open-ball bound at \(r+\varepsilon\) and letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) via Radon finiteness on bounded sets. No angular density, no isotropy, no spectral density are used; only \(\Gamma\ge0\), evenness in the convention, homogeneity, and positivity are. The zero covariance \(\Gamma=0\) gives \(M_\Gamma=0,C_\Gamma=0,\mu=0\), so all these bounds are trivially true.

## 2. OWN062 — auxiliary mask norm

\(\zeta_R=\zeta(\cdot/R)\), \(k_R=\widehat{\zeta_R}=R^d\widehat\zeta(R\cdot)\), \(\|k_R\|_1=\|\widehat\zeta\|_1=L\). \(\zeta\) real implies \(k_R(-u)=\overline{k_R(u)}\); evenness of \(\mu\) then gives evenness of

\[
\nu_R(\mathrm d\eta)=c_d^2L\left(\int|k_R(\xi-\eta)|\mu(\mathrm d\xi)\right)\mathrm d\eta.
\]

Ball growth: Tonelli over the nonnegative integrand gives

\[
\nu_R(B(y,r))
=c_d^2L\int\mu(\mathrm d\xi)\int_{B(y,r)}|k_R(\xi-\eta)|\,\mathrm d\eta.
\]

With \(u=\xi-\eta\), the condition \(\eta\in B(y,r)\) is \(\xi\in B(y+u,r)\); interchanging again,

\[
=c_d^2L\int|k_R(u)|\,\mu(B(y+u,r))\,\mathrm du
\le c_d^2L\int|k_R(u)|\,C_\Gamma r^\alpha\,\mathrm du
=c_d^2L^2C_\Gamma r^\alpha.
\]

Uniform in \(R\) because \(\|k_R\|_1=L\).

Hilbert-valued bound: Bochner Fourier inversion gives, for \(H\)-valued Schwartz \(F\),

\[
\widehat{\zeta_RF}(\xi)=c_d\int k_R(\xi-\eta)\widehat F(\eta)\,\mathrm d\eta,
\]

so by triangle inequality and the split \(|k_R|=\sqrt{|k_R|}\sqrt{|k_R|}\) with the total mass \(L\),

\[
\|\widehat{\zeta_RF}(\xi)\|_H^2
\le c_d^2L\int|k_R(\xi-\eta)|\|\widehat F(\eta)\|_H^2\,\mathrm d\eta.
\]

Integrating against \(\mu\) and re-Tonelli proves `E:masked-hilbert-fourier`. The finite-rank approximation is legitimate by pointwise-norm convergence of the orthonormal projections of \(F\). Temperateness of \(\nu_R\): the ball bound at one radius plus dyadic shells gives polynomial growth, hence \(\int(1+|\eta|)^{-N}\mathrm d\nu_R<\infty\) for large \(N\).

## 3. OWN063 — temporal completion

PD: \(|t-s|^{-a}=\Gamma_E(a)^{-1}\int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}e^{-\lambda|t-s|}\,\mathrm d\lambda\), and

\[
e^{-\lambda|t-s|}=\int_{-\infty}^{\min(t,s)}2\lambda e^{-\lambda(t-u)}e^{-\lambda(s-u)}\,\mathrm du
\]

(limits: substitute \(w=\min(t,s)-u\), the integral evaluates to \(e^{-\lambda|t-s|}\)). So the kernel is a nonnegative mixture of rank-one squares, hence positive semidefinite on real compact smooth tests.

Schur/\(\ell^2\) conversion:

\[
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_0^T|t-s|^{-a}\,\mathrm ds
=\sup_t\left(\frac{t^{1-a}+(T-t)^{1-a}}{1-a}\right)
\le\frac{2T^{1-a}}{1-a}.
\]

For a symmetric nonnegative kernel,

\[
\langle f,Kf\rangle=\tfrac12\int K(t,s)(|f(t)|^2+|f(s)|^2)=\int|f(t)|^2\int K(t,s)\,\mathrm ds,
\]

so `E:masked-time-schur`. Separability: a countable \(L^2\)-dense family of compact smooth tests is dense in the null-quotient completion by the continuous \(L^2\) inclusion. Complexification: the sesquilinear norm is \(\int_{[0,T]^2}f(t)\overline{f(s)}|t-s|^{-a}\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm ds\); the real/imaginary split contributes two nonnegative pieces and cancels the imaginary cross term by symmetry. Bounded measurable interval indicators are in the completion by smooth interior approximation.

## 6. OWN066–068 — temporal estimates

### 066

Recall \(c(v)=\int_s^{s+h-v}A(u+v)\overline{A(u)}\,\mathrm du\). Splitting the double integral into the two conjugate triangles,

\[
\|1_IAe^{i\omega t}\|_a^2=2\operatorname{Re}\int_0^hv^{-a}e^{i\omega v}c(v)\,\mathrm dv.
\]

\(c(h)=0\), \(|c|\le hM^2\), and the Leibniz derivative gives \(|c'|\le M^2+hMD\). Setting \(v=hy\), \(c(hy)=h f(y)\),

\[
\|1_IAe^{i\omega t}\|_a^2=2h^{2-a}\operatorname{Re}\int_0^1y^{-a}e^{i\omega hy}f(y)\,\mathrm dy,
\qquad f(1)=0,\quad |f|,|f'|\le(M+hD)^2.
\]

(Here the \(h^{2-a}\) power is verified: \(v^{-a}c(v)dv=(hy)^{-a}(hf(y))(hdy)=h^{2-a}y^{-a}f(y)dy\).) The \(|\omega h|\le1\) case bounds the norm by \(2h^{2-a}K^2/(1-a)\). The \(k=|\omega h|>1\) case splits \([0,1/k]\) (bound \(K^2k^{a-1}/(1-a)\)) and \([1/k,1]\) (integration by parts; endpoint \(f(1)=0\), the other endpoint \(\le K^2k^{a-1}\), derivative term \(\le K^2k^{a-1}(1+1/(1-a))\)), giving at most \(4K^2k^{a-1}/(1-a)\). Both signs of \(\omega\) are covered by \(|\omega|\).

### 068

\(b_r(t)=\langle r\rangle\sin(rt)/r\) for \(r>0\), \(b_0(t)=t\). For \(0\le r\le1\),

## 7. OWN069 — scalar frequency row

For \(\nu\) with \(\nu(B(y,r))\le Cr^\alpha\), define \(\nu_\xi\) as the pushforward \(\lambda\mapsto\eta=\xi-\lambda\), so \(\nu_\xi(B(0,R))\le CR^\alpha\). With \(q=\max(1,|\xi|)\), \(\epsilon=1-2\gamma>0\):

**Near region \(|\lambda|\le4q\)** gives \(|\eta|\le5q\) and \(\mathcal Q_h\le2\). Then \(J_h^{\rm near}\le2\langle\xi\rangle^{-\epsilon}A_\xi(q)\) where

\[
A_\xi(q)=\int_{|\eta|\le5q}\langle\eta\rangle^{-1}\nu_\xi(\mathrm d\eta)
\le C\left(1+2^\alpha\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}2^{j(\alpha-1)}\right),\qquad 5q\le2^N<10q.
\]

The geometric sum is bounded for \(\alpha<1\), is \(N\sim\log q\) for \(\alpha=1\), and is \(\le(10q)^{\alpha-1}/(2^{\alpha-1}-1)\) for \(\alpha>1\). Multiplying by \(q^{-\epsilon}\):

- \(\alpha<1\): bounded (the power \(q^{-\epsilon}\) kills nothing needed).
- \(\alpha=1\): \(e^{-\epsilon u}(1+u)\le1+1/(e\epsilon)\), \(u=\log q\).
- \(\alpha>1\): the power is \(q^{\alpha+2\gamma-2}\) with \(\alpha+2\gamma<2\) by \(\gamma<(2-\alpha)/2\).

So the near contribution is \(\le K(\alpha,\gamma)C\).

**Far region \(|\lambda|>4q\)**, \(r:=|\lambda|\): then \(|\eta|\ge3r/4\), \(||\eta|-|\xi||\ge r/2\), \(|\eta|+|\xi|\ge r/2\), hence

\[
\mathcal Q_h(|\xi|,|\eta|)\le2\langle hr/2\rangle^{a-1}\le2^{2-a}\langle hr\rangle^{a-1},
\]

and \(\Phi_h\le2^{2-a}\langle hr\rangle^{a-1}/(\langle\xi\rangle\langle\eta\rangle)\). Thus

\[
J_h^{\rm far}\le\frac43\,2^{2-a}\int_{|\lambda|>1}|\lambda|^{-1}\langle h|\lambda|\rangle^{a-1}\nu(\mathrm d\lambda).
\]

Dyadic splitting \(\frac43 2^{2-a}\le C(\alpha,a)\), now

\[
S(h)=\sum_{j\ge0}2^{j(\alpha-1)}\langle h2^j\rangle^{a-1}.
\]

With \(L=\lfloor\log_2(1/h)\rfloor\): for \(j\le L\), \(\langle h2^j\rangle^{a-1}\le1\), giving the finite sum bounded by \(1/(1-2^{\alpha-1})\) for \(\alpha<1\), by \(1+\log_2(1/h)\) for \(\alpha=1\), by \(2^{\alpha-1}h^{1-\alpha}/(2^{\alpha-1}-1)\) for \(\alpha>1\); for \(j\ge L+1\), \((h2^j)^{a-1}\) gives the tail \(\le h^{1-\alpha}/(1-2^{\alpha+a-2})\), using \(2^{L+1}>1/h\) and the strict \(\alpha+a<2\). Hence \(S(h)\le K_{\alpha,a}B_\alpha(h)\), with

\[
B_\alpha(h)=\mathbf 1_{\alpha<1}+(1+\log(1/h))\mathbf 1_{\alpha=1}+h^{1-\alpha}\mathbf 1_{\alpha>1}.
\]

Combining near and far: \(\sup_\xi J_h(\xi)\le K(\alpha,a,\gamma)CB_\alpha(h)\), with all constants \(d\)-independent (only shell estimates). No density, no evenness, no nonnegative inverse covariance assumption.
