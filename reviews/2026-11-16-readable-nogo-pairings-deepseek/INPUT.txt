\section{Pairings, ordering, and comparison kernels}\label{S:nogo-pairings}

The second moment contains two ordered chains and all their Gaussian
pairings, including pairs within a chain. This structure offers several
natural reductions: estimate each pairing separately, compare with a single
chain, or copy the first-moment clock. The obstructions below distinguish
loss of scale from failure of an inequality. A majorant can be too large even
when every step used to obtain it is valid.

Throughout this section write $a=\alpha_0$ and
$\beta=4-\alpha-a$. Let $X_n$ denote the ordered Stratonovich term of degree
$n$, and put
\[
  a_k=\E X_{2k}(1,x),\qquad
  A_k=\sum_{m+n=2k}\E[X_m(1,x)X_n(1,x)],\qquad
  b_1=\E X_1(1,x)^2.
\]
Thus $A_1=2a_1+b_1$. Homogeneity supplies the factor $t^{\beta k}$.
The nonnegative convergent moment expansions used here are those of
Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}; they include unequal chain lengths. In dimension
one we use $G_s(z)=\frac12\mathbf1_{\{|z|<s\}}$.

\subsection{NG-01: one common estimate loses a factorial power}
\label{S:nogo-01}

Chen's author version~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{Theorem~3.1, (3.6)}
bounds regularized two-chain integrals for arbitrary pairings under its
finite-measure and norm hypotheses. Proposition~\ref{P:envelope} gives the
application under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}. Applying one common norm bound
to every pairing is an attractive first attempt at the upper exponent.
The issue is what happens after optimization and summation.

\setcounter{nogo}{0}
\begin{nogo}[The common-parameter majorant has the wrong scale]
  \label{P:nogo-01}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, let $P_k=(2k)!/(2^k k!)$ and $K>0$.
  The numerical majorant
  \[
    B_K(t)=1+\sum_{k\ge1}(2k+1)P_kK^k
    \inf_{\theta>0}e^{2\theta t}\theta^{-\beta k}
  \]
  satisfies $\log B_K(t)\asymp t^{\beta/(\beta-1)}$ as $t\to\infty$.
  Since $\beta/(\beta-1)>\kappa=\beta/(3-\alpha)$, this majorant cannot
  establish a finite upper constant on the scale $t^\kappa$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  The infimum equals $e^{\beta k}(2t/(\beta k))^{\beta k}$.
  The inequalities $k!\le P_k\le2^k k!$ and
  $e^{-k}\le k!/k^k\le1$ show that $B_K$ lies between two series
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(c t^\beta)^k}{(k!)^q},\qquad q=\beta-1>1,
  \]
  with different positive constants $c$. For $z>0$, this series is at most
  $\exp(qz^{1/q})$, by the $\ell^q$--$\ell^1$ inequality. Its term with
  $k=\lfloor z^{1/q}/2\rfloor$ is at least $2^{qk}$ for large $z$.
  This proves the two-sided growth. Finally, $\beta-1=3-\alpha-a<3-\alpha$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-01}.
\end{proof}

This restates the obstruction in Proposition~\ref{P:envelope} and
Remark~\ref{R:G0-verdict}. The lower estimate is for $B_K$, not for the
moment. The valid upper estimate improves the crude quadratic exponent,
but its factorial denominator misses $(k!)^a$. The split count $2k+1$
costs only an exponential factor in $k$; removing it does not repair the
loss. The same calculation for one chain, with $e^{\theta t}$, has the
same defect. Reopening requires an estimate retaining ordering or grouping
pairings before taking this common bound. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper}
provides such a different argument.

\subsection{NG-02: a single adjacent diagram is too large}
\label{S:nogo-02}

There are roughly an exponential factor times $k!$ pairings of $2k$
vertices. In white space, a uniform denominator $(k!)^3$ for each diagram
would therefore give the desired denominator $(k!)^2$ after summation.
The following example explains why that convenient sufficient estimate is
stronger than the problem permits.

\setcounter{nogo}{1}
\begin{nogo}[No uniform cubic factorial denominator per diagram]
  \label{P:nogo-02}
  Let $d=1$, $\Gamma=\delta_0$ and $0<a<1$. For the split $(0,2k)$
  and adjacent matching $\{(1,2),(3,4),\ldots,(2k-1,2k)\}$, its contribution is
  \[
    J_{\rm adj}(t)=
    \left[\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1-a)}2\right]^k
    \frac{t^{(3-a)k}}{\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1+(3-a)k)}.
  \]
  Consequently, there is no finite $C=C(a)$ bounding every diagram by
  $C^k t^{(3-a)k}/(k!)^3$ for all $k$ and $t>0$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Use root-forward increments $h_1,\ldots,h_{2k}>0$ with
  $\sum h_i<t$. Each adjacent contraction evaluates the even Green factor
  at zero, giving $1/2$, and integration of the preceding free increment
  gives mass $h_{2j-1}$. The temporal factor is $h_{2j}^{-a}$. Therefore,
  \[
    J_{\rm adj}(t)=2^{-k}
    \int_{\sum h_i<t}\prod_{j=1}^k h_{2j-1}h_{2j}^{-a}\,\ud h.
  \]
  Here delta contractions mean limits of Gaussian approximate identities.
  For completeness, fix $k$ and a strictly positive increment vector.
  After convolution the paired positions approach equality. The even Green
  factors are continuous at zero; the remaining indicator boundaries are
  finitely many hyperplanes in the $k$ free positions and have measure zero.
  The limit is thus the stated diagonal integral. All positions in the
  original chain lie in $[-t,t]$. Uniformly in the smoothing widths, replacing
  its Green indicators by one bounds the spatial integral by
  $2^{-2k}(2t)^k$: each disjoint smoothed pair has integral at most $2t$
  over $[-t,t]^2$. The temporal factors on the full time cube are a product
  of $k$ disjoint factors $|s-r|^{-a}$, each integrable because $a<1$.
  Dominated convergence justifies the fixed-order spatial limit through the
  time integration, and also temporal truncation limits.

  The Dirichlet simplex formula, with parameters $2$ and $1-a$ for each
  pair, now gives the asserted expression. Stirling's formula yields
  \[
    \log\big((k!)^3J_{\rm adj}(1)\big)=a k\log k+O(k).
  \]
  This exceeds $k\log C$ eventually for every fixed $C$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-02}.
\end{proof}

The adjacent matching is only one diagram. Its denominator is, up to
exponential factors, $(k!)^{3-a}$, which is stronger than the desired
summed denominator $(k!)^2$. Hence, this witness does not obstruct the
summed bound. A viable successor must retain the number of large diagrams,
or sum before estimating; it cannot assign the same cubic denominator to
all of them.

\subsection[NG-03: a squared-mean comparison fails]{NG-03: doubled variance does not give the proposed squared mean}
\label{S:nogo-03}

A positive mixture of exponentials of linear Gaussian functionals would
satisfy a useful comparison between the second moment and the mean at
noise amplitude $\sqrt2$. Such a representation must be established for
the wave rather than inferred from a parabolic analogy.

\setcounter{nogo}{2}
\begin{nogo}[Failure of the all-time comparison with unit prefactor]
  \label{P:nogo-03}
  For the admissible Riesz case $d=1$, $\Gamma(x)=|x|^{-1/2}$ and $a=1/2$,
  \[
    \E u(t,x)^2>\big(\E u_{\sqrt2}(t,x)\big)^2
  \]
  for all sufficiently small $t>0$, where $u_\lambda$ uses noise amplitude
  $\lambda$ and the same initial data.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  More generally, for $\Gamma(x)=c|x|^{-\alpha}$, $0<\alpha<1$,
  direct integration of the length-two chain gives
  \[
    a_1=\frac{c}{(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha-a)(3-\alpha-a)(4-\alpha-a)}.
  \]
  Indeed the spatial integral is
  $c h_1h_2^{1-\alpha}/(1-\alpha)$ and its temporal factor is $h_2^{-a}$;
  integrate over $h_1+h_2<1$. At $\alpha=a=1/2$, $c=1$, this gives $a_1=1/3$.
  For independent uniforms $U$ on $[-s,s]$ and $V$ on $[-r,r]$, $0<r\le s$,
  \[
    \E|U-V|=\frac{s}{2}+\frac{r^2}{6s}\le\frac{2s}{3}.
  \]
  Jensen's inequality for $z\mapsto z^{-\alpha}$ implies
  \[
    \int G_s(x)G_r(y)|x-y|^{-\alpha}\,\ud x\ud y
    \ge (3/2)^\alpha s^{1-\alpha}r.
  \]
  Integrating on the two temporal triangles gives
  \[
    b_1\ge
    \frac{2(3/2)^\alpha}{(1-a)(2-a)(4-\alpha-a)}
    =\frac89\sqrt{\frac32}>\frac23=2a_1.
  \]
  The comparison is a finite-time failure, not just a formal coefficient test.
  Put $z=t^\beta$. The two nonnegative series under comparison have expansions
  \[
    \E u(t,x)^2=1+(2a_1+b_1)z+O(z^2),\qquad
    (\E u_{\sqrt2}(t,x))^2=1+4a_1z+O(z^2).
  \]
  To justify the remainders, choose any fixed positive $z_0$ at which these
  series converge, using the moment expansion also at amplitude $\sqrt2$.
  For each nonnegative coefficient sequence $(c_j)$,
  $\sum_{j\ge2}c_jz^j\le(z/z_0)^2\sum_{j\ge2}c_jz_0^j$ when
  $0\le z\le z_0$. Apply this to both series, including the nonnegative
  Cauchy product for the squared mean. Their difference therefore has the
  strictly positive leading coefficient $b_1-2a_1$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-03}.
\end{proof}

For a deterministic nonnegative mixture of Gaussian exponentials, the
inequality follows by Tonelli and
$\|h+k\|^2/2\le\|h\|^2+\|k\|^2$, provided the same mixture represents
all amplitudes in the comparison. That proposed mechanism is excluded by
the example. White noise, an eventual large-time comparison, or a prefactor
larger than one is not excluded. Reopening requires a restriction or a new
comparison that removes the contradicted first-coefficient requirement;
it cannot merely assert this positive-mixture representation.

\subsection[NG-04: constants near the time boundary]{NG-04: a coefficient constant cannot be uniform at the time boundary}
\label{S:nogo-04}

Comparing $A_k$ with $a_k$ retains the self-pairings of the Stratonovich
problem and would transfer a first-moment bound to the second moment.
However, a prescribed asymptotic constant must not be imposed on every
low-order coefficient without checking parameter uniformity.

\setcounter{nogo}{3}
\begin{nogo}[Divergence of the first coefficient ratio]
  \label{P:nogo-04}
  Fix $0<\alpha<1$ and $c>0$, with $\Gamma(x)=c|x|^{-\alpha}$ in $d=1$.
  Then
  \[
    \lim_{a\uparrow1}(1-a)\frac{A_1}{a_1}
    =2^{2-\alpha}(1-\alpha)>0.
  \]
  In particular no constant depending on $\alpha$ alone, including
  $2^{4-\alpha}$, can satisfy $A_k\le C^k a_k$ for every $k\ge1$
  and every $0<a<1$ at this fixed spatial covariance.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Let
  $K(s,r)=\int G_s(x)G_r(y)c|x-y|^{-\alpha}\,\ud x\ud y$.
  This is continuous on $[0,1]^2$ by local integrability of the Riesz kernel
  and dominated convergence on a fixed square. Direct integration gives
  \[
    K(s,s)=\frac{c\,2^{1-\alpha}s^{2-\alpha}}
    {(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)}.
  \]
  Writing $v=s-r$, define the continuous function
  $H(v)=2\int_v^1 K(s,s-v)\,\ud s$. Then
  \[
    b_1=\int_0^1v^{-a}H(v)\,\ud v,\qquad
    H(0)=\frac{c\,2^{2-\alpha}}
    {(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}.
  \]
  For any continuous $H$ on $[0,1]$,
  $\varepsilon\int_0^1v^{-1+\varepsilon}H(v)\,\ud v\to H(0)$.
  Indeed the measures have mass one, and their mass on $[\delta,1]$ is
  $1-\delta^\varepsilon\to0$; continuity controls $[0,\delta]$.
  Thus $(1-a)b_1\to H(0)$. The formula for $a_1$ in the preceding proof gives
  \[
    a_1\longrightarrow
    \frac{c}{(1-\alpha)^2(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}.
  \]
  Combine these limits with $A_1=2a_1+b_1$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-04}.
\end{proof}

For each fixed $a<1$, a finite constant $C=C(\alpha,a)$ remains possible;
the argument only forces it to diverge as $a\uparrow1$. It also says
nothing against comparisons beginning at large order or large time with
suitable prefactors. Reopening requires explicitly allowing that parameter
dependence or removing the low orders. Historical static-endpoint tests
likewise constrain an endpoint or a uniform family, not a separately
chosen constant at an arbitrary positive $a$.

\subsection{NG-05: chronological merging breaks wave support}
\label{S:nogo-05}

A single chain has the monotone ordering needed by the first-moment
representation. Sorting two chains by time looks like a direct way to
recover it. Finite propagation prevents the resulting pointwise comparison.

\setcounter{nogo}{4}
\begin{nogo}[Support obstruction to pointwise concatenation]
  \label{P:nogo-05}
  In $d=1$, no finite constant can bound every product of two root-forward
  chain kernels by the single-chain kernel obtained by chronological merging.
  The failure occurs in the $(2,2)$ split on a set of positive measure for
  Riesz covariance and, with domestic pairs, for white covariance.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Work at horizon one with root $(0,0)$. The first chain has times
  $(1/4,3/4)$ and positions $(x,x)$, $x=1/5$; the second has times
  $(1/2,7/8)$ and positions $(y,y)$, $y=-2/5$.
  The four original displacements and elapsed times are
  \[
    (1/5,1/4),\quad(0,1/2),\quad(2/5,1/2),\quad(0,3/8).
  \]
  Each displacement is strictly smaller than its elapsed time, so their
  Green product is $2^{-4}$. The merged chain instead includes the jump
  from $(1/4,1/5)$ to $(1/2,-2/5)$. Its displacement is $3/5>1/4$,
  so that Green factor vanishes. All these inequalities are strict.

  For white noise choose domestic pairs within each original chain. The
  delta contractions leave precisely the two free spatial variables $x,y$.
  Open neighborhoods of the displayed four times and these two variables
  preserve both conclusions and have positive contracted measure. Gaussian
  regularization justifies this contraction as in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-02}.
  For Riesz covariance perturb all four spatial positions independently
  in small neighborhoods. Avoiding the domestic diagonals gives a set of
  positive ordinary measure on which the covariance factors are finite and
  strictly positive, while the same support defect persists.
  No multiplier can turn the zero merged product into a positive upper bound.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-05}.
\end{proof}

The time coordinates here are root-forward chain coordinates; applying a
terminal-to-point convention again would change the object being tested.
The result concerns pointwise concatenation. Integrated comparisons and
comparisons of grouped Wick sums remain possible. Reopening requires
averaging, extra displacement variables, or another mechanism that preserves
the two original propagation constraints instead of imposing the false
merged one.

\subsection{NG-06: erasing geometry before summation loses the scale}
\label{S:nogo-06}

After white contractions, the temporal pairing sum is symmetric. One can
exploit that symmetry immediately by forgetting the spatial indicators.
The resulting bound is easy to sum but too weak for the desired exponent.

\setcounter{nogo}{5}
\begin{nogo}[The geometry-free majorant]
  \label{P:nogo-06}
  For $d=1$, $\Gamma=\delta_0$, $0<a<1$, replacing every wave indicator by
  one while retaining only the spatial box $[-t,t]$ gives the coefficient
  majorant
  \[
    A_k t^{(3-a)k}\le
    \frac{1}{k!}\left[\frac{2t^{3-a}}{(1-a)(2-a)}\right]^k.
  \]
  Its sum is $\exp(2t^{3-a}/((1-a)(2-a)))$, whose time exponent
  $3-a$ exceeds the target $(3-a)/2$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  The spatial replacement gives $(2t)^k/2^{2k}=t^k/2^k$ for each pairing.
  After this replacement the sum over all pairings is symmetric in the
  $2k$ times. Its integral over a split $(m,n)$ simplex equals the cube
  integral divided by $m!n!$. The identities
  \[
    \sum_{m+n=2k}\frac1{m!n!}=\frac{2^{2k}}{(2k)!},\qquad
    P_k=\frac{(2k)!}{2^k k!},\qquad
    \int_0^t\int_0^t|s-r|^{-a}\,\ud s\ud r
    =\frac{2t^{2-a}}{(1-a)(2-a)}
  \]
  give the stated product and its exponential sum.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-06}.
\end{proof}

This is a valid upper bound and a failure of its scale, not a moment lower
bound. Symmetry itself remains useful. The reopening condition is a spatial
estimate with enough order dependence retained before temporal
symmetrization; Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper} retains this order dependence
and sums the temporal pairings afterwards (see
Remark~\ref{R:d1-upper-checks}).

\subsection{NG-07: block labels are not chronological ranks}
\label{S:nogo-07}

Temporal singularities favor close paired times. A proposed penalty for
long pairs must therefore measure time separation or actual chronological
rank. Labeling all vertices of one chain before those of the other creates
an artificial distance unrelated to either.

\setcounter{nogo}{6}
\begin{nogo}[Large label span does not imply a cubic denominator]
  \label{P:nogo-07}
  In white space with $0<a<1$, consider two length-$k$ chains and the matching
  $j\leftrightarrow k+j$. Although every pair has label distance $k$, its
  unit-time diagram satisfies
  \[
    J_k(1)\ge \left(\frac{3\,4^a}{128}\right)^k k^{-(3-a)k}.
  \]
  Thus no estimate assigning $C^k/(k!)^3$ to these diagrams solely on account
  of their block-label span can hold uniformly in $k$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Let the chain times be $s_j,r_j$, with increments $h_j,g_j$ from the
  common root. White contractions identify their paired positions, and
  integration of the successive spatial increments gives
  $2^{-k}\prod_{j=1}^k\min(h_j,g_j)$. Restrict both $s_j,r_j$ to
  $((j-1/4)/k,j/k)$. The domain has volume $(4k)^{-2k}$,
  each increment is at least $3/(4k)$, and each paired difference is at most
  $1/(4k)$. Multiplication gives the lower bound. The contraction can again
  be obtained at fixed $k$ by Gaussian smoothing; the spatial box bound and
  integrable disjoint temporal factors justify the limit.
  Stirling's formula implies
  $\log((k!)^3J_k(1))\ge a k\log k+O(k)$, contradicting any fixed $C$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-07}.
\end{proof}

Within each window the paired vertices are adjacent in the actual
chronological ordering. This example only refutes the label substitution;
it supplies no new shuffle estimate or Skorohod theorem. Reopening would
require a statement conditioned on actual order and an independent proof
of its summed cost. The shared pure-cross direction is recorded for later work and is not
developed here.

\subsection[NG-10: crossing covariance survives merging]{NG-10: an unweighted merge does not remove a crossing covariance}
\label{S:nogo-10}

A two-wave convolution estimate can remove an interior chain vertex when
no other factor depends on it. In a crossing pairing that vertex still
interacts with a remote partner. The following order-four calculation
identifies the missing weight before any all-order iteration is attempted.

\setcounter{nogo}{9}
\begin{nogo}[No finite spatial-supremum pullout in the merge lens]
  \label{P:nogo-10}
  In $d=2$, take $\Gamma(z)=c|z|^{-\alpha}$, $c>0$,
  $0<\alpha_0<1/2$ and $3/2\le\alpha<2-\alpha_0$.
  For the root-forward $(0,4)$ diagram
  with pairs $(1,3),(2,4)$, merging vertex two requires the weighted integral
  \[
    W=c\int_a^b(T-v)^{-\alpha_0}
    \int_{\mathbb R^2}G_{v-a}(y-x)G_{b-v}(z-y)|w-y|^{-\alpha}
    \,\ud y\ud v,
    \qquad 0<a<b<T.
  \]
  Here the letters $a,b,T$ are vertex times, so the temporal exponent is
  written $\alpha_0$. The spatial weight has no finite uniform essential
  supremum on admissible merge lenses. Hence, pulling it out by a uniform
  supremum cannot reduce $W$ to an unweighted two-wave convolution.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Set $t=5$, $a=1$, $b=3$, $T=4$, $x=(0,0)$,
  $z=(1/2,0)$ and $w=(1/4,0)$. For $v$ near $2$, the point $y=w$
  is strictly inside both disks $|y-x|<v-a$ and $|z-y|<b-v$.
  It also satisfies the outer constraint $|w-z|<T-b$, and $|x|<a$.
  There is therefore an interior ball about $w$ in the merge lens.
  For every $M>0$ a smaller ball has positive Lebesgue measure and
  $|w-y|^{-\alpha}>M$ there. This proves infinite essential supremum;
  the strict inequalities persist under small changes of the fixed data.
  The other pair contributes $(b-a)^{-\alpha_0}c|z-x|^{-\alpha}$ outside
  the merge, whereas the two factors for pair $(2,4)$ depend on $v,y$
  and must remain in $W$. The temporal weight alone admits the bound
  $(T-v)^{-\alpha_0}\le(T-b)^{-\alpha_0}$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-10}.
\end{proof}

The unweighted powered-kernel convolution estimate in
\cite{bolanos-guerrero.nualart.ea:21:averaging}*{Lemma~4.3, p.~20}
assumes $1/2<q<1$ and $1\le\delta\le1/q$. It contains no covariance factor
linking the integration variable to a remote vertex and does not directly
estimate $W$ above. Infinite essential supremum does not imply divergence of $W$ or
of the diagram: the Riesz singularity is locally integrable for $\alpha<2$.
A reopening condition is a covariance-weighted convolution inequality,
or an explicit envelope integrable against the remaining diagram factors,
with the remote-gap cost retained. No such inequality is asserted here.

\subsection{NG-17: direct copying of the first-moment clock undercounts}
\label{S:nogo-17}

The source's first-moment clock representation suggests attaching one
Brownian clock to each ordered chain. A degree-two cross pair already tests
whether this copying preserves the interaction. It does not, even when
the two clocks are shared.

\setcounter{nogo}{16}
\begin{nogo}[Constant-correlation clock copying misses a positive factor]
  \label{P:nogo-17}
  Under the nonzero covariance hypotheses of
  Proposition~\ref{P:clock-copy-obstruction}, let $K_\Gamma(\theta)$ be the
  true cross-pair transform and $J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)$ the direct
  unweighted candidate~\eqref{E:naive-clock-candidate}, $-1\le\rho\le1$.
  Then for every $\theta>0$,
  \[
    0<J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)\le J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)<K_\Gamma(\theta).
  \]
  If $\alpha=1$, then $J_{\Gamma,1}=(2-2^a)K_\Gamma$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Recall $\mathcal R_\Gamma(q)=\int(q^2+|\xi|^2)^{-1}\mu(\ud\xi)$.
  The exact transform identities proved in
  Proposition~\ref{P:clock-copy-obstruction} give
  \[
    w_\theta(\lambda)=
    \frac{\lambda^{a-2}}{2\theta\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(a)}
    [\mathcal R_\Gamma(\theta)-\mathcal R_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda)],
    \qquad
    K_\Gamma=\int_0^\infty w_\theta(\lambda)\,\ud\lambda,
  \]
  \[
    J_{\Gamma,1}=\int_0^\infty
    \frac{2\theta}{2\theta+\lambda}w_\theta(\lambda)\,\ud\lambda.
  \]
  The density is strictly positive because $\mu\ne0$ and the resolvent
  strictly decreases. It is integrable: homogeneity bounds it by multiples
  of $\lambda^{a-1}$ near zero and $\lambda^{a-2}$ at infinity.
  The multiplier is strictly between zero and one. For smaller correlations,
  $u+v-2\rho\min(u,v)\ge|u-v|$, and the positive Laplace integral
  $\Psi_a(r,v)$ in~\eqref{E:averaged-clock-kernel} decreases with $v$.
  This proves the chain of inequalities. When $\alpha=1$, insert
  $\mathcal R_\Gamma(q)=\mathcal R_\Gamma(1)/q$ and use
  $\int_0^\infty x^{a-1}/(x+c)\,\ud x
  =c^{a-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(a)\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1-a)$ to obtain the ratio.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-17}.
\end{proof}

This is the already proved book obstruction, not a new clock impossibility
theorem. At the static endpoint the discrepancy disappears, so homogeneity
and static checks alone cannot detect it. At degree two the missing
shared-clock multiplier is exactly $1+\lambda/(2\theta)$; no higher-order
correction rule follows. Reopening requires relative-order weights or a
different coupling or representation that reproduces the actual cross-pair
transform. Remark~\ref{R:clock-copy-scope} gives the full boundary of this
statement, including the distinction between gap transforms and physical
unequal-time moments.

\subsection[NG-18: an oversized middle-cross envelope]{NG-18: erasing the middle-cross shift makes an envelope too large}
\label{S:nogo-18}

A later white-space comparison retains four vertices and a middle cross
pair. Removing the clock-overlap penalty gives a positive upper bound.
One further simplification, erasing the shift of its root kernel, also
increases the bound, but increases it beyond the comparator at an explicit
parameter point. This is an obstruction to closing that envelope, not a
counterexample to the original comparison.

\setcounter{nogo}{17}
\begin{nogo}[The shift-erased envelope is not below the wave transform]
  \label{P:nogo-18}
  Consider the white $(1,3)$ diagram with middle-cross partition $02\cdot13$,
  common gap damping one, cross rate $\lambda\ge0$ and self rate $\nu\ge0$.
  Put $b=1+\nu$, $c=1+\lambda$, $A=\lambda+\lambda^2/2$. Its actual wave
  transform $I$ and the shift-erased clock upper envelope $M_b(0;A)$ satisfy
  at $b=1,c=11/10$
  \[
    I=\frac{545}{11616},\qquad M_b(0;A)=\frac{1025}{21483},\qquad
    I-M_b(0;A)=-\frac{6005}{7562016}<0.
  \]
  Consequently the proposed pointwise closure $M_b(0;A)\le I$ is false.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  We give the integral identifications so that the rational test specifies
  which bound fails. With singleton gap $u$ and long-chain gaps $v,w,z$,
  the spatial factor is
  $\int G_u(X)G_v(Y)G_w(X-Y)G_z(Y-X)\,\ud X\ud Y$.
  Set $R=u+v$, $q=u-v$. Integration in $R$, including its Jacobian,
  gives the exact wave expression
  \[
    I=\frac1{16}\int_{w,z>0}\int_{\mathbb R}
    e^{-b(w+z)-\lambda|q-w|}
    \int_0^{\min(w,z)}e^{-\max(|q|,t)}\,\ud t\ud q\ud w\ud z.
  \]
  To check the spatial reduction, the derivative of the area
  $\{|X|<u,|Y|<v,|X-Y|<C\}$ with respect to $C$ is
  $2(R-\max(C,|q|))_+$; the area vanishes at $C=0$.
  Integrate this against $e^{-R}/32$ for $R>|q|$.
  Next integrate $z\ge t$ and put $w=t+x$. Splitting the remaining $q$
  integral into $q<0$, $0\le q\le t$, and $q\ge t$ gives respectively
  \[
    \frac{1/c+1/(2b+\lambda+1)}{16b(b+\lambda)(2b+2\lambda+1)},\quad
    \frac1{16b(b+\lambda)(2b+1)(2b+1+\lambda)},
  \]
  \[
    \frac{1/(b+\lambda)+1/c}{16b(2b+1)(b+1)}.
  \]
  Adding gives
  \[
    I=\frac{2b^2+4bc+2c^2-c+1}
    {8bc(b+1)(2b+1)(b+c-1)(2b+2c-1)}.
  \]

  For the clock envelope write $h(r)=(2\pi r)^{-1/2}$ and
  $m(s)=1/(\pi\sqrt2\sqrt{s-1/2})$, $s>1/2$.
  The two spatial pair differences have covariance matrix
  $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u+v+w&-w\\-w&w+z\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
  Writing $r=w+z$, its determinant is $rR+wz$, so their joint white
  density factors as $h(r)h(R+wz/r)$. After deleting the nonnegative
  clock-overlap penalty, the root kernel is
  \[
    H_\gamma(q)=\int_{|q|}^\infty e^{-R/2}h(R+\gamma)\,\ud R
    =\int_{1/2}^\infty\frac{m(s)}s
    e^{-(s-1/2)\gamma}e^{-s|q|}\,\ud s,
    \qquad \gamma=\frac{wz}{r}.
  \]
  The equality follows from the positive Gamma integral for $h$.
  The convolution of the mixture component $q\mapsto e^{-s|q|}$ with
  $q\mapsto e^{-A|q|}$, evaluated at $w$, equals $2S_{A,s}(w)/(s+A)$, where
  \[
    S_{A,s}(w)=e^{-Aw}+\int_0^w A e^{-At}e^{-s(w-t)}\,\ud t\le1.
  \]
  The inequality follows by bounding $e^{-s(w-t)}$ by one; the remaining
  integral is $1-e^{-Aw}$.
  Replacing $S$ by one is precisely the shift erasure. More generally, for
  $\eta\ge0$, replacing it by $e^{-\eta w}$ defines
  \[
    M_b(\eta;A)=\frac1{16}\int_{1/2}^\infty
    \frac{m(s)}{s(s+A)}\int_0^\infty h(r)e^{-b^2r/2}
    \int_0^r e^{-(s-1/2)w(r-w)/r-\eta w}\,\ud w\ud r\ud s.
  \]
  All integrals here are positive. Set $w=rt$ and $\tau=s-1/2$.
  For $\eta,\tau\ge0$, put $p=\sqrt{b^2+2\eta}$. The elementary bridge
  integral is
  \[
    \int_0^1[b^2+2\eta t+2\tau t(1-t)]^{-3/2}\,\ud t
    =\frac{2(p+b)}{bp((p+b)^2+2\tau)}.
  \]
  It follows by integrating a quadratic power; at $\eta=\tau=0$ both sides
  are $b^{-3}$. Substitution $z=\sqrt{2s-1}$ and partial fractions give
  \begin{align*}
    M_b(\eta;A)&=\frac{p+b}{2\pi bp}
    \int_0^\infty\frac{\ud z}
    {(z^2+1)(z^2+c^2)(z^2+(p+b)^2)}\\
    &=\frac{1+c+p+b}{4bp c(c+1)(c+p+b)(1+p+b)}.
  \end{align*}
  The usual identity $\int_0^\infty(z^2+q^2)^{-1}\ud z=\pi/(2q)$
  proves the partial fraction evaluation; coincident rates follow by
  continuity. At $\eta=0$, $p=b$. Substituting the asserted rational
  parameters in these two expressions gives the displayed strict gap.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-18}.
\end{proof}

The boundary value $\nu=0$ is a legitimate fixed-parameter transform;
continuity of both rational expressions also gives an overshoot for some
$\nu>0$ nearby. The no-go does not refute $J\le I$, where $J$ is the
original shared-clock candidate, nor does it decide an integrated
comparison using this envelope. The surviving middle-cross comparison
retains $S_{A,1/2}(w)$, instead of replacing it by one, and obtains a smaller
envelope. Reopening this particular closure requires keeping a shift cost
or proving a different integrated estimate.

\section{Pairings, ordering, and comparison kernels}\label{S:nogo-pairings}

The second moment contains two ordered chains and all their Gaussian
pairings, including pairs within a chain. This structure offers several
natural reductions: estimate each pairing separately, compare with a single
chain, or copy the first-moment clock. The obstructions below distinguish
loss of scale from failure of an inequality. A majorant can be too large even
when every step used to obtain it is valid.

Throughout this section write $a=\alpha_0$ and
$\beta=4-\alpha-a$. Let $X_n$ denote the ordered Stratonovich term of degree
$n$, and put
\[
  a_k=\E X_{2k}(1,x),\qquad
  A_k=\sum_{m+n=2k}\E[X_m(1,x)X_n(1,x)],\qquad
  b_1=\E X_1(1,x)^2.
\]
Thus $A_1=2a_1+b_1$. Homogeneity supplies the factor $t^{\beta k}$.
The nonnegative convergent moment expansions used here are those of
Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}; they include unequal chain lengths. In dimension
one we use $G_s(z)=\frac12\mathbf1_{\{|z|<s\}}$.

\subsection{NG-01: one common estimate loses a factorial power}
\label{S:nogo-01}

Chen's author version~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{Theorem~3.1, (3.6)}
bounds regularized two-chain integrals for arbitrary pairings under its
finite-measure and norm hypotheses. Proposition~\ref{P:envelope} gives the
application under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}. Applying one common norm bound
to every pairing is an attractive first attempt at the upper exponent.
The issue is what happens after optimization and summation.

\setcounter{nogo}{0}
\begin{nogo}[The common-parameter majorant has the wrong scale]
  \label{P:nogo-01}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, let $P_k=(2k)!/(2^k k!)$ and $K>0$.
  The numerical majorant
  \[
    B_K(t)=1+\sum_{k\ge1}(2k+1)P_kK^k
    \inf_{\theta>0}e^{2\theta t}\theta^{-\beta k}
  \]
  satisfies $\log B_K(t)\asymp t^{\beta/(\beta-1)}$ as $t\to\infty$.
  Since $\beta/(\beta-1)>\kappa=\beta/(3-\alpha)$, this majorant cannot
  establish a finite upper constant on the scale $t^\kappa$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  The infimum equals $e^{\beta k}(2t/(\beta k))^{\beta k}$.
  The inequalities $k!\le P_k\le2^k k!$ and
  $e^{-k}\le k!/k^k\le1$ show that $B_K$ lies between two series
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(c t^\beta)^k}{(k!)^q},\qquad q=\beta-1>1,
  \]
  with different positive constants $c$. For $z>0$, this series is at most
  $\exp(qz^{1/q})$, by the $\ell^q$--$\ell^1$ inequality. Its term with
  $k=\lfloor z^{1/q}/2\rfloor$ is at least $2^{qk}$ for large $z$.
  This proves the two-sided growth. Finally, $\beta-1=3-\alpha-a<3-\alpha$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-01}.
\end{proof}

This restates the obstruction in Proposition~\ref{P:envelope} and
Remark~\ref{R:G0-verdict}. The lower estimate is for $B_K$, not for the
moment. The valid upper estimate improves the crude quadratic exponent,
but its factorial denominator misses $(k!)^a$. The split count $2k+1$
costs only an exponential factor in $k$; removing it does not repair the
loss. The same calculation for one chain, with $e^{\theta t}$, has the
same defect. Reopening requires an estimate retaining ordering or grouping
pairings before taking this common bound. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper}
provides such a different argument.

\subsection{NG-02: a single adjacent diagram is too large}
\label{S:nogo-02}

There are roughly an exponential factor times $k!$ pairings of $2k$
vertices. In white space, a uniform denominator $(k!)^3$ for each diagram
would therefore give the desired denominator $(k!)^2$ after summation.
The following example explains why that convenient sufficient estimate is
stronger than the problem permits.

\setcounter{nogo}{1}
\begin{nogo}[No uniform cubic factorial denominator per diagram]
  \label{P:nogo-02}
  Let $d=1$, $\Gamma=\delta_0$ and $0<a<1$. For the split $(0,2k)$
  and adjacent matching $\{(1,2),(3,4),\ldots,(2k-1,2k)\}$, its contribution is
  \[
    J_{\rm adj}(t)=
    \left[\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1-a)}2\right]^k
    \frac{t^{(3-a)k}}{\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1+(3-a)k)}.
  \]
  Consequently, there is no finite $C=C(a)$ bounding every diagram by
  $C^k t^{(3-a)k}/(k!)^3$ for all $k$ and $t>0$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  We compute the adjacent diagram after its white contractions and justify
  that fixed-order limit. Its Dirichlet integral then shows why a uniform
  cubic factorial denominator is impossible.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We identify the adjacent-pair integral and its
  smoothing limit.
  Use root-forward increments $h_1,\ldots,h_{2k}>0$ with
  $\sum h_i<t$. Each adjacent contraction evaluates the even Green factor
  at zero, giving $1/2$, and integration of the preceding free increment
  gives mass $h_{2j-1}$. The temporal factor is $h_{2j}^{-a}$. Therefore,
  \[
    J_{\rm adj}(t)=2^{-k}
    \int_{\sum h_i<t}\prod_{j=1}^k h_{2j-1}h_{2j}^{-a}\,\ud h.
  \]
  Here delta contractions mean limits of Gaussian approximate identities.
  For completeness, fix $k$ and a strictly positive increment vector.
  After convolution the paired positions approach equality. The even Green
  factors are continuous at zero; the remaining indicator boundaries are
  finitely many hyperplanes in the $k$ free positions and have measure zero.
  The limit is thus the stated diagonal integral. All positions in the
  original chain lie in $[-t,t]$. Uniformly in the smoothing widths, replacing
  its Green indicators by one bounds the spatial integral by
  $2^{-2k}(2t)^k$: each disjoint smoothed pair has integral at most $2t$
  over $[-t,t]^2$. The temporal factors on the full time cube are a product
  of $k$ disjoint factors $|s-r|^{-a}$, each integrable because $a<1$.
  Dominated convergence justifies the fixed-order spatial limit through the
  time integration, and also temporal truncation limits.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We compare its growth with the proposed factorial
  bound.
  The Dirichlet simplex formula, with parameters $2$ and $1-a$ for each
  pair, now gives the asserted expression. Stirling's formula yields
  \[
    \log\big((k!)^3J_{\rm adj}(1)\big)=a k\log k+O(k).
  \]
  This exceeds $k\log C$ eventually for every fixed $C$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-02}.
\end{proof}

The adjacent matching is only one diagram. Its denominator is, up to
exponential factors, $(k!)^{3-a}$, which is stronger than the desired
summed denominator $(k!)^2$. Hence, this witness does not obstruct the
summed bound. A viable successor must retain the number of large diagrams,
or sum before estimating; it cannot assign the same cubic denominator to
all of them.

\subsection[NG-03: a squared-mean comparison fails]{NG-03: doubled variance does not give the proposed squared mean}
\label{S:nogo-03}

A positive mixture of exponentials of linear Gaussian functionals would
satisfy a useful comparison between the second moment and the mean at
noise amplitude $\sqrt2$. Such a representation must be established for
the wave rather than inferred from a parabolic analogy.

\setcounter{nogo}{2}
\begin{nogo}[Failure of the all-time comparison with unit prefactor]
  \label{P:nogo-03}
  For the admissible Riesz case $d=1$, $\Gamma(x)=|x|^{-1/2}$ and $a=1/2$,
  \[
    \E u(t,x)^2>\big(\E u_{\sqrt2}(t,x)\big)^2
  \]
  for all sufficiently small $t>0$, where $u_\lambda$ uses noise amplitude
  $\lambda$ and the same initial data.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  We compare the first nonconstant coefficients in the proposed inequality. We
  then bound both series remainders to turn that coefficient discrepancy into
  a strict small-time failure.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We compare the two first-order contributions.
  More generally, for $\Gamma(x)=c|x|^{-\alpha}$, $0<\alpha<1$,
  direct integration of the length-two chain gives
  \[
    a_1=\frac{c}{(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha-a)(3-\alpha-a)(4-\alpha-a)}.
  \]
  Indeed the spatial integral is
  $c h_1h_2^{1-\alpha}/(1-\alpha)$ and its temporal factor is $h_2^{-a}$;
  integrate over $h_1+h_2<1$. At $\alpha=a=1/2$, $c=1$, this gives $a_1=1/3$.
  For independent uniforms $U$ on $[-s,s]$ and $V$ on $[-r,r]$, $0<r\le s$,
  \[
    \E|U-V|=\frac{s}{2}+\frac{r^2}{6s}\le\frac{2s}{3}.
  \]
  Jensen's inequality for $z\mapsto z^{-\alpha}$ implies
  \[
    \int G_s(x)G_r(y)|x-y|^{-\alpha}\,\ud x\ud y
    \ge (3/2)^\alpha s^{1-\alpha}r.
  \]
  Integrating on the two temporal triangles gives
  \[
    b_1\ge
    \frac{2(3/2)^\alpha}{(1-a)(2-a)(4-\alpha-a)}
    =\frac89\sqrt{\frac32}>\frac23=2a_1.
  \]

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We justify the strict inequality for sufficiently
  small positive time.
  The comparison is a finite-time failure, not just a formal coefficient test.
  Put $z=t^\beta$. The two nonnegative series under comparison have expansions
  \[
    \E u(t,x)^2=1+(2a_1+b_1)z+O(z^2),\qquad
    (\E u_{\sqrt2}(t,x))^2=1+4a_1z+O(z^2).
  \]
  To justify the remainders, choose any fixed positive $z_0$ at which these
  series converge, using the moment expansion also at amplitude $\sqrt2$.
  For each nonnegative coefficient sequence $(c_j)$,
  $\sum_{j\ge2}c_jz^j\le(z/z_0)^2\sum_{j\ge2}c_jz_0^j$ when
  $0\le z\le z_0$. Apply this to both series, including the nonnegative
  Cauchy product for the squared mean. Their difference therefore has the
  strictly positive leading coefficient $b_1-2a_1$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-03}.
\end{proof}

For a deterministic nonnegative mixture of Gaussian exponentials, the
inequality follows by Tonelli and
$\|h+k\|^2/2\le\|h\|^2+\|k\|^2$, provided the same mixture represents
all amplitudes in the comparison. That proposed mechanism is excluded by
the example. White noise, an eventual large-time comparison, or a prefactor
larger than one is not excluded. Reopening requires a restriction or a new
comparison that removes the contradicted first-coefficient requirement;
it cannot merely assert this positive-mixture representation.

\subsection[NG-04: constants near the time boundary]{NG-04: a coefficient constant cannot be uniform at the time boundary}
\label{S:nogo-04}

Comparing $A_k$ with $a_k$ retains the self-pairings of the Stratonovich
problem and would transfer a first-moment bound to the second moment.
However, a prescribed asymptotic constant must not be imposed on every
low-order coefficient without checking parameter uniformity.

\setcounter{nogo}{3}
\begin{nogo}[Divergence of the first coefficient ratio]
  \label{P:nogo-04}
  Fix $0<\alpha<1$ and $c>0$, with $\Gamma(x)=c|x|^{-\alpha}$ in $d=1$.
  Then
  \[
    \lim_{a\uparrow1}(1-a)\frac{A_1}{a_1}
    =2^{2-\alpha}(1-\alpha)>0.
  \]
  In particular no constant depending on $\alpha$ alone, including
  $2^{4-\alpha}$, can satisfy $A_k\le C^k a_k$ for every $k\ge1$
  and every $0<a<1$ at this fixed spatial covariance.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  We isolate the contribution near equal times and compute its boundary
  divergence. Dividing by the finite limiting single-chain coefficient shows
  why no constant uniform in the temporal exponent can work.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We identify the diagonal time contribution.
  Let
  $K(s,r)=\int G_s(x)G_r(y)c|x-y|^{-\alpha}\,\ud x\ud y$.
  This is continuous on $[0,1]^2$ by local integrability of the Riesz kernel
  and dominated convergence on a fixed square. Direct integration gives
  \[
    K(s,s)=\frac{c\,2^{1-\alpha}s^{2-\alpha}}
    {(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)}.
  \]
  Writing $v=s-r$, define the continuous function
  $H(v)=2\int_v^1 K(s,s-v)\,\ud s$. Then
  \[
    b_1=\int_0^1v^{-a}H(v)\,\ud v,\qquad
    H(0)=\frac{c\,2^{2-\alpha}}
    {(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}.
  \]

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We take the boundary limit of the coefficient
  ratio.
  For any continuous $H$ on $[0,1]$,
  $\varepsilon\int_0^1v^{-1+\varepsilon}H(v)\,\ud v\to H(0)$.
  Indeed the measures have mass one, and their mass on $[\delta,1]$ is
  $1-\delta^\varepsilon\to0$; continuity controls $[0,\delta]$.
  Thus $(1-a)b_1\to H(0)$. The formula for $a_1$ in the preceding proof gives
  \[
    a_1\longrightarrow
    \frac{c}{(1-\alpha)^2(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}.
  \]
  Combine these limits with $A_1=2a_1+b_1$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-04}.
\end{proof}

For each fixed $a<1$, a finite constant $C=C(\alpha,a)$ remains possible;
the argument only forces it to diverge as $a\uparrow1$. It also says
nothing against comparisons beginning at large order or large time with
suitable prefactors. Reopening requires explicitly allowing that parameter
dependence or removing the low orders. Historical static-endpoint tests
likewise constrain an endpoint or a uniform family, not a separately
chosen constant at an arbitrary positive $a$.

\subsection{NG-05: chronological merging breaks wave support}
\label{S:nogo-05}

A single chain has the monotone ordering needed by the first-moment
representation. Sorting two chains by time looks like a direct way to
recover it. Finite propagation prevents the resulting pointwise comparison.

\setcounter{nogo}{4}
\begin{nogo}[Support obstruction to pointwise concatenation]
  \label{P:nogo-05}
  In $d=1$, no finite constant can bound every product of two root-forward
  chain kernels by the single-chain kernel obtained by chronological merging.
  The failure occurs in the $(2,2)$ split on a set of positive measure for
  Riesz covariance and, with domestic pairs, for white covariance.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  We choose two admissible chains whose chronological merge violates a strict
  propagation constraint. We then keep this defect on sets of positive measure
  for each of the two covariance cases.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We exhibit the positive original product and zero
  merged product.
  Work at horizon one with root $(0,0)$. The first chain has times
  $(1/4,3/4)$ and positions $(x,x)$, $x=1/5$; the second has times
  $(1/2,7/8)$ and positions $(y,y)$, $y=-2/5$.
  The four original displacements and elapsed times are
  \[
    (1/5,1/4),\quad(0,1/2),\quad(2/5,1/2),\quad(0,3/8).
  \]
  Each displacement is strictly smaller than its elapsed time, so their
  Green product is $2^{-4}$. The merged chain instead includes the jump
  from $(1/4,1/5)$ to $(1/2,-2/5)$. Its displacement is $3/5>1/4$,
  so that Green factor vanishes. All these inequalities are strict.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We retain the support defect under the stated
  covariance integrations.
  For white noise choose domestic pairs within each original chain. The
  delta contractions leave precisely the two free spatial variables $x,y$.
  Open neighborhoods of the displayed four times and these two variables
  preserve both conclusions and have positive contracted measure. Gaussian
  regularization justifies this contraction as in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-02}.
  For Riesz covariance perturb all four spatial positions independently
  in small neighborhoods. Avoiding the domestic diagonals gives a set of
  positive ordinary measure on which the covariance factors are finite and
  strictly positive, while the same support defect persists.
  No multiplier can turn the zero merged product into a positive upper bound.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-05}.
\end{proof}

The time coordinates here are root-forward chain coordinates; applying a
terminal-to-point convention again would change the object being tested.
The result concerns pointwise concatenation. Integrated comparisons and
comparisons of grouped Wick sums remain possible. Reopening requires
averaging, extra displacement variables, or another mechanism that preserves
the two original propagation constraints instead of imposing the false
merged one.

\subsection{NG-06: erasing geometry before summation loses the scale}
\label{S:nogo-06}

After white contractions, the temporal pairing sum is symmetric. One can
exploit that symmetry immediately by forgetting the spatial indicators.
The resulting bound is easy to sum but too weak for the desired exponent.

\setcounter{nogo}{5}
\begin{nogo}[The geometry-free majorant]
  \label{P:nogo-06}
  For $d=1$, $\Gamma=\delta_0$, $0<a<1$, replacing every wave indicator by
  one while retaining only the spatial box $[-t,t]$ gives the coefficient
  majorant
  \[
    A_k t^{(3-a)k}\le
    \frac{1}{k!}\left[\frac{2t^{3-a}}{(1-a)(2-a)}\right]^k.
  \]
  Its sum is $\exp(2t^{3-a}/((1-a)(2-a)))$, whose time exponent
  $3-a$ exceeds the target $(3-a)/2$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  The spatial replacement gives $(2t)^k/2^{2k}=t^k/2^k$ for each pairing.
  After this replacement the sum over all pairings is symmetric in the
  $2k$ times. Its integral over a split $(m,n)$ simplex equals the cube
  integral divided by $m!n!$. The identities
  \[
    \sum_{m+n=2k}\frac1{m!n!}=\frac{2^{2k}}{(2k)!},\qquad
    P_k=\frac{(2k)!}{2^k k!},\qquad
    \int_0^t\int_0^t|s-r|^{-a}\,\ud s\ud r
    =\frac{2t^{2-a}}{(1-a)(2-a)}
  \]
  give the stated product and its exponential sum.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-06}.
\end{proof}

This is a valid upper bound and a failure of its scale, not a moment lower
bound. Symmetry itself remains useful. The reopening condition is a spatial
estimate with enough order dependence retained before temporal
symmetrization; Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper} retains this order dependence
and sums the temporal pairings afterwards (see
Remark~\ref{R:d1-upper-checks}).

\subsection{NG-07: block labels are not chronological ranks}
\label{S:nogo-07}

Temporal singularities favor close paired times. A proposed penalty for
long pairs must therefore measure time separation or actual chronological
rank. Labeling all vertices of one chain before those of the other creates
an artificial distance unrelated to either.

\setcounter{nogo}{6}
\begin{nogo}[Large label span does not imply a cubic denominator]
  \label{P:nogo-07}
  In white space with $0<a<1$, consider two length-$k$ chains and the matching
  $j\leftrightarrow k+j$. Although every pair has label distance $k$, its
  unit-time diagram satisfies
  \[
    J_k(1)\ge \left(\frac{3\,4^a}{128}\right)^k k^{-(3-a)k}.
  \]
  Thus no estimate assigning $C^k/(k!)^3$ to these diagrams solely on account
  of their block-label span can hold uniformly in $k$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Let the chain times be $s_j,r_j$, with increments $h_j,g_j$ from the
  common root. White contractions identify their paired positions, and
  integration of the successive spatial increments gives
  $2^{-k}\prod_{j=1}^k\min(h_j,g_j)$. Restrict both $s_j,r_j$ to
  $((j-1/4)/k,j/k)$. The domain has volume $(4k)^{-2k}$,
  each increment is at least $3/(4k)$, and each paired difference is at most
  $1/(4k)$. Multiplication gives the lower bound. The contraction can again
  be obtained at fixed $k$ by Gaussian smoothing; the spatial box bound and
  integrable disjoint temporal factors justify the limit.
  Stirling's formula implies
  $\log((k!)^3J_k(1))\ge a k\log k+O(k)$, contradicting any fixed $C$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-07}.
\end{proof}

Within each window the paired vertices are adjacent in the actual
chronological ordering. This example only refutes the label substitution;
it supplies no new shuffle estimate or Skorohod theorem. Reopening would
require a statement conditioned on actual order and an independent proof
of its summed cost. The shared pure-cross direction is recorded for later work and is not
developed here.

\subsection[NG-10: crossing covariance survives merging]{NG-10: an unweighted merge does not remove a crossing covariance}
\label{S:nogo-10}

A two-wave convolution estimate can remove an interior chain vertex when
no other factor depends on it. In a crossing pairing that vertex still
interacts with a remote partner. The following order-four calculation
identifies the missing weight before any all-order iteration is attempted.

\setcounter{nogo}{9}
\begin{nogo}[No finite spatial-supremum pullout in the merge lens]
  \label{P:nogo-10}
  In $d=2$, take $\Gamma(z)=c|z|^{-\alpha}$, $c>0$,
  $0<\alpha_0<1/2$ and $3/2\le\alpha<2-\alpha_0$.
  For the root-forward $(0,4)$ diagram
  with pairs $(1,3),(2,4)$, merging vertex two requires the weighted integral
  \[
    W=c\int_a^b(T-v)^{-\alpha_0}
    \int_{\mathbb R^2}G_{v-a}(y-x)G_{b-v}(z-y)|w-y|^{-\alpha}
    \,\ud y\ud v,
    \qquad 0<a<b<T.
  \]
  Here the letters $a,b,T$ are vertex times, so the temporal exponent is
  written $\alpha_0$. The spatial weight has no finite uniform essential
  supremum on admissible merge lenses. Hence, pulling it out by a uniform
  supremum cannot reduce $W$ to an unweighted two-wave convolution.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Set $t=5$, $a=1$, $b=3$, $T=4$, $x=(0,0)$,
  $z=(1/2,0)$ and $w=(1/4,0)$. For $v$ near $2$, the point $y=w$
  is strictly inside both disks $|y-x|<v-a$ and $|z-y|<b-v$.
  It also satisfies the outer constraint $|w-z|<T-b$, and $|x|<a$.
  There is therefore an interior ball about $w$ in the merge lens.
  For every $M>0$ a smaller ball has positive Lebesgue measure and
  $|w-y|^{-\alpha}>M$ there. This proves infinite essential supremum;
  the strict inequalities persist under small changes of the fixed data.
  The other pair contributes $(b-a)^{-\alpha_0}c|z-x|^{-\alpha}$ outside
  the merge, whereas the two factors for pair $(2,4)$ depend on $v,y$
  and must remain in $W$. The temporal weight alone admits the bound
  $(T-v)^{-\alpha_0}\le(T-b)^{-\alpha_0}$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-10}.
\end{proof}

The unweighted powered-kernel convolution estimate in
\cite{bolanos-guerrero.nualart.ea:21:averaging}*{Lemma~4.3, p.~20}
assumes $1/2<q<1$ and $1\le\delta\le1/q$. It contains no covariance factor
linking the integration variable to a remote vertex and does not directly
estimate $W$ above. Infinite essential supremum does not imply divergence of $W$ or
of the diagram: the Riesz singularity is locally integrable for $\alpha<2$.
A reopening condition is a covariance-weighted convolution inequality,
or an explicit envelope integrable against the remaining diagram factors,
with the remote-gap cost retained. No such inequality is asserted here.

\subsection{NG-17: direct copying of the first-moment clock undercounts}
\label{S:nogo-17}

The source's first-moment clock representation suggests attaching one
Brownian clock to each ordered chain. A degree-two cross pair already tests
whether this copying preserves the interaction. It does not, even when
the two clocks are shared.

\setcounter{nogo}{16}
\begin{nogo}[Constant-correlation clock copying misses a positive factor]
  \label{P:nogo-17}
  Under the nonzero covariance hypotheses of
  Proposition~\ref{P:clock-copy-obstruction}, let $K_\Gamma(\theta)$ be the
  true cross-pair transform and $J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)$ the direct
  unweighted candidate~\eqref{E:naive-clock-candidate}, $-1\le\rho\le1$.
  Then for every $\theta>0$,
  \[
    0<J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)\le J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)<K_\Gamma(\theta).
  \]
  If $\alpha=1$, then $J_{\Gamma,1}=(2-2^a)K_\Gamma$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Recall $\mathcal R_\Gamma(q)=\int(q^2+|\xi|^2)^{-1}\mu(\ud\xi)$.
  The exact transform identities proved in
  Proposition~\ref{P:clock-copy-obstruction} give
  \[
    w_\theta(\lambda)=
    \frac{\lambda^{a-2}}{2\theta\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(a)}
    [\mathcal R_\Gamma(\theta)-\mathcal R_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda)],
    \qquad
    K_\Gamma=\int_0^\infty w_\theta(\lambda)\,\ud\lambda,
  \]
  \[
    J_{\Gamma,1}=\int_0^\infty
    \frac{2\theta}{2\theta+\lambda}w_\theta(\lambda)\,\ud\lambda.
  \]
  The density is strictly positive because $\mu\ne0$ and the resolvent
  strictly decreases. It is integrable: homogeneity bounds it by multiples
  of $\lambda^{a-1}$ near zero and $\lambda^{a-2}$ at infinity.
  The multiplier is strictly between zero and one. For smaller correlations,
  $u+v-2\rho\min(u,v)\ge|u-v|$, and the positive Laplace integral
  $\Psi_a(r,v)$ in~\eqref{E:averaged-clock-kernel} decreases with $v$.
  This proves the chain of inequalities. When $\alpha=1$, insert
  $\mathcal R_\Gamma(q)=\mathcal R_\Gamma(1)/q$ and use
  $\int_0^\infty x^{a-1}/(x+c)\,\ud x
  =c^{a-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(a)\Gamma_{\mathrm E}(1-a)$ to obtain the ratio.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-17}.
\end{proof}

This is the already proved book obstruction, not a new clock impossibility
theorem. At the static endpoint the discrepancy disappears, so homogeneity
and static checks alone cannot detect it. At degree two the missing
shared-clock multiplier is exactly $1+\lambda/(2\theta)$; no higher-order
correction rule follows. Reopening requires relative-order weights or a
different coupling or representation that reproduces the actual cross-pair
transform. Remark~\ref{R:clock-copy-scope} gives the full boundary of this
statement, including the distinction between gap transforms and physical
unequal-time moments.

\subsection[NG-18: an oversized middle-cross envelope]{NG-18: erasing the middle-cross shift makes an envelope too large}
\label{S:nogo-18}

A later white-space comparison retains four vertices and a middle cross
pair. Removing the clock-overlap penalty gives a positive upper bound.
One further simplification, erasing the shift of its root kernel, also
increases the bound, but increases it beyond the comparator at an explicit
parameter point. This is an obstruction to closing that envelope, not a
counterexample to the original comparison.

\setcounter{nogo}{17}
\begin{nogo}[The shift-erased envelope is not below the wave transform]
  \label{P:nogo-18}
  Consider the white $(1,3)$ diagram with middle-cross partition $02\cdot13$,
  common gap damping one, cross rate $\lambda\ge0$ and self rate $\nu\ge0$.
  Put $b=1+\nu$, $c=1+\lambda$, $A=\lambda+\lambda^2/2$. Its actual wave
  transform $I$ and the shift-erased clock upper envelope $M_b(0;A)$ satisfy
  at $b=1,c=11/10$
  \[
    I=\frac{545}{11616},\qquad M_b(0;A)=\frac{1025}{21483},\qquad
    I-M_b(0;A)=-\frac{6005}{7562016}<0.
  \]
  Consequently the proposed pointwise closure $M_b(0;A)\le I$ is false.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  We identify the actual wave transform and the particular shift-erased
  envelope separately. We then evaluate both expressions at the stated
  rational parameters to disprove this envelope closure, without replacing the
  original clock candidate by the envelope.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We compute the actual middle-cross wave transform.
  We give the integral identifications so that the rational test specifies
  which bound fails. With singleton gap $u$ and long-chain gaps $v,w,z$,
  the spatial factor is
  $\int G_u(X)G_v(Y)G_w(X-Y)G_z(Y-X)\,\ud X\ud Y$.
  Set $R=u+v$, $q=u-v$. Integration in $R$, including its Jacobian,
  gives the exact wave expression
  \[
    I=\frac1{16}\int_{w,z>0}\int_{\mathbb R}
    e^{-b(w+z)-\lambda|q-w|}
    \int_0^{\min(w,z)}e^{-\max(|q|,t)}\,\ud t\ud q\ud w\ud z.
  \]
  To check the spatial reduction, the derivative of the area
  $\{|X|<u,|Y|<v,|X-Y|<C\}$ with respect to $C$ is
  $2(R-\max(C,|q|))_+$; the area vanishes at $C=0$.
  Integrate this against $e^{-R}/32$ for $R>|q|$.
  Next integrate $z\ge t$ and put $w=t+x$. Splitting the remaining $q$
  integral into $q<0$, $0\le q\le t$, and $q\ge t$ gives respectively
  \[
    \frac{1/c+1/(2b+\lambda+1)}{16b(b+\lambda)(2b+2\lambda+1)},\quad
    \frac1{16b(b+\lambda)(2b+1)(2b+1+\lambda)},
  \]
  \[
    \frac{1/(b+\lambda)+1/c}{16b(2b+1)(b+1)}.
  \]
  Adding gives
  \[
    I=\frac{2b^2+4bc+2c^2-c+1}
    {8bc(b+1)(2b+1)(b+c-1)(2b+2c-1)}.
  \]


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We identify the root kernel and the shift factor
  that is discarded.
  For the clock envelope write $h(r)=(2\pi r)^{-1/2}$ and
  $m(s)=1/(\pi\sqrt2\sqrt{s-1/2})$, $s>1/2$.
  The two spatial pair differences have covariance matrix
  $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u+v+w&-w\\-w&w+z\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
  Writing $r=w+z$, its determinant is $rR+wz$, so their joint white
  density factors as $h(r)h(R+wz/r)$. After deleting the nonnegative
  clock-overlap penalty, the root kernel is
  \[
    H_\gamma(q)=\int_{|q|}^\infty e^{-R/2}h(R+\gamma)\,\ud R
    =\int_{1/2}^\infty\frac{m(s)}s
    e^{-(s-1/2)\gamma}e^{-s|q|}\,\ud s,
    \qquad \gamma=\frac{wz}{r}.
  \]
  The equality follows from the positive Gamma integral for $h$.
  The convolution of the mixture component $q\mapsto e^{-s|q|}$ with
  $q\mapsto e^{-A|q|}$, evaluated at $w$, equals $2S_{A,s}(w)/(s+A)$, where
  \[
    S_{A,s}(w)=e^{-Aw}+\int_0^w A e^{-At}e^{-s(w-t)}\,\ud t\le1.
  \]
  The inequality follows by bounding $e^{-s(w-t)}$ by one; the remaining
  integral is $1-e^{-Aw}$.
  Replacing $S$ by one is precisely the shift erasure. More generally, for
  $\eta\ge0$, replacing it by $e^{-\eta w}$ defines
  \[
    M_b(\eta;A)=\frac1{16}\int_{1/2}^\infty
    \frac{m(s)}{s(s+A)}\int_0^\infty h(r)e^{-b^2r/2}
    \int_0^r e^{-(s-1/2)w(r-w)/r-\eta w}\,\ud w\ud r\ud s.
  \]

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We evaluate the shift-erased envelope and compare
  the rational values.
  All integrals here are positive. Set $w=rt$ and $\tau=s-1/2$.
  For $\eta,\tau\ge0$, put $p=\sqrt{b^2+2\eta}$. The elementary bridge
  integral is
  \[
    \int_0^1[b^2+2\eta t+2\tau t(1-t)]^{-3/2}\,\ud t
    =\frac{2(p+b)}{bp((p+b)^2+2\tau)}.
  \]
  It follows by integrating a quadratic power; at $\eta=\tau=0$ both sides
  are $b^{-3}$. Substitution $z=\sqrt{2s-1}$ and partial fractions give
  \begin{align*}
    M_b(\eta;A)&=\frac{p+b}{2\pi bp}
    \int_0^\infty\frac{\ud z}
    {(z^2+1)(z^2+c^2)(z^2+(p+b)^2)}\\
    &=\frac{1+c+p+b}{4bp c(c+1)(c+p+b)(1+p+b)}.
  \end{align*}
  The usual identity $\int_0^\infty(z^2+q^2)^{-1}\ud z=\pi/(2q)$
  proves the partial fraction evaluation; coincident rates follow by
  continuity. At $\eta=0$, $p=b$. Substituting the asserted rational
  parameters in these two expressions gives the displayed strict gap.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-18}.
\end{proof}

The boundary value $\nu=0$ is a legitimate fixed-parameter transform;
continuity of both rational expressions also gives an overshoot for some
$\nu>0$ nearby. The no-go does not refute $J\le I$, where $J$ is the
original shared-clock candidate, nor does it decide an integrated
comparison using this envelope. The surviving middle-cross comparison
retains $S_{A,1/2}(w)$, instead of replacing it by one, and obtains a smaller
envelope. Reopening this particular closure requires keeping a shift cost
or proving a different integrated estimate.

\section{Model and standing assumptions}\label{S:model}

We study the hyperbolic Anderson model
\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(1.1)}
\begin{equation}\label{E:HAM}
  \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\W(t,x)u(t,x),
  \qquad u(0,x)=1,\quad \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=0,
\end{equation}
for $(t,x) \in \R_+\times\R^d$, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, interpreted in the
Stratonovich sense (limit of classical wave equations driven by smoothed noises
$\W_{\epsilon,\delta}$). The noise is centered Gaussian with
\begin{equation}\label{E:cov}
  \operatorname{Cov}\big(\W(s,x),\W(t,y)\big)=|s-t|^{-\alpha_0}\gamma(x-y),
  \qquad 0<\alpha_0<1,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma \ge 0$ is nonnegative definite with spectral measure $\mu$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Homogeneous covariance]\label{H:homog}
  Let $d \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$. The spatial covariance is a
  nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon measure $\Gamma$ on
  $\R^d$, with positive even tempered Radon spectral measure $\mu$ in the
  convention
  \[
    \Gamma=\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu(\ud\xi)
    \quad\text{in the sense of tempered distributions}.
  \]
  The symbol $\gamma$ in~\eqref{E:cov} denotes this covariance as a function
  when one exists, and as a distribution otherwise. For every Borel set $A$ and
  $c > 0$, $\Gamma(cA) = c^{d-\alpha}\Gamma(A)$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:threshold}
    \alpha_0+\alpha<2,
  \end{equation}
  with $0 < \alpha < d$, or $\alpha = d = 1$. In particular, the last case
  includes spatial white noise $\Gamma = \delta_0$.
\end{hypothesis}

\paragraph{Zero covariance.}
Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} allows $\Gamma = 0$ by design. In this case the
centered Gaussian noise is $\W = 0$, and the solution with initial data
$(1,0)$ is $u(t,x) = 1$. Thus $\E|u(t,x)|^p = 1$ for every $p > 0$, and
all normalized logarithmic moment rates are zero. Results asserting strict
positivity of covariance-dependent quantities impose $\Gamma \ne 0$ locally.

Throughout we write
\begin{equation}\label{E:kappa}
  \LeanEqMarker{E:kappa}
  \kappa:=\frac{4-\alpha-\alpha_0}{3-\alpha}\in(1,2).
\end{equation}

\section{The complete two-chain expansion}

For $m+n = 2k$, label the two chains by $I_1 = \{1,\ldots,m\}$ and $I_2 =
\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}$. A self-pairing has both endpoints in one chain; a
cross-pairing has one endpoint in each chain. Both are included in $\Pi_k$. Let
$p(l)$ denote the preceding vertex in its own chain; at each first vertex set
$(s_{p(l)},y_{p(l)}) = (0,0)$. Thus, the second chain has its own root, rather
than the last vertex of the first chain. For $D \in \Pi_k$, set
\begin{equation}\label{E:mixed-diagram}
  \begin{split}
    J_{m,n,D}(t)
    ={}&\int_{[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^n_<}\ud\mathbf{s}
    \int_{(\R^d)^{m+n}}\ud\mathbf{y}\,
    \prod_{\rho=1}^2\prod_{l\in I_\rho}
    G(s_l-s_{p(l)},y_l-y_{p(l)})\\
    &\hspace{20mm}\times
    \prod_{(j,h)\in D}|s_j-s_h|^{-\alpha_0}\gamma(y_j-y_h).
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
An empty chain contributes the factor one. For $d = 3$, the spatial Green
kernels are measures; for spatial white noise the covariance is a delta measure.
Integrals in~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram} use the generalized-kernel and
regularization conventions of the source, not pointwise values of these
measures. Lemmas~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures}--\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}
construct them with the convention
of~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{Remark~2.3, p.~11, and the Green
regularization on pp.~13--14}.

\begin{proposition}[Mixed terms]\label{P:mixed}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog},
  \begin{equation}\label{E:mixed-moment}
    \E[X_m(t,x)X_n(t,x)]
    =\begin{cases}
      \displaystyle\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}J_{m,n,D}(t),&m+n=2k,\\
      0,&m+n\text{ odd},
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  where the case $m = n = 0$ equals one. In particular,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:full-second}
    \E u^2(t,x)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\sum_{m=0}^{2k}
    \sum_{D\in\Pi_k}J_{m,2k-m,D}(t).
  \end{equation}
  This is a convergent series of nonnegative terms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order} below constructs each $X_n$ and each
  individual two-chain diagram at arbitrary vertexwise widths, including unequal
  and empty chains. Its proof uses the positive spatial-measure limit and
  Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope}; it uses neither the present
  proposition nor any infinite solution series.

  At positive noise widths Wick's formula gives zero at odd total order and the
  finite pairing sum at even total order. The Green-width limit is taken first,
  as in the construction preceding Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures}. At fixed
  $m,n$, joint $L^2$ convergence of the two factors gives product convergence in
  $L^1$:
  \[
    \|Y_mY_n-X_mX_n\|_1
    \le\|Y_m-X_m\|_2\|Y_n\|_2
    +\|X_m\|_2\|Y_n-X_n\|_2\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  A zero-order factor stays equal to one. Each individual Wick diagram also has
  the finite nonnegative limit in Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}.

  To identify its initial-chain form, for terminal variables in the first chain
  set
  \[
    s'_j=t-s_{m+1-j},\qquad y'_j=x-y_{m+1-j},\qquad1\le j\le m,
  \]
  and use the corresponding length-$n$ transformation on the second chain. Each
  transformed chain has initial root $(0,0)$. For example, $G(t-s_m,x-y_m)$
  becomes $G(s'_1,y'_1)$; every other factor becomes the corresponding
  successive increment. The differences in each covariance change sign, absorbed
  by evenness. The vertex involution is
  \[
    \rho(j)=m+1-j\ (1\le j\le m),\qquad
    \rho(m+j)=m+n+1-j\ (1\le j\le n).
  \]
  It induces the pairing bijection $D\mapsto\{\{\rho(a),\rho(b)\}:\{a,b\}\in
  D\}$. The same transformation holds before removing noise smoothing. Passing
  to the finitely many diagram limits proves \eqref{E:mixed-moment} with
  precisely~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram}.

  Lemmas~\ref{L:crude-corrected} and~\ref{L:vertex-crude} independently prove
  fixed-$t$ norm summability of the $X_n$, using the fixed-order construction
  just cited and Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope}.
  Proposition~\ref{P:mild-construction} identifies their series as a
  Stratonovich mild solution. None of those proofs uses the present mixed-term
  identity. Hence, $U_N = \sum_{n=0}^N X_n \to u$ in $L^2$, and
  \[
    |\E U_N^2-\E u^2|
    \le\|U_N-u\|_2(\|U_N\|_2+\|u\|_2)\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  The finite expansions of $\E U_N^2$ increase, since every mixed expectation
  computed above is nonnegative. Their limit is the full double sum, independent
  of its enumeration. Regrouping by total order gives~\eqref{E:full-second}.
  This proves Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}.
\end{proof}

The two chain orders must remain distinct: chronological concatenation can
violate wave support, and a difference of block labels need not measure
chronological separation. No-gos~\ref{P:nogo-05} and~\ref{P:nogo-07}
give explicit positive-measure witnesses.

\section{What homogeneity recovers, and what the bound still loses}

Under homogeneity the norms scale exactly in $\theta$. We now use this
scaling in place of~\eqref{E:crude-norms}.

\begin{proposition}[The uniform pairing estimate]\label{P:envelope}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, there is $K > 0$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{E:homogeneous-envelope}
    \E[X_m(t,x)X_n(t,x)]
    \le P_k K^k e^{2\theta t}\theta^{-\beta k},
    \qquad m+n=2k\ge2,\quad\theta>0.
  \end{equation}
  Define the numerical majorant produced by applying this estimate separately at
  every total order by
  \begin{equation}\label{E:B-envelope}
    B_K(t)=1+\sum_{k\ge1}(2k+1)P_kK^k
    \inf_{\theta>0}\{e^{2\theta t}\theta^{-\beta k}\}.
  \end{equation}
  Then $\E u^2(t,x) \le B_K(t)$ and, for some $c,C > 0$ and all large $t$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:envelope-growth}
    ct^{\rho}\le\log B_K(t)\le Ct^{\rho},\qquad
    \rho=\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}
    =\frac{4-\alpha-\alpha_0}{3-\alpha-\alpha_0}>\kappa.
  \end{equation}
  The lower bound here concerns the numerical majorant $B_K$, not the second
  moment.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The wave kernel scales as $G(cs,c\ud y) = cG(s,\ud y)$, and the covariance has
  degree $-\alpha$. Changing all time and spatial variables by $\theta^{-1}$ in
  the norms yields
  \begin{equation}\label{E:homogeneous-norms}
    N_0(\theta)=\theta^{-2},\quad
    N_1(\theta)=N_1(1)\theta^{\alpha+\alpha_0-2},\quad
    N_2(\theta)^2=N_2(1)^2\theta^{-\beta}.
  \end{equation}
  All constants and scalings follow directly from
  Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}. The products $2N_0N_1$ and $N_2^2$
  therefore both have degree $-\beta$. Choose $K \ge \max\{2N_1(1),N_2(1)^2\}$,
  increasing it to a positive constant if needed.
  Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope} and $q_0+q_2/2 = k$ give
  $K^k\theta^{-\beta k}$ for every admissible product. The finite horizons
  contribute at most $e^{2\theta t}$. Apply this first to regularized moments.
  In $d = 3$, fix $(\epsilon,\delta)$ and let $\eta\downarrow0$ in each factor's
  $L^2$ limit; Cauchy--Schwarz gives $L^1$ convergence of their product. Then
  let $(\epsilon,\delta) \to 0$ in the same way, using
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order} for each chain. In $d = 1,2$ only the noise
  limit is required. The estimates are uniform at both stages, by the multiplier
  bounds in Lemma~\ref{L:crude-corrected}. This
  proves~\eqref{E:homogeneous-envelope}. Empty-chain cases satisfy the same
  bound (using $e^{2\theta t}$ is harmless). Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} now gives
  the majorant.

  The infimum in~\eqref{E:B-envelope} is attained at $\theta = \beta k/(2t)$ and
  equals $e^{\beta k}(2t/(\beta k))^{\beta k}$. Put $q = \beta-1 > 1$. The
  elementary estimates
  \[
    k!\le P_k\le2^k k!,\qquad
    (k/e)^k\le k!\le k^k,\qquad 2k+1\le3^k
  \]
  show that, for fixed positive $a,b$ depending only on $K,\beta$,
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(at^\beta)^k}{(k!)^q}
    \le B_K(t)\le
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(bt^\beta)^k}{(k!)^q}.
  \]
  Indeed, the additional factor $k!^\beta/k^{\beta k}$ lies between $e^{-\beta
  k}$ and one and can be absorbed into $a^k,b^k$. For $z > 0$, the right-hand
  series is at most $(\sum_{k\ge0}z^{k/q}/k!)^q = e^{qz^{1/q}}$, by the
  elementary $\ell^q$ versus $\ell^1$ inequality for nonnegative sequences. For
  a lower bound choose $k = \lfloor z^{1/q}/2\rfloor$ and use $k! \le k^k$; its
  single summand is at least $2^{qk}$. These estimates
  prove~\eqref{E:envelope-growth}. Finally, $\beta-1 = 3-\alpha-\alpha_0 <
  3-\alpha$, so $\rho > \kappa$. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:envelope}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Limitation of the uniform pairing estimate]\label{R:G0-verdict}
  Since $\beta > 2$, we have $1 < \rho < 2$. Thus, Proposition~\ref{P:envelope}
  improves the crude quadratic time scale under homogeneity. It does not itself
  prove Theorem~\ref{T:intro-upper}; Section~\ref{S:interval-upper} uses a different
  argument. Theorem~3.1 used through its uniform pairing estimate is \emph{not
  sufficient to prove Theorem~\ref{T:intro-upper}}: even with exact homogeneous norm scaling, the
  resulting majorant has exponent $\rho > \kappa$. The order coefficient has
  factorial denominator $(k!)^{3-\alpha-\alpha_0}$, whereas the desired scale
  requires $(k!)^{3-\alpha}$; the missing factor is $(k!)^{\alpha_0}$, up to
  exponential factors in $k$. Counting the $2k+1$ chain-length splits does not
  cause this loss, since it can be absorbed into an exponential factor. This
  conclusion does not rule out a more refined application retaining temporal
  order or grouping pairings before estimating them, and does not disprove the
  conjecture. In the formal boundary limit $\alpha_0\downarrow0$, both $\rho$
  and $\kappa$ tend to $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$. This necessary time-independent
  calibration does not distinguish them and asserts no endpoint theorem or
  uniformity of constants in $\alpha_0$. At $p = 1$ use the empty-chain case
  with $e^{\theta t}$ in place of $e^{2\theta t}$; minimizing at $\theta = \beta
  k/t$ changes only a constant to the power $k$, leaving the same factorial
  denominator $(k!)^{3-\alpha-\alpha_0}$. It therefore also loses the factorial
  power $\alpha_0$ relative to $\beta/\kappa = 3-\alpha$, the power
  corresponding to the target series scale. This is a comparison of scales, not
  a coefficient estimate asserted by the source's Theorem~1.2. The loss is not
  specific to the second moment. Both the crude and homogeneous upper bounds are
  consistent with the Jensen lower bound, being at larger time scales. No
  identification with the Skorohod second moment is used: its exclusion of
  self-pairings changes the combinatorics, already visible in the $(0,2)$ and
  $(2,0)$ terms of Lemma~\ref{L:white-test}.
\end{remark}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-01} isolates this scale obstruction. The adjacent-diagram
calculation in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-02} also explains why one cannot repair it
by assigning a cubic-factorial estimate to every individual pairing.

\section{Upper bounds for spatial exponents at most one}\label{S:d1-upper}

The upper bound at scale $t^\kappa$ is proved for $0 < \alpha \le 1$ in
dimensions one, two and three under the standing assumptions. The separate
one-dimensional Riesz estimates retain their sharper constants and white
endpoint. Degree-one covariance in dimensions two and three does not mean full
spatial white noise. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper} treats the higher exponents
by a separate argument. The intrinsic constant is identified  in
Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.

We retain the two-chain expansion of Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, but estimate its
spatial and temporal factors in a different order. The spatial estimate keeps a
power of the number of pairs. After taking a common spatial maximum, we sum all
temporal pairings before using the ordered time domains.

Throughout this section $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$. We first work in dimension one with
either of the spatial covariances
\begin{equation}\label{E:d1-covariances}
  \begin{cases}
    \gamma(y)=c_\alpha|y|^{-\alpha},&0<\alpha<1,\quad c_\alpha>0,\\
    \gamma=c_\delta\delta_0,&\alpha=1,\quad c_\delta>0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Put $b = 2-\alpha$, $q = 3-\alpha$, $\beta = 4-\alpha-\alpha_0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{E:d1-constants}
  Z=\frac{2}{(1-\alpha_0)(2-\alpha_0)},\qquad
  K_{\rm sp}=
  \begin{cases}
    \displaystyle\frac{c_\alpha 2^{2-\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-\alpha}}
    {(2-\alpha)^{2-\alpha}},&0<\alpha<1,\\[2mm]
    c_\delta,&\alpha=1.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
These parameters satisfy Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, so the fixed-order
integrability and mixed expansion already established apply.

\subsection{A spatial bound retaining the ordered increments}

For $m+n = 2k$, label the two chains in block order as in~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram}. For each vertex $l$ write
$h_l = s_l-s_{p(l)} > 0$ and $y_l = x_l-x_{p(l)}$, with a separate root zero
in each chain. Thus, $x_l$ is the sum of increments in its own chain
up to label $l$. The spatial change of variables has determinant one.
In this section $x_l$ denotes a spatial position and $y_l$ its increment; this local
notation differs from the position variables $y_l$
in~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram}. The terminal evaluation point of $u(t,x)$
remains denoted by $x$.
Let $p_\epsilon$ be the centered Brownian Gaussian density of variance
$\epsilon$ and mass one, and set
$\gamma_\epsilon = p_\epsilon*\gamma$. Allow a separate positive
$\epsilon_e$ for every pair $e \in D$ and define
\[
  S_{D,\boldsymbol\epsilon}(h)
  =\int_{\R^{2k}}\prod_{l=1}^{2k}G(h_l,y_l)
  \prod_{(i,j)\in D}\gamma_{\epsilon_{i,j}}(x_j-x_i)\ud y,
  \qquad G(h,y)=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{|y|<h\}}.
\]
This is an ordinary nonnegative integral; no pointwise delta value is used.

\LeanMarker{L:d1-spatial}
\begin{lemma}[Spatial estimate]\label{L:d1-spatial}
  Suppose~\eqref{E:d1-covariances} holds, $k \ge 1$, and $D$ is any pairing of
  the two chains. Orient every pair $(i,j)$ by $i < j$ and let $O_D$ and $C_D$
  be its opening and closing label sets. Then, uniformly in
  $\boldsymbol\epsilon$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-spatial-product}
    S_{D,\boldsymbol\epsilon}(h)\le
    \begin{cases}
      \displaystyle\left(\frac{c_\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)^k
      \prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{1-\alpha},
      &0<\alpha<1,\\[2mm]
      \displaystyle\left(\frac{c_\delta}{2}\right)^k
      \prod_{i\in O_D}h_i,&\alpha=1.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  In particular, if $\sum_{l=1}^{2k}h_l \le 2t$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-spatial-max}
    S_{D,\boldsymbol\epsilon}(h)
    \le K_{\rm sp}^{\,k}\frac{t^{bk}}{k^{bk}}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We first record the convolution bounds. For $0 < \alpha < 1$, the
  integral of the even decreasing function $|y|^{-\alpha}$ over an
  interval of fixed length is maximal when the interval is centered
  at zero. Therefore,
  \[
    \sup_z\int_\R G(h,y)c_\alpha|y+z|^{-\alpha}\ud y
    =\frac{c_\alpha h^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.
  \]
  By Tonelli's theorem, the integral with $\gamma_\epsilon$ equals the
  $p_\epsilon(w)\ud w$ average of the unsmoothed integrals with shifts $z-w$.
  Its nonnegative mass-one average is therefore bounded by the same supremum. In
  the white case, $\gamma_\epsilon = c_\delta p_\epsilon$, so
  \[
    \sup_z\int_\R G(h,y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)\ud y
    \le\frac{c_\delta}{2}.
  \]
  Both estimates are uniform in $\epsilon > 0$.

  Now integrate \emph{all} spatial increments in descending label order
  $2k,2k-1,\ldots,1$. Before processing label $l$, the remaining covariance
  factors are exactly those pairs whose closing labels are at most $l$; the
  bounds from the removed pairs depend only on $h$. If $l \in C_D$, its pair is
  $(i,l)$ with $i < l$ and $x_l-x_i = y_l+z$, where $z$ is independent of $y_l$.
  Every other remaining pair has both endpoints smaller than $l$, so its two
  positions are independent of $y_l$: positions involve only earlier increments
  in their own block chain. Thus, the only covariance involving $y_l$ is its own
  pair, and the convolution bound removes it. If $l \in O_D$, its partner has a
  larger label and its covariance factor has already been removed. All remaining
  covariances have endpoints smaller than $l$, so integration of $y_l$ gives
  only $\int G(h_l,y_l)\ud y_l = h_l$. This also preserves the stated invariant
  for the next label. Tonelli's theorem and the nonnegativity of all factors
  justify each step. There are $k$ labels of each type,
  proving~\eqref{E:d1-spatial-product} for every split and pairing.

  For Riesz covariance, weighted arithmetic--geometric mean gives
  \[
    \prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{1-\alpha}
    \le\left(\frac{2t}{bk}\right)^{bk}
    (1-\alpha)^{(1-\alpha)k}.
  \]
  Indeed, the weights are one at the $k$ openings and $1-\alpha$ at the $k$
  closings, with total $bk$, and the sum of the increments is at most $2t$.
  Multiplication by $(c_\alpha/(1-\alpha))^k$ gives~\eqref{E:d1-spatial-max}
  with the stated $K_{\rm sp}$. In the white case, $\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i \le
  (2t/k)^k$; multiplying by $(c_\delta/2)^k$ gives the same conclusion with $b =
  1$ and $K_{\rm sp} = c_\delta$. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Summing temporal pairings before using time ordering}

Define the total-order coefficients
\[
  A_k(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\E[X_m(t,x)X_{2k-m}(t,x)],\qquad A_0(t)=1.
\]
They do not depend on $x$, by spatial stationarity. By
Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, $\E u^2(t,x) = \sum_{k\ge0}A_k(t)$.

\LeanMarker{P:d1-upper}
\begin{proposition}[One-dimensional upper bound]\label{P:d1-upper}
  Under~\eqref{E:d1-covariances}, for every $k \ge 1$ and $t > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-coefficient}
    A_k(t)\le
    \frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp})^k t^{\beta k}}{k!\,k^{bk}}
    \le\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp})^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-upper}
    \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp(C_{\rm dir}t^\kappa),\qquad
    C_{\rm dir}=q(2ZK_{\rm sp})^{1/q},\quad \kappa=\frac{\beta}{q}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We first work with the Gaussian spatial smoothing and temporal truncation
  in~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.9), (2.11), (2.23)--(2.24)}. Let
  $X_n^{\epsilon,\delta}$ be the smoothed Stratonovich term, using common
  parameters in every factor. Wick's formula expresses each mixed expectation of
  total order $2k$ as a sum over $D \in \Pi_k$. After the chain reversal in
  Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, its spatial factor is the one in
  Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}; its temporal factors are nonnegative and bounded by
  $|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$. For times in $[0,t]^m_ < \times[0,t]^{2k-m}_ <$, the
  increments sum to the two terminal times and therefore to at most $2t$.
  Extract~\eqref{E:d1-spatial-max}, which is independent of the time vector, the
  split, the pairing, and the smoothing parameters. The remaining temporal sum
  is
  \[
    F_k(s_1,\ldots,s_{2k})
    =\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}\prod_{(i,j)\in D}|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}.
  \]
  Every permutation of the $2k$ labels bijects $\Pi_k$, so $F_k$ is fully
  symmetric. Moreover,
  \[
    \int_{[0,t]^2}|s-r|^{-\alpha_0}\ud s\ud r
    =Zt^{2-\alpha_0},\qquad
    \int_{[0,t]^{2k}}F_k(s)\ud s
    =P_k(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k<\infty.
  \]
  The second equality uses the disjoint endpoints of every pairing. Partitioning
  the time cube according to the order within each block now gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-time-symmetry}
    \int_{[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^{2k-m}_<}F_k(s)\ud s
    =\frac{P_k(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k}{m!(2k-m)!}.
  \end{equation}
  Ties have measure zero. Crucially, this symmetry is applied to the \emph{sum}
  of pairings after taking the common spatial bound, not to an individual
  temporally weighted diagram.

  The number of pairings obeys $P_0 = 1$ and $P_k = (2k-1)P_{k-1}$, by choosing
  the partner of the smallest label. Hence, $P_k = (2k)!/(2^kk!)$. The binomial
  identity gives
  \[
    \sum_{m=0}^{2k}\frac1{m!(2k-m)!}=\frac{2^{2k}}{(2k)!}.
  \]
  Multiplying~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry} by the spatial bound and summing over
  $m$ yields
  \[
    \sum_{m=0}^{2k}\E[X_m^{\epsilon,\delta}X_{2k-m}^{\epsilon,\delta}]
    \le K_{\rm sp}^{\,k}\frac{t^{bk}}{k^{bk}}
    \frac{2^{2k}}{(2k)!}
    \frac{(2k)!}{2^kk!}(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k
    =\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp})^kt^{\beta k}}{k!\,k^{bk}}.
  \]
  The same proof covers empty chains, with $X_0^{\epsilon,\delta} = 1$.

  By Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}, each smoothed factor converges in $L^2$.
  Products converge in $L^1$ by Cauchy--Schwarz; for fixed $k$ the sum over
  splits is finite. The bound is uniform in $\epsilon,\delta$, so it passes to
  $A_k(t)$. Since $k! \le k^k$ and $b > 0$, we have $k!\,k^{bk} \ge (k!)^{b+1} =
  (k!)^q$, proving both inequalities in~\eqref{E:d1-coefficient}.

  Finally, for $z \ge 0$ and $q \ge 1$,
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^k}{(k!)^q}
    =\sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    \le\left(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    =\exp(qz^{1/q}).
  \]
  Apply this inequality with $z = 2ZK_{\rm sp}t^\beta$, adding the separately
  defined term $A_0 = 1$. Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} then
  gives~\eqref{E:d1-upper}. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectral bounds for $0 < \alpha \le 1$ in all three dimensions}

We now retain Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} with $d \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $0 < \alpha
\le 1$. The covariance is nonnegative and nonnegative definite as in
Section~\ref{S:model}; when it is a distribution, both these properties and its
homogeneity are understood distributionally. Let $\mu$ be its positive Radon
spectral measure in the convention $\gamma(x) = \int e^{i\xi\cdot
x}\mu(\ud\xi)$, and put $V = \mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le1\})$. No isotropy or spectral
density is assumed. Write $G_h$ for the positive wave-kernel measure of mass
$h$, and $G_{h,\eta} = p_\eta*G_h$ for $\eta > 0$; set $G_{h,0} = G_h$. The
measure notation includes the sphere kernel in dimension three. The mixed-moment
definition of $A_k(t)$ is now applied to the present $d$-dimensional solution.

\LeanMarker{L:low-alpha-convolution}
\begin{lemma}[Low-$\alpha$ convolution bound]\label{L:low-alpha-convolution}
  Under the preceding assumptions with $0 < \alpha < 1$, for every $R,h > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-radial}
    \mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le R\})=VR^\alpha,\qquad
    \int_{\R^d}\min\{h,|\xi|^{-1}\}\mu(\ud\xi)
    =\frac{V}{1-\alpha}h^{1-\alpha}.
  \end{equation}
  For $\epsilon > 0$ and $\eta \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-convolution}
    0\le\int_{\R^d}G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    \le\frac{V}{1-\alpha}h^{1-\alpha},
    \qquad z\in\R^d.
  \end{equation}
  The estimate is uniform in both smoothing parameters and in $z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The spectral measure of $\gamma(c\,\cdot)$ is the pushforward of $\mu$ under
  $\xi\mapsto c\xi$. Homogeneity and uniqueness of the Fourier transform on
  tempered distributions therefore give $\mu(A/c) = c^{-\alpha}\mu(A)$ for every
  Borel set $A$. Equivalently, $\mu(cA) = c^\alpha\mu(A)$. This proves the
  ball-mass formula. Since $\mu$ is Radon, $V < \infty$, and continuity from
  above gives $\mu(\{0\}) = \lim_{R\downarrow0}VR^\alpha = 0$. By the layer-cake
  identity and Tonelli's theorem,
  \[
    \int_{\R^d}\min\{h,|\xi|^{-1}\}\mu(\ud\xi)
    =\int_0^h\mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le u^{-1}\})\ud u
    =V\int_0^h u^{-\alpha}\ud u.
  \]
  The endpoint of each indicator has no effect on its $u$ integral. Since
  $\alpha < 1$, the last expression is the finite quantity
  in~\eqref{E:low-alpha-radial}. This computation uses ball masses, not a radial
  density for $\mu$.

  Gaussian weighting gives a finite measure, by the polynomial ball growth of
  $\mu$. Thus, Fourier Fubini is justified for $\gamma_\epsilon$ and the finite
  measure $G_{h,\eta}$. From the source's wave-kernel
  formulas~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.3)--(2.5), p.~7},
  \[
    \widehat{G}_h(\xi)=\frac{\sin(h|\xi|)}{|\xi|},\qquad
    G_h(\R^d)=h,
  \]
  with value $h$ in the Fourier formula at zero. The Fourier multipliers of
  $p_\epsilon$ and $p_\eta$ have absolute value at most one, with the latter
  multiplier equal to one when $\eta = 0$. Consequently,
  \[
    \left|\int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)\right|
    \le\int_{\R^d}\frac{|\sin(h|\xi|)|}{|\xi|}\mu(\ud\xi)
    \le\int_{\R^d}\min\{h,|\xi|^{-1}\}\mu(\ud\xi).
  \]
  Nonnegativity of $\gamma$ as a distribution implies $\gamma_\epsilon \ge 0$,
  since Gaussian translates are nonnegative test functions; $G_{h,\eta}$ is also
  nonnegative. This proves the lower bound in~\eqref{E:low-alpha-convolution}.
  Combining the preceding displays proves its upper bound. No positivity of the
  oscillatory Fourier integrand is asserted. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:low-alpha-convolution}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:alpha-one-convolution}
\begin{lemma}[Degree-one convolution bound]\label{L:alpha-one-convolution}
  Under the preceding covariance assumptions with $\alpha = 1$, for every $h >
  0$, $\epsilon > 0$, $\eta \ge 0$, and $z \in \R^d$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:alpha-one-convolution}
    0\le\int_{\R^d}G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    \le\frac{\pi V}{2}.
  \end{equation}
  No isotropy or angular density is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Fourier uniqueness, applied to the distributional homogeneity as above, gives
  $\mu(cA) = c\mu(A)$ and $\mu(\{0\}) = 0$. Define a finite measure on the unit
  sphere by
  \[
    \sigma(E)=\mu\{\xi:0<|\xi|\le1,\ \xi/|\xi|\in E\}.
  \]
  Its total mass is $V$. Homogeneity implies that the corresponding sector of
  radius $R$ has mass $R\sigma(E)$. Subtracting two such sectors shows that a
  polar rectangle $(r_1,r_2]\times E$ has mass $(r_2-r_1)\sigma(E)$. Uniqueness
  of measures on these generating rectangles therefore gives the polar
  decomposition $\mu(\ud\xi) = \ud r\,\sigma(\ud\theta)$, $r > 0$, $\theta \in
  \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$. The real stationary covariance is even; Fourier uniqueness
  makes $\mu$, and hence $\sigma$, even.

  Use the Brownian Gaussian convention, so the combined covariance and Green
  smoothing multiplier is $e^{-ar^2}$ with $a = (\epsilon+\eta)/2 > 0$. Gaussian
  weighting makes the spectral measure finite. Fourier Fubini and evenness give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:alpha-one-polar}
    \begin{split}
      \int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
      &=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}} I_a(h,\theta\cdot z)\sigma(\ud\theta),\\
      I_a(h,v)&=\int_0^\infty e^{-ar^2}
      \frac{\sin(hr)}r\cos(rv)\ud r.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  The radial integral is absolutely convergent: its integrand is bounded near
  zero and Gaussian-decaying at infinity. Passing from the complex exponential
  to cosine introduces no factor two, since both antipodal directions are
  already included in $\sigma$.

  Let $g_h(v) = \tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{|v|<h\}}$ and let $p_{2a}^{(1)}(v) = (4\pi
  a)^{-1/2}e^{-v^2/(4a)}$ be the one-dimensional Gaussian density. Their Fourier
  transforms are $\sin(hr)/r$ and $e^{-ar^2}$. The one-dimensional inversion
  factor is $1/(2\pi)$ in the source convention, so
  \[
    (g_h*p_{2a}^{(1)})(v)
    =\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R e^{irv}e^{-ar^2}
    \frac{\sin(hr)}r\ud r
    =\frac1\pi I_a(h,v).
  \]
  The sign in the inversion exponential is immaterial because the integrand
  pairs evenly under $r\mapsto-r$. Since $p_{2a}^{(1)}$ has mass one and $0 \le
  g_h \le 1/2$, we obtain $0 \le I_a(h,v) \le \pi/2$. Integrating against
  $\sigma$, whose mass is $V$, proves~\eqref{E:alpha-one-convolution}. This
  retains the oscillation until the one-dimensional integral is evaluated; the
  divergent absolute Fourier envelope at $\alpha = 1$ is not used. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{P:low-alpha-upper}
\begin{proposition}[Upper bound for $\alpha \le 1$]\label{P:low-alpha-upper}
  Suppose that Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} and the standing covariance positivity
  assumptions hold, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, $0 < \alpha \le 1$, and $V > 0$. Define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-constants}
    \begin{split}
      K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen}
      &=\begin{cases}
        \displaystyle\frac{V2^{2-\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-\alpha}}
        {(2-\alpha)^{2-\alpha}},&0<\alpha<1,\\[2mm]
        \pi V,&\alpha=1,
      \end{cases}\\
      C_{\rm dir}^{\rm gen}&=q(2ZK_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^{1/q}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  With the same $A_k$, $b$, $q$, $\beta$, and $Z$ as above, for
  $k \ge 1$ and $t > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-coefficient}
    A_k(t)\le\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^k t^{\beta k}}
    {k!\,k^{bk}}
    \le\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q},
    \qquad
    \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp(C_{\rm dir}^{\rm gen}t^\kappa).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  First smooth the noise as in~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.9), (2.11), (2.23)--(2.24)}.
  In dimension three also use $G_{h,\eta}$ with $\eta > 0$, as in the
  source's pp.~13--14. The increment change of variables is now a
  triangular map on vectors in $\R^d$, still of determinant one.
  The descending-label proof of Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial} depends only
  on which increments enter each position, not on their dimension.
  Its invariant therefore remains valid: at a closing label there
  is exactly one covariance depending on that vector increment, and
  at an opening label its pair has already been removed.
  If $0 < \alpha < 1$, Lemma~\ref{L:low-alpha-convolution} gives the
  closing bound $Mh_l^{1-\alpha}$ with $M = V/(1-\alpha)$.
  At $\alpha = 1$, Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution} gives the
  constant closing bound $M = \pi V/2$. Every opening gives mass
  $h_l$, uniformly in $\eta$ and all noise-smoothing parameters.
  For $\alpha < 1$, the same weighted arithmetic--geometric mean
  calculation gives
  \[
    M^k\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{1-\alpha}
    \le (K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^k\frac{t^{bk}}{k^{bk}}.
  \]
  At $\alpha = 1$, ordinary arithmetic--geometric mean on the $k$ opening
  increments gives $M^k\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i \le (2M)^kt^k/k^k$, which is the same
  bound with $b = 1$ and $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen} = \pi V$. Both cases use
  $\sum_lh_l \le 2t$. For $d = 1,2$ one may use the Green densities directly;
  their opening mass and closing convolution obey these same bounds.

  Replace the nonnegative truncated temporal factors by $|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$
  before taking their pairing sum. The temporal symmetry, cube integral and
  split count in~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry} do not involve the spatial
  dimension. Thus, exactly the multiplication of counts in the proof of
  Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper} gives the first bound
  in~\eqref{E:low-alpha-coefficient} for the regularized moments.

  We spell out the limit in dimension three. Fix every noise-smoothing parameter
  and first let $\eta\downarrow0$. At each time vector, the Gaussian moment
  formula is a finite sum of bounded continuous products of the smoothed
  covariance functions, integrated against products of the finite Green
  measures. These products of measures converge weakly as $\eta\downarrow0$,
  with the same masses $\prod_l h_l$. At fixed noise smoothing the Gaussian
  covariance factors are uniformly bounded, so a constant times $\prod_lh_l$
  dominates on each bounded time simplex. Dominated convergence applies also to
  the covariance between two approximations with different $\eta$ values. The
  resulting common joint covariance limit proves the $L^2$ Cauchy property of
  each fixed-order random integral. This is the inner limit used in the source's
  definition on pp.~13--14. Products of two factors converge in $L^1$ by
  Cauchy--Schwarz, so the moment inequality survives this limit. Next send the
  noise-smoothing parameters to zero using the fixed-order $L^2$ convergence of
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}, and again pass products in $L^1$. In
  dimensions one and two only the latter limit is required. The bounds at both
  stages are uniform.

  We have proved the first coefficient bound for $A_k(t)$. The factorial
  inequality and nonnegative-series argument in Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}
  apply unchanged, with $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen}$ in place of $K_{\rm sp}$ and $A_0
  = 1$. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the spectral estimates]\label{R:low-alpha-scope}
  If $V = 0$, spectral homogeneity gives $\mu(B_R) = 0$ for every $R$, hence
  $\mu = 0$ and $u = 1$; the noise-free conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, all
  angular and dimensional dependence is allowed in $V$. For $0 < \alpha < 1$,
  the example $\gamma(x) = c|x_1|^{-\alpha}$ in dimensions two or three has its
  spectral measure supported on an axis; a full-dimensional density is not
  required. The absolute Fourier envelope diverges at $\alpha = 1$, where the
  separate oscillatory calculation in Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution} is
  essential. The endpoint is not obtained by taking a limit in $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm
  gen}$ for $\alpha < 1$. For $d = 1$ and $\gamma = c_\delta\delta_0$, the
  source convention gives $\mu(\ud\xi) = c_\delta\ud\xi/(2\pi)$ and $V =
  c_\delta/\pi$. Hence, the endpoint constant $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen} = \pi V =
  c_\delta$ agrees exactly with Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}, with no extra
  antipodal factor. In dimensions two and three the endpoint covers degree-one
  homogeneous covariance, not full spatial white noise. No result for $\alpha >
  1$ follows from these estimates.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Checks on the scale, constants and scope}

\LeanMarker{C:d1-first-calibration}
\begin{corollary}[First-moment calibration]\label{C:d1-first-calibration}
  Let $K_*$ be $K_{\rm sp}$ under~\eqref{E:d1-covariances}, or $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm
  gen}$ under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}. Put
  $C_{2,*} = q(2ZK_*)^{1/q}$. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-first-calibration}
    \E u(t,x)\le\exp(C_{1,*}t^\kappa),\qquad
    C_{1,*}=q\left(\frac{ZK_*}{2^{3-\alpha}}\right)^{1/q}
    =\frac{C_{2,*}}{2^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}}.
  \end{equation}
  In either nonzero case $2C_{1,*} < C_{2,*}$. Conditionally on a first-moment
  lower rate at least $L_*$, $2L_* \le 2C_{1,*} < C_{2,*}$, consistently with
  Proposition~\ref{P:Jensen}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  For one chain of length $2k$, the increments sum to at most $t$. The spatial
  estimate in Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}, or in the proof of
  Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}, therefore applies with $t/2$ in place of
  $t$. The temporal pairing sum on one ordered simplex is
  $P_k(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k/(2k)!$. Using the same regularization and limit order
  as in the respective upper-bound proof gives
  \[
    \E X_{2k}(t,x)
    \le\frac{(ZK_*/2^{b+1})^k t^{\beta k}}{k!\,k^{bk}}
    \le\frac{(ZK_*/2^{3-\alpha})^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}.
  \]
  Every odd term has zero expectation. The $L^2$ expansion permits taking
  expectations of partial sums, and the resulting nonnegative even series is
  summed by the scalar inequality in the proof of Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}.
  This gives~\eqref{E:d1-first-calibration}. If the first-moment lower rate is
  at least $L_*$, \eqref{E:d1-first-calibration} implies $L_* \le C_{1,*}$. In
  the stated nonzero cases $K_* > 0$, hence $C_{1,*} > 0$; and
  $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha) > 1$ gives $2C_{1,*} < C_{2,*}$. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:d1-first-calibration}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Checks and comparison with the uniform pairing bound]\label{R:d1-upper-checks}
  The argument preserves the factor $k^{-(2-\alpha)k}$ from the spatial
  increment maximum. Replacing the spatial volume merely by a constant times
  $t^{(2-\alpha)k}$ would lose the factorial power needed
  in~\eqref{E:d1-coefficient}. This proof does not use the
  common-Laplace-parameter norm majorant of Proposition~\ref{P:envelope}, and it
  does not assert a stronger factorial estimate for every individual diagram.
  Instead, the temporal ordering is exploited after summing pairings
  in~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry}. Thus, the scoped G0 obstruction remains valid.
  For unit white covariance, the bound on the total-order-two coefficient is
  $2Z$, whereas the exact value from Lemma~\ref{L:white-test} is $2c_0$; their
  ratio is $2(3-\alpha_0) > 4$. At the static endpoint the exact order-four
  coefficient is $11/540$, while the first bound in~\eqref{E:d1-coefficient}
  gives $1/2$. These are checks of normalization, not evidence of sharp
  constants.
\end{remark}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-06} quantifies the loss avoided here: discarding the
spatial constraints before summation gives the wrong time exponent.


\begin{remark}[Boundary and literature comparisons]\label{R:d1-comparisons}
  As $\alpha_0\downarrow0$, the time exponent tends to $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$,
  the Chen--Hu static exponent cited in Remark~\ref{R:G1-scope}. This is an
  exponent calibration, not an interchange of the static and large-time limits.
  With the Riesz normalization $c_\alpha = (1-\alpha)/2$, the displayed $K_{\rm
  sp}$ tends to one as $\alpha\uparrow1$, matching unit white covariance. For
  comparison only, the Skorohod upper bound of
  Balan--Conus~\cite{balan.conus:16:intermittency}*{Theorem~2.1(b), (8), (10),
  p.~1492} has time exponent $(2H+2-a)/(3-a)$. Setting $H = 1-\alpha_0/2$ and $a
  = \alpha$ yields the same $\kappa$. Their covariance normalization contains
  $H(2H-1)$ and they use a Skorohod mild solution. No constants or solutions are
  identified, and their theorem is not an input to the present proof. The
  current coverage of this section is $0 < \alpha \le 1$ in $d = 1,2,3$,
  including the one-dimensional white case. It does not address $1 < \alpha <
  2-\alpha_0$ in dimensions two and three.
\end{remark}

\section{A small-order test for direct Brownian clock copying}
\label{S:two-clock-screen}

The result concerns a specified gap-transform substitution. It does not rule out
ordering corrections, a diagonal-only representation, one-sided/asymptotic use,
or the original sharp conjecture.

Theorem~6.1 of~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{pp.~37--42} represents
the time Laplace transform of a \emph{fixed-order} mean by a spatial Brownian
path and an independent scalar Brownian clock. The proof uses nonnegative cut
sums \(c_l\), obtained from one-chain time order on p.~38, before the
wave-to-heat substitution on p.~39. Remark~6.3, p.~42, singles out that order as
the obstruction for more copies. We test the direct replacement by two spatial
Brownian paths and two scalar Brownian clocks, without an additional ordering
weight.

All transforms below are at fixed total order. We begin with the original
admissible case \(d=\alpha=1\), \(\Gamma=\delta_0\), \(0<a<1\), and
\(G_s(y)=\frac12\mathbf1_{\{|y|<s\}}\). This is a falsification case for an
intended general representation, not a restriction of Theorem~\ref{T:intro-second-moment}.

\LeanMarker{L:white-clock-laplace}
\begin{lemma}[White-space low-order Laplace transforms]
  \label{L:white-clock-laplace}
  The \((1,1)\) cross-pair gap transform is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:white-cross-clock-transform}
    \begin{split}
      K_\times(\theta_1,\theta_2)
      &:=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
      e^{-\theta_1s-\theta_2t}|s-t|^{-a}
      \frac{\min(s,t)}2\ud s\ud t\\
      &=\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)}
      {2(\theta_1+\theta_2)^2}
      \big(\theta_1^{a-1}+\theta_2^{a-1}\big),
      \qquad \theta_1,\theta_2>0.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  In particular, \(K_\times(\theta,\theta)=
  \Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)/(4\theta^{3-a})\). For the empty-plus-length-two
  self-pair, independent gap damping gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:white-self-clock-transform}
    K_{\rm self}(\eta_1,\eta_2)
    :=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\eta_1r_1-\eta_2r_2}r_2^{-a}\frac{r_1}2
    \ud r_1\ud r_2
    =\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)}
    {2\eta_1^2\eta_2^{1-a}}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The spatial cross factor is
  \[
    \int G_s(y)G_t(z)\delta_0(y-z)\ud y\ud z
    =\frac14\int\mathbf1_{\{|y|<s\wedge t\}}\ud y
    =\frac{\min(s,t)}2.
  \]
  Split its time integral into \(s=t+v\) and \(t=s+v\), with \(v>0\). Each part
  is one half the product of
  \[
    \int_0^\infty t e^{-(\theta_1+\theta_2)t}\ud t
    =(\theta_1+\theta_2)^{-2}
  \]
  and \(\int_0^\infty e^{-\theta_i v}v^{-a}\ud v
  =\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)\theta_i^{a-1}\). All integrands are nonnegative and
  finite since \(a<1\), so Tonelli justifies the separation. This proves
  \eqref{E:white-cross-clock-transform}.

  For one length-two chain, its delta covariance forces the two positions equal.
  The second Green factor is \(G_{r_2}(0)=1/2\), and integration of the first
  gives mass \(r_1\). The time difference is \(r_2\). The two elementary Laplace
  integrals therefore give \eqref{E:white-self-clock-transform}. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:white-clock-laplace}.
\end{proof}

The source's analytic time covariance is
\[
  \gamma_a^{\rm an}(z)
  =\big(z\,\operatorname{sgn}\Re z\big)^{-a},\qquad \Re z\ne0,
\]
with the power taken on the right half-plane. For \(r>0,v\ge0\), put
\begin{equation}\label{E:averaged-clock-kernel}
  \Psi_a(r,v)=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
  \int_0^\infty \lambda^{a-1}e^{-r\lambda-v\lambda^2/2}\ud\lambda.
\end{equation}
From the source's complex Laplace identity
\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(6.3), p.~41}, and the Gaussian
characteristic function, \(\Psi_a(r,v)=\E(r+iZ)^{-a}\) when \(Z\) is centered
normal with variance \(v\). Fubini at fixed \(r>0\) is justified by
\(\int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}e^{-r\lambda}\ud\lambda<\infty\). In particular,
\(\Psi_a\) is nonnegative, decreases with \(v\), and \(\Psi_a(r,v)\le r^{-a}\).

Return to an arbitrary nonzero covariance in Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, with
temporal exponent \(a\), so \(0<a<1\), \(0<\alpha<2\) and \(a+\alpha<2\). Define
\[
  h_\Gamma(w)=\int e^{-w|\xi|^2/2}\mu(\ud\xi)
  =(p_w*\Gamma)(0),\qquad
  \mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)=\int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{q^2+|\xi|^2}.
\]
Both are positive and finite for \(w,q>0\). Spectral homogeneity gives
\(h_\Gamma(w)=w^{-\alpha/2}h_\Gamma(1)\) and
\(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)=q^{\alpha-2}\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)\). The cross-pair gap
transform at common damping is
\[
  K_\Gamma(\theta)=N_2(\theta)^2
\]
with temporal exponent \(a\) in~\eqref{E:damped-norm-definitions}. This is the
spatial-regularization limit of the \((1,1)\) diagram.

Let \(B_1,B_2\) be independent spatial standard Brownian motions. For auxiliary
standard Brownian clocks with \(\E[\beta_1(u)\beta_2(v)]=\rho\min(u,v)\),
\(-1\le\rho\le1\), independent of the spatial paths, the clock difference has
variance
\[
  v_\rho(u,v)=u+v-2\rho\min(u,v).
\]
At common damping \(\theta>0\), the direct unweighted, unrestricted two-clock
covariance substitution yields the averaged candidate
\begin{equation}\label{E:naive-clock-candidate}
  J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)
  :=\frac14\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
  e^{-\theta^2(u+v)/2}h_\Gamma(u+v)
  \Psi_a(\theta|u-v|,v_\rho(u,v))\ud u\ud v .
\end{equation}
Values on \(u=v\) do not affect the integral. The factor \(1/4\) is one factor
\(1/2\) for each wave-to-heat gap transform; \(h_\Gamma(u+v)\) is the spatial
average of \(\Gamma(B_1(u)-B_2(v))\). This average is interpreted by spatial
heat regularization. For the white covariance it is \((2\pi(u+v))^{-1/2}\). The
analytic covariance, not its absolute-value bound, is being copied.

\LeanMarker{P:clock-copy-obstruction}
\begin{proposition}[Obstruction to unweighted constant-correlation clock copying]
  \label{P:clock-copy-obstruction}
  For every covariance and parameters just specified, put
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cross-clock-lambda-weight}
    w_\theta(\lambda)
    =\frac{\lambda^{a-2}}
    {2\theta\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
    \big(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta)
    -\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda)\big),\qquad\lambda>0.
  \end{equation}
  This is a positive integrable density, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:clock-copy-obstruction}
    \begin{split}
      K_\Gamma(\theta)&=\int_0^\infty w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda,\\
      0<J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)
      &\le J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)
      =\int_0^\infty
      \frac{2\theta}{2\theta+\lambda}w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda
      <K_\Gamma(\theta).
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  When \(\alpha=1\),
  \begin{equation}\label{E:degree-one-clock-loss}
    J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)=(2-2^a)K_\Gamma(\theta).
  \end{equation}
  Thus, this direct analytic clock-copying family, including independent clocks
  and a single shared clock, fails to reproduce the cross-pair transform
  throughout the original nonzero covariance class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  First evaluate the cross transform. Temporarily smooth the spatial covariance,
  so Fourier and time Fubini are absolute. For \(r=|\xi|\), direct integration
  over \(s=t+v\) and \(t=s+v\) gives
  \[
    \int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\theta(s+t)-\lambda|s-t|}
    \widehat{G}_s(\xi)\overline{\widehat{G}_t(\xi)}\ud s\ud t
    =\frac{2\theta+\lambda}
    {2\theta(\theta^2+r^2)((\theta+\lambda)^2+r^2)} .
  \]
  For example, on the first cone the \(v\)-integral uses
  \[
    \int_0^\infty e^{-(\theta+\lambda)v}
    \sin(r(t+v))\ud v
    =\frac{(\theta+\lambda)\sin(rt)+r\cos(rt)}
    {(\theta+\lambda)^2+r^2}.
  \]
  Integrating the remaining sine-square and sine-cosine terms against
  \(e^{-2\theta t}\), and adding the second cone, gives the displayed identity.
  The value at \(r=0\) is its continuous limit.

  The resulting spectral integrand is nonnegative. Removing the spatial Gaussian
  multiplier and using the Laplace formula for the power covariance therefore
  gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      K_\Gamma(\theta)
      &=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}
      \frac{2\theta+\lambda}{2\theta}
      \int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}
      {(\theta^2+|\xi|^2)((\theta+\lambda)^2+|\xi|^2)}
      \ud\lambda\\
      &=\int_0^\infty w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda.
    \end{split}
  \]
  The second equality is partial fractions, with
  \((\theta+\lambda)^2-\theta^2=\lambda(2\theta+\lambda)\). The smoothing
  passage is justified by monotone convergence in this nonnegative spectral
  expression and the finite quadratic form in
  Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}. Alternatively, homogeneity directly
  bounds the density near zero by a constant times \(\lambda^{a-1}\), and at
  infinity by a constant times \(\lambda^{a-2}\). Its strict positivity follows
  from \(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta) -\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda)>0\),
  since \(\mu\ne0\).

  The candidate integrals are well defined and positive. The bound
  \(\Psi_a(\theta|u-v|,v_\rho)\le (\theta|u-v|)^{-a}\), together with
  \(h_\Gamma(w)=h_\Gamma(1)w^{-\alpha/2}\), supplies an absolute majorant. With
  \(w=u+v\), \(q=u-v\), the Jacobian is \(1/2\) and \(-w<q<w\). It is a constant
  times
  \[
    \int_0^\infty e^{-\theta^2w/2}w^{-\alpha/2}
    \int_{-w}^w|q|^{-a}\ud q\ud w
    =\frac2{1-a}\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\theta^2w/2}w^{1-\alpha/2-a}\ud w<\infty .
  \]
  Here \(a<1\) and \(a+\alpha/2<2\). The same bound dominates the spatial
  heat-regularized averages, whose spatial factor is \(h_\Gamma(w+2\epsilon)\le
  h_\Gamma(w)\). This is an averaged spectral bound, not pointwise covariance
  monotonicity. Since \(v_\rho(u,v)\ge|u-v|=v_1(u,v)\), monotonicity of
  \(\Psi_a\) gives \(J_{\Gamma,\rho}\le J_{\Gamma,1}\).

  We compute the latter by nonnegative Tonelli. Write
  \(A=\theta\lambda+\lambda^2/2\). Integration in \(q\) gives
  \(2(1-e^{-Aw})/A\). Also
  \[
    \mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)
    =\frac12\int_0^\infty e^{-q^2w/2}h_\Gamma(w)\ud w,
  \]
  by the elementary resolvent integral and spectral Tonelli. Consequently,
  \[
    \begin{split}
      J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)
      &=\frac1{4\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\frac{\lambda^{a-1}}A
      \int_0^\infty h_\Gamma(w)
      \left(e^{-\theta^2w/2}
      -e^{-(\theta+\lambda)^2w/2}\right)\ud w\ud\lambda\\
      &=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty
      \frac{\lambda^{a-2}
      (\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta)-\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda))}
      {2\theta+\lambda}\ud\lambda\\
      &=\int_0^\infty
      \frac{2\theta}{2\theta+\lambda}w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda.
    \end{split}
  \]
  The \(q\)-integral's factor two cancels its coordinate Jacobian \(1/2\); the
  resolvent identity supplies the other factor two. Since \(w_\theta>0\) and
  \(2\theta/(2\theta+\lambda)<1\) for every \(\lambda>0\), the strict inequality
  follows.

  Finally, if \(\alpha=1\), homogeneity gives
  \(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)=\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)/q\). Substitution in the two
  densities gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      K_\Gamma(\theta)
      &=\frac{\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)}
      {2\theta^2\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\frac{\lambda^{a-1}}{\theta+\lambda}\ud\lambda,\\
      J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)
      &=\frac{\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)}
      {\theta^2\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}
      \left(\frac1{\theta+\lambda}
      -\frac1{2\theta+\lambda}\right)\ud\lambda .
    \end{split}
  \]
  The real Beta identity
  \[
    \int_0^\infty\frac{x^{a-1}}{x+c}\ud x
    =c^{a-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a),
    \qquad c>0,
  \]
  follows by scaling and \((r,s)\mapsto(r+s,r/s)\) in the positive product of
  the two Gamma integrals. Their finiteness uses \(0<a<1\). Evaluation of these
  two integrals gives \(J_{\Gamma,1}/K_\Gamma=2(1-2^{a-1})=2-2^a\), proving
  \eqref{E:degree-one-clock-loss}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:clock-copy-obstruction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Normalization and scope of the obstruction]
  \label{R:clock-copy-scope}
  At the static endpoint define the time covariance directly as one. The
  candidate with \(\Psi_0=1\) gives
  \[
    J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)
    =\int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{(\theta^2+|\xi|^2)^2}
    =K_\Gamma(\theta).
  \]
  This follows by integrating \(w e^{-\theta^2w/2}h_\Gamma(w)/4\) spectrally. In
  the white case it equals \(1/(4\theta^3)\). Thus, static and
  homogeneous-degree checks alone miss the positive-\(a\) loss. Relative to the
  shared-clock value \(\rho=1\), the omitted cross-pair multiplier is exactly
  \(1+\lambda/(2\theta)\). For smaller correlations the deficit can be larger.
  This does not assert a correction rule at higher orders.

  The transforms \(K_\times\) use gap damping. For the auxiliary cutoff function
  \[
    F(T_1,T_2)=\int_0^{T_1}\int_0^{T_2}
    |s-t|^{-a}\frac{\min(s,t)}2\ud s\ud t,
  \]
  nonnegative Fubini adds \(1/(\theta_1\theta_2)\) to its double
  external-horizon transform. This function is not an unequal-time solution
  correlation. Reversing chains ending at different physical times instead
  shifts the cross covariance to \(|T_1-T_2-s+t|^{-a}\). Setting the two damping
  parameters equal is also not the single Laplace transform of a
  diagonal-horizon moment. For a self-pair at common gap damping, one external
  horizon instead adds \(1/\theta\), recovering the known first-mean
  coefficient.

  The proposition rules out exactly the analytic covariance substitution
  in~\eqref{E:naive-clock-candidate}, with product heat-chain measure,
  unrestricted operational-time rectangle and constant Brownian correlation.
  Additional relative-order weights,other derived clock couplings,matrix
  representations,modulus inequalities and their possible asymptotics are not
  covered. In particular, the inequality at this first cross pair leaves open a
  shared-clock lower comparison or asymptotic equivalence at higher orders. This
  finite clock calculation does not identify the intrinsic value proved  in
  Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment} with the source's all-moment
  conjecture. This is not a Skorohod result or a no-go for every two-clock
  method.
\end{remark}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-17} summarizes the missing degree-two multiplier.
No-go~\ref{P:nogo-18} records a different degree-four obstruction: erasing
an additional shift makes one upper envelope too large to close its
comparison, without refuting the comparison itself.

\section{Covariance bounds for physically masked wave fields}
\label{S:masked-covariance}

\input{notes/figures/mask-window-figure}

We establish the covariance estimates needed for the operator construction.
Throughout this section, Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} holds, $a = \alpha_0$, and
$\Gamma \ne 0$. For zero spatial covariance, the spatial spectral measures and
noise variances vanish; the purely temporal estimates remain valid independently
of $\Gamma$. We use the unitary Fourier transform
\[
  \widehat{f}(\xi) = c_d\int_{\R^d}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}f(x)\ud x,
  \qquad c_d = (2\pi)^{-d/2}.
\]
The spectral convention in Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} means that, for every
complex Schwartz test $\varphi$,
\begin{equation}\label{E:masked-spectral-convention}
  \int_{\R^d}\varphi(x)\Gamma(\ud x)
  = \int_{\R^d}\left(\int_{\R^d}e^{i\xi\cdot x}
  \varphi(x)\ud x\right)\mu(\ud\xi).
\end{equation}
This is an identity of distribution pairings. We will not interchange two
infinite measures in the associated oscillatory expression.

A positive tempered Radon measure has polynomial mass growth. Indeed, choose a
nonnegative compact smooth test that is at least one on the unit ball, and apply
a defining Schwartz seminorm bound for the distribution to its dilations. Its
mass on a ball of radius $R \ge 1$ is bounded by a power of $R$. Summation over
dyadic shells then gives integrability of $(1+|x|)^{-N}$ for some $N$, and hence
absolute integrability of every Schwartz test. These facts apply to both
$\Gamma$ and $\mu$. Smooth cutoffs converge in the Schwartz topology, so these
integrals agree with the tempered pairings.

\subsection{Translated spectral growth and physical masks}

\LeanMarker{P:masked-spectral-growth}
\begin{proposition}[Translated spectral mass]\label{P:masked-spectral-growth}
  Put
  \[
    M_\Gamma = \int_{\R^d}e^{-|x|^2/4}\Gamma(\ud x),
    \qquad C_\Gamma = \frac{eM_\Gamma}{(2\sqrt{\pi})^d}.
  \]
  Then $0 < M_\Gamma < \infty$, and for $y \in \R^d$ and $r > 0$,
  \begin{align}
    \int_{\R^d}e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}\mu(\ud\xi)
    &= \left(\frac{r}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\right)^d
    \int_{\R^d}e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{-iy\cdot x}\Gamma(\ud x),
    \label{E:masked-gaussian-transform}\\
    0 \le \int_{\R^d}e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}\mu(\ud\xi)
    &\le \frac{r^\alpha M_\Gamma}{(2\sqrt{\pi})^d},
    \qquad \mu(B(y,r)) \le C_\Gamma r^\alpha.
    \label{E:masked-translated-ball}
  \end{align}
  The ball may be open or closed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Temperedness gives finiteness of $M_\Gamma$. Its positivity follows from
  $\Gamma \ne 0$. To check the Fourier constant, set $J(b) = \int_\R
  e^{-Ax^2}e^{ibx}\ud x$ for $A > 0$. Squaring the real Gaussian integral and
  using polar coordinates gives $J(0) = \sqrt{\pi/A}$. Differentiation and
  integration by parts give $J'(b) = -bJ(b)/(2A)$. Therefore, $J(b) =
  \sqrt{\pi/A}e^{-b^2/(4A)}$. Taking products over the coordinates gives
  \[
    \int_{\R^d}e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{i(\xi-y)\cdot x}\ud x
    = \left(\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}r\right)^d e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}.
  \]
  Apply~\eqref{E:masked-spectral-convention} to the single Schwartz test
  $e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{-iy\cdot x}$ to obtain \eqref{E:masked-gaussian-transform}.
  Its left side is nonnegative. Taking the absolute value on the right removes
  the complex phase because $\Gamma$ is nonnegative. The pushforward of $\Gamma$
  by $x \mapsto rx$ assigns mass $r^{\alpha-d}\Gamma(A)$ to a Borel set $A$.
  Thus,
  \[
    \int e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}\Gamma(\ud x) = r^{\alpha-d}M_\Gamma.
  \]
  This proves the Gaussian bound. On either ball $B(y,r)$ the integrand
  $e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}$ is at least $e^{-1}$, proving the mass bound. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-spectral-growth}.
\end{proof}

Choose and fix a real nonzero Schwartz function $\zeta$. For $R > 0$ put
$\zeta_R(x) = \zeta(x/R)$, $k_R = \widehat{\zeta_R}$ and $L =
\|\widehat{\zeta}\|_1$. Then $k_R(u) = R^d\widehat{\zeta}(Ru)$ and $\|k_R\|_1 =
L > 0$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{E:masked-auxiliary-measure}
  \nu_R(\ud\eta) = \rho_R(\eta)\ud\eta,
  \qquad \rho_R(\eta) = c_d^2L\int |k_R(\xi-\eta)|\mu(\ud\xi).
\end{equation}

\LeanMarker{P:masked-auxiliary-norm}
\begin{proposition}[Auxiliary mask norm]\label{P:masked-auxiliary-norm}
  The measure $\nu_R$ is even, tempered and Radon, with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-auxiliary-growth}
    \nu_R(B(y,r)) \le c_d^2L^2C_\Gamma r^\alpha
  \end{equation}
  for all $y \in \R^d$ and $r > 0$, uniformly in $R > 0$. If $H$ is a separable
  complex Hilbert space and $F$ is an $H$-valued Schwartz function, then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-hilbert-fourier}
    \int \|\widehat{\zeta_RF}(\xi)\|_H^2\mu(\ud\xi)
    \le \int \|\widehat{F}(\eta)\|_H^2\nu_R(\ud\eta).
  \end{equation}
  For a real $H$, the same assertion holds after complexification.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Schwartz decay of $k_R$ and polynomial growth of $\mu$ show that
  $\rho_R(\eta)$ is finite at every $\eta$. On compact sets of $\eta$, the
  integrands have a common integrable bound. Dominated convergence therefore
  makes $\rho_R$ continuous, and $\nu_R$ is Radon. Since $\zeta$ is real,
  $k_R(-u) = \overline{k_R(u)}$. Evenness of $\mu$ gives evenness of $\rho_R$.
  Tonelli's theorem gives
  \[
    \nu_R(B(y,r)) = c_d^2L\int |k_R(u)|\mu(B(y+u,r))\ud u.
  \]
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-spectral-growth} and $\|k_R\|_1 = L$ give
  \eqref{E:masked-auxiliary-growth}. Polynomial growth on centered balls proves
  temperedness by the same dyadic argument as above.

  Bochner Fourier integration gives
  \[
    \widehat{\zeta_RF}(\xi)
    = c_d\int k_R(\xi-\eta)\widehat{F}(\eta)\ud\eta.
  \]
  For scalar Schwartz functions this follows by Fourier inversion and absolute
  integrability. It follows next for finite-rank Hilbert-valued functions. For
  general $F$, take projections onto the successive spans of a countable
  orthonormal basis of its separable range closure. They converge pointwise in
  norm. The Schwartz bounds on $F$ dominate the Fourier integrals, proving the
  identity by dominated convergence. In a finite-dimensional space the full
  identity projection suffices; the zero space is immediate. The triangle
  inequality and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz yield
  \[
    \|\widehat{\zeta_RF}(\xi)\|_H^2
    \le c_d^2L\int |k_R(\xi-\eta)|\|\widehat{F}(\eta)\|_H^2\ud\eta.
  \]
  Integrating against $\mu$ and applying Tonelli's theorem proves
  \eqref{E:masked-hilbert-fourier}. Its right side is finite by Schwartz decay
  and temperedness of $\nu_R$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-auxiliary-norm}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Temporal completion and the original noise}

The noise in~\eqref{E:cov} is also correlated in time, with kernel
$|t-s|^{-a}$. We now complete the temporal tests in that form.

\LeanMarker{L:masked-time-completion}
\begin{lemma}[Temporal covariance completion]\label{L:masked-time-completion}
  Let $T > 0$. The form
  \[
    \langle f,g\rangle_a
    = \int_0^T\int_0^T f(t)g(s)|t-s|^{-a}\ud t\ud s
  \]
  is positive semidefinite on real compact smooth tests. Its null-quotient
  completion $H_a$ is separable. The inclusion of tests extends continuously
  from $L^2(0,T)$, with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-time-schur}
    \LeanEqMarker{E:masked-time-schur}
    \|f\|_a^2 \le \frac{2T^{1-a}}{1-a}\|f\|_2^2.
  \end{equation}
  In its complexification the norm is given by the same double integral with
  $f(t)\overline{f(s)}$. In particular, bounded measurable amplitudes on a
  subinterval, extended by zero, belong to this completion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Write $\Gamma_E$ for Euler's Gamma function. Away from the null diagonal,
  \[
    |t-s|^{-a} = \frac1{\Gamma_E(a)}\int_0^\infty
    \lambda^{a-1}e^{-\lambda|t-s|}\ud\lambda.
  \]
  This follows by substitution in the defining Gamma integral. Moreover,
  \[
    e^{-\lambda|t-s|}
    = \int_{-\infty}^{\min(t,s)}2\lambda
    e^{-\lambda(t-u)}e^{-\lambda(s-u)}\ud u.
  \]
  For each $\lambda > 0$ its quadratic form is an integral of a square. All
  exchanges for compact smooth tests are absolutely integrable because the
  kernel row integral is at most $2T^{1-a}/(1-a)$. The same bound and $2|xy| \le
  |x|^2+|y|^2$ prove~\eqref{E:masked-time-schur}. A countable $L^2$-dense family
  of compact smooth tests is consequently dense in $H_a$, proving separability.
  The complex quadratic form is the sum of those of the real and imaginary
  parts, since symmetry cancels the imaginary cross term. Thus, the same bound
  holds after complexification. Smooth density in $L^2$, together with the Schur
  bound on the absolute bilinear form, extends the inclusion and the integral
  identity to $L^2$. This includes the stated interval amplitudes. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-time-completion}.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal{D}$ consist of finite sums $F(t,x) = \sum_j\tau_j(t)f_j(x)$ with
real $\tau_j \in C_c^\infty(0,T)$ and real Schwartz $f_j$. These give
$H_a$-valued Schwartz functions of $x$. Let $W$ denote the original real
isonormal noise in~\eqref{E:cov}.

\LeanMarker{P:masked-noise-completion}
\begin{proposition}[Completion on the same noise]\label{P:masked-noise-completion}
  The space $\mathcal{D}$ belongs to the original test-noise completion and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-actual-noise-norm}
    \E W(F)^2 = (2\pi)^d\int
    \|\widehat{F}(\xi)\|_{H_a^{\mathbb{C}}}^2\mu(\ud\xi).
  \end{equation}
  Define the auxiliary seminorm
  \[
    N_R(F)^2 = (2\pi)^d\int
    \|\widehat{F}(\eta)\|_{H_a^{\mathbb{C}}}^2\nu_R(\ud\eta).
  \]
  Then $F \mapsto W(\zeta_RF)$ extends as a contraction from the null-quotient
  completion for $N_R$ into the original $L^2(\Omega)$. The extension is
  centered and jointly Gaussian. For finitely many real $F_1,\ldots,F_m \in
  \mathcal{D}$, their masked covariance matrix is bounded in positive
  semidefinite order by the matrix of auxiliary inner products. This gives
  covariance comparison for every finite linear Gaussian image and every convex
  norm power $p \ge 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For real spatial Schwartz $f,g$, put $h(z) = \int f(y+z)g(y)\ud y$. Fourier
  inversion and Parseval give
  \[
    h(z) = \int e^{i\xi\cdot z}\widehat{f}(\xi)
    \overline{\widehat{g}(\xi)}\ud\xi.
  \]
  Therefore,
  \[
    \int e^{i\xi\cdot z}h(z)\ud z
    = (2\pi)^d\widehat{f}(-\xi)\overline{\widehat{g}(-\xi)}.
  \]
  Apply~\eqref{E:masked-spectral-convention} to the single test $h$ and reflect
  $\xi$ using evenness of $\mu$. The spatial covariance is thus
  $(2\pi)^d\int\widehat{f}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi)}\mu(\ud\xi)$. Summing
  over the finitely many temporal factors gives
  \eqref{E:masked-actual-noise-norm}. Schwartz decay, temperedness and temporal
  integrability justify these pairings. If $W$ is initially given only on
  compact spatial tests, smooth expanding cutoffs converge in the Schwartz
  topology. The spectral form is continuous in that topology, admitting
  $\mathcal{D}$ in the same completion.

  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-auxiliary-norm}, applied with $H =
  H_a^{\mathbb{C}}$, gives $\E W(\zeta_RF)^2 \le N_R(F)^2$. It annihilates
  auxiliary-null tests. An auxiliary Cauchy sequence thus gives a Cauchy
  sequence of noise evaluations in $L^2(\Omega)$, proving the contraction
  extension and its uniqueness. Every finite linear combination of the limits is
  an $L^2$ limit of centered Gaussian variables. Their variances converge, so
  their characteristic functions prove that the limits remain jointly centered
  Gaussian.

  For the finite family let $A$ be the covariance matrix and $B$ the auxiliary
  Gram matrix. Applying the norm inequality to $\sum_i b_iF_i$ gives
  $b^{\mathsf{T}}Ab \le b^{\mathsf{T}}Bb$ for every real vector $b$.
  Diagonalizing $B-A$ constructs a centered Gaussian vector $Z$ of that
  covariance, independent of the evaluation vector $X$, on a product space. Then
  $X+Z$ has covariance $B$. Conditional Jensen gives $\E\|X\|^p \le \E\|X+Z\|^p$
  for every norm and $p \ge 1$. Finite Gaussian moments ensure finiteness of
  both sides. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:masked-noise-completion}.
\end{proof}

The auxiliary completion depends on $R$ and $\zeta$. Its construction does not
define multiplication by $\zeta_R$ on the original noise quotient. Nor does the
finite linear Gaussian comparison assert a covariance comparison for nonlinear
wave solutions. The auxiliary and original test norms need not dominate one
another. No spatial representative for every auxiliary class, nonnegative
physical inverse covariance for $\nu_R$, or uniform density bound in $R$ is
asserted.

\LeanMarker{P:masked-quotient-obstruction}
\begin{proposition}[A physical mask need not preserve noise-null tests]
  \label{P:masked-quotient-obstruction}
  In the admissible case $d = 2$, $\alpha = 1$, $0 < a < 1$ and $\Gamma =
  \delta_0(\ud x_1)\ud x_2$, there is a real compact smooth mask $\zeta$ such
  that no finite $C$ satisfies $\E W(\zeta F)^2 \le C\E W(F)^2$ for every
  compact smooth test $F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Fourier inversion on Schwartz tests gives $\mu =
  (2\pi)^{-1}\ud\xi_1\delta_0(\ud\xi_2)$ in the stated spectral convention. The
  measure $\Gamma$ is nonnegative, even, positive definite and tempered, with
  $\Gamma(cA) = c\Gamma(A)$. It therefore satisfies Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}
  with $\alpha = 1$. This is not spatial white noise in dimension two. For real
  compact smooth $g$, its spatial covariance is, by ordinary Fubini,
  \[
    V(g) = \int_\R\left(\int_\R g(x_1,x_2)\ud x_2\right)^2\ud x_1.
  \]
  Choose real even $\chi \in C_c^\infty(\R)$ equal to one on $[-1,1]$ and zero
  outside $[-2,2]$. For example, on $1 < r = |x| < 2$ use
  \[
    \chi(x) = \frac{e^{-1/(2-r)}}{e^{-1/(2-r)}+e^{-1/(r-1)}}.
  \]
  The endpoint exponentials vanish to every order, so the plateau pastes
  smoothly. Let $I_\chi = \int(\chi')^2 > 0$ and $J_\chi = \int\chi^4 > 0$.
  Positivity of $I_\chi$ follows because $\chi$ is not constant, and $J_\chi \ge
  2$. Put $g(x) = \chi(x_1)\chi''(x_2)$ and $\zeta(x) = \chi(x_1)\chi(x_2)$.
  Their unmasked marginal vanishes since $\int\chi'' = 0$, whereas integration
  by parts gives the masked marginal $-I_\chi\chi(x_1)^2$. Thus, $V(g) = 0$ and
  $V(\zeta g) = J_\chi I_\chi^2 > 0$. For a nonzero nonnegative $\tau \in
  C_c^\infty(0,T)$, set $F(t,x) = \tau(t)g(x)$. The temporal factor
  $\|\tau\|_a^2$ is finite and strictly positive because its nonnegative
  integrand is positive on a set of positive two-dimensional measure.
  Consequently, the full unmasked variance is zero and the masked variance is
  positive. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:masked-quotient-obstruction}.
\end{proof}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-22} records the quotient interpretation of this witness:
the mask separates two representatives of the same noise-null class.
This is why we mask tests before constructing the auxiliary completion.


\subsection{Temporal modulation and continuous wave factors}

Write $\langle x\rangle = \sqrt{1+x^2}$. Let $I = [s,s+h] \subset [0,T]$, with
$0 < h \le 1$. Norms below are those of the complexified temporal completion in
Lemma~\ref{L:masked-time-completion}.

\LeanMarker{L:masked-temporal-modulation}
\begin{lemma}[Modulated amplitudes]\label{L:masked-temporal-modulation}
  Let $A \in C^1(I;\mathbb{C})$, $M = \sup_I|A|$ and $D = \sup_I|A'|$. For every
  real $\omega$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-temporal-modulation}
    \|1_IA(t)e^{i\omega t}\|_a^2
    \le C_0(a)h^{2-a}(M+hD)^2\langle h\omega\rangle^{a-1},
    \qquad C_0(a) = \frac{16}{1-a}.
  \end{equation}
  The bound is uniform in $T,s,h,A$ and $\omega$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Put $c(v) = \int_s^{s+h-v}A(u+v)\overline{A(u)}\ud u$ for $0 \le v \le h$.
  Differentiation at the moving endpoint gives
  \[
    c'(v) = -A(s+h)\overline{A(s+h-v)}
    +\int_s^{s+h-v}A'(u+v)\overline{A(u)}\ud u.
  \]
  Thus, $c(h) = 0$, $|c(v)| \le hM^2$ and $|c'(v)| \le M^2+hMD$. Splitting the
  covariance square into its two conjugate triangles gives
  \[
    \|1_IA(t)e^{i\omega t}\|_a^2
    = 2\operatorname{Re}\int_0^h v^{-a}e^{i\omega v}c(v)\ud v
    = 2h^{2-a}\operatorname{Re}\int_0^1 y^{-a}e^{i\omega hy}f(y)\ud y,
  \]
  where $f(y) = c(hy)/h$. Set $K = M+hD$. Then $f(1) = 0$ and $|f|,|f'| \le
  K^2$. If $K = 0$ the assertion is immediate. If $|\omega h| \le 1$, the last
  integral is at most $K^2/(1-a)$ in absolute value, and $\langle\omega
  h\rangle^{a-1} \ge 2^{(a-1)/2}$. The claimed constant is sufficient in this
  case.

  Now suppose $k = |\omega h| > 1$. On $[0,1/k]$ the integral is at most
  $K^2k^{a-1}/(1-a)$. On $[1/k,1]$, integrate the exponential by parts. The
  endpoint at one vanishes, while the other endpoint contributes at most
  $K^2k^{a-1}$. The derivative integral, divided by $k$, is bounded by
  \[
    \frac{K^2}k\left(a\int_{1/k}^1 y^{-a-1}\ud y
    +\int_{1/k}^1 y^{-a}\ud y\right)
    \le K^2 k^{a-1}\left(1+\frac1{1-a}\right).
  \]
  Here $k^{-1} \le k^{a-1}$ since $a > 0$. The total is at most
  $4K^2k^{a-1}/(1-a)$. Finally, $k^{a-1} \le \sqrt{2}\langle\omega
  h\rangle^{a-1}$ and $8\sqrt{2} \le 16$. Including the factor $2h^{2-a}$ proves
  the bound for either sign of $\omega$. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-temporal-modulation}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:masked-temporal-modulation} treats one exponential mode. A
product of two sines or cosines is a sum of four such modes, which we bound
next.

\LeanMarker{L:masked-trig-products}
\begin{lemma}[Correlated trigonometric products]\label{L:masked-trig-products}
  Let $r,\rho \ge 0$. Let $P_r$ be either $\cos(rt)$ or $\sin(rt)$, and let
  $Q_\rho$ be either $\cos(\rho t)$ or $\sin(\rho t)$. With the amplitude and
  constant above,
  \begin{align}
    \|1_IAP_rQ_\rho\|_a^2
    &\le C_0(a)h^{2-a}(M+hD)^2\mathcal{Q}_h(r,\rho),
    \label{E:masked-trig-products}\\
    \mathcal{Q}_h(r,\rho)
    &:= \langle h(r-\rho)\rangle^{a-1}
    +\langle h(r+\rho)\rangle^{a-1}.
    \label{E:masked-frequency-pair}
  \end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Expanding the two trigonometric factors gives four complex exponential modes
  with frequencies $\sigma r+\tau\rho$, $\sigma,\tau \in \{-1,1\}$, and
  coefficients of absolute value $1/4$. In a Hilbert space,
  \[
    \left\|\sum_j c_jv_j\right\|^2
    \le \left(\sum_j|c_j|\right)\sum_j|c_j|\|v_j\|^2.
  \]
  The first coefficient sum is one. Apply
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-temporal-modulation} to each mode. Two frequencies have
  absolute value $|r-\rho|$ and two have absolute value $r+\rho$. This gives one
  half of the asserted right side, and hence the bound. Repeated or zero
  frequencies are permitted; no orthogonality or independence of the modes is
  used. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:masked-trig-products}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:masked-continuous-wave}
\begin{lemma}[Continuous sine factors]\label{L:masked-continuous-wave}
  Define $c_r(t) = \cos(rt)$ and
  \[
    b_r(t) = \langle r\rangle\frac{\sin(rt)}r\quad(r > 0),
    \qquad b_0(t) = t.
  \]
  For $F_r \in \{c_r,b_r\}$ and $G_\rho \in \{c_\rho,b_\rho\}$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-continuous-wave}
    \|1_IF_rG_\rho\|_a^2
    \le C_1(a)(1+T)^4h^{2-a}\mathcal{Q}_h(r,\rho),
    \qquad C_1(a) = 4\cdot3^{1-a}C_0(a).
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  For $r \ge 1$, $b_r$ is a sine times a coefficient at most $\sqrt{2}$. For $0
  \le r \le 1$, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
  \[
    b_r(t) = t\langle r\rangle\int_0^1\cos(vrt)\ud v.
  \]
  This includes $r = 0$. Hence, a product $F_rG_\rho$ is a scalar coefficient of
  absolute value at most two times a normalized integral of
  $t^jP_{r'}(t)Q_{\rho'}(t)$, where $j \in \{0,1,2\}$ and $|r'-r|,|\rho'-\rho|
  \le 1$. The integral is over zero, one or two copies of $[0,1]$; with no
  averages, use a one-point probability space. Its parameter dependence is
  continuous in $L^2(0,T)$ by dominated convergence.
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-time-completion} makes it continuous in $H_a$ as well.
  Thus, the averages are Bochner integrals, and their $H_a$ images agree with
  the pointwise averages by continuity of the inclusion. Hilbert Jensen bounds
  the squared norm of an average by the average of the squared norms.

  Apply Lemma~\ref{L:masked-trig-products} to each integrand with $A(t) = t^j$.
  For $j = 0,1,2$, respectively, $M+hD$ is at most $1$, $T+1$ and $T^2+2T$. In
  every case $(M+hD)^2 \le (1+T)^4$. For real $x,e$, the triangle inequality and
  its reverse imply
  \[
    \frac{\langle x\rangle}{1+|e|}
    \le \langle x+e\rangle \le (1+|e|)\langle x\rangle.
  \]
  The changes in $h(r\pm\rho)$ have magnitude at most two, since $h \le 1$.
  Because $a-1 < 0$, each altered bracket to the power $a-1$ is at most
  $3^{1-a}$ times the original one. Apply these comparisons, Hilbert Jensen and
  the squared scalar coefficient bound four to obtain
  \eqref{E:masked-continuous-wave}. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-continuous-wave}.
\end{proof}

These estimates concern covariance forms and temporal factors. Their application
to displacement and velocity is in Section~\ref{S:wave-variance}; the subsequent
operator, source and moment results require their separate proofs. They do not
follow from covariance domination alone.
