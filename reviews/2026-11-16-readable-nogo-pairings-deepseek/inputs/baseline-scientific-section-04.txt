\section{A small-order test for direct Brownian clock copying}
\label{S:two-clock-screen}

The result concerns a specified gap-transform substitution. It does not rule out
ordering corrections, a diagonal-only representation, one-sided/asymptotic use,
or the original sharp conjecture.

Theorem~6.1 of~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{pp.~37--42} represents
the time Laplace transform of a \emph{fixed-order} mean by a spatial Brownian
path and an independent scalar Brownian clock. The proof uses nonnegative cut
sums \(c_l\), obtained from one-chain time order on p.~38, before the
wave-to-heat substitution on p.~39. Remark~6.3, p.~42, singles out that order as
the obstruction for more copies. We test the direct replacement by two spatial
Brownian paths and two scalar Brownian clocks, without an additional ordering
weight.

All transforms below are at fixed total order. We begin with the original
admissible case \(d=\alpha=1\), \(\Gamma=\delta_0\), \(0<a<1\), and
\(G_s(y)=\frac12\mathbf1_{\{|y|<s\}}\). This is a falsification case for an
intended general representation, not a restriction of Theorem~\ref{T:intro-second-moment}.

\LeanMarker{L:white-clock-laplace}
\begin{lemma}[White-space low-order Laplace transforms]
  \label{L:white-clock-laplace}
  The \((1,1)\) cross-pair gap transform is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:white-cross-clock-transform}
    \begin{split}
      K_\times(\theta_1,\theta_2)
      &:=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
      e^{-\theta_1s-\theta_2t}|s-t|^{-a}
      \frac{\min(s,t)}2\ud s\ud t\\
      &=\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)}
      {2(\theta_1+\theta_2)^2}
      \big(\theta_1^{a-1}+\theta_2^{a-1}\big),
      \qquad \theta_1,\theta_2>0.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  In particular, \(K_\times(\theta,\theta)=
  \Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)/(4\theta^{3-a})\). For the empty-plus-length-two
  self-pair, independent gap damping gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:white-self-clock-transform}
    K_{\rm self}(\eta_1,\eta_2)
    :=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\eta_1r_1-\eta_2r_2}r_2^{-a}\frac{r_1}2
    \ud r_1\ud r_2
    =\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)}
    {2\eta_1^2\eta_2^{1-a}}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The spatial cross factor is
  \[
    \int G_s(y)G_t(z)\delta_0(y-z)\ud y\ud z
    =\frac14\int\mathbf1_{\{|y|<s\wedge t\}}\ud y
    =\frac{\min(s,t)}2.
  \]
  Split its time integral into \(s=t+v\) and \(t=s+v\), with \(v>0\). Each part
  is one half the product of
  \[
    \int_0^\infty t e^{-(\theta_1+\theta_2)t}\ud t
    =(\theta_1+\theta_2)^{-2}
  \]
  and \(\int_0^\infty e^{-\theta_i v}v^{-a}\ud v
  =\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)\theta_i^{a-1}\). All integrands are nonnegative and
  finite since \(a<1\), so Tonelli justifies the separation. This proves
  \eqref{E:white-cross-clock-transform}.

  For one length-two chain, its delta covariance forces the two positions equal.
  The second Green factor is \(G_{r_2}(0)=1/2\), and integration of the first
  gives mass \(r_1\). The time difference is \(r_2\). The two elementary Laplace
  integrals therefore give \eqref{E:white-self-clock-transform}. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:white-clock-laplace}.
\end{proof}

The source's analytic time covariance is
\[
  \gamma_a^{\rm an}(z)
  =\big(z\,\operatorname{sgn}\Re z\big)^{-a},\qquad \Re z\ne0,
\]
with the power taken on the right half-plane. For \(r>0,v\ge0\), put
\begin{equation}\label{E:averaged-clock-kernel}
  \Psi_a(r,v)=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
  \int_0^\infty \lambda^{a-1}e^{-r\lambda-v\lambda^2/2}\ud\lambda.
\end{equation}
From the source's complex Laplace identity
\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(6.3), p.~41}, and the Gaussian
characteristic function, \(\Psi_a(r,v)=\E(r+iZ)^{-a}\) when \(Z\) is centered
normal with variance \(v\). Fubini at fixed \(r>0\) is justified by
\(\int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}e^{-r\lambda}\ud\lambda<\infty\). In particular,
\(\Psi_a\) is nonnegative, decreases with \(v\), and \(\Psi_a(r,v)\le r^{-a}\).

Return to an arbitrary nonzero covariance in Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, with
temporal exponent \(a\), so \(0<a<1\), \(0<\alpha<2\) and \(a+\alpha<2\). Define
\[
  h_\Gamma(w)=\int e^{-w|\xi|^2/2}\mu(\ud\xi)
  =(p_w*\Gamma)(0),\qquad
  \mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)=\int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{q^2+|\xi|^2}.
\]
Both are positive and finite for \(w,q>0\). Spectral homogeneity gives
\(h_\Gamma(w)=w^{-\alpha/2}h_\Gamma(1)\) and
\(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)=q^{\alpha-2}\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)\). The cross-pair gap
transform at common damping is
\[
  K_\Gamma(\theta)=N_2(\theta)^2
\]
with temporal exponent \(a\) in~\eqref{E:damped-norm-definitions}. This is the
spatial-regularization limit of the \((1,1)\) diagram.

Let \(B_1,B_2\) be independent spatial standard Brownian motions. For auxiliary
standard Brownian clocks with \(\E[\beta_1(u)\beta_2(v)]=\rho\min(u,v)\),
\(-1\le\rho\le1\), independent of the spatial paths, the clock difference has
variance
\[
  v_\rho(u,v)=u+v-2\rho\min(u,v).
\]
At common damping \(\theta>0\), the direct unweighted, unrestricted two-clock
covariance substitution yields the averaged candidate
\begin{equation}\label{E:naive-clock-candidate}
  J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)
  :=\frac14\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
  e^{-\theta^2(u+v)/2}h_\Gamma(u+v)
  \Psi_a(\theta|u-v|,v_\rho(u,v))\ud u\ud v .
\end{equation}
Values on \(u=v\) do not affect the integral. The factor \(1/4\) is one factor
\(1/2\) for each wave-to-heat gap transform; \(h_\Gamma(u+v)\) is the spatial
average of \(\Gamma(B_1(u)-B_2(v))\). This average is interpreted by spatial
heat regularization. For the white covariance it is \((2\pi(u+v))^{-1/2}\). The
analytic covariance, not its absolute-value bound, is being copied.

\LeanMarker{P:clock-copy-obstruction}
\begin{proposition}[Obstruction to unweighted constant-correlation clock copying]
  \label{P:clock-copy-obstruction}
  For every covariance and parameters just specified, put
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cross-clock-lambda-weight}
    w_\theta(\lambda)
    =\frac{\lambda^{a-2}}
    {2\theta\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
    \big(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta)
    -\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda)\big),\qquad\lambda>0.
  \end{equation}
  This is a positive integrable density, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:clock-copy-obstruction}
    \begin{split}
      K_\Gamma(\theta)&=\int_0^\infty w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda,\\
      0<J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)
      &\le J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)
      =\int_0^\infty
      \frac{2\theta}{2\theta+\lambda}w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda
      <K_\Gamma(\theta).
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  When \(\alpha=1\),
  \begin{equation}\label{E:degree-one-clock-loss}
    J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)=(2-2^a)K_\Gamma(\theta).
  \end{equation}
  Thus, this direct analytic clock-copying family, including independent clocks
  and a single shared clock, fails to reproduce the cross-pair transform
  throughout the original nonzero covariance class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  First evaluate the cross transform. Temporarily smooth the spatial covariance,
  so Fourier and time Fubini are absolute. For \(r=|\xi|\), direct integration
  over \(s=t+v\) and \(t=s+v\) gives
  \[
    \int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\theta(s+t)-\lambda|s-t|}
    \widehat{G}_s(\xi)\overline{\widehat{G}_t(\xi)}\ud s\ud t
    =\frac{2\theta+\lambda}
    {2\theta(\theta^2+r^2)((\theta+\lambda)^2+r^2)} .
  \]
  For example, on the first cone the \(v\)-integral uses
  \[
    \int_0^\infty e^{-(\theta+\lambda)v}
    \sin(r(t+v))\ud v
    =\frac{(\theta+\lambda)\sin(rt)+r\cos(rt)}
    {(\theta+\lambda)^2+r^2}.
  \]
  Integrating the remaining sine-square and sine-cosine terms against
  \(e^{-2\theta t}\), and adding the second cone, gives the displayed identity.
  The value at \(r=0\) is its continuous limit.

  The resulting spectral integrand is nonnegative. Removing the spatial Gaussian
  multiplier and using the Laplace formula for the power covariance therefore
  gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      K_\Gamma(\theta)
      &=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}
      \frac{2\theta+\lambda}{2\theta}
      \int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}
      {(\theta^2+|\xi|^2)((\theta+\lambda)^2+|\xi|^2)}
      \ud\lambda\\
      &=\int_0^\infty w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda.
    \end{split}
  \]
  The second equality is partial fractions, with
  \((\theta+\lambda)^2-\theta^2=\lambda(2\theta+\lambda)\). The smoothing
  passage is justified by monotone convergence in this nonnegative spectral
  expression and the finite quadratic form in
  Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}. Alternatively, homogeneity directly
  bounds the density near zero by a constant times \(\lambda^{a-1}\), and at
  infinity by a constant times \(\lambda^{a-2}\). Its strict positivity follows
  from \(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta) -\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda)>0\),
  since \(\mu\ne0\).

  The candidate integrals are well defined and positive. The bound
  \(\Psi_a(\theta|u-v|,v_\rho)\le (\theta|u-v|)^{-a}\), together with
  \(h_\Gamma(w)=h_\Gamma(1)w^{-\alpha/2}\), supplies an absolute majorant. With
  \(w=u+v\), \(q=u-v\), the Jacobian is \(1/2\) and \(-w<q<w\). It is a constant
  times
  \[
    \int_0^\infty e^{-\theta^2w/2}w^{-\alpha/2}
    \int_{-w}^w|q|^{-a}\ud q\ud w
    =\frac2{1-a}\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\theta^2w/2}w^{1-\alpha/2-a}\ud w<\infty .
  \]
  Here \(a<1\) and \(a+\alpha/2<2\). The same bound dominates the spatial
  heat-regularized averages, whose spatial factor is \(h_\Gamma(w+2\epsilon)\le
  h_\Gamma(w)\). This is an averaged spectral bound, not pointwise covariance
  monotonicity. Since \(v_\rho(u,v)\ge|u-v|=v_1(u,v)\), monotonicity of
  \(\Psi_a\) gives \(J_{\Gamma,\rho}\le J_{\Gamma,1}\).

  We compute the latter by nonnegative Tonelli. Write
  \(A=\theta\lambda+\lambda^2/2\). Integration in \(q\) gives
  \(2(1-e^{-Aw})/A\). Also
  \[
    \mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)
    =\frac12\int_0^\infty e^{-q^2w/2}h_\Gamma(w)\ud w,
  \]
  by the elementary resolvent integral and spectral Tonelli. Consequently,
  \[
    \begin{split}
      J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)
      &=\frac1{4\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\frac{\lambda^{a-1}}A
      \int_0^\infty h_\Gamma(w)
      \left(e^{-\theta^2w/2}
      -e^{-(\theta+\lambda)^2w/2}\right)\ud w\ud\lambda\\
      &=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty
      \frac{\lambda^{a-2}
      (\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta)-\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda))}
      {2\theta+\lambda}\ud\lambda\\
      &=\int_0^\infty
      \frac{2\theta}{2\theta+\lambda}w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda.
    \end{split}
  \]
  The \(q\)-integral's factor two cancels its coordinate Jacobian \(1/2\); the
  resolvent identity supplies the other factor two. Since \(w_\theta>0\) and
  \(2\theta/(2\theta+\lambda)<1\) for every \(\lambda>0\), the strict inequality
  follows.

  Finally, if \(\alpha=1\), homogeneity gives
  \(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)=\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)/q\). Substitution in the two
  densities gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      K_\Gamma(\theta)
      &=\frac{\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)}
      {2\theta^2\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\frac{\lambda^{a-1}}{\theta+\lambda}\ud\lambda,\\
      J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)
      &=\frac{\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)}
      {\theta^2\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}
      \left(\frac1{\theta+\lambda}
      -\frac1{2\theta+\lambda}\right)\ud\lambda .
    \end{split}
  \]
  The real Beta identity
  \[
    \int_0^\infty\frac{x^{a-1}}{x+c}\ud x
    =c^{a-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a),
    \qquad c>0,
  \]
  follows by scaling and \((r,s)\mapsto(r+s,r/s)\) in the positive product of
  the two Gamma integrals. Their finiteness uses \(0<a<1\). Evaluation of these
  two integrals gives \(J_{\Gamma,1}/K_\Gamma=2(1-2^{a-1})=2-2^a\), proving
  \eqref{E:degree-one-clock-loss}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:clock-copy-obstruction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Normalization and scope of the obstruction]
  \label{R:clock-copy-scope}
  At the static endpoint define the time covariance directly as one. The
  candidate with \(\Psi_0=1\) gives
  \[
    J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)
    =\int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{(\theta^2+|\xi|^2)^2}
    =K_\Gamma(\theta).
  \]
  This follows by integrating \(w e^{-\theta^2w/2}h_\Gamma(w)/4\) spectrally. In
  the white case it equals \(1/(4\theta^3)\). Thus, static and
  homogeneous-degree checks alone miss the positive-\(a\) loss. Relative to the
  shared-clock value \(\rho=1\), the omitted cross-pair multiplier is exactly
  \(1+\lambda/(2\theta)\). For smaller correlations the deficit can be larger.
  This does not assert a correction rule at higher orders.

  The transforms \(K_\times\) use gap damping. For the auxiliary cutoff function
  \[
    F(T_1,T_2)=\int_0^{T_1}\int_0^{T_2}
    |s-t|^{-a}\frac{\min(s,t)}2\ud s\ud t,
  \]
  nonnegative Fubini adds \(1/(\theta_1\theta_2)\) to its double
  external-horizon transform. This function is not an unequal-time solution
  correlation. Reversing chains ending at different physical times instead
  shifts the cross covariance to \(|T_1-T_2-s+t|^{-a}\). Setting the two damping
  parameters equal is also not the single Laplace transform of a
  diagonal-horizon moment. For a self-pair at common gap damping, one external
  horizon instead adds \(1/\theta\), recovering the known first-mean
  coefficient.

  The proposition rules out exactly the analytic covariance substitution
  in~\eqref{E:naive-clock-candidate}, with product heat-chain measure,
  unrestricted operational-time rectangle and constant Brownian correlation.
  Additional relative-order weights,other derived clock couplings,matrix
  representations,modulus inequalities and their possible asymptotics are not
  covered. In particular, the inequality at this first cross pair leaves open a
  shared-clock lower comparison or asymptotic equivalence at higher orders. This
  finite clock calculation does not identify the intrinsic value proved  in
  Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment} with the source's all-moment
  conjecture. This is not a Skorohod result or a no-go for every two-clock
  method.
\end{remark}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-17} summarizes the missing degree-two multiplier.
No-go~\ref{P:nogo-18} records a different degree-four obstruction: erasing
an additional shift makes one upper envelope too large to close its
comparison, without refuting the comparison itself.
