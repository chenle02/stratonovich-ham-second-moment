\section{Compact mean families and the spectral gap for wave evolution}
\label{S:compact-mean-family}

Let $I = [0,1]$, let $K_x \subset \R^d$ be compact, and let $\sigma\mapsto
V_\sigma$ be continuous from $I$ into $C_c^\infty(K_x)$, real and nonnegative.
The index $\sigma$ is a family parameter, distinct from physical propagation
time $t$. Assume that one real $w \in H^1(\R^d)$ with $\|w\|_2 = 1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{E:compact-common-trial}
  \|\nabla w\|_2^2-\int V_\sigma w^2\le-\varepsilon
  \quad\hbox{for all }\sigma\in I,\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\end{equation}
Put $M = \sup_I\|V_\sigma\|_\infty$ and $H_\sigma = -\Delta-V_\sigma$. The
phases $E,F$, inclusion $i$, data $e_0$ and observable $O_\phi$ are exactly
those of Section~\ref{S:constant-phase-young}.
Proposition~\ref{P:constant-compact-base}, with deterministic coefficient $h =
V_\sigma$ and noise amplitude zero, gives the ordinary physical flow
$P_\sigma(t)$ on $F$. Its $E$ restriction is $P_\sigma^E(t)$. Uniqueness of the
Duhamel solution makes finite-horizon constructions agree on overlaps; integer
horizons and autonomy give the same semigroup for every $t \ge 0$.

We import the following statements from the \emph{second edition}
of~\cite{teschl:14:mathematical}: Theorem~10.2, p.~258, gives the self-adjoint
$H^2$ operator, lower bound, essential spectrum $[0,\infty)$ and compact smooth
core for real compact smooth potentials in the dimensions considered here.
Theorem~10.13, p.~273, gives a simple positive ground state \emph{if the
spectral bottom is an eigenvalue}. Theorem~4.12 and Corollary~4.13,
pp.~140--141, give max--min and form monotonicity, with the essential threshold
used after discrete eigenvalues end and with the quadratic-form domain allowed.
Uniformity, fractional norms and all applications to $F$ are proved below.

\subsection{The ground family and the global phase}

\begin{proposition}[Ground states and a uniform rate gap]
  \label{P:compact-ground-family}
  The common form domain of $H_\sigma$ is $H^1$ with form $q_\sigma(u) =
  \|\nabla u\|_2^2-\int V_\sigma|u|^2$. Its bottom $E_1(\sigma)$ is a simple
  eigenvalue with a unique real positive-a.e. normalized ground state
  $p_\sigma$. Let $E_2(\sigma)$ be the second max--min level, set to zero when
  there is no second negative eigenvalue. Then
  \begin{align}
    -M&\le E_1(\sigma)\le-\varepsilon,\qquad
    E_1(\sigma)<E_2(\sigma)\le0,\label{E:compact-energy-levels}\\
    |E_j(\sigma)-E_j(\tau)|
    &\le\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty,\qquad j=1,2.
    \label{E:compact-level-continuity}
  \end{align}
  The map $\sigma\mapsto p_\sigma$ is continuous into $H^2$ and bounded there.
  With $\lambda_\sigma = \sqrt{-E_1(\sigma)}$ and $\mu_\sigma =
  \sqrt{-E_2(\sigma)}$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-rate-gap}
    \sqrt\varepsilon\le\lambda_\sigma\le\sqrt M,\qquad
    d_0:=\inf_I(\lambda_\sigma-\mu_\sigma)>0.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The imported Theorem~10.2 applies to $-V_\sigma$, which is real, compactly
  supported and in $L^2$ for $d \le 3$. It gives the stated self-adjoint
  operator on $H^2$ and essential threshold zero. Write $c = 1+M$ and $A_\sigma
  = H_\sigma+c \ge 1$. On $H^2$, integration by parts and the spectral theorem
  give
  \[
    \|A_\sigma^{1/2}u\|_2^2
    =q_\sigma(u)+c\|u\|_2^2,\qquad
    \|u\|_{H^1}^2\le\|A_\sigma^{1/2}u\|_2^2
    \le c\|u\|_{H^1}^2.
  \]
  Spectral cutoffs $1_{[1,n]}(A_\sigma)u$ show that $H^2$ is a core for the
  half-power form norm. Fourier cutoffs show it is dense in $H^1$. The two
  equivalent completions are therefore $D(A_\sigma^{1/2})$ and $H^1$. Both norms
  dominate $L^2$ and give the same $L^2$ limit, so these are equal as subspaces,
  with the displayed form. This verifies the form domain used below.

  The form lower bound and~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial} give $-M \le E_1 \le
  -\varepsilon$ by the imported form max--min principle. The bottom belongs to
  the spectrum and lies below the essential threshold, so it is an isolated
  eigenvalue of finite multiplicity. Only now apply the imported Theorem~10.13:
  it is simple with a positive ground state. Conjugation preserves the real
  operator; positivity and unit $L^2$ norm fix its real representative.

  Simplicity, isolation and threshold zero give $E_1 < E_2 \le 0$. For every
  common unit form test, $|q_\sigma(u)-q_\tau(u)| \le
  \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty$. Taking the same infima over orthogonal
  complements and suprema in the source max--min formula proves
  \eqref{E:compact-level-continuity}, including passage of $E_2$ to the
  threshold. Compactness gives $\Delta_*: = \min_I(E_2-E_1) > 0$. Thus,
  \[
    \lambda_\sigma-\mu_\sigma
    =\frac{E_2(\sigma)-E_1(\sigma)}
    {\lambda_\sigma+\mu_\sigma}
    \ge\frac{\Delta_*}{2\sqrt M}>0.
  \]
  Here $M \ge \varepsilon > 0$, and no division by $\mu_\sigma$ occurs.

  Put $d_V = \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty$ and decompose $p_\tau = a p_\sigma+r$,
  with $r\perp p_\sigma$ and $0 < a \le 1$. The variational bound gives
  $q_\sigma(p_\tau) \le E_1(\sigma)+2d_V$, whereas the complementary spectral
  bound gives $q_\sigma(p_\tau) \ge E_1(\sigma)+\Delta_*\|r\|_2^2$. Hence,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-ground-continuity}
    \|p_\tau-p_\sigma\|_2^2
    =2(1-a)\le2(1-a^2)\le4d_V/\Delta_*.
  \end{equation}
  The eigenfunction equation is
  \[
    (1-\Delta)p_\sigma
    =(1-\lambda_\sigma^2+V_\sigma)p_\sigma.
  \]
  Its right side is continuous in $L^2$ and bounded there by $1+2M$. The inverse
  of $1-\Delta$ is an isometry from $L^2$ to the Bessel $H^2$ space, proving the
  asserted $H^2$ continuity and bound. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-ground-family}.
\end{proof}

The rate gap~\eqref{E:compact-rate-gap} is a statement about the spectrum of
$H_\sigma$. The wave on $E$ is measured in the fixed norm of $E$, so we first
compare the two.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform fractional phase norms]\label{L:compact-fractional-phase}
  Let $B = 1-\Delta$, $c = 1+M$ and $A_\sigma = H_\sigma+c$. The quarter-power
  domain is $H^{1/2}$ and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-quarter-power}
    \|B^{1/4}u\|_2\le\|A_\sigma^{1/4}u\|_2
    \le c^{1/4}\|B^{1/4}u\|_2.
  \end{equation}
  The norm
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-spectral-phase-norm}
    \|(u,z)\|_{E_\sigma}^2
    =\|A_\sigma^{1/4}u\|_2^2
    +\|A_\sigma^{-1/4}z\|_2^2
  \end{equation}
  is uniformly equivalent to the fixed norm on $E = H^{1/2}\times H^{-1/2}$,
  interpreting the negative entry by completion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  On the common form domain $H^1$, $B \le A_\sigma \le cB$ and all three
  operators are at least one. For positive self-adjoint $T,S$ with common form
  domain, $T \le S$ implies $(T+t)^{-1} \ge (S+t)^{-1}$ for $t > 0$. Indeed,
  \[
    \langle f,(T+t)^{-1}f\rangle
    =\sup_u\{2\operatorname{Re}\langle f,u\rangle
    -q_T(u)-t\|u\|_2^2\};
  \]
  completing the square in the closed form norm proves the identity, and
  comparison of the suprema proves the order. Consequently, $I-t(T+t)^{-1} \le
  I-t(S+t)^{-1}$. The scalar identity
  \[
    \sqrt x=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty
    t^{-1/2}\frac{x}{x+t}\ud t
  \]
  follows by $t = xr^2$ and $\int_0^\infty(1+r^2)^{-1}\ud r = \pi/2$. Spectral
  integration and positive Tonelli, first over finite intervals and then by
  monotone convergence, give $T^{1/2} \le S^{1/2}$ as forms, including domain
  inclusions. Apply this to $B \le A_\sigma \le cB$ to obtain
  \eqref{E:compact-quarter-power} and equality of the quarter-power domains with
  $H^{1/2}$.

  Under the $L^2$ pairing the dual norm of $\|A_\sigma^{1/4}u\|_2$ at $z \in
  L^2$ is $\|A_\sigma^{-1/4}z\|_2$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives the upper bound, and a
  scalar multiple of $A_\sigma^{-1/2}z$ attains it. Completion
  and~\eqref{E:compact-quarter-power} identify this negative space with
  $H^{-1/2}$, uniformly in $\sigma$. Adding the squared norms proves the claim.
  This proves Lemma~\ref{L:compact-fractional-phase}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The complementary wave on $E$]\label{P:compact-E-wave}
  Put
  \[
    e_\sigma^E=(p_\sigma,\lambda_\sigma p_\sigma),\qquad
    \ell_\sigma^E(u,z)
    =\frac{\langle p_\sigma,u\rangle+
    \langle p_\sigma,z\rangle/\lambda_\sigma}{2},
    \qquad \Pi_\sigma^E=e_\sigma^E\ell_\sigma^E.
  \]
  These are bounded real objects, continuous in their respective norms, with
  $\ell_\sigma^E(e_\sigma^E) = 1$, and
  \begin{align}
    P_\sigma^E(t)e_\sigma^E
    &=e^{\lambda_\sigma t}e_\sigma^E,\qquad
    \ell_\sigma^EP_\sigma^E(t)
    =e^{\lambda_\sigma t}\ell_\sigma^E,\label{E:compact-E-eigen}\\
    \|P_\sigma^E(t)(I-\Pi_\sigma^E)\|_{\mathcal{B}(E)}
    &\le C(1+t)e^{\mu_\sigma t},\qquad t\ge0.
    \label{E:compact-E-complement}
  \end{align}
  The constant is uniform over $\sigma \in I$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Define real spectral functions $C_t(h),S_t(h)$ by $\cos(t\sqrt h),\sin(t\sqrt
  h)/\sqrt h$ for $h > 0$, by $1,t$ at zero, and by
  $\cosh(t\sqrt{-h}),\sinh(t\sqrt{-h})/\sqrt{-h}$ for $h < 0$. The spectral wave
  matrix is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-spectral-wave}
    \begin{pmatrix}
      C_t(H_\sigma)&S_t(H_\sigma)\\
      -H_\sigma S_t(H_\sigma)&C_t(H_\sigma)
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \end{equation}
  Real potentials, their resolvents and spectral resolutions commute with
  conjugation. All these real functions and positive $A_\sigma$ powers preserve
  the real phases.

  The spatial projection $Q_\sigma^{\mathrm{sp}} = I-|p_\sigma\rangle\langle
  p_\sigma|$ commutes with $H_\sigma,A_\sigma$ and is contractive on both
  entries of~\eqref{E:compact-spectral-phase-norm}. On its support $h \ge E_2 =
  -\mu_\sigma^2$, the phase isometry gives scalar matrix entries
  \[
    C_t(h),\quad \sqrt{h+c}\,S_t(h),\quad
    -hS_t(h)/\sqrt{h+c},\quad C_t(h).
  \]
  For $h \ge 0$, use $|S_t(h)| \le \min(t,h^{-1/2})$ and split $h \le 1$ from $h
  \ge 1$; the lower off-diagonal entry is at most one. For $-\mu_\sigma^2 \le h
  < 0$, use $|C_t(h)| \le e^{\mu_\sigma t}$ and $|S_t(h)| \le t e^{\mu_\sigma
  t}$, with $|h| \le M$ and $1 \le h+c \le c$. Thus, the complementary spatial
  matrix has norm at most $C(1+t)e^{\mu_\sigma t}$, including $\mu_\sigma = 0$.

  The two spatial ground branches are $(p_\sigma,\pm\lambda_\sigma p_\sigma)$.
  Besides $\Pi_\sigma^E$, their projections include the decaying branch
  \[
    (p_\sigma,-\lambda_\sigma p_\sigma)
    \frac{\langle p_\sigma,u\rangle
    -\langle p_\sigma,z\rangle/\lambda_\sigma}{2}.
  \]
  Their sum is the spatial ground projection on both phase entries. Both
  projection norms are uniformly bounded in the spectral phase norm because
  $\sqrt\varepsilon \le \lambda_\sigma \le \sqrt M$ and $1 \le
  c-\lambda_\sigma^2 \le c$. The decaying branch has factor $e^{-\lambda_\sigma
  t}$ and remains in $I-\Pi_\sigma^E$. The fractional norm equivalence now
  proves the stated complement estimate for the spectral matrix.

  We identify this matrix with the physical flow. The same scalar bounds at
  graph levels $(A_\sigma u,A_\sigma^{1/2}z)$ give continuous solutions in
  $H^2\times H^1$. These graph norms are uniformly equivalent to the Sobolev
  norms: the $H^1$ case was proved above, and $A_\sigma-B = M-V_\sigma$ is
  bounded by $M$ while $A_\sigma,B \ge 1$. Spectral cutoffs give $u_t = z$ in
  $H^1$ and $z_t = -H_\sigma u$ in $L^2$, with continuous derivatives there. The
  free Fourier group on $H^1\times L^2$ has, after its Bessel isometry, the same
  bounded weighted entries and is strongly continuous by dominated Fourier
  convergence. Its generator is $(z,\Delta u)$ on $H^2\times H^1$, as the
  Fourier multipliers show. For compact smooth initial data, differentiating the
  free interaction picture at these graph levels gives Duhamel with force
  $(0,V_\sigma u)$. The identity also holds in $E$. Multiplication by $V_\sigma$
  maps $H^{1/2}$ into $L^2$ and then $H^{-1/2}$ with bound $M$, so Picard
  uniqueness identifies the spectral solution with the ordinary $E$ flow on
  these data. Density within $E$ and finite-horizon boundedness extend equality
  to all $E$. No density of $E$ in $F$ is used. Finally, the ground branches
  give~\eqref{E:compact-E-eigen}; their pairings are continuous because
  $p_\sigma \in H^2$. Continuity and uniform bounds of the displayed vectors and
  duals follow from Proposition~\ref{P:compact-ground-family}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-E-wave}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The growing projection and its complementary wave evolution}

For the constant coordinates write $C(t)(c_1,c_2) = (c_1+tc_2,c_2)$ and
$D_\sigma(c_1,c_2) = (0,V_\sigma c_1) \in E$.
The data $e_0 = (0,1,0)$ lie entirely in these constant coordinates, outside
$iE$. So we follow the flow on all of $F$, where $D_\sigma$ couples the
constants to $E$.

\begin{proposition}[The growing projection on the wave phase space]
  \label{P:compact-F-projection}
  The physical flow satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-F-block}
    P_\sigma(t)(e,c)
    =\left(P_\sigma^E(t)e+
    \int_0^tP_\sigma^E(t-r)D_\sigma C(r)c\ud r,\ C(t)c\right).
  \end{equation}
  Set
  \begin{align}
    f_\sigma&=\frac{\int V_\sigma p_\sigma}{2\lambda_\sigma}>0,
    &\ell_\sigma^C(c)&=
    f_\sigma\left(\frac{c_1}{\lambda_\sigma}
    +\frac{c_2}{\lambda_\sigma^2}\right),
    \label{E:compact-constant-dual}\\
    e_\sigma&=ie_\sigma^E,
    &\ell_\sigma(e,c)&=\ell_\sigma^E(e)+\ell_\sigma^C(c),\qquad
    \Pi_\sigma=e_\sigma\ell_\sigma,\quad Q_\sigma=I-\Pi_\sigma.
    \label{E:compact-F-projection}
  \end{align}
  These vectors, duals and rank-one projections are norm-continuous and
  uniformly bounded, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-F-eigen}
    \ell_\sigma(e_\sigma)=1,\qquad
    P_\sigma(t)\Pi_\sigma=\Pi_\sigma P_\sigma(t)
    =e^{\lambda_\sigma t}\Pi_\sigma.
  \end{equation}
  For every fixed nonnegative smooth compact $\phi$ of integral one,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-positive-margins}
    \inf_I\ell_\sigma(e_0)>0,\qquad
    \inf_I O_\phi(e_\sigma)>0.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The constant coordinates obey $C(t)$ because the potential force has range in
  $iE$. To justify variation of constants on the $E$ component, let $g$ be a
  continuous $E$ force and substitute the ordinary free-Duhamel equation for
  $P_\sigma^E(t-r)g(r)$ in its time integral. With $B_\sigma^E(u,z) =
  (0,V_\sigma u)$, strong $E$-valued Fubini gives
  \begin{align*}
    &\int_0^t\int_r^t S(t-s)B_\sigma^E
    P_\sigma^E(s-r)g(r)\ud s\ud r\\
    &\qquad=\int_0^tS(t-s)B_\sigma^E
    \left[\int_0^sP_\sigma^E(s-r)g(r)\ud r\right]\ud s.
  \end{align*}
  All norms are bounded on the finite triangle, so this exchange is absolute in
  $E$. Thus, $Y(t) = P_\sigma^E(t)e+\int_0^tP_\sigma^E(t-r)g(r)\ud r$ solves
  free Duhamel with force $B_\sigma^EY+g$. Take $g(r) = D_\sigma C(r)c$ and
  adjoin the constant block. This is the same $F$ Duhamel equation as the
  ordinary flow; uniqueness proves~\eqref{E:compact-F-block}. Its finite-horizon
  bounds are uniform since the family has bounded $L^\infty$ and $L^2$ norms on
  one compact support.

  The integral defining $f_\sigma$ is finite by $L^2$ Cauchy--Schwarz and
  positive because $V_\sigma$ is nonzero by the common trial and $p_\sigma > 0$
  a.e. Its coefficients are continuous and uniformly bounded by the $H^2$
  continuity and positive lower bound for the rate; no $p_\sigma \in L^1$
  assumption is used. Directly,
  \begin{align*}
    \ell_\sigma^ED_\sigma C(r)c&=f_\sigma(c_1+rc_2),\\
    \ell_\sigma^C(c)
    &=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda_\sigma r}
    f_\sigma(c_1+rc_2)\ud r,\\
    \ell_\sigma^C(C(t)c)
    &=\int_t^\infty e^{\lambda_\sigma(t-r)}
    f_\sigma(c_1+rc_2)\ud r.
  \end{align*}
  These integrals converge absolutely. Applying $\ell_\sigma$
  to~\eqref{E:compact-F-block}, the finite integral and this tail combine to
  give $\ell_\sigma P_\sigma(t) = e^{\lambda_\sigma t}\ell_\sigma$. The ground
  mode also gives $P_\sigma(t)e_\sigma = e^{\lambda_\sigma t}e_\sigma$ and
  $\ell_\sigma(e_\sigma) = 1$, proving~\eqref{E:compact-F-eigen}. Bounded
  bilinear multiplication and the preceding continuous coefficients prove norm
  continuity of all objects. Finally, $\ell_\sigma(e_0) =
  f_\sigma/\lambda_\sigma > 0$ and $O_\phi(e_\sigma) = \int\phi p_\sigma > 0$.
  Both are continuous on compact $I$, proving
  \eqref{E:compact-positive-margins}. Positivity is in integrals, without a
  point trace or a one-dimensional ODE. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-F-projection}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{P:compact-F-projection} isolates the growing part
$\Pi_\sigma$. It remains to bound the flow on the complement $Q_\sigma$. The
proof of Lemma~\ref{L:raw-block-indexed-cone} uses the normalized form of this
bound.

\begin{proposition}[Complementary wave evolution and parameter dependence]
  \label{P:compact-F-complement}
  Uniformly over $\sigma$ and $t \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-F-complement}
    \|P_\sigma(t)Q_\sigma\|_{\mathcal{B}(F)}
    \le C(1+t)^3e^{\mu_\sigma t}.
  \end{equation}
  For every fixed $0 < \eta < d_0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-normalized-complement}
    \|e^{-\lambda_\sigma t}P_\sigma(t)Q_\sigma\|
    \le C_\eta e^{-\eta t}.
  \end{equation}
  For every finite $T_0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-parameter-flow}
    \sup_{0\le t\le T_0}\|P_\sigma(t)-P_\tau(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(F)}
    \le C_{T_0}
    \big(\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty+\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_2\big).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Write $Q_\sigma^E = I-\Pi_\sigma^E$. Split $D_\sigma$ into its growing and
  complementary parts in~\eqref{E:compact-F-block} and use the two tail
  integrals in the preceding proof. The exact $E$ component of
  $P_\sigma(t)Q_\sigma(e,c)$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-complement-forcing}
    P_\sigma^E(t)Q_\sigma^Ee
    +\int_0^tP_\sigma^E(t-r)Q_\sigma^ED_\sigma C(r)c\ud r
    -e_\sigma^E\ell_\sigma^C(C(t)c),
  \end{equation}
  and the constant component is $C(t)c$. The final subtraction retains the
  growing response to constants in the projection. Since $\|Q_\sigma^ED_\sigma
  C(r)\| \le C(1+r)$, the integral in~\eqref{E:compact-complement-forcing} has
  norm at most
  \[
    Ce^{\mu_\sigma t}|c|
    \int_0^t(1+t-r)(1+r)\ud r
    =Ce^{\mu_\sigma t}(t+t^2+t^3/6)|c|.
  \]
  The first term is bounded by $C(1+t)e^{\mu_\sigma t}\|e\|_E$; the final term
  and constant component by $C(1+t)|c|$. Since $\mu_\sigma \ge 0$ this proves
  \eqref{E:compact-F-complement}. The boundedness of $(1+t)^3e^{-(d_0-\eta)t}$
  proves \eqref{E:compact-normalized-complement}. In particular, the
  constant-velocity polynomial is included in this estimate.

  Put $\mathcal{B}_\sigma = iB_F(V_\sigma)$ and $d_{\sigma,\tau} =
  \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty+ \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_2$. The bound for $B_F$ gives
  $\|\mathcal{B}_\sigma-\mathcal{B}_\tau\| \le d_{\sigma,\tau}$. Subtract the
  two free-Duhamel equations on states. Their difference is the sum of the
  integral with $S_F(t-r)\mathcal{B}_\sigma(P_\sigma(r)-P_\tau(r))$ and that
  with $S_F(t-r)(\mathcal{B}_\sigma-\mathcal{B}_\tau)P_\tau(r)$. The uniform
  finite-horizon bounds and scalar Gronwall prove
  \eqref{E:compact-parameter-flow}. The operator norm is measurable:
  separability of $F$ expresses it as the supremum over a countable dense set of
  unit states, each with continuous image. Hence, Gronwall applies without a
  $\mathcal{B}(F)$-valued derivative or operator-norm continuity of the free
  group. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:compact-F-complement}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The original nonnegative control and a common trial}

So far $V_\sigma$ was any continuous family
satisfying~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial}. We now produce such a family from a
nonnegative test $\Psi$ and the original covariance $\Gamma$, in two steps.

\begin{lemma}[The original mean and variance]\label{L:compact-raw-profile}
  Let $\Gamma \ne 0$ satisfy Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, let $a = \alpha_0$, and
  let real nonnegative nonzero $\Psi \in C_c^\infty((0,1)\times\R^d)$ be
  arbitrary, without a separability assumption. Define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-raw-mean}
    h_\sigma(x)=\int_0^1|\sigma-u|^{-a}
    (\Gamma*\Psi(u,\cdot))(x)\ud u,\qquad
    \overline{q}(x)=\int_0^1\Psi(u,x)\ud u,\qquad Z=W(\Psi).
  \end{equation}
  Then $h$ is smooth in time and space on compact sets, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-raw-cost}
    0<v_\Psi:=\E Z^2
    =\int_0^1\int\Psi(\sigma,x)h_\sigma(x)\ud x\ud\sigma<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  Against every smooth compact positive-time test $F_0$,
  \[
    \E[W(\Psi)W(F_0)]=\int F_0(s,x)h_s(x)\ud x\ud s.
  \]
  Moreover, on $I$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-positive-mean-floor}
    h_\sigma\ge\Gamma*\overline{q},\qquad
    v_\Psi\ge Q(\overline{q})
    :=\int\Gamma(\ud z)\int\overline{q}(x)\overline{q}(x-z)\ud x>0.
  \end{equation}
  For every fixed mask, $V_\sigma = m_Rh_\sigma$ is a smooth compact family,
  Lipschitz in $\sigma$ in $L^\infty$ and $L^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Choose $A \ge 1$ containing the spatial support of $\Psi$ in $B_A$. The
  original covariance identity and absolute Fubini give the stated mean pairing:
  the two compact tests restrict spatial differences to a compact set of finite
  $\Gamma$ mass, and the time singularity is locally integrable since $a < 1$.
  This identifies the linear covariance mean without evaluating noise at a
  point. The same calculation gives~\eqref{E:compact-raw-cost}. Its spatial
  integrand is bounded in integral by
  $\|\Psi\|_\infty^2|B_A|\Gamma(\overline{B}_{2A})$, and
  $\int_0^1\int_0^1|\sigma-u|^{-a}\ud u\ud\sigma = 2/((1-a)(2-a))$, proving
  finiteness.

  Nonnegative nonzero smooth $\Psi$ has positive integral, so $\overline{q} \ne
  0$. Nonzero $\Gamma$ charges some ball, and homogeneity gives $\Gamma(B_1) =
  r^{\alpha-d}\Gamma(B_r) > 0$. Thus, every origin-centered ball $B_r$, $r > 0$,
  has positive finite mass. The continuous compact autocorrelation
  $\int\overline{q}(x)\overline{q}(x-z)\ud x$ is positive at zero and bounded
  below on some ball. This proves $0 < Q(\overline{q}) < \infty$. Since
  $|\sigma-u|^{-a} \ge 1$ on the unit time square, positive Tonelli gives both
  inequalities in~\eqref{E:compact-positive-mean-floor}.

  Extend $\Psi$ by zero in time and write
  \[
    h_s(x)=\int_\R |r|^{-a}
    \int\Gamma(\ud z)\Psi(s-r,x-z)\ud r.
  \]
  On compact $s,x$ sets the relevant $r,z$ range over fixed compact sets;
  $|r|^{-a}$ is integrable there. Every derivative falls on the smooth test, not
  on the singular time kernel. Dominated differentiation gives smoothness and
  continuity of all derivatives on compacts. Multiplication by $m_R$ now gives
  the claimed compact family and its fixed-$R$ Lipschitz bounds. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:compact-raw-profile}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:compact-raw-profile} gives a smooth compact family for every
fixed mask, but not yet the common negative
trial~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial}.

\begin{proposition}[Binding with the original control and cost fixed]
  \label{P:compact-original-common-trial}
  For every $\Psi$ in Lemma~\ref{L:compact-raw-profile}, a sufficiently large
  finite mask $m_R$ gives $V_\sigma = m_Rh_\sigma$ a common negative
  trial~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial}. Thus, all preceding ground, projection,
  positive-margin and complementary-wave conclusions apply. The rate is
  Lipschitz on $I$ and has $\lambda_0 > 0$. The original test $\Psi$, original
  variable $Z$ and variance $v_\Psi$ are unchanged by this choice. The mask can
  additionally cover any fixed compact variational test supports.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Choose a real unit $L^2$ function $\psi \in C_c^\infty(B_2)$ with $\psi \ge
  c_0 > 0$ on $B_1$, and put $\psi_L(x) = L^{-d/2}\psi(x/L)$, $D_\psi =
  \|\nabla\psi\|_2^2$, where $L \ge \max(1,2A)$. Choose the even mask with $R
  \ge 2L$, so $V_\sigma = h_\sigma$ on $\operatorname{supp}\psi_L$. This is the
  allowed real mask $0 \le m_R \le 1$, equal to one on $B_R$ and with support in
  $\overline{B}_{2R}$. Positive Tonelli
  and~\eqref{E:compact-positive-mean-floor} give
  \begin{align}
    \int V_\sigma\psi_L^2
    &\ge c_0^2L^{-d}\int_{B_L}(\Gamma*\overline{q})(x)\ud x\nonumber\\
    &=c_0^2L^{-d}\int\overline{q}(y)\Gamma(B_L-y)\ud y\nonumber\\
    &\ge c_0^2L^{-d}\Gamma(B_{L/2})\int\overline{q}
    =C_\Gamma L^{-\alpha},\label{E:compact-trial-potential}\\
    C_\Gamma&=c_0^22^{\alpha-d}\Gamma(B_1)\int\overline{q}>0.\nonumber
  \end{align}
  Here $B_{L/2} \subset B_L-y$ for $|y| \le A$ and $L \ge 2A$. All covariance
  masses in the argument are compact and finite. The kinetic energy is $D_\psi
  L^{-2}$. Since $\alpha < 2$, choose $L$ large enough that $\varepsilon_R: =
  C_\Gamma L^{-\alpha}-D_\psi L^{-2} > 0$. Then
  \[
    \|\nabla\psi_L\|_2^2-\int V_\sigma\psi_L^2
    \le-\varepsilon_R\quad\hbox{for every }\sigma\in I.
  \]
  Fix this finite $R$ before invoking Proposition~\ref{P:compact-ground-family}.
  Its level bound and the mean Lipschitz estimate give
  \[
    |\lambda_\sigma-\lambda_\tau|
    \le\frac{\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon_R}}
    \le C_R|\sigma-\tau|.
  \]
  The positive mean integrates the entire time test, so $\lambda_0 > 0$ even
  though $\Psi(0,\cdot) = 0$. Only the trial and mask have grown; the original
  test, its variable and~\eqref{E:compact-raw-cost} were fixed first. Enlarging
  $R$ to contain additional fixed compact test supports preserves the trial and
  gives equality of masked and unmasked potential energies on those supports.
  This includes singular invariant-direction covariances and the one-dimensional
  white case. It is not an all-strength binding assertion for one fixed compact
  potential. Masks are fixed before source or time asymptotics; a later supremum
  over controls must follow their individual bounds. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-original-common-trial}.
\end{proof}

\section{Compact mean families and the spectral gap for wave evolution}
\label{S:compact-mean-family}

Let $I = [0,1]$, let $K_x \subset \R^d$ be compact, and let $\sigma\mapsto
V_\sigma$ be continuous from $I$ into $C_c^\infty(K_x)$, real and nonnegative.
The index $\sigma$ is a family parameter, distinct from physical propagation
time $t$. Assume that one real $w \in H^1(\R^d)$ with $\|w\|_2 = 1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{E:compact-common-trial}
  \|\nabla w\|_2^2-\int V_\sigma w^2\le-\varepsilon
  \quad\hbox{for all }\sigma\in I,\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\end{equation}
Put $M = \sup_I\|V_\sigma\|_\infty$ and $H_\sigma = -\Delta-V_\sigma$. The
phases $E,F$, inclusion $i$, data $e_0$ and observable $O_\phi$ are exactly
those of Section~\ref{S:constant-phase-young}.
Proposition~\ref{P:constant-compact-base}, with deterministic coefficient $h =
V_\sigma$ and noise amplitude zero, gives the ordinary physical flow
$P_\sigma(t)$ on $F$. Its $E$ restriction is $P_\sigma^E(t)$. Uniqueness of the
Duhamel solution makes finite-horizon constructions agree on overlaps; integer
horizons and autonomy give the same semigroup for every $t \ge 0$.

We import the following statements from the \emph{second edition}
of~\cite{teschl:14:mathematical}: Theorem~10.2, p.~258, gives the self-adjoint
$H^2$ operator, lower bound, essential spectrum $[0,\infty)$ and compact smooth
core for real compact smooth potentials in the dimensions considered here.
Theorem~10.13, p.~273, gives a simple positive ground state \emph{if the
spectral bottom is an eigenvalue}. Theorem~4.12 and Corollary~4.13,
pp.~140--141, give max--min and form monotonicity, with the essential threshold
used after discrete eigenvalues end and with the quadratic-form domain allowed.
Uniformity, fractional norms and all applications to $F$ are proved below.

\subsection{The ground family and the global phase}

\begin{proposition}[Ground states and a uniform rate gap]
  \label{P:compact-ground-family}
  The common form domain of $H_\sigma$ is $H^1$ with form $q_\sigma(u) =
  \|\nabla u\|_2^2-\int V_\sigma|u|^2$. Its bottom $E_1(\sigma)$ is a simple
  eigenvalue with a unique real positive-a.e. normalized ground state
  $p_\sigma$. Let $E_2(\sigma)$ be the second max--min level, set to zero when
  there is no second negative eigenvalue. Then
  \begin{align}
    -M&\le E_1(\sigma)\le-\varepsilon,\qquad
    E_1(\sigma)<E_2(\sigma)\le0,\label{E:compact-energy-levels}\\
    |E_j(\sigma)-E_j(\tau)|
    &\le\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty,\qquad j=1,2.
    \label{E:compact-level-continuity}
  \end{align}
  The map $\sigma\mapsto p_\sigma$ is continuous into $H^2$ and bounded there.
  With $\lambda_\sigma = \sqrt{-E_1(\sigma)}$ and $\mu_\sigma =
  \sqrt{-E_2(\sigma)}$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-rate-gap}
    \sqrt\varepsilon\le\lambda_\sigma\le\sqrt M,\qquad
    d_0:=\inf_I(\lambda_\sigma-\mu_\sigma)>0.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We first identify the common form domain and place the spectral bottom below
  the essential threshold. We then use simplicity and continuity to obtain a
  uniform gap, and compare ground states to prove their continuity in $H^2$.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We identify the common form domain.
  The imported Theorem~10.2 applies to $-V_\sigma$, which is real, compactly
  supported and in $L^2$ for $d \le 3$. It gives the stated self-adjoint
  operator on $H^2$ and essential threshold zero. Write $c = 1+M$ and $A_\sigma
  = H_\sigma+c \ge 1$. On $H^2$, integration by parts and the spectral theorem
  give
  \[
    \|A_\sigma^{1/2}u\|_2^2
    =q_\sigma(u)+c\|u\|_2^2,\qquad
    \|u\|_{H^1}^2\le\|A_\sigma^{1/2}u\|_2^2
    \le c\|u\|_{H^1}^2.
  \]
  Since $0 \le V_\sigma \le M$ and $c = 1+M$, the shifted potential term
  $c-V_\sigma$ lies between one and $c$. This gives the two form bounds on
  $H^2$. The issue is then to identify their completions, rather than assume
  the form domain from the formal expression.
  Spectral cutoffs $1_{[1,n]}(A_\sigma)u$ show that $H^2$ is a core for the
  half-power form norm. Fourier cutoffs show it is dense in $H^1$. The two
  equivalent completions are therefore $D(A_\sigma^{1/2})$ and $H^1$. Both norms
  dominate $L^2$ and give the same $L^2$ limit, so these are equal as subspaces,
  with the displayed form. This verifies the form domain used below.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We obtain the isolated simple ground state.
  The form lower bound and~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial} give $-M \le E_1 \le
  -\varepsilon$ by the imported form max--min principle. The bottom belongs to
  the spectrum and lies below the essential threshold, so it is an isolated
  eigenvalue of finite multiplicity. Only now apply the imported Theorem~10.13:
  it is simple with a positive ground state. Conjugation preserves the real
  operator; positivity and unit $L^2$ norm fix its real representative.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We make the spectral and rate gaps uniform.
  Simplicity, isolation and threshold zero give $E_1 < E_2 \le 0$. For every
  common unit form test, $|q_\sigma(u)-q_\tau(u)| \le
  \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty$. Taking the same infima over orthogonal
  complements and suprema in the source max--min formula proves
  \eqref{E:compact-level-continuity}, including passage of $E_2$ to the
  threshold. Compactness gives $\Delta_*: = \min_I(E_2-E_1) > 0$. Thus,
  \[
    \lambda_\sigma-\mu_\sigma
    =\frac{E_2(\sigma)-E_1(\sigma)}
    {\lambda_\sigma+\mu_\sigma}
    \ge\frac{\Delta_*}{2\sqrt M}>0.
  \]
  The level difference is continuous and strictly positive at every point of
  the compact parameter interval. It therefore has positive minimum
  $\Delta_*$. Factoring $\lambda_\sigma^2-\mu_\sigma^2$ gives the displayed
  rate difference, whose denominator is at most $2\sqrt M$.
  Here $M \ge \varepsilon > 0$, and no division by $\mu_\sigma$ occurs.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We prove continuity of the positive normalized
  ground state.
  Put $d_V = \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty$ and decompose $p_\tau = a p_\sigma+r$,
  with $r\perp p_\sigma$ and $0 < a \le 1$. The variational bound gives
  $q_\sigma(p_\tau) \le E_1(\sigma)+2d_V$, whereas the complementary spectral
  bound gives $q_\sigma(p_\tau) \ge E_1(\sigma)+\Delta_*\|r\|_2^2$. Hence,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-ground-continuity}
    \|p_\tau-p_\sigma\|_2^2
    =2(1-a)\le2(1-a^2)\le4d_V/\Delta_*.
  \end{equation}
  Positivity of both ground states gives $a = \langle p_\tau,p_\sigma\rangle >
  0$. Orthogonality gives $a^2+\|r\|_2^2 = 1$. The upper form bound follows by
  changing the potential and then the lowest eigenvalue, each at cost at most
  $d_V$. The complementary lower bound uses the uniform gap on $r$. Combining
  them gives $\|r\|_2^2 \le 2d_V/\Delta_*$ and hence the displayed distance
  estimate.
  The eigenfunction equation is
  \[
    (1-\Delta)p_\sigma
    =(1-\lambda_\sigma^2+V_\sigma)p_\sigma.
  \]
  Its right side is continuous in $L^2$ and bounded there by $1+2M$. The inverse
  of $1-\Delta$ is an isometry from $L^2$ to the Bessel $H^2$ space, proving the
  asserted $H^2$ continuity and bound. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-ground-family}.
\end{proof}

The rate gap~\eqref{E:compact-rate-gap} is a statement about the spectrum of
$H_\sigma$. The wave on $E$ is measured in the fixed norm of $E$, so we first
compare the two.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform fractional phase norms]\label{L:compact-fractional-phase}
  Let $B = 1-\Delta$, $c = 1+M$ and $A_\sigma = H_\sigma+c$. The quarter-power
  domain is $H^{1/2}$ and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-quarter-power}
    \|B^{1/4}u\|_2\le\|A_\sigma^{1/4}u\|_2
    \le c^{1/4}\|B^{1/4}u\|_2.
  \end{equation}
  The norm
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-spectral-phase-norm}
    \|(u,z)\|_{E_\sigma}^2
    =\|A_\sigma^{1/4}u\|_2^2
    +\|A_\sigma^{-1/4}z\|_2^2
  \end{equation}
  is uniformly equivalent to the fixed norm on $E = H^{1/2}\times H^{-1/2}$,
  interpreting the negative entry by completion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We prove the needed fractional order comparison from resolvents. The
  resulting quarter-power norm comparison identifies the positive phase entry,
  and duality identifies the negative entry with uniform constants.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We compare the quarter-power norms and their
  domains.
  On the common form domain $H^1$, $B \le A_\sigma \le cB$ and all three
  operators are at least one. For positive self-adjoint $T,S$ with common form
  domain, $T \le S$ implies $(T+t)^{-1} \ge (S+t)^{-1}$ for $t > 0$. Indeed,
  \[
    \langle f,(T+t)^{-1}f\rangle
    =\sup_u\{2\operatorname{Re}\langle f,u\rangle
    -q_T(u)-t\|u\|_2^2\};
  \]
  completing the square in the closed form norm proves the identity, and
  comparison of the suprema proves the order. Consequently, $I-t(T+t)^{-1} \le
  I-t(S+t)^{-1}$. The scalar identity
  \[
    \sqrt x=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty
    t^{-1/2}\frac{x}{x+t}\ud t
  \]
  follows by $t = xr^2$ and $\int_0^\infty(1+r^2)^{-1}\ud r = \pi/2$. Spectral
  integration and positive Tonelli, first over finite intervals and then by
  monotone convergence, give $T^{1/2} \le S^{1/2}$ as forms, including domain
  inclusions. Apply this to $B \le A_\sigma \le cB$ to obtain
  \eqref{E:compact-quarter-power}: the form of $A_\sigma^{1/2}$ is the squared
  norm of $A_\sigma^{1/4}$. The lower and upper comparisons give both domain
  inclusions, so they also give
  equality of the quarter-power domains with
  $H^{1/2}$.

  The supremum is over the common form domain. Increasing the quadratic form
  decreases every expression inside that supremum. This explains why the
  resolvent order is opposite to the original form order.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We identify the negative phase norm by duality.
  Under the $L^2$ pairing the dual norm of $\|A_\sigma^{1/4}u\|_2$ at $z \in
  L^2$ is $\|A_\sigma^{-1/4}z\|_2$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives the upper bound, and a
  scalar multiple of $A_\sigma^{-1/2}z$ attains it. Comparison
  of these dual norms preserves the comparison with reversed inequalities. The
  constants remain uniform because the positive norm constants depend only on
  $c$. Completion
  and~\eqref{E:compact-quarter-power} identify this negative space with
  $H^{-1/2}$, uniformly in $\sigma$. Adding the squared norms proves the claim.
  This proves Lemma~\ref{L:compact-fractional-phase}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The complementary wave on $E$]\label{P:compact-E-wave}
  Put
  \[
    e_\sigma^E=(p_\sigma,\lambda_\sigma p_\sigma),\qquad
    \ell_\sigma^E(u,z)
    =\frac{\langle p_\sigma,u\rangle+
    \langle p_\sigma,z\rangle/\lambda_\sigma}{2},
    \qquad \Pi_\sigma^E=e_\sigma^E\ell_\sigma^E.
  \]
  These are bounded real objects, continuous in their respective norms, with
  $\ell_\sigma^E(e_\sigma^E) = 1$, and
  \begin{align}
    P_\sigma^E(t)e_\sigma^E
    &=e^{\lambda_\sigma t}e_\sigma^E,\qquad
    \ell_\sigma^EP_\sigma^E(t)
    =e^{\lambda_\sigma t}\ell_\sigma^E,\label{E:compact-E-eigen}\\
    \|P_\sigma^E(t)(I-\Pi_\sigma^E)\|_{\mathcal{B}(E)}
    &\le C(1+t)e^{\mu_\sigma t},\qquad t\ge0.
    \label{E:compact-E-complement}
  \end{align}
  The constant is uniform over $\sigma \in I$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We estimate the spectral wave matrix in the uniformly equivalent phase norm,
  separating the complementary spatial spectrum from the two ground-state
  branches. We then identify this spectral matrix with the ordinary physical
  flow.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We write the spectral wave and its complementary
  spatial bound.
  Define real spectral functions $C_t(h),S_t(h)$ by $\cos(t\sqrt h),\sin(t\sqrt
  h)/\sqrt h$ for $h > 0$, by $1,t$ at zero, and by
  $\cosh(t\sqrt{-h}),\sinh(t\sqrt{-h})/\sqrt{-h}$ for $h < 0$. The spectral wave
  matrix is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-spectral-wave}
    \begin{pmatrix}
      C_t(H_\sigma)&S_t(H_\sigma)\\
      -H_\sigma S_t(H_\sigma)&C_t(H_\sigma)
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \end{equation}
  Real potentials, their resolvents and spectral resolutions commute with
  conjugation. All these real functions and positive $A_\sigma$ powers preserve
  the real phases.

  The spatial projection $Q_\sigma^{\mathrm{sp}} = I-|p_\sigma\rangle\langle
  p_\sigma|$ commutes with $H_\sigma,A_\sigma$ and is contractive on both
  entries of~\eqref{E:compact-spectral-phase-norm}. On its support $h \ge E_2 =
  -\mu_\sigma^2$, the phase isometry gives scalar matrix entries
  \[
    C_t(h),\quad \sqrt{h+c}\,S_t(h),\quad
    -hS_t(h)/\sqrt{h+c},\quad C_t(h).
  \]
  The isometry multiplies the displacement by $(h+c)^{1/4}$ and the velocity
  by $(h+c)^{-1/4}$. Conjugating the two off-diagonal entries by these weights
  produces the displayed factors $\sqrt{h+c}$ and its reciprocal. Thus, an
  ordinary scalar matrix bound gives an operator bound in the spectral phase
  norm.
  For $h \ge 0$, use $|S_t(h)| \le \min(t,h^{-1/2})$ and split $h \le 1$ from $h
  \ge 1$; the lower off-diagonal entry is at most one. For $-\mu_\sigma^2 \le h
  < 0$, use $|C_t(h)| \le e^{\mu_\sigma t}$ and $|S_t(h)| \le t e^{\mu_\sigma
  t}$, with $|h| \le M$ and $1 \le h+c \le c$. Thus, the complementary spatial
  matrix has norm at most $C(1+t)e^{\mu_\sigma t}$, including $\mu_\sigma = 0$.

  At nonnegative $h \le 1$, the upper off-diagonal entry is bounded by
  $\sqrt{1+c}\,t$. At $h \ge 1$, it is bounded by $\sqrt{1+c}$. The lower
  entry is bounded by $\sqrt h/\sqrt{h+c} \le 1$. These are uniform bounds on
  the nonnegative part of the spectrum.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We retain the decaying ground branch in the phase
  complement.
  The two spatial ground branches are $(p_\sigma,\pm\lambda_\sigma p_\sigma)$.
  Besides $\Pi_\sigma^E$, their projections include the decaying branch
  \[
    (p_\sigma,-\lambda_\sigma p_\sigma)
    \frac{\langle p_\sigma,u\rangle
    -\langle p_\sigma,z\rangle/\lambda_\sigma}{2}.
  \]
  Their sum is the spatial ground projection on both phase entries. Both
  projection norms are uniformly bounded in the spectral phase norm because
  $\sqrt\varepsilon \le \lambda_\sigma \le \sqrt M$ and $1 \le
  c-\lambda_\sigma^2 \le c$. The decaying branch has factor $e^{-\lambda_\sigma
  t}$ and remains in $I-\Pi_\sigma^E$. The fractional norm equivalence now
  proves the stated complement estimate for the spectral matrix.

  Removing the growing phase branch does not remove both copies of the spatial
  ground state. The displayed decaying projection accounts for the remaining
  copy. Its exponential factor is at most one for $t \ge 0$, and hence is
  bounded by the same complementary estimate. The uniform equivalence of phase
  norms then returns that estimate to the fixed space $E$.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We identify the spectral matrix with the ordinary
  flow.
  We identify this matrix with the physical flow. The same scalar bounds at
  graph levels $(A_\sigma u,A_\sigma^{1/2}z)$ give continuous solutions in
  $H^2\times H^1$. These graph norms are uniformly equivalent to the Sobolev
  norms: the $H^1$ case was proved above, and $A_\sigma-B = M-V_\sigma$ is
  bounded by $M$ while $A_\sigma,B \ge 1$. Spectral cutoffs give $u_t = z$ in
  $H^1$ and $z_t = -H_\sigma u$ in $L^2$, with continuous derivatives there.
  The
  free Fourier group is used at these graph levels so that the
  interaction-picture derivative is justified in $H^1\times L^2$. This
  derivative is not an operator-norm derivative on the whole phase. The
  free Fourier group on $H^1\times L^2$ has, after its Bessel isometry, the same
  bounded weighted entries and is strongly continuous by dominated Fourier
  convergence. Its generator is $(z,\Delta u)$ on $H^2\times H^1$, as the
  Fourier multipliers show. For compact smooth initial data, differentiating the
  free interaction picture at these graph levels gives Duhamel with force
  $(0,V_\sigma u)$. The identity also holds in $E$. Multiplication by $V_\sigma$
  maps $H^{1/2}$ into $L^2$ and then $H^{-1/2}$ with bound $M$, so Picard
  uniqueness identifies the spectral solution with the ordinary $E$ flow on
  these data. Density within $E$ and finite-horizon boundedness extend equality
  to all $E$. No density of $E$ in $F$ is used. Finally, the ground branches
  give~\eqref{E:compact-E-eigen}; their pairings are continuous because
  $p_\sigma \in H^2$. Continuity and uniform bounds of the displayed vectors and
  duals follow from Proposition~\ref{P:compact-ground-family}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-E-wave}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The growing projection and its complementary wave evolution}

For the constant coordinates write $C(t)(c_1,c_2) = (c_1+tc_2,c_2)$ and
$D_\sigma(c_1,c_2) = (0,V_\sigma c_1) \in E$.
The data $e_0 = (0,1,0)$ lie entirely in these constant coordinates, outside
$iE$. So we follow the flow on all of $F$, where $D_\sigma$ couples the
constants to $E$.

\begin{proposition}[The growing projection on the wave phase space]
  \label{P:compact-F-projection}
  The physical flow satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-F-block}
    P_\sigma(t)(e,c)
    =\left(P_\sigma^E(t)e+
    \int_0^tP_\sigma^E(t-r)D_\sigma C(r)c\ud r,\ C(t)c\right).
  \end{equation}
  Set
  \begin{align}
    f_\sigma&=\frac{\int V_\sigma p_\sigma}{2\lambda_\sigma}>0,
    &\ell_\sigma^C(c)&=
    f_\sigma\left(\frac{c_1}{\lambda_\sigma}
    +\frac{c_2}{\lambda_\sigma^2}\right),
    \label{E:compact-constant-dual}\\
    e_\sigma&=ie_\sigma^E,
    &\ell_\sigma(e,c)&=\ell_\sigma^E(e)+\ell_\sigma^C(c),\qquad
    \Pi_\sigma=e_\sigma\ell_\sigma,\quad Q_\sigma=I-\Pi_\sigma.
    \label{E:compact-F-projection}
  \end{align}
  These vectors, duals and rank-one projections are norm-continuous and
  uniformly bounded, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-F-eigen}
    \ell_\sigma(e_\sigma)=1,\qquad
    P_\sigma(t)\Pi_\sigma=\Pi_\sigma P_\sigma(t)
    =e^{\lambda_\sigma t}\Pi_\sigma.
  \end{equation}
  For every fixed nonnegative smooth compact $\phi$ of integral one,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-positive-margins}
    \inf_I\ell_\sigma(e_0)>0,\qquad
    \inf_I O_\phi(e_\sigma)>0.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We derive the block formula by variation of constants in $E$. We then
  compute the constant-coordinate contribution to the growing dual, combine
  its finite-time contribution with an exponential tail, and obtain uniform
  positive margins by compactness.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We derive the block formula for the full phase.
  The constant coordinates obey $C(t)$ because the potential force has range in
  $iE$. To justify variation of constants on the $E$ component, let $g$ be a
  continuous $E$ force and substitute the ordinary free-Duhamel equation for
  $P_\sigma^E(t-r)g(r)$ in its time integral. With $B_\sigma^E(u,z) =
  (0,V_\sigma u)$, strong $E$-valued Fubini gives
  \begin{align*}
    &\int_0^t\int_r^t S(t-s)B_\sigma^E
    P_\sigma^E(s-r)g(r)\ud s\ud r\\
    &\qquad=\int_0^tS(t-s)B_\sigma^E
    \left[\int_0^sP_\sigma^E(s-r)g(r)\ud r\right]\ud s.
  \end{align*}
  The integration region is the triangle $0 \le r \le s \le t$. Reversing the
  order changes the outer variable from $r$ to $s$, with $0 \le r \le s$ in
  the inner integral. This is exactly the forcing term in the free-Duhamel
  equation for the proposed $E$ component.
  All norms are bounded on the finite triangle, so this exchange is absolute in
  $E$. Thus, $Y(t) = P_\sigma^E(t)e+\int_0^tP_\sigma^E(t-r)g(r)\ud r$ solves
  free Duhamel with force $B_\sigma^EY+g$. Take $g(r) = D_\sigma C(r)c$ and
  adjoin the constant block. This is the same $F$ Duhamel equation as the
  ordinary flow; uniqueness proves~\eqref{E:compact-F-block}. Its finite-horizon
  bounds are uniform since the family has bounded $L^\infty$ and $L^2$ norms on
  one compact support.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We compute the growing dual on the constant
  coordinates.
  The integral defining $f_\sigma$ is finite by $L^2$ Cauchy--Schwarz and
  positive because $V_\sigma$ is nonzero by the common trial and $p_\sigma > 0$
  a.e. Its coefficients are continuous and uniformly bounded by the $H^2$
  continuity and positive lower bound for the rate; no $p_\sigma \in L^1$
  assumption is used. Directly,
  \begin{align*}
    \ell_\sigma^ED_\sigma C(r)c&=f_\sigma(c_1+rc_2),\\
    \ell_\sigma^C(c)
    &=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda_\sigma r}
    f_\sigma(c_1+rc_2)\ud r,\\
    \ell_\sigma^C(C(t)c)
    &=\int_t^\infty e^{\lambda_\sigma(t-r)}
    f_\sigma(c_1+rc_2)\ud r.
  \end{align*}
  The first integral uses $\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda_\sigma r}\ud r =
  1/\lambda_\sigma$ and $\int_0^\infty r e^{-\lambda_\sigma r}\ud r =
  1/\lambda_\sigma^2$. The last identity follows by translating the
  integration variable by $t$ and using $C(t)c = (c_1+tc_2,c_2)$.
  These integrals converge absolutely. Applying $\ell_\sigma$
  to~\eqref{E:compact-F-block}, the finite integral and this tail combine to
  give $\ell_\sigma P_\sigma(t) = e^{\lambda_\sigma t}\ell_\sigma$. The ground
  mode also gives $P_\sigma(t)e_\sigma = e^{\lambda_\sigma t}e_\sigma$ and
  $\ell_\sigma(e_\sigma) = 1$, proving~\eqref{E:compact-F-eigen}. Bounded
  bilinear multiplication and the preceding continuous coefficients prove norm
  continuity of all objects. Finally, $\ell_\sigma(e_0) =
  f_\sigma/\lambda_\sigma > 0$ and $O_\phi(e_\sigma) = \int\phi p_\sigma > 0$.
  Nonnegativity and positive integral of $\phi$ give a set of positive measure
  on which $\phi > 0$. Since $p_\sigma > 0$ almost everywhere, its pairing
  with $\phi$ is strictly positive. Continuity converts this pointwise
  positivity in the parameter into a positive uniform minimum.
  Both are continuous on compact $I$, proving
  \eqref{E:compact-positive-margins}. Positivity is in integrals, without a
  point trace or a one-dimensional ODE. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-F-projection}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{P:compact-F-projection} isolates the growing part
$\Pi_\sigma$. It remains to bound the flow on the complement $Q_\sigma$. The
proof of Lemma~\ref{L:raw-block-indexed-cone} uses the normalized form of this
bound.

\begin{proposition}[Complementary wave evolution and parameter dependence]
  \label{P:compact-F-complement}
  Uniformly over $\sigma$ and $t \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-F-complement}
    \|P_\sigma(t)Q_\sigma\|_{\mathcal{B}(F)}
    \le C(1+t)^3e^{\mu_\sigma t}.
  \end{equation}
  For every fixed $0 < \eta < d_0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-normalized-complement}
    \|e^{-\lambda_\sigma t}P_\sigma(t)Q_\sigma\|
    \le C_\eta e^{-\eta t}.
  \end{equation}
  For every finite $T_0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-parameter-flow}
    \sup_{0\le t\le T_0}\|P_\sigma(t)-P_\tau(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(F)}
    \le C_{T_0}
    \big(\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty+\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_2\big).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We use the block formula to keep track of the growing response to the
  constant coordinates when subtracting the projection. We estimate the
  remaining integral and absorb its polynomial factor into the rate gap.
  Finally, we compare two potentials through their Duhamel equations.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We bound the complementary flow on the full phase.
  Write $Q_\sigma^E = I-\Pi_\sigma^E$. Split $D_\sigma$ into its growing and
  complementary parts in~\eqref{E:compact-F-block} and use the two tail
  integrals in the preceding proof. The exact $E$ component of
  $P_\sigma(t)Q_\sigma(e,c)$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-complement-forcing}
    P_\sigma^E(t)Q_\sigma^Ee
    +\int_0^tP_\sigma^E(t-r)Q_\sigma^ED_\sigma C(r)c\ud r
    -e_\sigma^E\ell_\sigma^C(C(t)c),
  \end{equation}
  and the constant component is $C(t)c$. The final subtraction retains the
  growing response to constants in the projection. Since $\|Q_\sigma^ED_\sigma
  C(r)\| \le C(1+r)$, the integral in~\eqref{E:compact-complement-forcing} has
  norm at most
  \[
    Ce^{\mu_\sigma t}|c|
    \int_0^t(1+t-r)(1+r)\ud r
    =Ce^{\mu_\sigma t}(t+t^2+t^3/6)|c|.
  \]
  The integrand has one factor $1+t-r$ from the complementary $E$ flow and one
  factor $1+r$ from the free constant block. Its exponential is bounded by
  $e^{\mu_\sigma t}$ because $\mu_\sigma \ge 0$. Integrating the product of
  the two linear factors gives the displayed cubic polynomial.
  The first term is bounded by $C(1+t)e^{\mu_\sigma t}\|e\|_E$; the final term
  and constant component by $C(1+t)|c|$. Since $\mu_\sigma \ge 0$ this proves
  \eqref{E:compact-F-complement}. The boundedness of $(1+t)^3e^{-(d_0-\eta)t}$
  proves \eqref{E:compact-normalized-complement}. In particular, the
  constant-velocity polynomial is included in this estimate.

  After multiplying by $e^{-\lambda_\sigma t}$, the exponential factor is at
  most $e^{-d_0t}$. For $\eta < d_0$, the remaining factor
  $(1+t)^3e^{-(d_0-\eta)t}$ has finite supremum on $[0,\infty)$, giving the
  normalized estimate.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We prove finite-horizon dependence on the
  potential.
  Put $\mathcal{B}_\sigma = iB_F(V_\sigma)$ and $d_{\sigma,\tau} =
  \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty+ \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_2$. The bound for $B_F$ gives
  $\|\mathcal{B}_\sigma-\mathcal{B}_\tau\| \le d_{\sigma,\tau}$. Subtract the
  two free-Duhamel equations on states. Their difference is the sum of the
  integral with $S_F(t-r)\mathcal{B}_\sigma(P_\sigma(r)-P_\tau(r))$ and that
  with $S_F(t-r)(\mathcal{B}_\sigma-\mathcal{B}_\tau)P_\tau(r)$. The uniform
  finite-horizon bounds control the second integral by a constant times
  $d_{\sigma,\tau}$. The first integral is bounded by a constant times the
  time integral of the unknown flow difference. Thus, the
  finite-horizon bounds and scalar Gronwall prove
  \eqref{E:compact-parameter-flow}. The operator norm is measurable:
  separability of $F$ expresses it as the supremum over a countable dense set of
  unit states, each with continuous image. Hence, Gronwall applies without a
  $\mathcal{B}(F)$-valued derivative or operator-norm continuity of the free
  group. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:compact-F-complement}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The original nonnegative control and a common trial}

So far $V_\sigma$ was any continuous family
satisfying~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial}. We now produce such a family from a
nonnegative test $\Psi$ and the original covariance $\Gamma$, in two steps.

\begin{lemma}[The original mean and variance]\label{L:compact-raw-profile}
  Let $\Gamma \ne 0$ satisfy Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, let $a = \alpha_0$, and
  let real nonnegative nonzero $\Psi \in C_c^\infty((0,1)\times\R^d)$ be
  arbitrary, without a separability assumption. Define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-raw-mean}
    h_\sigma(x)=\int_0^1|\sigma-u|^{-a}
    (\Gamma*\Psi(u,\cdot))(x)\ud u,\qquad
    \overline{q}(x)=\int_0^1\Psi(u,x)\ud u,\qquad Z=W(\Psi).
  \end{equation}
  Then $h$ is smooth in time and space on compact sets, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-raw-cost}
    0<v_\Psi:=\E Z^2
    =\int_0^1\int\Psi(\sigma,x)h_\sigma(x)\ud x\ud\sigma<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  Against every smooth compact positive-time test $F_0$,
  \[
    \E[W(\Psi)W(F_0)]=\int F_0(s,x)h_s(x)\ud x\ud s.
  \]
  Moreover, on $I$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-positive-mean-floor}
    h_\sigma\ge\Gamma*\overline{q},\qquad
    v_\Psi\ge Q(\overline{q})
    :=\int\Gamma(\ud z)\int\overline{q}(x)\overline{q}(x-z)\ud x>0.
  \end{equation}
  For every fixed mask, $V_\sigma = m_Rh_\sigma$ is a smooth compact family,
  Lipschitz in $\sigma$ in $L^\infty$ and $L^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We identify the original covariance mean and show its cost is finite.
  Positivity and homogeneity give a nonnegative, nonzero time-independent lower
  profile and a strictly positive covariance cost. We then differentiate the
  test, rather than the singular time kernel, to obtain regularity.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We identify the mean and bound its covariance
  cost.
  Choose $A \ge 1$ containing the spatial support of $\Psi$ in $B_A$. The
  original covariance identity and absolute Fubini give the stated mean pairing:
  the two compact tests restrict spatial differences to a compact set of finite
  $\Gamma$ mass, and the time singularity is locally integrable since $a < 1$.
  This identifies the linear covariance mean without evaluating noise at a
  point. The same calculation gives~\eqref{E:compact-raw-cost}. Its spatial
  integrand is bounded in integral by
  $\|\Psi\|_\infty^2|B_A|\Gamma(\overline{B}_{2A})$, and
  $\int_0^1\int_0^1|\sigma-u|^{-a}\ud u\ud\sigma = 2/((1-a)(2-a))$, proving
  finiteness.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We obtain strict positivity and the uniform mean
  floor.
  Nonnegative nonzero smooth $\Psi$ has positive integral, so $\overline{q} \ne
  0$. Nonzero $\Gamma$ charges some ball, and homogeneity gives $\Gamma(B_1) =
  r^{\alpha-d}\Gamma(B_r) > 0$. Thus, every origin-centered ball $B_r$, $r > 0$,
  has positive finite mass. The continuous compact autocorrelation
  $\int\overline{q}(x)\overline{q}(x-z)\ud x$ is positive at zero and bounded
  below on some ball. This proves $0 < Q(\overline{q}) < \infty$. Since
  $|\sigma-u|^{-a} \ge 1$ on the unit time square, positive Tonelli gives both
  inequalities in~\eqref{E:compact-positive-mean-floor}.

  The value of the spatial autocorrelation at zero is $\|\overline q\|_2^2 >
  0$. Its continuity makes it uniformly positive on a small origin-centered
  ball. Positive mass of that ball under $\Gamma$ gives strict positivity of
  $Q(\overline q)$. The lower bound on the time kernel can then be integrated
  without changing signs, because both tests and the covariance measure are
  nonnegative.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We prove smoothness and the fixed-mask Lipschitz
  bounds.
  Extend $\Psi$ by zero in time and write
  \[
    h_s(x)=\int_\R |r|^{-a}
    \int\Gamma(\ud z)\Psi(s-r,x-z)\ud r.
  \]
  On compact $s,x$ sets the relevant $r,z$ range over fixed compact sets;
  $|r|^{-a}$ is integrable there. Every derivative falls on the smooth test, not
  on the singular time kernel. Dominated differentiation gives smoothness and
  continuity of all derivatives on compacts. Multiplication by $m_R$ now gives
  the claimed compact family and its fixed-$R$ Lipschitz bounds. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:compact-raw-profile}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:compact-raw-profile} gives a smooth compact family for every
fixed mask, but not yet the common negative
trial~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial}.

\begin{proposition}[Binding with the original control and cost fixed]
  \label{P:compact-original-common-trial}
  For every $\Psi$ in Lemma~\ref{L:compact-raw-profile}, a sufficiently large
  finite mask $m_R$ gives $V_\sigma = m_Rh_\sigma$ a common negative
  trial~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial}. Thus, all preceding ground, projection,
  positive-margin and complementary-wave conclusions apply. The rate is
  Lipschitz on $I$ and has $\lambda_0 > 0$. The original test $\Psi$, original
  variable $Z$ and variance $v_\Psi$ are unchanged by this choice. The mask can
  additionally cover any fixed compact variational test supports.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We dilate a fixed trial function and choose the mask to contain its support.
  Homogeneity gives a potential contribution of order $L^{-\alpha}$, which
  dominates the kinetic contribution of order $L^{-2}$. We then apply the
  ground-family estimates at this fixed mask.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We bound the potential contribution of the dilated
  trial.
  Choose a real unit $L^2$ function $\psi \in C_c^\infty(B_2)$ with $\psi \ge
  c_0 > 0$ on $B_1$, and put $\psi_L(x) = L^{-d/2}\psi(x/L)$, $D_\psi =
  \|\nabla\psi\|_2^2$, where $L \ge \max(1,2A)$. Choose the even mask with $R
  \ge 2L$, so $V_\sigma = h_\sigma$ on $\operatorname{supp}\psi_L$. This is the
  allowed real mask $0 \le m_R \le 1$, equal to one on $B_R$ and with support in
  $\overline{B}_{2R}$. Positive Tonelli
  and~\eqref{E:compact-positive-mean-floor} give
  \begin{align}
    \int V_\sigma\psi_L^2
    &\ge c_0^2L^{-d}\int_{B_L}(\Gamma*\overline{q})(x)\ud x\nonumber\\
    &=c_0^2L^{-d}\int\overline{q}(y)\Gamma(B_L-y)\ud y\nonumber\\
    &\ge c_0^2L^{-d}\Gamma(B_{L/2})\int\overline{q}
    =C_\Gamma L^{-\alpha},\label{E:compact-trial-potential}\\
    C_\Gamma&=c_0^22^{\alpha-d}\Gamma(B_1)\int\overline{q}>0.\nonumber
  \end{align}
  On $B_L$, the dilated trial has squared value at least $c_0^2L^{-d}$. Every
  $y$ in the support of $\overline q$ has $|y| \le A$. Hence, translating
  $B_L$ by such a $y$ still contains $B_{L/2}$. Homogeneity changes its mass
  to $(L/2)^{d-\alpha}\Gamma(B_1)$, giving exactly the displayed constant.
  Here $B_{L/2} \subset B_L-y$ for $|y| \le A$ and $L \ge 2A$. All covariance
  masses in the argument are compact and finite. The kinetic energy is $D_\psi
  L^{-2}$. Since $\alpha < 2$, choose $L$ large enough that $\varepsilon_R: =
  C_\Gamma L^{-\alpha}-D_\psi L^{-2} > 0$. Then
  \[
    \|\nabla\psi_L\|_2^2-\int V_\sigma\psi_L^2
    \le-\varepsilon_R\quad\hbox{for every }\sigma\in I.
  \]

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We fix a negative trial and obtain the rate
  regularity.
  Fix this finite $R$ before invoking Proposition~\ref{P:compact-ground-family}.
  Its level bound and the mean Lipschitz estimate give
  \[
    |\lambda_\sigma-\lambda_\tau|
    \le\frac{\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon_R}}
    \le C_R|\sigma-\tau|.
  \]
  The lowest eigenvalue remains at most $-\varepsilon_R$ for every parameter.
  Factoring the difference of its negative square roots gives a denominator
  $\lambda_\sigma+\lambda_\tau \ge 2\sqrt{\varepsilon_R}$. The level
  comparison then gives the displayed Lipschitz estimate.
  The positive mean integrates the entire time test, so $\lambda_0 > 0$ even
  though $\Psi(0,\cdot) = 0$. Only the trial and mask have grown; the original
  test, its variable and~\eqref{E:compact-raw-cost} were fixed first. Enlarging
  $R$ to contain additional fixed compact test supports preserves the trial and
  gives equality of masked and unmasked potential energies on those supports.
  This includes singular invariant-direction covariances and the one-dimensional
  white case. It is not an all-strength binding assertion for one fixed compact
  potential. Masks are fixed before source or time asymptotics; a later supremum
  over controls must follow their individual bounds. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-original-common-trial}.
\end{proof}

\section{Model and standing assumptions}\label{S:model}

We study the hyperbolic Anderson model
\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(1.1)}
\begin{equation}\label{E:HAM}
  \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\W(t,x)u(t,x),
  \qquad u(0,x)=1,\quad \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=0,
\end{equation}
for $(t,x) \in \R_+\times\R^d$, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, interpreted in the
Stratonovich sense (limit of classical wave equations driven by smoothed noises
$\W_{\epsilon,\delta}$). The noise is centered Gaussian with
\begin{equation}\label{E:cov}
  \operatorname{Cov}\big(\W(s,x),\W(t,y)\big)=|s-t|^{-\alpha_0}\gamma(x-y),
  \qquad 0<\alpha_0<1,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma \ge 0$ is nonnegative definite with spectral measure $\mu$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Homogeneous covariance]\label{H:homog}
  Let $d \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$. The spatial covariance is a
  nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon measure $\Gamma$ on
  $\R^d$, with positive even tempered Radon spectral measure $\mu$ in the
  convention
  \[
    \Gamma=\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu(\ud\xi)
    \quad\text{in the sense of tempered distributions}.
  \]
  The symbol $\gamma$ in~\eqref{E:cov} denotes this covariance as a function
  when one exists, and as a distribution otherwise. For every Borel set $A$ and
  $c > 0$, $\Gamma(cA) = c^{d-\alpha}\Gamma(A)$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:threshold}
    \alpha_0+\alpha<2,
  \end{equation}
  with $0 < \alpha < d$, or $\alpha = d = 1$. In particular, the last case
  includes spatial white noise $\Gamma = \delta_0$.
\end{hypothesis}

\paragraph{Zero covariance.}
Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} allows $\Gamma = 0$ by design. In this case the
centered Gaussian noise is $\W = 0$, and the solution with initial data
$(1,0)$ is $u(t,x) = 1$. Thus $\E|u(t,x)|^p = 1$ for every $p > 0$, and
all normalized logarithmic moment rates are zero. Results asserting strict
positivity of covariance-dependent quantities impose $\Gamma \ne 0$ locally.

\section{Young evolution with constant initial data}
\label{S:constant-phase-young}

We use the fixed $R,T,E,\gamma,\theta,\beta$ of~\eqref{E:driver-parameters}, and
choose the allowed even mask $m = \zeta_R$ with $0 \le m \le 1$. All constants
in this section can depend on these fixed data, the model and the auxiliary
physical cutoffs. There is no uniform estimate as $R$ or $T$ grows.

\subsection{The distributional phase and its free evolution}

Define the real Hilbert space
\begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-space}
  F=E\oplus\R^2,\qquad
  \|(e,c_1,c_2)\|_F^2=\|e\|_E^2+c_1^2+c_2^2,\qquad
  e_0=(0,1,0).
\end{equation}
Its distributional interpretation is $(e,c_1,c_2)\mapsto(e_1+c_1,e_2+c_2)$. Let
$i:E \to F$ denote $e\mapsto(e,0,0)$ and let $\pi_C:F \to \R^2$ be the
constant-coordinate projection.

\begin{lemma}[Constants and finite propagation]\label{L:constant-phase-free}
  The distributional interpretation of $F$ is continuous and injective. The
  space is complete and separable, and $iE$ is closed and proper. For every real
  $\psi \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-cutoff}
    L_\psi(e,c_1,c_2)=\psi e+(c_1\psi,c_2\psi)
  \end{equation}
  is bounded from $F$ to $E$. The free wave group on $F$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-free}
    S_F(t)(e,c_1,c_2)=(S(t)e,c_1+tc_2,c_2).
  \end{equation}
  It is strongly continuous and bounded on finite horizons and has finite
  propagation for the total distributional data, including local cancellation
  between $e$ and the constants.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  A nonzero constant has Fourier transform a nonzero multiple of $\delta_0$.
  Every $H^\sigma$ Fourier transform is locally $L^2$ near zero, since its
  weight is bounded above and below there. A locally $L^2$ function $f$ cannot
  represent $\delta_0$: testing against $\varphi(\xi/\varepsilon)$, with
  $\varphi(0) = 1$ and $\varphi$ compactly supported in $B_1$, bounds its
  integral by
  \[
    \|f\|_{L^2(B_\varepsilon)}
    \varepsilon^{d/2}\|\varphi\|_2\longrightarrow0,
  \]
  whereas $\delta_0$ gives one. Thus, constants lie in neither $H^{1/2}$ nor
  $H^{-1/2}$; the interpretation is injective. Sobolev duality and the two test
  integrals prove continuity into distributions, uniformly on bounded families
  of compact tests. Completeness, separability and the closed proper inclusion
  follow from the Hilbert direct sum.

  Multiplication by $\psi$ is bounded on both Sobolev entries. Indeed, the
  Peetre inequalities give $\langle\xi\rangle^\sigma\langle\eta\rangle^{-\sigma}
  \le 2^{|\sigma|/2}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{|\sigma|}$. In the unitary Fourier
  convention its weighted kernel is therefore bounded by convolution with the
  $L^1$ function $2^{|\sigma|/2}c_d|\widehat{\psi}(q)|\langle
  q\rangle^{|\sigma|}$, where $c_d = (2\pi)^{-d/2}$. The $L^1*L^2$ bound applies
  at $\sigma = \pm1/2$; the two constant columns are also in $E$. This proves
  boundedness of~\eqref{E:constant-phase-cutoff}.

  The finite-dimensional block in~\eqref{E:constant-phase-free} and the strong,
  finite-horizon bound for $S(t)$ from Lemma~\ref{L:driver-gain-localization}
  prove the group assertions. To verify its physical meaning, for $t > 0$
  consider the matrix of compactly supported distributions
  \[
    \begin{pmatrix}
      \partial_tG_d(t)&G_d(t)\\
      \Delta G_d(t)&\partial_tG_d(t)
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  The source Green convention gives $\widehat{G}_d(t)(\xi) =
  c_d\sin(t|\xi|)/|\xi|$; hence its convolution multiplier is the sine quotient,
  and the differentiated multipliers are $\cos(t|\xi|)$ and
  $-|\xi|\sin(t|\xi|)$. The matrix agrees with $S(t)$ on Schwartz phases.
  Convolution by a compact distribution is continuous on tempered distributions:
  its transpose convolves a Schwartz test with the reflected compact
  distribution, continuously in all Schwartz seminorms. Schwartz approximation
  in $E$ extends the identity to $E$. The masses of the four entries are
  $1,t,0,1$, so constants give exactly~\eqref{E:constant-phase-free}. Time
  reversal handles $t < 0$. Each entry is supported in $B_{|t|}$, also for the
  three-dimensional sphere kernel. If the total initial distribution vanishes on
  an enlarged region, each translated kernel tests zero there, so the evolved
  total distribution vanishes on the smaller cone. This argument permits
  cancellation between its Sobolev and constant parts. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:constant-phase-free}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two columns for constant initial data}

Choose real $\chi \in C_c^\infty$, equal to one on $B_{2R+T+1}$ and supported in
$B_{2R+T+2}$, and choose real compact smooth $\psi = 1$ on a neighborhood of
$\operatorname{supp}\chi$. Write $M_E = M_R$ and $M_E^\rho = M_{Y_\rho}$ for the
paths already constructed, with the same original deterministic coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[Extended operator path]\label{P:constant-phase-driver}
  As identities of continuous operator paths,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-localization}
    D_\chi M_E=M_E=M_ED_\chi,
  \end{equation}
  and the same holds for each fixed $\rho$. Define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-driver}
    A_E=M_EL_\psi,\qquad A=iA_E,\qquad
    A_E^\rho=M_E^\rho L_\psi,\qquad A^\rho=iA_E^\rho.
  \end{equation}
  These are independent of $\psi$, have compact values, and satisfy $Ai = iM_E$,
  $\pi_CA = 0$ and their source counterparts. In every finite $L^pC^\theta$,
  $A^\rho \to A$ at arbitrary relative source-width rates. Their norms have
  uniform $\sqrt p$ moment bounds and a common finite square-exponential moment.
  The original $A$ is measurable with respect to the given $W$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Lemma~\ref{L:driver-gain-localization} gives both localizations for every
  deterministic $A_j(0,t)$. The original finite sums retain them.
  Proposition~\ref{P:source-gaussian-transfer} constructs the source finite sums
  with exactly these coefficients and correlated $Y_\rho(f_j)$. Their $C^\theta$
  limits preserve both identities. A uniformly convergent subsequence, or
  rational times and continuous paths,
  gives~\eqref{E:constant-phase-localization} for all $t$ on one event. Use a
  separate event for each fixed $\rho$.

  For two choices $\psi,\psi'$, $(L_\psi-L_{\psi'})z \in E$ and
  $D_\chi(L_\psi-L_{\psi'})z = 0$. The right localization kills this difference.
  For $z = ie$, $D_\chi D_\psi e = D_\chi e$ and hence $Ai = iM_E$. The constant
  columns are precisely $iM_E(\psi,0)$ and $iM_E(0,\psi)$; they are retained.
  Bounded composition preserves compactness and transfers
  \eqref{E:source-driver-limit} and the Gaussian norm bounds
  to~\eqref{E:constant-phase-driver}. If $\|X\|_{L^p} \le C\sqrt p$, then $\E
  X^{2n} \le C^{2n}(2n)^n$. Since $n! \ge (n/e)^n$, the exponential series gives
  $\E e^{cX^2} < \infty$ uniformly when $2ecC^2 < 1$. Finally, $A$ is a
  deterministic bounded transform of the original $W$-measurable operator path.
  This proves Proposition~\ref{P:constant-phase-driver}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ordinary response on all of $F$]\label{L:constant-source-response}
  For fixed $\rho$ put $b(r) = mY_\rho(r)$ and define
  \[
    B_F(b)(e,c_1,c_2)=(0,b(e_1+c_1))\in E.
  \]
  Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-source-response}
    A_E^\rho(t)z=\int_0^tS(-r)B_F(b(r))S_F(r)z\ud r
  \end{equation}
  as a statewise strong integral. Its primitive is Lipschitz in
  $\mathcal{B}(F,E)$ and the identity holds for all states and times on one
  event for this fixed $\rho$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The source regularity gives one compact spatial support for $b$, continuity in
  $L^\infty$ and $L^2$, and bounded spatial derivatives on the horizon. Since
  $e_1 \in L^2$ and $\|\cdot\|_{H^{-1/2}} \le \|\cdot\|_2$,
  \[
    \|B_F(b)\|_{\mathcal{B}(F,E)}
    \le\|b\|_\infty+\|b\|_2.
  \]
  Strong continuity and finite-horizon bounds of both free groups make the
  integrand strongly continuous on each state and uniformly bounded as an
  operator. Integrating its bound proves operator-norm Lipschitz increments
  without assuming a Banach-valued operator derivative.

  The total initial distributions of $z$ and $iL_\psi z$ agree on the entire
  enlarged ball where $\psi = 1$. Lemma~\ref{L:constant-phase-free} gives
  equality of the free displacements near $\operatorname{supp}m$ for $0 \le r
  \le T$. Multiplication by $b(r)$ kills the difference. Apply
  Proposition~\ref{P:source-ordinary-response} to the $E$ state $L_\psi z$ to
  obtain~\eqref{E:constant-source-response}. Separability and rational times,
  followed by boundedness and continuity, give its path identity. This proves
  the constant columns directly; no density of $E$ in $F$ is used. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-response}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact linear Young equation}

We use Young's integration theory~\cite{young:36:inequality} in its
Banach-space form. The estimate is stated by
Friz and Hairer~\cite{friz.hairer:20:course}*{Sections~1.4--1.5, 4.1, (4.3), pp.~13, 61--62}.
For $D,V$ with exponent $\theta > 1/2$ and bounded bilinear multiplication, left
composition gives
\begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-estimate}
  \left\|\int_s^t\ud D(r)V(r)-D(s,t)V(s)\right\|
  \le C_\theta[D]_\theta[V]_\theta|t-s|^{2\theta}.
\end{equation}
The Banach spaces here are $\mathcal{B}(F)$, and multiplication is bounded with
constant one. We derive the linear solution and stability estimates below rather
than invoke a nonlinear bounded-vector-field theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Linear Young evolution]\label{P:constant-young-linear}
  For every $D \in C^\theta([0,T];\mathcal{B}(F))$ with $D(0) = 0$ there is a
  unique $C^\theta$ solution, in the terminal variable, of
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-equation}
    U_D(t,s)=I+\int_s^t\ud D(r)U_D(r,s).
  \end{equation}
  It has the composition law, invertible values and joint operator-norm
  continuity on $0 \le s \le t \le T$. The map $D\mapsto U_D$ is locally
  Lipschitz into the uniform time-triangle norm, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-growth}
    \sup_{s\le t\le T}\|U_D(t,s)\|
    \le C_T\exp\big(C_T(1+\|D\|_{C^\theta})^{1/\theta}\big).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  On an interval $[a,a+\ell]$ use the complete scaled norm $N_\ell(V) =
  \sup\|V\|+\ell^\theta[V]_\theta$ and put $I_D(V)(t) = \int_a^t\ud D\,V$.
  Applying~\eqref{E:constant-young-estimate} to every subinterval gives
  \begin{align*}
    \sup\|I_D(V)\|
    &\le[D]_\theta\ell^\theta\sup\|V\|
    +C_\theta[D]_\theta\ell^{2\theta}[V]_\theta,\\
    [I_D(V)]_\theta
    &\le[D]_\theta\sup\|V\|
    +C_\theta[D]_\theta\ell^\theta[V]_\theta.
  \end{align*}
  Thus, with $c_\theta = 2\max(1,C_\theta)$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-local}
    N_\ell(I_D(V))
    \le c_\theta[D]_\theta\ell^\theta N_\ell(V).
  \end{equation}
  Take $K \ge 1$, $[D]_\theta \le K$, and choose $\ell$ with $c_\theta
  K\ell^\theta \le 1/4$. The map $V\mapsto V_a+I_D(V)$ is a contraction on the
  complete affine space of paths starting at $V_a$. Its solution satisfies
  $N_\ell(V) \le (4/3)\|V_a\| \le 2\|V_a\|$.

  To compare $D,D'$, require both seminorms at most $K$ and write $\delta =
  \|D-D'\|_{C^\theta}$, $W = V-V'$. Subtracting gives $W =
  W_a+I_D(W)+I_{D-D'}(V')$. Absorbing the factor $1/4$ yields
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-difference}
    N_\ell(W)\le2\|W_a\|
    +2c_\theta\ell^\theta\delta N_\ell(V').
  \end{equation}
  Cover $[s,T]$ by at most $n_{K,T}$ such pieces. If the second initial norm is
  $B$, its norm on piece $j$, indexed from zero, is at most $2^{j+1}B$. With
  $E_j$ the difference at its left endpoint, induction gives
  \[
    E_{j+1}\le2E_j+2^{j+2}c_\theta\ell^\theta\delta B,\qquad
    E_j\le2^jE_0+j2^{j+1}c_\theta\ell^\theta\delta B.
  \]
  Equation~\eqref{E:constant-young-difference} gives the same bound inside each
  piece; a shorter final piece only reduces the coefficient. For evolutions,
  $E_0 = 0$ and $B = 1$. The number of pieces is uniform in $s,t$, proving local
  Lipschitz dependence in the full triangle norm.

  Now choose $K = 1+\|D\|_{C^\theta}$. At most
  $C_T(1+\|D\|_{C^\theta}^{1/\theta})$ pieces suffice. Each multiplies the norm
  by at most two, giving \eqref{E:constant-young-growth}. Uniqueness patches the
  solutions; right multiplication by the preceding evolution gives the
  composition law. On a small piece~\eqref{E:constant-young-local} gives
  $\|U_D-I\|_\infty \le 1/3$, so a Neumann series proves local invertibility,
  then products give global invertibility. Finally, $U_D(t,s) =
  U_D(t,0)U_D(s,0)^{-1}$ proves joint norm continuity, since $U_D(\cdot,0)$ is
  continuous and inversion is continuous on invertible operators. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-young-linear}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{P:constant-young-linear} concerns one deterministic operator
path. We now apply it to the random paths $A$ and $A^\rho$
of~\eqref{E:constant-phase-driver} and pass to the physical flow on $F$.

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian Young and physical flows]
  \label{P:constant-gaussian-flow}
  Apply Proposition~\ref{P:constant-young-linear} to $A,A^\rho$. The resulting
  flows are strongly measurable in uniform triangle norm, have every finite norm
  moment and satisfy
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-flow-limit}
    U_{A^\rho}\longrightarrow U_A
    \quad\hbox{in every finite }L^p
    \left(\sup_{0\le s\le t\le T}\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}(F)}\right).
  \end{equation}
  They preserve the interaction constant coordinates:
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-flow-range}
    \pi_CU_A(t,s)=\pi_C,\qquad
    (U_A(t,s)-I)F\subset iE,
  \end{equation}
  and likewise for the source. The physical flow is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-physical-flow}
    P_A(t,s)=S_F(t)U_A(t,s)S_F(-s).
  \end{equation}
  It has composition and strong continuity on states, physical constant block
  $(c_1,c_2)\mapsto(c_1+(t-s)c_2,c_2)$, all finite moments and the analogous
  uniform $L^p$ source convergence. Its difference from the free flow has range
  in $iE$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The original paths lie in separable closed path spans by their Gaussian
  construction. Bounded composition in~\eqref{E:constant-phase-driver} and the
  continuous Young map preserve strong measurability into
  $C(\Delta_T;\mathcal{B}(F))$, where $\Delta_T = \{(t,s):0\le s\le t\le T\}$
  has the uniform norm. Indeed, the continuous image of the separable space of
  operator paths has separable closure in this triangle space. Applying $\pi_C$
  to Young Riemann sums gives the first identity
  in~\eqref{E:constant-flow-range}. Each increment applied to any state lies in
  $iE$; closedness of this subspace passes the property through sums and limits,
  giving the second identity.

  Write $X_\rho = \|A^\rho\|_{C^\theta}$ and $X_0 = \|A\|_{C^\theta}$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-phase-driver} supplies $c_0 > 0$ and $H_0 <
  \infty$ such that
  \[
    \sup_\rho\E e^{c_0X_\rho^2}\le H_0,\qquad
    \E e^{c_0X_0^2}\le H_0.
  \]
  For $K \ge 1$, let $L_K$ be the Lipschitz constant of
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-young-linear} on the ball of operator paths of
  radius $K$, in the whole time-triangle norm. On $\{X_\rho\le K,\ X_0\le K\}$
  it gives
  \[
    \|U_{A^\rho}-U_A\|_\infty
    \le L_K\|A^\rho-A\|_{C^\theta}.
  \]
  Consequently, for $\varepsilon > 0$ and any finite $q \ge 1$, Markov and the
  union bound give
  \[
    \mathbb{P}(\|U_{A^\rho}-U_A\|_\infty>\varepsilon)
    \le2H_0e^{-c_0K^2}
    +(L_K/\varepsilon)^q\E\|A^\rho-A\|_{C^\theta}^q.
  \]
  At fixed $K$ the second term tends to zero at arbitrary relative source-width
  rates. Only then let $K \to \infty$ to obtain convergence in probability. No
  independence of the original, source and residual operator paths is required.

  Put $\nu = 1/\theta < 2$. For each finite $r \ge 1$ and $c > 0$, there is
  $C_{r,c} < \infty$ such that
  \[
    rC_T(1+x)^\nu\le cx^2+C_{r,c},\qquad x\ge0.
  \]
  This follows because $(1+x)^\nu/x^2 \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$, while the
  remainder is bounded on compact intervals. Use $c \le c_0$
  and~\eqref{E:constant-young-growth} to obtain
  \[
    \sup_\rho\E\|U_{A^\rho}\|_\infty^r<\infty,\qquad
    \E\|U_A\|_\infty^r<\infty.
  \]
  In particular, for fixed finite $p \ge 1$ and $V_\rho =
  \|U_{A^\rho}-U_A\|_\infty$, the $2p$ moments of $V_\rho$ are uniformly
  bounded. For every $\varepsilon > 0$,
  \[
    \E V_\rho^p
    \le\varepsilon^p+
    (\E V_\rho^{2p})^{1/2}
    \mathbb{P}(V_\rho>\varepsilon)^{1/2}.
  \]
  The second term tends to zero by convergence in probability; then let
  $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This proves \eqref{E:constant-flow-limit}. The
  square-exponential bound used here is an operator path bound; no
  square-exponential flow bound is needed.

  The free group is strongly continuous and bounded on the horizon. Define
  $\mathcal{B}_\infty(\Delta_T;\mathcal{B}(F))$ to be the Banach space of all
  bounded operator families on $\Delta_T$. The deterministic map
  \[
    \mathcal{L}(V)(t,s)=S_F(t)V(t,s)S_F(-s)
  \]
  is bounded linear from $C(\Delta_T;\mathcal{B}(F))$ into this bounded-family
  space, with norm at most $C_T^2$, where $C_T \ge \sup_{|r|\le T}\|S_F(r)\|$.
  Hence, it preserves strong measurability on the separable ranges and gives, in
  particular,
  \[
    \|P_{A^\rho}-P_A\|_\infty
    \le C_T^2\|U_{A^\rho}-U_A\|_\infty.
  \]
  All finite moments and uniform $L^p$ convergence therefore transfer to the
  physical flows. This does not place those flows in the norm-continuous
  triangle space or assert operator-norm continuity of the free group. For each
  fixed state, joint continuity follows by splitting a physical-flow difference
  into the norm-continuous $U$ difference and two strong free-group differences,
  using the uniform group bound. Group cancellation gives composition. The
  finite constant block $J_t(c_1,c_2) = (c_1+tc_2,c_2)$ satisfies $J_tJ_{-s} =
  J_{t-s}$, which gives the asserted physical constant coordinates from
  $\pi_CU_A = \pi_C$. Finally,
  \[
    P_A(t,s)-S_F(t-s)
    =S_F(t)(U_A(t,s)-I)S_F(-s)
  \]
  has range in $iE$, since $S_F(t)iE = iE$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-gaussian-flow}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Ordinary source solutions and the original tested observable}

So far $P_{A^\rho}$ is defined through Young integrals, not through the source
equation of Proposition~\ref{P:masked-source-moments}. We now connect the two
at a fixed source width $\rho$.

\begin{lemma}[Identification with the ordinary source equation]
  \label{L:constant-source-solution}
  For fixed $\rho$, the displacement distribution of $P_{A^\rho}(t,0)e_0$ is the
  locally continuous ordinary source solution $u_\rho^m(t,\cdot)$ from
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-source-moments}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  In~\eqref{E:constant-source-response} let $C(r) = S(-r)B_F(b(r))S_F(r)$. The
  primitive is $A_E^\rho$. For left endpoints $r_j = t_j$, the Young sums
  applied to a state $v$ are
  \[
    \sum_j\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}iC(r)U_{A^\rho}(r_j,s)v\ud r.
  \]
  Uniform continuity of $U$ and the uniform bound for $C$ make these converge to
  the strong integral with $U(r,s)v$. Thus, the Young equation is precisely the
  ordinary interaction integral on states. Multiplying by free groups gives the
  Duhamel equation for displacement and velocity. Its unique $C([0,T];F)$
  solution follows by the bounded-coefficient Picard argument with the strongly
  continuous, bounded free group. No operator-norm derivative of $S_F$ is used.

  More explicitly, put $W_0(t) = e_0$ and, for $n \ge 1$,
  \[
    W_n(t)=\int_0^t S_F(t-r)iB_F(b(r))W_{n-1}(r)\ud r.
  \]
  If $B_T = \sup_r\|B_F(b(r))\|_{\mathcal{B}(F,E)}$, induction on the time
  simplex gives $\sup_t\|W_n(t)\|_F \le C_T^{n+1}B_T^nT^n/n!$. We take $C_T \ge
  \max(1,\sup_{|t|\le T}\|S_F(t)\|)$, which also supplies the bound at order
  zero. The series converges in $C([0,T];F)$. The Duhamel map there has norm at
  most $C_TB_TT$, so its sum solves the same integral equation and equals the
  ordinary phase by uniqueness.

  Let $X_0(t,x) = 1$ and $X_n(t) = \int_0^tG_d(t-r)*(b(r)X_{n-1}(r))\ud r$. At
  fixed $n$ these are continuous in time and smooth in space. Derivatives fall
  on the smooth compact factor $bX_{n-1}$; Green mass $t-r$ bounds the
  integrals, while the unit-time measure representation gives time continuity.
  This is induction using only finitely many bounded derivatives for each fixed
  derivative order. At $n = 0$ the phase displacement is $X_0$. If the equality
  holds at $n-1$, multiplication by smooth $b$ identifies the forcing with
  $(0,f_r)$, where $f_r = b(r)X_{n-1}(r)$ is smooth and compactly supported. For
  a compact smooth test $\varphi$, continuous distributional testing of the
  phase integral yields
  \[
    \langle[W_n(t)]_1,\varphi\rangle
    =\int_0^t\int f_r(y)
    \int G_d(t-r,\ud z)\varphi(y+z)\ud y\ud r
    =\langle X_n(t),\varphi\rangle.
  \]
  Absolute Fubini follows from $\|\varphi\|_\infty\|f_r\|_1(t-r)$, integrable on
  the horizon because $f_r$ has one compact support and continuous $L^1$ norm.
  This also covers sphere measures in $d = 3$. Iterating the scalar recursion
  gives exactly $X_{\rho,n}^m$, with chronological order and all physical mask
  factors. The scalar compact-cone factorial bound gives locally uniform
  convergence to $u_\rho^m$, whereas the phase series converges in $F$ and hence
  in distributions. Their displacement limits agree. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-solution}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-solution} holds at each fixed $\rho$. To reach
the original noise we test against a fixed $\phi$ and use the source
limit~\eqref{E:constant-flow-limit}.

\begin{proposition}[Original average and original point moment]
  \label{P:constant-raw-average}
  Let real $\phi \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$ be nonnegative with integral one. The
  bounded functional and original random variable
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-raw-average}
    O_\phi(e,c_1,c_2)=\langle e_1,\phi\rangle+c_1,\qquad
    Z_m(t,\phi)=O_\phi P_A(t,0)e_0
  \end{equation}
  are well defined, and $Z_m$ is $W$-measurable. At arbitrary relative
  source-width rates, for every finite $p$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-tested-limit}
    \int\phi(x)u_\rho^m(t,x)\ud x
    \longrightarrow Z_m(t,\phi)\quad\text{in }L^p.
  \end{equation}
  In particular,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-original-point-transfer}
    \E|Z_m(t,\phi)|^2\le\E|u(t,0)|^2.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Sobolev duality gives $\|O_\phi\| \le (\|\phi\|_{H^{-1/2}}^2+1)^{1/2}$. The
  original operator path is $W$-measurable, and the Young and deterministic maps
  above are continuous or bounded measurable on their separable ranges. Thus,
  $Z_m$ is $W$-measurable and does not depend on the auxiliary $J$ used in
  source realizations. At each fixed $\rho$,
  Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-solution} identifies the displacement as a
  distribution with the ordinary source solution. Testing by this same $\phi$,
  with $\int\phi = 1$, therefore gives the almost sure identity
  \[
    O_\phi P_{A^\rho}(t,0)e_0
    =C_\phi:=\int\phi(x)u_\rho^m(t,x)\ud x.
  \]
  This uses the displacement pairing and its constant displacement coordinate
  in~\eqref{E:constant-raw-average}; it takes no point trace of an $H^{1/2}$
  state. All identities here are at one fixed $\rho$, so no uncountable
  intersection of source-version events is used.

  We verify that $C_\phi$ equals the Bochner $L^2$ average $B_\phi$ from
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-source-moments}. That proposition gives the
  spatially uniform bound $\|u_\rho^m(t,x)\|_2 \le Q_t$, with $Q_t$ also
  independent of source widths. Scalar Fubini gives
  \[
    \E\int|\phi(x)u_\rho^m(t,x)|\ud x
    \le Q_t\|\phi\|_1<\infty.
  \]
  Thus, the ordinary spatial integral exists almost surely. Weighted
  Cauchy--Schwarz and scalar Fubini also give
  \[
    \E|C_\phi|^2
    \le\|\phi\|_1\int|\phi(x)|\E|u_\rho^m(t,x)|^2\ud x
    \le Q_t^2\|\phi\|_1^2.
  \]
  For every bounded real random variable $\Xi$ on the fixed source space,
  $V\mapsto\E[\Xi V]$ is a continuous functional on $L^2$. It therefore commutes
  with the Bochner integral, while scalar Fubini is justified by $\|\Xi\|_\infty
  Q_t\|\phi\|_1$. Consequently,
  \[
    \E[\Xi B_\phi]
    =\int\phi(x)\E[\Xi u_\rho^m(t,x)]\ud x
    =\E[\Xi C_\phi].
  \]
  Both averages are real $L^2$ variables. Taking $\Xi =
  \operatorname{sign}(B_\phi-C_\phi)$ yields $\E|B_\phi-C_\phi| = 0$, so the two
  averages agree almost surely.

  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-gaussian-flow} now gives, for every finite $p$,
  \[
    \|B_\phi-Z_m(t,\phi)\|_{L^p}
    \le\|O_\phi\|\|e_0\|
    \big\|\|P_{A^\rho}-P_A\|_\infty\big\|_{L^p}
    \longrightarrow0.
  \]
  This is~\eqref{E:constant-tested-limit}, including its $L^2$ case, for the
  masked Bochner average, with arbitrary relative source-width rates. It
  supplies the conditional convergence premise of
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-canonical-transfer} at the same fixed mask, horizon,
  original covariance and observable. Since $\phi \ge 0$ and $\|\phi\|_1 = 1$,
  that proposition gives \eqref{E:constant-original-point-transfer}. Zero
  covariance gives the free constant solution and equality. All source limits
  are at fixed mask and horizon, before time asymptotics. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-raw-average}.
\end{proof}

So far the operator path comes from the noise alone.
Section~\ref{S:compact-mean-family} also needs a deterministic potential.

\begin{proposition}[Compact deterministic base]\label{P:constant-compact-base}
  Let $h \in C([0,T];C_c^\infty(K))$ be real for one fixed compact $K$, with all
  spatial derivatives continuous on that support. The statewise primitive
  \[
    A_h(t)=\int_0^t iS(-r)B_F(h(r))S_F(r)\ud r
  \]
  is Lipschitz in $\mathcal{B}(F)$, takes values with range in $iE$, and has a
  bound $C_T\sup_r(\|h(r)\|_\infty+\|h(r)\|_2)$. The flow conclusions above,
  including moments, source convergence and ordinary-source identification, hold
  for $A_h+gA$ and $A_h+gA^\rho$, uniformly on bounded displayed deterministic
  bounds and bounded real $g$. No Cameron-shift identity is asserted here.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The estimate for $B_F$ and the strong free groups prove the primitive and its
  Lipschitz bound exactly as in Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-response}. If
  $\|A_h\|_{C^\theta} \le H$, $|g| \le G$ and $X = \|A\|_{C^\theta}$, then
  $\|A_h+gA\|_{C^\theta} \le H+GX$. If $\E e^{cX^2}$ is bounded, choose $c' \le
  c/(2(1+G^2))$ to obtain
  \[
    \E e^{c'\|A_h+gA\|_{C^\theta}^2}
    \le e^{2c'H^2}\E e^{2c'G^2X^2}<\infty.
  \]
  Alternatively $pC(1+H+Gx)^{1/\theta} \le cx^2+C_{p,H,G,c}$ transfers the Young
  growth estimate directly to all finite moments. Source operator-path
  convergence, local Young stability and uniform integrability give the same
  flow convergence. The state-integral and Picard-to-chain arguments apply to
  $h+gmY_\rho$, whose spatial support lies in one fixed compact union. These are
  individual estimates and versions, without an uncountable common-parameter
  event. Only $h = 0$, $g = 1$ enters
  \eqref{E:constant-original-point-transfer}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-compact-base}.
\end{proof}

\section{Covariance bounds for physically masked wave fields}
\label{S:masked-covariance}

\input{notes/figures/mask-window-figure}

We establish the covariance estimates needed for the operator construction.
Throughout this section, Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} holds, $a = \alpha_0$, and
$\Gamma \ne 0$. For zero spatial covariance, the spatial spectral measures and
noise variances vanish; the purely temporal estimates remain valid independently
of $\Gamma$. We use the unitary Fourier transform
\[
  \widehat{f}(\xi) = c_d\int_{\R^d}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}f(x)\ud x,
  \qquad c_d = (2\pi)^{-d/2}.
\]
The spectral convention in Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} means that, for every
complex Schwartz test $\varphi$,
\begin{equation}\label{E:masked-spectral-convention}
  \int_{\R^d}\varphi(x)\Gamma(\ud x)
  = \int_{\R^d}\left(\int_{\R^d}e^{i\xi\cdot x}
  \varphi(x)\ud x\right)\mu(\ud\xi).
\end{equation}
This is an identity of distribution pairings. We will not interchange two
infinite measures in the associated oscillatory expression.

A positive tempered Radon measure has polynomial mass growth. Indeed, choose a
nonnegative compact smooth test that is at least one on the unit ball, and apply
a defining Schwartz seminorm bound for the distribution to its dilations. Its
mass on a ball of radius $R \ge 1$ is bounded by a power of $R$. Summation over
dyadic shells then gives integrability of $(1+|x|)^{-N}$ for some $N$, and hence
absolute integrability of every Schwartz test. These facts apply to both
$\Gamma$ and $\mu$. Smooth cutoffs converge in the Schwartz topology, so these
integrals agree with the tempered pairings.

\subsection{Translated spectral growth and physical masks}

\LeanMarker{P:masked-spectral-growth}
\begin{proposition}[Translated spectral mass]\label{P:masked-spectral-growth}
  Put
  \[
    M_\Gamma = \int_{\R^d}e^{-|x|^2/4}\Gamma(\ud x),
    \qquad C_\Gamma = \frac{eM_\Gamma}{(2\sqrt{\pi})^d}.
  \]
  Then $0 < M_\Gamma < \infty$, and for $y \in \R^d$ and $r > 0$,
  \begin{align}
    \int_{\R^d}e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}\mu(\ud\xi)
    &= \left(\frac{r}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\right)^d
    \int_{\R^d}e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{-iy\cdot x}\Gamma(\ud x),
    \label{E:masked-gaussian-transform}\\
    0 \le \int_{\R^d}e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}\mu(\ud\xi)
    &\le \frac{r^\alpha M_\Gamma}{(2\sqrt{\pi})^d},
    \qquad \mu(B(y,r)) \le C_\Gamma r^\alpha.
    \label{E:masked-translated-ball}
  \end{align}
  The ball may be open or closed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Temperedness gives finiteness of $M_\Gamma$. Its positivity follows from
  $\Gamma \ne 0$. To check the Fourier constant, set $J(b) = \int_\R
  e^{-Ax^2}e^{ibx}\ud x$ for $A > 0$. Squaring the real Gaussian integral and
  using polar coordinates gives $J(0) = \sqrt{\pi/A}$. Differentiation and
  integration by parts give $J'(b) = -bJ(b)/(2A)$. Therefore, $J(b) =
  \sqrt{\pi/A}e^{-b^2/(4A)}$. Taking products over the coordinates gives
  \[
    \int_{\R^d}e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{i(\xi-y)\cdot x}\ud x
    = \left(\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}r\right)^d e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}.
  \]
  Apply~\eqref{E:masked-spectral-convention} to the single Schwartz test
  $e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}e^{-iy\cdot x}$ to obtain \eqref{E:masked-gaussian-transform}.
  Its left side is nonnegative. Taking the absolute value on the right removes
  the complex phase because $\Gamma$ is nonnegative. The pushforward of $\Gamma$
  by $x \mapsto rx$ assigns mass $r^{\alpha-d}\Gamma(A)$ to a Borel set $A$.
  Thus,
  \[
    \int e^{-r^2|x|^2/4}\Gamma(\ud x) = r^{\alpha-d}M_\Gamma.
  \]
  This proves the Gaussian bound. On either ball $B(y,r)$ the integrand
  $e^{-|\xi-y|^2/r^2}$ is at least $e^{-1}$, proving the mass bound. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-spectral-growth}.
\end{proof}

Choose and fix a real nonzero Schwartz function $\zeta$. For $R > 0$ put
$\zeta_R(x) = \zeta(x/R)$, $k_R = \widehat{\zeta_R}$ and $L =
\|\widehat{\zeta}\|_1$. Then $k_R(u) = R^d\widehat{\zeta}(Ru)$ and $\|k_R\|_1 =
L > 0$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{E:masked-auxiliary-measure}
  \nu_R(\ud\eta) = \rho_R(\eta)\ud\eta,
  \qquad \rho_R(\eta) = c_d^2L\int |k_R(\xi-\eta)|\mu(\ud\xi).
\end{equation}

\LeanMarker{P:masked-auxiliary-norm}
\begin{proposition}[Auxiliary mask norm]\label{P:masked-auxiliary-norm}
  The measure $\nu_R$ is even, tempered and Radon, with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-auxiliary-growth}
    \nu_R(B(y,r)) \le c_d^2L^2C_\Gamma r^\alpha
  \end{equation}
  for all $y \in \R^d$ and $r > 0$, uniformly in $R > 0$. If $H$ is a separable
  complex Hilbert space and $F$ is an $H$-valued Schwartz function, then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-hilbert-fourier}
    \int \|\widehat{\zeta_RF}(\xi)\|_H^2\mu(\ud\xi)
    \le \int \|\widehat{F}(\eta)\|_H^2\nu_R(\ud\eta).
  \end{equation}
  For a real $H$, the same assertion holds after complexification.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Schwartz decay of $k_R$ and polynomial growth of $\mu$ show that
  $\rho_R(\eta)$ is finite at every $\eta$. On compact sets of $\eta$, the
  integrands have a common integrable bound. Dominated convergence therefore
  makes $\rho_R$ continuous, and $\nu_R$ is Radon. Since $\zeta$ is real,
  $k_R(-u) = \overline{k_R(u)}$. Evenness of $\mu$ gives evenness of $\rho_R$.
  Tonelli's theorem gives
  \[
    \nu_R(B(y,r)) = c_d^2L\int |k_R(u)|\mu(B(y+u,r))\ud u.
  \]
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-spectral-growth} and $\|k_R\|_1 = L$ give
  \eqref{E:masked-auxiliary-growth}. Polynomial growth on centered balls proves
  temperedness by the same dyadic argument as above.

  Bochner Fourier integration gives
  \[
    \widehat{\zeta_RF}(\xi)
    = c_d\int k_R(\xi-\eta)\widehat{F}(\eta)\ud\eta.
  \]
  For scalar Schwartz functions this follows by Fourier inversion and absolute
  integrability. It follows next for finite-rank Hilbert-valued functions. For
  general $F$, take projections onto the successive spans of a countable
  orthonormal basis of its separable range closure. They converge pointwise in
  norm. The Schwartz bounds on $F$ dominate the Fourier integrals, proving the
  identity by dominated convergence. In a finite-dimensional space the full
  identity projection suffices; the zero space is immediate. The triangle
  inequality and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz yield
  \[
    \|\widehat{\zeta_RF}(\xi)\|_H^2
    \le c_d^2L\int |k_R(\xi-\eta)|\|\widehat{F}(\eta)\|_H^2\ud\eta.
  \]
  Integrating against $\mu$ and applying Tonelli's theorem proves
  \eqref{E:masked-hilbert-fourier}. Its right side is finite by Schwartz decay
  and temperedness of $\nu_R$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-auxiliary-norm}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Temporal completion and the original noise}

The noise in~\eqref{E:cov} is also correlated in time, with kernel
$|t-s|^{-a}$. We now complete the temporal tests in that form.

\LeanMarker{L:masked-time-completion}
\begin{lemma}[Temporal covariance completion]\label{L:masked-time-completion}
  Let $T > 0$. The form
  \[
    \langle f,g\rangle_a
    = \int_0^T\int_0^T f(t)g(s)|t-s|^{-a}\ud t\ud s
  \]
  is positive semidefinite on real compact smooth tests. Its null-quotient
  completion $H_a$ is separable. The inclusion of tests extends continuously
  from $L^2(0,T)$, with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-time-schur}
    \LeanEqMarker{E:masked-time-schur}
    \|f\|_a^2 \le \frac{2T^{1-a}}{1-a}\|f\|_2^2.
  \end{equation}
  In its complexification the norm is given by the same double integral with
  $f(t)\overline{f(s)}$. In particular, bounded measurable amplitudes on a
  subinterval, extended by zero, belong to this completion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Write $\Gamma_E$ for Euler's Gamma function. Away from the null diagonal,
  \[
    |t-s|^{-a} = \frac1{\Gamma_E(a)}\int_0^\infty
    \lambda^{a-1}e^{-\lambda|t-s|}\ud\lambda.
  \]
  This follows by substitution in the defining Gamma integral. Moreover,
  \[
    e^{-\lambda|t-s|}
    = \int_{-\infty}^{\min(t,s)}2\lambda
    e^{-\lambda(t-u)}e^{-\lambda(s-u)}\ud u.
  \]
  For each $\lambda > 0$ its quadratic form is an integral of a square. All
  exchanges for compact smooth tests are absolutely integrable because the
  kernel row integral is at most $2T^{1-a}/(1-a)$. The same bound and $2|xy| \le
  |x|^2+|y|^2$ prove~\eqref{E:masked-time-schur}. A countable $L^2$-dense family
  of compact smooth tests is consequently dense in $H_a$, proving separability.
  The complex quadratic form is the sum of those of the real and imaginary
  parts, since symmetry cancels the imaginary cross term. Thus, the same bound
  holds after complexification. Smooth density in $L^2$, together with the Schur
  bound on the absolute bilinear form, extends the inclusion and the integral
  identity to $L^2$. This includes the stated interval amplitudes. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-time-completion}.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal{D}$ consist of finite sums $F(t,x) = \sum_j\tau_j(t)f_j(x)$ with
real $\tau_j \in C_c^\infty(0,T)$ and real Schwartz $f_j$. These give
$H_a$-valued Schwartz functions of $x$. Let $W$ denote the original real
isonormal noise in~\eqref{E:cov}.

\LeanMarker{P:masked-noise-completion}
\begin{proposition}[Completion on the same noise]\label{P:masked-noise-completion}
  The space $\mathcal{D}$ belongs to the original test-noise completion and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-actual-noise-norm}
    \E W(F)^2 = (2\pi)^d\int
    \|\widehat{F}(\xi)\|_{H_a^{\mathbb{C}}}^2\mu(\ud\xi).
  \end{equation}
  Define the auxiliary seminorm
  \[
    N_R(F)^2 = (2\pi)^d\int
    \|\widehat{F}(\eta)\|_{H_a^{\mathbb{C}}}^2\nu_R(\ud\eta).
  \]
  Then $F \mapsto W(\zeta_RF)$ extends as a contraction from the null-quotient
  completion for $N_R$ into the original $L^2(\Omega)$. The extension is
  centered and jointly Gaussian. For finitely many real $F_1,\ldots,F_m \in
  \mathcal{D}$, their masked covariance matrix is bounded in positive
  semidefinite order by the matrix of auxiliary inner products. This gives
  covariance comparison for every finite linear Gaussian image and every convex
  norm power $p \ge 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For real spatial Schwartz $f,g$, put $h(z) = \int f(y+z)g(y)\ud y$. Fourier
  inversion and Parseval give
  \[
    h(z) = \int e^{i\xi\cdot z}\widehat{f}(\xi)
    \overline{\widehat{g}(\xi)}\ud\xi.
  \]
  Therefore,
  \[
    \int e^{i\xi\cdot z}h(z)\ud z
    = (2\pi)^d\widehat{f}(-\xi)\overline{\widehat{g}(-\xi)}.
  \]
  Apply~\eqref{E:masked-spectral-convention} to the single test $h$ and reflect
  $\xi$ using evenness of $\mu$. The spatial covariance is thus
  $(2\pi)^d\int\widehat{f}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi)}\mu(\ud\xi)$. Summing
  over the finitely many temporal factors gives
  \eqref{E:masked-actual-noise-norm}. Schwartz decay, temperedness and temporal
  integrability justify these pairings. If $W$ is initially given only on
  compact spatial tests, smooth expanding cutoffs converge in the Schwartz
  topology. The spectral form is continuous in that topology, admitting
  $\mathcal{D}$ in the same completion.

  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-auxiliary-norm}, applied with $H =
  H_a^{\mathbb{C}}$, gives $\E W(\zeta_RF)^2 \le N_R(F)^2$. It annihilates
  auxiliary-null tests. An auxiliary Cauchy sequence thus gives a Cauchy
  sequence of noise evaluations in $L^2(\Omega)$, proving the contraction
  extension and its uniqueness. Every finite linear combination of the limits is
  an $L^2$ limit of centered Gaussian variables. Their variances converge, so
  their characteristic functions prove that the limits remain jointly centered
  Gaussian.

  For the finite family let $A$ be the covariance matrix and $B$ the auxiliary
  Gram matrix. Applying the norm inequality to $\sum_i b_iF_i$ gives
  $b^{\mathsf{T}}Ab \le b^{\mathsf{T}}Bb$ for every real vector $b$.
  Diagonalizing $B-A$ constructs a centered Gaussian vector $Z$ of that
  covariance, independent of the evaluation vector $X$, on a product space. Then
  $X+Z$ has covariance $B$. Conditional Jensen gives $\E\|X\|^p \le \E\|X+Z\|^p$
  for every norm and $p \ge 1$. Finite Gaussian moments ensure finiteness of
  both sides. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:masked-noise-completion}.
\end{proof}

The auxiliary completion depends on $R$ and $\zeta$. Its construction does not
define multiplication by $\zeta_R$ on the original noise quotient. Nor does the
finite linear Gaussian comparison assert a covariance comparison for nonlinear
wave solutions. The auxiliary and original test norms need not dominate one
another. No spatial representative for every auxiliary class, nonnegative
physical inverse covariance for $\nu_R$, or uniform density bound in $R$ is
asserted.

\LeanMarker{P:masked-quotient-obstruction}
\begin{proposition}[A physical mask need not preserve noise-null tests]
  \label{P:masked-quotient-obstruction}
  In the admissible case $d = 2$, $\alpha = 1$, $0 < a < 1$ and $\Gamma =
  \delta_0(\ud x_1)\ud x_2$, there is a real compact smooth mask $\zeta$ such
  that no finite $C$ satisfies $\E W(\zeta F)^2 \le C\E W(F)^2$ for every
  compact smooth test $F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Fourier inversion on Schwartz tests gives $\mu =
  (2\pi)^{-1}\ud\xi_1\delta_0(\ud\xi_2)$ in the stated spectral convention. The
  measure $\Gamma$ is nonnegative, even, positive definite and tempered, with
  $\Gamma(cA) = c\Gamma(A)$. It therefore satisfies Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}
  with $\alpha = 1$. This is not spatial white noise in dimension two. For real
  compact smooth $g$, its spatial covariance is, by ordinary Fubini,
  \[
    V(g) = \int_\R\left(\int_\R g(x_1,x_2)\ud x_2\right)^2\ud x_1.
  \]
  Choose real even $\chi \in C_c^\infty(\R)$ equal to one on $[-1,1]$ and zero
  outside $[-2,2]$. For example, on $1 < r = |x| < 2$ use
  \[
    \chi(x) = \frac{e^{-1/(2-r)}}{e^{-1/(2-r)}+e^{-1/(r-1)}}.
  \]
  The endpoint exponentials vanish to every order, so the plateau pastes
  smoothly. Let $I_\chi = \int(\chi')^2 > 0$ and $J_\chi = \int\chi^4 > 0$.
  Positivity of $I_\chi$ follows because $\chi$ is not constant, and $J_\chi \ge
  2$. Put $g(x) = \chi(x_1)\chi''(x_2)$ and $\zeta(x) = \chi(x_1)\chi(x_2)$.
  Their unmasked marginal vanishes since $\int\chi'' = 0$, whereas integration
  by parts gives the masked marginal $-I_\chi\chi(x_1)^2$. Thus, $V(g) = 0$ and
  $V(\zeta g) = J_\chi I_\chi^2 > 0$. For a nonzero nonnegative $\tau \in
  C_c^\infty(0,T)$, set $F(t,x) = \tau(t)g(x)$. The temporal factor
  $\|\tau\|_a^2$ is finite and strictly positive because its nonnegative
  integrand is positive on a set of positive two-dimensional measure.
  Consequently, the full unmasked variance is zero and the masked variance is
  positive. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:masked-quotient-obstruction}.
\end{proof}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-22} records the quotient interpretation of this witness:
the mask separates two representatives of the same noise-null class.
This is why we mask tests before constructing the auxiliary completion.


\subsection{Temporal modulation and continuous wave factors}

Write $\langle x\rangle = \sqrt{1+x^2}$. Let $I = [s,s+h] \subset [0,T]$, with
$0 < h \le 1$. Norms below are those of the complexified temporal completion in
Lemma~\ref{L:masked-time-completion}.

\LeanMarker{L:masked-temporal-modulation}
\begin{lemma}[Modulated amplitudes]\label{L:masked-temporal-modulation}
  Let $A \in C^1(I;\mathbb{C})$, $M = \sup_I|A|$ and $D = \sup_I|A'|$. For every
  real $\omega$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-temporal-modulation}
    \|1_IA(t)e^{i\omega t}\|_a^2
    \le C_0(a)h^{2-a}(M+hD)^2\langle h\omega\rangle^{a-1},
    \qquad C_0(a) = \frac{16}{1-a}.
  \end{equation}
  The bound is uniform in $T,s,h,A$ and $\omega$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Put $c(v) = \int_s^{s+h-v}A(u+v)\overline{A(u)}\ud u$ for $0 \le v \le h$.
  Differentiation at the moving endpoint gives
  \[
    c'(v) = -A(s+h)\overline{A(s+h-v)}
    +\int_s^{s+h-v}A'(u+v)\overline{A(u)}\ud u.
  \]
  Thus, $c(h) = 0$, $|c(v)| \le hM^2$ and $|c'(v)| \le M^2+hMD$. Splitting the
  covariance square into its two conjugate triangles gives
  \[
    \|1_IA(t)e^{i\omega t}\|_a^2
    = 2\operatorname{Re}\int_0^h v^{-a}e^{i\omega v}c(v)\ud v
    = 2h^{2-a}\operatorname{Re}\int_0^1 y^{-a}e^{i\omega hy}f(y)\ud y,
  \]
  where $f(y) = c(hy)/h$. Set $K = M+hD$. Then $f(1) = 0$ and $|f|,|f'| \le
  K^2$. If $K = 0$ the assertion is immediate. If $|\omega h| \le 1$, the last
  integral is at most $K^2/(1-a)$ in absolute value, and $\langle\omega
  h\rangle^{a-1} \ge 2^{(a-1)/2}$. The claimed constant is sufficient in this
  case.

  Now suppose $k = |\omega h| > 1$. On $[0,1/k]$ the integral is at most
  $K^2k^{a-1}/(1-a)$. On $[1/k,1]$, integrate the exponential by parts. The
  endpoint at one vanishes, while the other endpoint contributes at most
  $K^2k^{a-1}$. The derivative integral, divided by $k$, is bounded by
  \[
    \frac{K^2}k\left(a\int_{1/k}^1 y^{-a-1}\ud y
    +\int_{1/k}^1 y^{-a}\ud y\right)
    \le K^2 k^{a-1}\left(1+\frac1{1-a}\right).
  \]
  Here $k^{-1} \le k^{a-1}$ since $a > 0$. The total is at most
  $4K^2k^{a-1}/(1-a)$. Finally, $k^{a-1} \le \sqrt{2}\langle\omega
  h\rangle^{a-1}$ and $8\sqrt{2} \le 16$. Including the factor $2h^{2-a}$ proves
  the bound for either sign of $\omega$. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-temporal-modulation}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:masked-temporal-modulation} treats one exponential mode. A
product of two sines or cosines is a sum of four such modes, which we bound
next.

\LeanMarker{L:masked-trig-products}
\begin{lemma}[Correlated trigonometric products]\label{L:masked-trig-products}
  Let $r,\rho \ge 0$. Let $P_r$ be either $\cos(rt)$ or $\sin(rt)$, and let
  $Q_\rho$ be either $\cos(\rho t)$ or $\sin(\rho t)$. With the amplitude and
  constant above,
  \begin{align}
    \|1_IAP_rQ_\rho\|_a^2
    &\le C_0(a)h^{2-a}(M+hD)^2\mathcal{Q}_h(r,\rho),
    \label{E:masked-trig-products}\\
    \mathcal{Q}_h(r,\rho)
    &:= \langle h(r-\rho)\rangle^{a-1}
    +\langle h(r+\rho)\rangle^{a-1}.
    \label{E:masked-frequency-pair}
  \end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Expanding the two trigonometric factors gives four complex exponential modes
  with frequencies $\sigma r+\tau\rho$, $\sigma,\tau \in \{-1,1\}$, and
  coefficients of absolute value $1/4$. In a Hilbert space,
  \[
    \left\|\sum_j c_jv_j\right\|^2
    \le \left(\sum_j|c_j|\right)\sum_j|c_j|\|v_j\|^2.
  \]
  The first coefficient sum is one. Apply
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-temporal-modulation} to each mode. Two frequencies have
  absolute value $|r-\rho|$ and two have absolute value $r+\rho$. This gives one
  half of the asserted right side, and hence the bound. Repeated or zero
  frequencies are permitted; no orthogonality or independence of the modes is
  used. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:masked-trig-products}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:masked-continuous-wave}
\begin{lemma}[Continuous sine factors]\label{L:masked-continuous-wave}
  Define $c_r(t) = \cos(rt)$ and
  \[
    b_r(t) = \langle r\rangle\frac{\sin(rt)}r\quad(r > 0),
    \qquad b_0(t) = t.
  \]
  For $F_r \in \{c_r,b_r\}$ and $G_\rho \in \{c_\rho,b_\rho\}$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:masked-continuous-wave}
    \|1_IF_rG_\rho\|_a^2
    \le C_1(a)(1+T)^4h^{2-a}\mathcal{Q}_h(r,\rho),
    \qquad C_1(a) = 4\cdot3^{1-a}C_0(a).
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  For $r \ge 1$, $b_r$ is a sine times a coefficient at most $\sqrt{2}$. For $0
  \le r \le 1$, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
  \[
    b_r(t) = t\langle r\rangle\int_0^1\cos(vrt)\ud v.
  \]
  This includes $r = 0$. Hence, a product $F_rG_\rho$ is a scalar coefficient of
  absolute value at most two times a normalized integral of
  $t^jP_{r'}(t)Q_{\rho'}(t)$, where $j \in \{0,1,2\}$ and $|r'-r|,|\rho'-\rho|
  \le 1$. The integral is over zero, one or two copies of $[0,1]$; with no
  averages, use a one-point probability space. Its parameter dependence is
  continuous in $L^2(0,T)$ by dominated convergence.
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-time-completion} makes it continuous in $H_a$ as well.
  Thus, the averages are Bochner integrals, and their $H_a$ images agree with
  the pointwise averages by continuity of the inclusion. Hilbert Jensen bounds
  the squared norm of an average by the average of the squared norms.

  Apply Lemma~\ref{L:masked-trig-products} to each integrand with $A(t) = t^j$.
  For $j = 0,1,2$, respectively, $M+hD$ is at most $1$, $T+1$ and $T^2+2T$. In
  every case $(M+hD)^2 \le (1+T)^4$. For real $x,e$, the triangle inequality and
  its reverse imply
  \[
    \frac{\langle x\rangle}{1+|e|}
    \le \langle x+e\rangle \le (1+|e|)\langle x\rangle.
  \]
  The changes in $h(r\pm\rho)$ have magnitude at most two, since $h \le 1$.
  Because $a-1 < 0$, each altered bracket to the power $a-1$ is at most
  $3^{1-a}$ times the original one. Apply these comparisons, Hilbert Jensen and
  the squared scalar coefficient bound four to obtain
  \eqref{E:masked-continuous-wave}. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:masked-continuous-wave}.
\end{proof}

These estimates concern covariance forms and temporal factors. Their application
to displacement and velocity is in Section~\ref{S:wave-variance}; the subsequent
operator, source and moment results require their separate proofs. They do not
follow from covariance domination alone.

\section{Finite Gaussian moments and whole-space Sobolev compactness}
\label{S:compact-gaussian}

We prove the finite Gaussian and compact-cutoff estimates needed to construct
the wave operator path. All traces and Schatten norms in this section are
unnormalized. The spatial states remain on $\R^d$, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$. Here $p$
is an auxiliary Schatten and Gaussian-moment index. The auxiliary periodic space
below is used only to factor a compactly supported multiplication operator. No
infinite Gaussian series or time path is constructed in this section.

\subsection{Finite Gaussian matrices with both variance powers}

\LeanMarker{L:gaussian-scalar-powers}
\begin{lemma}[Scalar power estimates]\label{L:gaussian-scalar-powers}
  For $0 < \theta < 1$ and $u,v \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:gaussian-weighted-power}
    u^\theta v^{1-\theta} \le \theta u+(1-\theta)v.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently, if $L > 0$ and $0 \le q \le L$ are integers, then for real
  $x,y$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:gaussian-integer-power}
    |x|^q|y|^{L-q}
    \le \frac qL|x|^L+\frac{L-q}L|y|^L.
  \end{equation}
  For nonnegative finite sequences, the usual H\"older inequality holds with
  exponents $m$ and $m/(m-1)$, $m > 1$. If $Z \ge 0$ has finite expectation $F$
  and $0 < r < 1$, then $\E Z^r \le F^r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  If $v > 0$, divide~\eqref{E:gaussian-weighted-power} by $v$ and put $t = u/v$.
  The function $\theta t+1-\theta-t^\theta$ decreases to zero at one and then
  increases, by its derivative $\theta(1-t^{\theta-1})$. Continuity covers $t =
  0$; if $v = 0$ the inequality is immediate. For $0 < q < L$, apply this with
  $u = |x|^L$, $v = |y|^L$ and $\theta = q/L$. The endpoint cases $q = 0,L$ are
  identities, with the ordinary integer convention $a^0 = 1$.

  To prove finite H\"older, divide the two sequences by their respective
  $\ell^m$ and $\ell^{m/(m-1)}$ norms. If either norm vanishes, the result is
  immediate. Apply~\eqref{E:gaussian-weighted-power} with $\theta = 1/m$ to the
  corresponding normalized powers and sum; the sum of products is at most one.
  Undo the normalization. Finally, $F = 0$ gives $Z = 0$ almost surely. If $F >
  0$, apply \eqref{E:gaussian-weighted-power} to $u = Z/F$, $v = 1$ and $\theta
  = r$, and integrate. The right side has expectation one. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:gaussian-scalar-powers}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:gaussian-trace-bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Two Hermitian trace bounds]\label{L:gaussian-trace-bounds}
  Let $B,Y$ be finite Hermitian matrices, $m \ge 2$ an integer, and $L = 2m-2$.
  For integers $0 \le q \le L$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:gaussian-trace-word}
    \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(BY^qBY^{L-q})
    \le \operatorname{Tr}(B^2|Y|^L).
  \end{equation}
  If $D \ge 0$ is Hermitian, then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:gaussian-trace-holder}
    \operatorname{Tr}(D|Y|^{2m-2})
    \le (\operatorname{Tr}D^m)^{1/m}
    (\operatorname{Tr}|Y|^{2m})^{(m-1)/m}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Diagonalize $Y$, with real eigenvalues $\lambda_i$, and write $b_{ij}$ for the
  entries of $B$ in that basis. Hermitian symmetry gives $b_{ji} =
  \overline{b_{ij}}$. The trace word is the real sum
  \[
    \sum_{i,j}|b_{ij}|^2\lambda_j^q\lambda_i^{L-q}.
  \]
  Bound each product by its absolute value and use
  \eqref{E:gaussian-integer-power}. After interchanging $i,j$, the two resulting
  sums agree because $|b_{ij}| = |b_{ji}|$. Each equals
  $\sum_{i,j}|b_{ij}|^2|\lambda_i|^L = \operatorname{Tr}(B^2|Y|^L)$. The two
  weights sum to one, proving \eqref{E:gaussian-trace-word}. No commutation of
  $B$ and $Y$ is used.

  In the same basis put $d_i = D_{ii} \ge 0$. If $\theta_j$ are the nonnegative
  eigenvalues of $D$, a unitary change of basis gives $d_i =
  \sum_j|U_{ij}|^2\theta_j$. Both row and column sums of $|U_{ij}|^2$ equal one.
  Finite convexity therefore yields
  \[
    \sum_i d_i^m \le \sum_{i,j}|U_{ij}|^2\theta_j^m
    = \operatorname{Tr}D^m.
  \]
  For completeness, the convexity step follows from $t^m \ge s^m+ms^{m-1}(t-s)$
  for $s,t \ge 0$, obtained by differentiating the difference. Average this
  inequality with $s$ equal to the weighted mean. Now apply finite H\"older from
  Lemma~\ref{L:gaussian-scalar-powers} to $\sum_i d_i|\lambda_i|^{2m-2}$. Since
  $(2m-2)m/(m-1) = 2m$, the last display proves \eqref{E:gaussian-trace-holder},
  including zero sums. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:gaussian-trace-bounds}.
\end{proof}

The estimate below is a finite-dimensional Gaussian noncommutative Khintchine
inequality. For its origins in the work of Lust-Piquard and a Gaussian
integration-by-parts proof, see Tropp~\cite{tropp:source2018:second-order}*{arXiv:1504.05919v2,
Proposition~2.3 and Section~7}. We include the proof and Hermitian dilation
to fix the normalization and retain both variance operators.

\LeanMarker{P:finite-gaussian-matrix}
\begin{proposition}[Finite Gaussian matrix moment]\label{P:finite-gaussian-matrix}
  Let $A_1,\ldots,A_N$ be complex $o$-by-$n$ matrices, with $o,n,N \ge 1$, and
  let $g_k$ be independent real standard Gaussians. Put
  \[
    X=\sum_k g_kA_k,\qquad V_{\rm out}=\sum_k A_kA_k^*,
    \qquad V_{\rm in}=\sum_k A_k^*A_k.
  \]
  For even $p = 2m \ge 2$, with $\|A\|_{S_p}^p = \operatorname{Tr}((A^*A)^m)$,
  \begin{align}
    (\E\|X\|_{S_p}^p)^{1/p}
    &\le \sqrt{p-1}\left(
    \frac{\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^m+
    \operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^m}{2}\right)^{1/p}
    \label{E:finite-gaussian-average}\\
    &\le \sqrt{p-1}\max\left(
    \|V_{\rm out}^{1/2}\|_{S_p},\|V_{\rm in}^{1/2}\|_{S_p}\right).
    \label{E:finite-gaussian-maximum}
  \end{align}
  The constants are independent of $o,n,N$. The first inequality is an equality
  at $p = 2$. Empty or zero families give the zero identity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  First suppose $B_k$ are Hermitian and put $Y = \sum_k g_kB_k$ and $D = \sum_k
  B_k^2$. All polynomial moments are finite because Gaussian decay dominates
  every fixed power. Componentwise integration by parts against the product
  Gaussian density gives $\E[g_kP(g)] = \E[\partial_kP(g)]$, with zero boundary
  terms. It applies to complex polynomials through their real and imaginary
  parts; absolute polynomial integrability justifies Fubini.

  For $m \ge 2$, put $F = \E\operatorname{Tr}Y^{2m}$ and $L = 2m-2$. Write
  $\operatorname{Tr}Y^{2m} = \sum_k g_k \operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^{2m-1})$ and
  differentiate the $2m-1$ factors in their order. This gives exactly
  \[
    F=\sum_k\sum_{q=0}^{L}
    \E\operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^qB_kY^{L-q}).
  \]
  The eigenbasis calculation above shows these traces are real. The
  differentiated polynomial is real by conjugation and trace cyclicity. No
  derivative of a spectral absolute value or measurable choice of an eigenbasis
  is needed. The two trace bounds give
  \begin{align*}
    F&\le (2m-1)\E\operatorname{Tr}(D|Y|^{2m-2})\\
    &\le (2m-1)(\operatorname{Tr}D^m)^{1/m}
    \E(\operatorname{Tr}|Y|^{2m})^{(m-1)/m}\\
    &\le (2m-1)(\operatorname{Tr}D^m)^{1/m}F^{(m-1)/m}.
  \end{align*}
  Here $\operatorname{Tr}|Y|^{2m} = \operatorname{Tr}Y^{2m}$, and the last
  inequality is the expectation bound in Lemma~\ref{L:gaussian-scalar-powers}.
  If $F = 0$ the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise divide by its positive
  fractional power and raise to $m$ to obtain
  \begin{equation}\label{E:finite-hermitian-gaussian}
    \E\operatorname{Tr}Y^{2m} \le (2m-1)^m\operatorname{Tr}D^m.
  \end{equation}
  For $m = 1$, independence gives directly $\E\operatorname{Tr}Y^2 =
  \sum_k\operatorname{Tr}B_k^2 = \operatorname{Tr}D$, proving the same formula
  as equality.

  For rectangular $A_k$, use the Hermitian dilation
  \[
    B_k=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_k\\A_k^*&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\X^*&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    D=\operatorname{diag}(V_{\rm out},V_{\rm in}).
  \]
  Finite entrywise summation proves $\operatorname{Tr}(UV) =
  \operatorname{Tr}(VU)$ for compatible rectangular matrices. Since $(XX^*)^m =
  X(X^*X)^{m-1}X^*$, it follows that
  \[
    \operatorname{Tr}Y^{2m}=2\operatorname{Tr}((X^*X)^m),\qquad
    \operatorname{Tr}D^m=\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^m+
    \operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^m.
  \]
  Substitute in~\eqref{E:finite-hermitian-gaussian}, divide by two and take the
  $p$th root. This proves~\eqref{E:finite-gaussian-average}. For positive $V$,
  spectral diagonalization gives $\|V^{1/2}\|_{S_p}^p = \operatorname{Tr}V^m$,
  and the average of two nonnegative numbers is bounded by their maximum. At $p
  = 2$, $\E\operatorname{Tr}X^*X = \operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in} =
  \operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}$, proving the claimed equality. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:finite-gaussian-matrix}.
\end{proof}

Both variance directions are needed: the row-matrix example in
No-go~\ref{P:nogo-23} rules out a dimension-independent estimate that
keeps only one of them.


\LeanMarker{R:gaussian-one-variance-test}
\begin{remark}[Both variance directions are necessary]
  \label{R:gaussian-one-variance-test}
  Let $n = 16$ and $A_k = e_k^{\mathsf{T}}$ be the coordinate row matrices. The
  only nonzero singular value of $X$ is $(\sum_k g_k^2)^{1/2}$. Gaussian
  integration by parts gives $\E g^2 = 1$, $\E g^4 = 3$, and hence
  \[
    \E\|X\|_{S_4}^4=3n+n(n-1)=n(n+2)=288.
  \]
  Here $V_{\rm out} = n$ and $V_{\rm in} = I_n$. Keeping only the input variance
  in~\eqref{E:finite-gaussian-maximum} would give fourth power
  $9\operatorname{Tr}I_n^2 = 144$, which is false. Transposing the family
  refutes the corresponding output-only bound. The average and maximum bounds
  have fourth powers $1224$ and $2304$, respectively. This finite example
  asserts nothing about a specialized wave estimate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Whole-space cutoffs through an auxiliary periodic space}

Use the unitary Fourier transform with $c_d = (2\pi)^{-d/2}$ and $\Lambda =
(1-\Delta)^{1/2}$. For real $r$, the norm on $H^r(\R^d)$ is
$\|\langle\xi\rangle^r\widehat{u}\|_2$. The deterministic cutoff arguments allow
any smoothing order $\gamma > 0$. Fix real $\sigma$ and a real compact smooth
$\chi$. Singular values $s_j$, $j \ge 1$, are counted with multiplicity, with
zeros appended when necessary. For compact $K$, put $\|K\|_{S_p}^p = \sum_j
s_j(K)^p$.

\begin{lemma}[Cutoff factorization]\label{L:cutoff-factorization}
  Choose a cube $Q_L = (-L/2,L/2)^d$ containing $\operatorname{supp}\chi$ in its
  interior, and a real $\psi \in C_c^\infty(Q_L)$ equal to one near
  $\operatorname{supp}\chi$. On the auxiliary torus $\mathbb{T}_L$ use Lebesgue
  measure and the orthonormal basis
  \[
    e_k(x)=L^{-d/2}e^{i\kappa_k\cdot x},\qquad
    \kappa_k=2\pi k/L,\qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}^d,
  \]
  with $\|v\|_{H^r(\mathbb{T}_L)}^2 = \sum_k\langle\kappa_k\rangle^{2r}|v_k|^2$.
  Periodization of $\psi u$ and multiplication of a periodic function by $\chi$
  extend to bounded maps
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cutoff-periodic-maps}
    \mathcal{P}_\psi:H^r(\R^d)\to H^r(\mathbb{T}_L),\qquad
    \mathcal{E}_\chi:H^r(\mathbb{T}_L)\to H^r(\R^d)
  \end{equation}
  for every real $r$. If $\mathcal{I}_\gamma$ denotes the identity inclusion
  from $H^{\sigma+\gamma}(\mathbb{T}_L)$ to $H^\sigma(\mathbb{T}_L)$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cutoff-whole-space-recovery}
    \mathcal{E}_\chi\mathcal{I}_\gamma\mathcal{P}_\psi u=\chi u,
    \qquad u \in H^{\sigma+\gamma}(\R^d).
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  For $x,y \in \R^d$ and real $r$, the elementary inequality $\langle x\rangle
  \le \sqrt{2}\langle y\rangle\langle x-y\rangle$ gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cutoff-peetre}
    \left(\frac{\langle x\rangle}{\langle y\rangle}\right)^r
    \le 2^{|r|/2}\langle x-y\rangle^{|r|}.
  \end{equation}
  For negative $r$, interchange $x,y$ before raising to $|r|$. Integration by
  parts gives rapid decay of the Fourier transform of every compact smooth
  cutoff. For any lattice spacing $a > 0$ and $N > d$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cutoff-lattice-sum}
    \sup_y\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^d}\langle y-ak\rangle^{-N}<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  Indeed, translate $y$ into a centered lattice cell, where $|y| \le a\sqrt
  d/2$. For $|k| \ge \sqrt d$ we have $|y-ak| \ge a|k|/2$. The remaining terms
  are finite; the tail converges by dyadic lattice counting, since there are at
  most $C_d2^{jd}$ lattice points in the $j$th shell in $k$. More precisely, for
  $|k| \ge \sqrt d$, the preceding inequality gives
  \[
    \langle y-ak\rangle^{-N}
    \le \max(1,2/a)^N\langle k\rangle^{-N}.
  \]
  The comparison-series terms are bounded by $2^{-jN}$ on each dyadic $k$-shell.
  Thus, its convergent geometric sum carries constants depending on $a,d,N$, as
  does~\eqref{E:cutoff-lattice-sum}.

  We use the mixed Schur bound for a kernel whose absolute row and column
  integrals are bounded by $M_1,M_2$. Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz at each output
  point, followed by Tonelli, bounds its squared operator norm by $M_1M_2$. The
  same proof works with counting measure on one side and Lebesgue measure on the
  other, first on dense finite or compact inputs.

  For Schwartz $u$, the $k$th coefficient of its cutoff periodization is
  \[
    (\mathcal{P}_\psi u)_k
    = L^{-d/2}\int\psi(x)u(x)e^{-i\kappa_k\cdot x}\ud x
    = L^{-d/2}\int\widehat{\psi}(\kappa_k-\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)\ud\xi.
  \]
  The $c_d$ from Fourier inversion cancels the $c_d^{-1}$ in the ordinary
  Fourier integral of $\psi$. After weighting, its exact kernel is
  \[
    L^{-d/2}\left(\frac{\langle\kappa_k\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle}\right)^r
    \widehat{\psi}(\kappa_k-\xi).
  \]
  For a finite torus polynomial $v$, the normalized Fourier kernel of
  $\mathcal{E}_\chi v$ is
  \[
    L^{-d/2}\left(\frac{\langle\xi\rangle}{\langle\kappa_k\rangle}\right)^r
    \widehat{\chi}(\xi-\kappa_k).
  \]
  There is no additional $c_d$ in this kernel. By~\eqref{E:cutoff-peetre}, each
  is bounded in absolute value by a rapidly decreasing function of its frequency
  difference, times a fixed constant. One Schur quantity is its $L^1$ integral
  and the other a uniformly translated lattice sum, bounded
  by~\eqref{E:cutoff-lattice-sum}. This proves~\eqref{E:cutoff-periodic-maps},
  including negative $r$.

  Smooth compact approximation in weighted Fourier $L^2$ proves Schwartz density
  on $\R^d$; weighted sequence truncation proves polynomial density on the
  torus. Hence, these maps extend uniquely. Their distributional meanings follow
  by testing against compact smooth functions and density: each periodic
  coefficient pairs the distribution with a compact smooth test. No point
  sampling of a rough Fourier function is used. The same full-Lebesgue Schur
  argument proves smooth compact multiplication bounded on every whole-space
  $H^r$.

  For Schwartz $u$, the periodic Fourier series of $\psi u$ converges smoothly
  on the cube. Since $\chi\psi = \chi$ and $\chi$ is supported strictly inside
  the cube, multiplying this series by $\chi$ recovers $\chi u$ with no alias
  term. Both sides are bounded from $H^{\sigma+\gamma}(\R^d)$ to
  $H^\sigma(\R^d)$, using the just-proved maps, the inclusion and smooth
  multiplication. Density proves \eqref{E:cutoff-whole-space-recovery} on that
  full domain. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:cutoff-factorization}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Whole-space cutoff singular values]\label{P:cutoff-singular-values}
  For real $\sigma$ and $\gamma > 0$, the initially Schwartz-defined operator
  $K_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma} = \Lambda^\sigma\chi\Lambda^{-\sigma-\gamma}$ extends
  uniquely to a compact operator on $L^2(\R^d)$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cutoff-singular-values}
    s_j(K_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma})
    \le C_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma,d}j^{-\gamma/d},\qquad j \ge 1.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently, $K_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma} \in S_p$ whenever $p \ge 1$ and $p\gamma
  > d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The zero cutoff is immediate. Fix one cube and cutoff pair $L,\psi$ as in
  Lemma~\ref{L:cutoff-factorization}. Its recovery identity gives
  \[
    K_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma}
    =\Lambda^\sigma\mathcal{E}_\chi\mathcal{I}_\gamma
    \mathcal{P}_\psi\Lambda^{-\sigma-\gamma}.
  \]
  In the weighted torus coordinates, $\mathcal{I}_\gamma$ is diagonal with
  entries $\langle\kappa_k\rangle^{-\gamma}$. Let $Q_n$ retain $|k|_\infty \le
  n$. Its rank is $(2n+1)^d$, and
  \[
    \|(1-Q_n)\mathcal{I}_\gamma\|
    \le [1+(2\pi/L)^2(n+1)^2]^{-\gamma/2}\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  Replacing the inclusion by $Q_n\mathcal{I}_\gamma$ gives finite-rank $K_n$
  converging in norm to $K_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma}$. This proves compactness:
  finite-dimensional images have finite nets of each positive radius, and norm
  approximation transfers those nets to the limiting image of the unit ball.

  Put
  \[
    M=\|\mathcal{E}_\chi:H^\sigma(\mathbb{T}_L)\to H^\sigma(\R^d)\|
    \|\mathcal{P}_\psi:H^{\sigma+\gamma}(\R^d)
    \to H^{\sigma+\gamma}(\mathbb{T}_L)\|.
  \]
  The outer Bessel maps are unitary. For $j \ge 3^d$, choose $n = \lfloor
  j^{1/d}/4\rfloor$. Then
  \[
    (2n+1)^d\le(j^{1/d}/2+1)^d<j,\qquad
    \|K-K_n\|\le M(2L/\pi)^\gamma j^{-\gamma/d}.
  \]
  The second estimate follows from $n+1 > j^{1/d}/4$. For a compact operator,
  every rank less than $j$ approximation $F$ satisfies $s_j(K) \le \|K-F\|$: the
  span of the first $j$ right singular vectors contains a unit vector in $\ker
  F$, on which $\|K\cdot\| \ge s_j(K)$. If $s_j(K) = 0$ the inequality is
  immediate. For $j < 3^d$ use $s_j(K) \le \|K\| \le M$. Thus, one may take
  \[
    C_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma,d}
    =M\max(3^\gamma,(2L/\pi)^\gamma).
  \]
  Finally, $\sum_j j^{-p\gamma/d}$ converges for $p\gamma > d$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:cutoff-singular-values}.
\end{proof}

No uniform support-radius constant or endpoint necessity is asserted in
Proposition~\ref{P:cutoff-singular-values}. The periodic inclusion was only a
factor of the recovered whole-space operator.

\subsection{Balanced adjoints and localized variance envelopes}

For $E = H^{1/2}(\R^d)\times H^{-1/2}(\R^d)$ use the physical unitary
coordinates
\[
  J_0(u,v)=(\Lambda^{1/2}u,\Lambda^{-1/2}v),\qquad
  H=L^2(\R^d;\mathbb{C}^2).
\]
The frequency map $J$ in Section~\ref{S:wave-variance} is
$(\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{F})J_0$. Let $D_\chi$ multiply both phase entries
and define
\begin{align}
  P_\chi&=J_0D_\chi J_0^{-1},\qquad
  D_\gamma=\operatorname{diag}(\Lambda^{-\gamma},\Lambda^{-\gamma}),
  \label{E:compact-balanced-cutoff}\\
  K_{\rm out}&=P_\chi D_\gamma,\qquad
  K_{\rm in}=P_\chi^*D_\gamma.
  \label{E:compact-two-factors}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Both compact envelopes]\label{P:compact-two-envelopes}
  The operator $P_\chi$ is bounded. For the unitary swap $U(v,w) = (w,v)$,
  \[
    P_\chi^*=UP_\chi U,\qquad K_{\rm in}=UK_{\rm out}U.
  \]
  Both factors are compact and have equal $S_p$ norms for $p \ge 1$, $p\gamma >
  d$. For $p \ge 2$, $p\gamma > d$, the positive compact operators
  \[
    B_{\rm out}=P_\chi D_\gamma^2P_\chi^*=K_{\rm out}K_{\rm out}^*,
    \qquad B_{\rm in}=P_\chi^*D_\gamma^2P_\chi=K_{\rm in}K_{\rm in}^*
  \]
  satisfy
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-envelope-traces}
    \operatorname{Tr}B_{\rm out}^{p/2}
    =\operatorname{Tr}B_{\rm in}^{p/2}=\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Put $A_\sigma = \Lambda^\sigma\chi\Lambda^{-\sigma}$. The full-Lebesgue Schur
  argument in Lemma~\ref{L:cutoff-factorization} shows boundedness for every
  real $\sigma$. On Schwartz functions, self-adjointness of real multiplication
  and of the Bessel powers gives $A_\sigma^* = A_{-\sigma}$. Density and
  boundedness extend this to the Hilbert adjoints. No evenness of $\chi$ is
  needed. Thus,
  \[
    P_\chi=\operatorname{diag}(A_{1/2},A_{-1/2}),\qquad
    P_\chi^*=\operatorname{diag}(A_{-1/2},A_{1/2})=UP_\chi U.
  \]
  The swap commutes with $D_\gamma$, giving the claimed factor identity. The
  blocks of $K_{\rm out}$ are $K_{\chi,1/2,\gamma}$ and $K_{\chi,-1/2,\gamma}$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:cutoff-singular-values} gives compactness and $S_p$
  membership. The singular values of their direct sum form the multiset union,
  so
  \[
    \|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p
    =\|K_{\chi,1/2,\gamma}\|_{S_p}^p+
    \|K_{\chi,-1/2,\gamma}\|_{S_p}^p.
  \]
  Unitary conjugation preserves them. Finally, the positive eigenvalues of
  $KK^*$ and $K^*K$ agree with multiplicity: on an eigenspace with eigenvalue
  $\lambda > 0$, the maps $K/\sqrt\lambda$ and $K^*/\sqrt\lambda$ are inverse
  isometries. Zero eigenvalues contribute nothing to the trace. Summing these
  scalar powers proves~\eqref{E:compact-envelope-traces}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-two-envelopes}.
\end{proof}

We use the usual positive square root of a bounded positive operator and the
spectral decomposition of compact positive operators. The needed order argument
is recorded explicitly.

\begin{lemma}[Compact positive order and trace]\label{L:compact-positive-order}
  If $A,B$ are bounded self-adjoint operators with $0 \le A \le B$ and $B$
  compact positive, then $A$ is compact and its ordered eigenvalues satisfy
  $\lambda_j(A) \le \lambda_j(B)$, including zero padding. Consequently, for $t
  \ge 1$ with finite $\operatorname{Tr}B^t$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-positive-trace}
    \operatorname{Tr}A^t\le\operatorname{Tr}B^t.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Let $Q_n$ be finite spectral projections of $B$ whose tails tend to zero in
  norm. Then
  \[
    \|(1-Q_n)A^{1/2}\|^2
    =\|(1-Q_n)A(1-Q_n)\|
    \le\|(1-Q_n)B(1-Q_n)\|\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  Thus, $A^{1/2}$ is a norm limit of finite-rank operators and is compact, as is
  $A$. For compact positive operators, the max-min formula for $\lambda_j$
  follows in an eigenbasis: the span of the first $j$ eigenvectors gives the
  lower bound for the supremum over $j$-dimensional subspaces of their minimum
  Rayleigh quotient, while every such subspace meets the orthogonal complement
  of the first $j-1$ eigenvectors, giving the upper bound. Compare the Rayleigh
  quotients of $A$ and $B$ to obtain the eigenvalue inequality. Sum the ordered
  scalar powers to prove \eqref{E:compact-positive-trace}. No inequality $A^t
  \le B^t$ is asserted. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:compact-positive-order}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Localized finite variance bounds]\label{P:localized-finite-variance}
  Suppose finitely many bounded operators $Z_\ell$ on $H$ satisfy
  \begin{equation}\label{E:localized-finite-hypotheses}
    P_\chi Z_\ell=Z_\ell=Z_\ell P_\chi,\qquad
    V_{\rm out}:=\sum_\ell Z_\ell Z_\ell^*\le CD_\gamma^2,\qquad
    V_{\rm in}:=\sum_\ell Z_\ell^*Z_\ell\le CD_\gamma^2,
  \end{equation}
  with $C \ge 0$. If $p \ge 2$ and $p\gamma > d$, every $Z_\ell$ is compact in
  $S_p$, the two variances are compact in $S_{p/2}$, and
  \begin{align}
    V_{\rm out}&\le CB_{\rm out},\qquad V_{\rm in}\le CB_{\rm in},
    \label{E:localized-two-envelopes}\\
    \operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^{p/2},\quad
    \operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^{p/2}
    &\le C^{p/2}\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p.
    \label{E:localized-trace-bounds}
  \end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Left localization gives $V_{\rm out} = P_\chi V_{\rm out}P_\chi^*$. Apply
  positive congruence to the output bound
  in~\eqref{E:localized-finite-hypotheses} to get $V_{\rm out} \le CP_\chi
  D_\gamma^2P_\chi^* = CB_{\rm out}$. Right localization similarly gives $V_{\rm
  in} = P_\chi^*V_{\rm in}P_\chi \le CB_{\rm in}$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-two-envelopes} and
  Lemma~\ref{L:compact-positive-order} prove compactness and the two trace
  bounds with $t = p/2$. If $C = 0$, both variances and every $Z_\ell$ vanish,
  so all assertions are immediate.

  For each $\ell$, $Z_\ell Z_\ell^* \le V_{\rm out}$ is compact by the same
  lemma. Its finite spectral projections $Q_n$ give $\|(1-Q_n)Z_\ell\|^2 =
  \|(1-Q_n)Z_\ell Z_\ell^*(1-Q_n)\| \to 0$. Hence, $Z_\ell$ is compact. Its
  singular values squared are the nonzero eigenvalues of $Z_\ell Z_\ell^*$.
  Comparing with $V_{\rm out}$ and summing proves $Z_\ell \in S_p$ with
  $\|Z_\ell\|_{S_p}^p \le \operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^{p/2}$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:localized-finite-variance}.
\end{proof}

The two localization identities are hypotheses here; a noise coefficient family
has not yet been shown to satisfy them. In particular, $P_\chi$ is not assumed
self-adjoint or a projection.

\begin{corollary}[Finite Gaussian operators]\label{C:finite-gaussian-operators}
  Under~\eqref{E:localized-finite-hypotheses}, let $g_\ell$ be independent real
  standard Gaussians. For even $p \ge 2$ with $p\gamma > d$, the finite sum $X =
  \sum_\ell g_\ell Z_\ell$ satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:finite-gaussian-operator-bound}
    (\E\|X\|_{S_p}^p)^{1/p}
    \le\sqrt{p-1}\sqrt C\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}.
  \end{equation}
  Its extended norm is measurable, with finite expectation as displayed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Choose finite-rank orthogonal projections $\Pi_n$ from a countable orthonormal
  basis of $H$, with $\Pi_n \to I$ strongly. Put $Z_{\ell,n} = \Pi_nZ_\ell\Pi_n$
  and $X_n = \sum_\ell g_\ell Z_{\ell,n}$, viewed as matrices on
  $\operatorname{ran}\Pi_n$. Their output variance satisfies
  \[
    \sum_\ell\Pi_nZ_\ell\Pi_nZ_\ell^*\Pi_n
    \le\Pi_nV_{\rm out}\Pi_n,
  \]
  since the difference is $\sum_\ell\Pi_nZ_\ell(1-\Pi_n)Z_\ell^*\Pi_n \ge 0$.
  The input inequality is identical with adjoints interchanged. Compact max-min
  also gives $\lambda_j(\Pi_nV\Pi_n) \le \lambda_j(V)$ for positive compact $V$,
  since Rayleigh subspaces inside $\operatorname{ran}\Pi_n$ form a subset of all
  subspaces. Thus, both variance trace powers of these matrices are bounded by
  $C^{p/2}\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p$.

  Proposition~\ref{P:finite-gaussian-matrix} applies to these finite complex
  matrices, the same real independent Gaussians and the even $p$. Taking the
  average of the two already bounded trace powers gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:finite-gaussian-compression-bound}
    \E\|X_n\|_{S_p}^p
    \le(p-1)^{p/2}C^{p/2}\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p.
  \end{equation}
  No factor two is added by this average.

  For each Gaussian coordinate vector, $X$ is a finite sum of compact operators.
  Strong convergence of $\Pi_n$ implies $\Pi_nX\Pi_n \to X$ in operator norm:
  approximate $X$ by a finite-rank $F$, use uniform strong convergence on the
  finite-dimensional ranges of $F$ and $F^*$, and control the remainder using
  $\|\Pi_n\| \le 1$. Singular values are norm-Lipschitz. Indeed, their formula
  \[
    s_j(A)=\inf_{\dim L<j}
    \sup_{v\in L^\perp,\ \|v\|=1}\|Av\|
  \]
  follows from compact singular vectors and gives $s_j(A) \le s_j(B)+\|A-B\|$,
  and the reverse bound by interchanging $A,B$. Hence, $s_j(X_n) \to s_j(X)$ for
  each $j$. Fatou for the nonnegative singular-value sum gives $\|X\|_{S_p}^p
  \le \liminf_n\|X_n\|_{S_p}^p$, allowing extended infinity. These variables are
  measurable: the finite Gaussian coordinate map into operators is
  norm-continuous, each singular value is continuous, and the norm power is
  their nonnegative measurable sum. Fatou in probability
  and~\eqref{E:finite-gaussian-compression-bound} prove the bound and
  almost-sure finiteness. No $S_p$-Bochner convergence is assumed. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:finite-gaussian-operators}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A nonzero witness and the need for a compact cutoff}

Proposition~\ref{P:localized-finite-variance} and
Corollary~\ref{C:finite-gaussian-operators} assume
\eqref{E:localized-finite-hypotheses}. We check that these hypotheses admit a
nonzero operator, and then test what is lost without the compact cutoff.

\begin{proposition}[Whole-space cutoff tests]\label{P:cutoff-boundary-tests}
  If $\chi$ equals one on a nonempty open set, there is a nonzero rank-one
  operator satisfying~\eqref{E:localized-finite-hypotheses} with a finite common
  $C$. On the other hand, $\Lambda^{-\gamma}$ on $L^2(\R^d)$ is not compact for
  any $\gamma > 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Choose nonzero real $v,f \in C_c^\infty$ supported inside the plateau, and put
  $\widetilde{v} = (\Lambda^{1/2}v,0)$ and $\widetilde{w} =
  (\Lambda^{-1/2}f,0)$. Both are Schwartz. The block formulas give
  $P_\chi\widetilde{v} = \widetilde{v}$ and $P_\chi^*\widetilde{w} =
  \widetilde{w}$. Therefore, $Zx = \langle x,\widetilde{w}\rangle\widetilde{v}$
  satisfies $P_\chi Z = Z = ZP_\chi$. Its two variances are
  $\|\widetilde{w}\|^2\widetilde{v}\otimes\widetilde{v}^*$ and
  $\|\widetilde{v}\|^2\widetilde{w}\otimes\widetilde{w}^*$. For any Schwartz $a$
  and any $x \in H$,
  \[
    |\langle x,a\rangle|
    =|\langle D_\gamma x,D_\gamma^{-1}a\rangle|
    \le\|D_\gamma x\|\|D_\gamma^{-1}a\|.
  \]
  The inverse multiplier is applied only to Schwartz $a$, so it belongs to $H$.
  Apply the inequality with $a = \widetilde{v},\widetilde{w}$ to obtain the two
  variance bounds with
  \[
    C=\max\left(
    \|\widetilde{w}\|^2\|D_\gamma^{-1}\widetilde{v}\|^2,
    \|\widetilde{v}\|^2\|D_\gamma^{-1}\widetilde{w}\|^2\right)<\infty.
  \]
  This witness uses a plateau; no nonzero family is inferred for every compact
  cutoff or identified with a source family.

  For noncompactness, take $f$ with nonzero Gaussian Fourier transform. Then $g
  = \Lambda^{-\gamma}f$ is a nonzero Schwartz function. The images of translates
  of $f$ are the same translates of $g$. With $T_hg(x) = g(x-h)$, Plancherel
  gives
  \[
    \langle T_hg,g\rangle
    =\int e^{-i\xi\cdot h}|\widehat{g}(\xi)|^2\ud\xi
    =c_d^{-1}\widehat{|\widehat{g}|^2}(h).
  \]
  This tends to zero because $|\widehat{g}|^2$ is Schwartz. Choose a large
  spacing so that any two distinct translates in the resulting sequence have
  image distance at least $\|g\|$. The input norms are identical, but the images
  have no Cauchy subsequence. This disproves compactness. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:cutoff-boundary-tests}.
\end{proof}

The translation obstruction is restated in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-24}:
frequency decay alone does not replace compact spatial localization.


Thus, the choice $\chi = 1$ cannot be substituted into the compact-cutoff
argument. No necessity at the Schatten endpoint $p\gamma = d$ is asserted. The
results concern finite matrices and conditional finite operator sums.
Coefficient localization, compact variance tails, an infinite Gaussian series
and time regularity are proved in Section~\ref{S:actual-operator-driver}; source
and Young identification are proved in
Sections~\ref{S:source-driver-convergence}--\ref{S:constant-phase-young}. They
are not consequences of the finite comparison alone.
