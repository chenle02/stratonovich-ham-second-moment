\section{Topology, localization and limits of variational arguments}
\label{S:nogo-topology}

A useful approximation must be small in the topology in which the next
argument uses it. The obstructions below distinguish a local operator from a
Hilbert--Schmidt operator, a fixed spatial cutoff from a growing one, and
pointwise convergence from convergence of variational suprema. Throughout,
$a=\alpha_0\in(0,1)$ and $\Lambda=(1-\Delta)^{1/2}$, except that the positive
number $L$ denotes the temporal feature cutoff in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-32}.

\subsection{The first driver cannot always be Hilbert--Schmidt}
\label{S:nogo-21}

The one-dimensional operator construction suggests trying the same
Hilbert--Schmidt space in higher dimensions. A compact spatial observation
removes infinite volume, but it does not remove the following high-frequency
obstruction. The relevant compatibility is with the given noise, through its
bilinear first-driver tests.

\setcounter{nogo}{20}
\begin{nogo}[A same-noise Hilbert--Schmidt obstruction]
  \label{P:nogo-21}
  Suppose that $d\in\{2,3\}$, $s\in\R$, and $0<T\le1$. Put
  $E_s=H^s(\R^d)\times H^{s-1}(\R^d)$ and let $S(t)$ be the massless wave
  phase group. For every nonzero covariance satisfying Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog},
  there are a nonnegative compact smooth physical coefficient mask $\zeta$ and
  a compact smooth observation cutoff $\chi$, equal to one on a sufficiently
  large ball, for which the localized Gaussian first-driver form has no
  compatible realization in $L^2(\Omega;\operatorname{HS}(E_s,E_s))$.
  Here compatibility means equality with the given $W$ for each fixed smooth
  phase pair, as specified in the proof.
\end{nogo}

\begin{proof}
  First consider a deterministic coefficient $h(u,x)=k(u)H(x)$, where
  $H\in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$ is nonzero, even and nonnegative, and
  $k\in W^{1,\infty}[0,T]$ satisfies $k\ge k_0>0$. Write
  $B(b)(v,w)=(0,bv)$ and
  \[
    A_h=\int_0^T S(-u)B(h(u))S(u)\ud u,\qquad
    S(u,\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}
      \cos(u|\xi|)&\sin(u|\xi|)/|\xi|\\
      -|\xi|\sin(u|\xi|)&\cos(u|\xi|)
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  The sine quotient has its continuous value at zero. The integral is a strong
  operator integral on $E_s$: $S(u)$ is strongly continuous and uniformly
  bounded on compact time intervals, and multiplication by $H$ is bounded on
  $H^s$. For the latter assertion, the weighted Fourier kernel is bounded in
  absolute value by a constant times
  $\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{|s|}|\widehat H(\xi-\eta)|$, an integrable
  convolution kernel. This proves boundedness for every fixed real $s$.

  Normalize the Fourier coordinates by
  $\operatorname{diag}(\langle\xi\rangle^s,\langle\xi\rangle^{s-1})$.
  Using the unitary Fourier transform, the lower-left block of $A_h$ then has
  kernel
  \[
    K_s(\xi,\eta)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}
    \langle\xi\rangle^{s-1}\langle\eta\rangle^{-s}
    \widehat H(\xi-\eta)
    \int_0^T k(u)\cos(u|\xi|)\cos(u|\eta|)\ud u.
  \]
  This formula follows from both phase coordinates: before applying the weights,
  the full product, with $r=|\xi|$, $\rho=|\eta|$, is
  \[
    \widehat H(\xi-\eta)
    \begin{pmatrix}
      -\dfrac{\sin(ru)}r\cos(\rho u)&
      -\dfrac{\sin(ru)}r\dfrac{\sin(\rho u)}\rho\\
      \cos(ru)\cos(\rho u)&\cos(ru)\dfrac{\sin(\rho u)}\rho
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  All Fourier and time exchanges are justified on Schwartz pairs by the rapid
  decay of $\widehat H$ and then by density.

  Let $J_0=\int_0^T k>0$ and
  $B_0=\|k\|_\infty+T\|k'\|_\infty$. Choose $\epsilon>0$ so that
  $\epsilon\le1$, $\epsilon T\le1$ and
  $\widehat H(z)\ge\widehat H(0)/2>0$ when $|z|\le\epsilon$.
  If $|\xi-\eta|\le\epsilon$, then
  \[
    \int_0^T k(u)\cos((r-\rho)u)\ud u\ge J_0/2,
    \qquad
    \left|\int_0^T k(u)\cos((r+\rho)u)\ud u\right|
    \le B_0/(r+\rho).
  \]
  The second bound is integration by parts in the absolutely continuous
  function $k$. Product-to-sum therefore bounds the time integral in $K_s$
  from below by $J_0/8$ when
  $r\ge R_0:=\max(1,2\epsilon,4B_0/J_0)$.
  On the same region,
  \[
    \langle\xi\rangle^{s-1}\langle\eta\rangle^{-s}
    =\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}
    (\langle\xi\rangle/\langle\eta\rangle)^s
    \ge 2^{-|s|}/(\sqrt2 r).
  \]
  Consequently, $|K_s(\xi,\eta)|\ge c_s/|\xi|$ there, for $c_s>0$.
  If $P_N$ cuts off Fourier frequencies to $B_N$, its truncated kernel is
  square integrable and Parseval followed by Tonelli gives
  \[
    \|P_NK_sP_N\|_{\operatorname{HS}}^2
    \ge c_s^2|B_\epsilon||\mathbb S^{d-1}|
    \int_{R_0}^{N-\epsilon}r^{d-3}\ud r\longrightarrow\infty.
  \]
  Indeed Parseval in the second kernel variable identifies its square integral
  with the sum of squared images of an orthonormal basis. If $A_h$ were
  Hilbert--Schmidt, every block compression would have bounded
  Hilbert--Schmidt norm. The displayed divergence proves otherwise. Real
  Hilbert--Schmidt operators and their complexifications have the same norm,
  so Fourier complexification has not changed the conclusion.

  Suppose that $\operatorname{supp}H\subset B_A$, and let $\chi=1$ on
  $B_{A+T+1}$. With $D_\chi$ denoting multiplication of both phase coordinates,
  finite propagation gives
  \[
    D_\chi A_h=A_h=A_hD_\chi.
  \]
  For completeness, the local wave energy on a shrinking ball has boundary
  flux $v_t\partial_n v-(v_t^2+|\nabla v|^2)/2\le0$. Zero initial data
  therefore remain zero in the interior cone; time reversal gives the same
  support assertion for $S(-u)$. Apply this first to smooth data and then use
  Sobolev continuity and distributional testing to pass to $E_s$. The
  coefficient acts only in $B_A$, and the resulting output lies in
  $B_{A+T}$. Thus the failure persists after this compact observation.

  We next produce $h$ as an actual covariance mean. Choose $L_0\ge1$ with
  $0<\Gamma(B_{L_0})<\infty$, and an even nonnegative $q\in C_c^\infty$
  equal to at least one on $B_{2L_0+1}$. Set $U=\Gamma*q$. Local finiteness
  of $\Gamma$ permits differentiation on every compact set, so $U$ is smooth,
  even and nonnegative, with $U(0)>0$. Moreover,
  \[
    Q=\int q(x)U(x)\ud x
    \ge |B_{L_0+1}|\Gamma(B_{L_0})>0.
  \]
  Choose an even nonnegative compact smooth $\zeta$, equal to one near zero,
  and put $H=\zeta U$. Choose $f\in C_c^\infty(0,1)$ nonnegative and
  nonzero, and set
  \[
    Z=W(f\otimes q),\qquad
    k(u)=\int_0^1|u-v|^{-a}f(v)\ud v.
  \]
  For $u\in[0,1]$, $k(u)\ge\int f>0$. Moving the derivative to the smooth
  zero extension of $f$ shows $k\in C^1[0,1]$, since $|\cdot|^{-a}$ is
  locally integrable. Also $\E Z^2=Q\int_0^1 f(u)k(u)\ud u\in(0,\infty)$.
  The mask is applied after the covariance convolution: $H=\zeta(\Gamma*q)$.

  It remains to specify the compatible first-driver form with the correct
  Sobolev adjoint. For real Schwartz phases $z,w$ put
  \[
    v_u=[S(u)z]_1,\qquad
    G_w(u)=-\frac{\sin(u|D|)}{|D|}(\chi\Lambda^{2s}w_1)
    +\cos(u|D|)(\chi\Lambda^{2s-2}w_2),
  \]
  and define
  $F_{z,w}(u,x)=\mathbf1_{(0,T)}(u)\zeta(x)v_u(x)G_w(u,x)$.
  This test lies in the raw noise completion. In fact, on a fixed compact
  spatial support $K$, its covariance norm is bounded by a constant times
  $\|u\mapsto\|F(u)\|_\infty\|_{L^2(0,T)}$, by local Radon mass
  $\Gamma(K-K)$ and the time Schur bound for $|u-v|^{-a}$. Smooth interior
  time approximation gives membership. Define
  $\ell_\chi(z,w)=W(F_{z,w})$ on the given noise space.
  Direct transposition, with the Sobolev weights retained, gives
  \[
    G_w(u)=\Lambda^{2s-2}
    \big((D_\chi S(-u))^{*_{E_s}}w\big)_2.
  \]
  In particular, multiplication by $\chi$ has not been treated as self-adjoint
  in $E_s$. Absolute Fubini in the covariance pairing now gives
  \[
    \E[Z\ell_\chi(z,w)]
    =\int_0^T\!\int k(u)H(x)v_u(x)G_w(u,x)\ud x\ud u
    =\langle D_\chi A_hz,w\rangle_{E_s}
    =\langle A_hz,w\rangle_{E_s}.
  \]
  All spatial factors in the raw tests have compact support and the temporal
  singularity is integrable, which supplies the absolute Fubini bound.

  If $X\in L^2(\Omega;\operatorname{HS}(E_s,E_s))$ satisfied
  $\langle Xz,w\rangle_{E_s}=\ell_\chi(z,w)$ almost surely for every fixed
  Schwartz pair, then Cauchy--Schwarz would make
  $Y=\E[ZX]$ a Hilbert--Schmidt Bochner integral. Continuous matrix evaluation
  would give $\langle Yz,w\rangle=\langle A_hz,w\rangle$ for all such pairs.
  Density implies $Y=A_h$, a contradiction. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-21}.
\end{proof}

This result concerns the same-index phase spaces $E_s$ and a compatible
$L^2$ Hilbert--Schmidt realization. It does not concern every cutoff: the zero
cutoff, for example, has no such obstruction. It is not a nonexistence result
for the Stratonovich solution or its moments. A route using loss of regularity
between input and output, operator norms, or a different Schatten class must
supply its own estimates; changing the fixed index $s$ alone cannot reopen
this particular route.

\subsection{A physical mask may not act on the noise quotient}
\label{S:nogo-22}

A smooth physical mask is harmless on ordinary test functions. It need not
preserve the zero classes of a covariance seminorm. The following is the
obstruction already proved in Proposition~\ref{P:masked-quotient-obstruction};
we include its witness to explain why the auxiliary completion is needed.

\setcounter{nogo}{21}
\begin{nogo}[A mask that separates noise-null representatives]
  \label{P:nogo-22}
  For $d=2$, $\alpha=1$, $0<a<1$ and
  $\Gamma=\delta_0(\ud x_1)\ud x_2$, there exist real compact smooth $F$ and
  a nonnegative smooth square mask $\zeta$ for which
  $\E W(F)^2=0$ but $\E W(\zeta F)^2>0$.
  Thus multiplication by this mask does not descend to the original noise
  quotient, and no finite uniform covariance-norm multiplier bound holds.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Choose a nonnegative nonconstant compact smooth plateau function $\psi$ and
  put $\chi=\psi^2$. The spatial covariance seminorm is
  \[
    V(g)=\int_\R\left(\int_\R g(x_1,x_2)\ud x_2\right)^2\ud x_1.
  \]
  This also shows positive definiteness of the nonnegative even tempered
  measure $\Gamma$; its scaling is $\Gamma(cA)=c\Gamma(A)$, as required
  for $d=2$, $\alpha=1$. Put
  $g(x)=\chi(x_1)\chi''(x_2)$ and
  $\zeta(x)=\chi(x_1)\chi(x_2)=(\psi(x_1)\psi(x_2))^2$.
  Then $\int\chi''=0$, whereas integration by parts gives
  \[
    \int\zeta(x)g(x)\ud x_2=-\chi(x_1)^2\int(\chi')^2.
  \]
  Consequently, $V(g)=0$ and
  $V(\zeta g)=\int\chi^4\,\big(\int(\chi')^2\big)^2>0$.
  Tensor with a nonzero nonnegative $\tau\in C_c^\infty(0,T)$. Its temporal
  variance is finite and strictly positive, so $F=\tau\otimes g$ has the
  asserted full-noise variances. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-22}.
\end{proof}

This covariance is not two-dimensional spatial white noise. One can still
mask individual tests before taking an auxiliary completion. To reopen a
multiplier argument on the original quotient, one must first prove that the
chosen mask preserves its null space and then prove the required norm bound.

\subsection{Frequency decay does not give whole-space compactness}
\label{S:nogo-24}

The compact-cutoff estimates use spatial confinement as well as frequency
smoothing. Removing the former loses compactness even for a deterministic
Bessel multiplier; see Proposition~\ref{P:cutoff-boundary-tests}.

\setcounter{nogo}{23}
\begin{nogo}[Translations defeat whole-space compactness]
  \label{P:nogo-24}
  For every $d\ge1$ and $\gamma>0$, the operator $\Lambda^{-\gamma}$ on
  $L^2(\R^d)$ is not compact.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Take a nonzero Schwartz function $f$ with Gaussian Fourier transform and put
  $g=\Lambda^{-\gamma}f$. Then $g$ is nonzero and Schwartz. For translations
  $T_hg(x)=g(x-h)$, Plancherel gives
  \[
    \langle T_hg,g\rangle
    =\int e^{-i\xi\cdot h}|\widehat g(\xi)|^2\ud\xi\longrightarrow0
    \quad (|h|\longrightarrow\infty).
  \]
  The limit follows, for example, by integration by parts in the Fourier
  transform of the Schwartz function $|\widehat g|^2$.
  Choose a sufficiently large spacing $b$ and $h_n=nb e_1$ so that distinct
  images $T_{h_n}g$ have squared distance at least $\|g\|_2^2$.
  The inputs $T_{h_n}f$ have constant norm and
  $\Lambda^{-\gamma}T_{h_n}f=T_{h_n}g$. Their images have no Cauchy
  subsequence. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-24}.
\end{proof}

Compact spatial cutoffs and the conditional Schatten estimates in the main
text survive. No necessity at a Schatten endpoint is asserted here. A
whole-space successor needs a tightness condition that prevents translation
of mass, or an explicitly different topology.

\subsection{Fixed projections do not control growing spatial masks}
\label{S:nogo-25}

At each fixed radius the Cameron mean map can be compact. Its finite-rank
approximations need not be uniform in that radius. The following witness uses
actual unit controls for one-dimensional spatial white noise. The fixed-radius
map and its compact approximation are those of
Proposition~\ref{P:cameron-full-mean}.

\setcounter{nogo}{24}
\begin{nogo}[Escape from every fixed smooth projection]
  \label{P:nogo-25}
  Let $\Gamma=\delta_0$ in $d=1$, and let $H_I$ be the closed raw-noise
  space generated by tests supported in $I\times\R$, $I=(0,1)$. Let $P_J$
  be any fixed finite-rank orthogonal projection in $H_I$ whose range is
  spanned by smooth compact tests supported in $I\times\R$. For the physical masked mean multiplier $T_R$ on
  $F=(H^{1/2}\times H^{-1/2})\oplus\R^2$, with the last coordinates
  representing constant displacement and velocity, one has, for every
  $2<r<2/a$,
  \[
    \sup_R\|T_R(I-P_J)\|_{H_I\to L^r((0,1);\mathcal K(F))}\ge c_a>0,
  \]
  where $c_a$ can be chosen independently of $J$. Here the smooth masks $m_R$
  equal one on $[-R,R]$. For a smooth control $\Psi$ with covariance mean
  $h_\Psi$, the operator sends $(v,w,c,d)$ to
  $(0,m_Rh_\Psi(s)(v+c),0,0)$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Use the temporal equilibrium density $f_a$ of
  Lemma~\ref{L:temporal-equilibrium}, so that
  $\int_0^1|s-t|^{-a}f_a(t)\ud t=1$ and
  $\|f_a\|_{H_a}^2=M_a>0$.
  Choose nonnegative $q\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ with $\|q\|_2=1$, and put
  $q_n(x)=q(x-x_n)$, where $x_n\to\infty$. The controls
  \[
    \Psi_n=\frac{f_a\otimes q_n}{\sqrt{M_a}}
  \]
  have unit raw norm and time-independent covariance mean
  $q_n/\sqrt{M_a}$. Membership follows by $L^2$ approximation of $f_a$ and
  the Schur bound for the temporal kernel.
  The finitely many tests spanning $P_J$ have common compact spatial support.
  For all sufficiently large $n$, spatial whiteness gives
  $P_J\Psi_n=0$ exactly. Choose $R_n$ to contain $\operatorname{supp}q_n$
  in the plateau of $m_{R_n}$.

  The constant-displacement input $e=(0,0,1,0)$ has norm one. The phase dual
  \[
    w_n=(0,\Lambda q_n/\|q\|_{H^{1/2}},0,0)
  \]
  also has norm one. Indeed its velocity norm is $H^{-1/2}$, and translation
  preserves $\|q\|_{H^{1/2}}$. The mean multiplier sends $e$ to the velocity
  $q_n/\sqrt{M_a}$, so the $H^{-1/2}$ inner product gives, for every $s$,
  \[
    \langle T_{R_n}\Psi_n(s)e,w_n\rangle_F
    =\frac{\|q_n\|_2^2}{\sqrt{M_a}\|q\|_{H^{1/2}}}
    =:c_a>0.
  \]
  Integrating over the unit time interval proves the bound for all $r$ stated.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-25}.
\end{proof}

For a fixed $R$, sufficiently distant translates miss its mask, so there is
no contradiction with fixed-radius compactness. A replacement must use
projections depending on the radius with quantitative control, or a different
localization argument. The witness does not exclude every such strategy.

\subsection{Two distinct limitations of block costs}
\label{S:nogo-26}

A nonlinear block argument must handle both small perturbations and rare large
blocks. A power larger than one misses the first effect; an averaged budget
alone does not bound the largest block.

\setcounter{nogo}{25}
\begin{nogo}[A pure Young power and a maximum from an average]
  \label{P:nogo-26}
  On $[0,1]$, with $\theta=2/3$ and $\nu=1/\theta=3/2$, no finite constant
  $C>0$ gives
  $\log U(1)\le C\|D\|_{C^\theta}^{\nu}$ for all scalar paths starting at zero
  and solving $\ud U=U\ud D$, $U(0)=1$, when
  $\|D\|_{C^\theta}=\|D\|_\infty+[D]_{C^\theta}$.
  Moreover, a bound on $N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N b_j^r$, $r>0$, does not give a
  bound independent of $N$ on $\max_j b_j$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  For $D(t)=xt$, $x>0$, the solution is $U(t)=e^{xt}$ and
  $\|D\|_{C^{2/3}}=2x$. Taking $x=1/(16C^2)$ gives
  $C(2x)^{3/2}=x/\sqrt2<x=\log U(1)$.
  For the second assertion, take $b_1=N^{1/r}$ and $b_j=0$ for $j>1$.
  Their averaged $r$th power is one while their maximum tends to infinity.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-26}.
\end{proof}

A linear-plus-power cost is not refuted. For any $\tau>0$ and $\nu>1$,
$x\le\tau+\tau^{1-\nu}x^\nu$ gives an explicit alternative with slack.
Also, if $0<\nu<r$ and $N^{-1}\sum b_j^r\le M$, then H\"older gives
$N^{-1}\sum b_j^\nu\le M^{\nu/r}$, and
\[
  \frac1N\sum_{b_j>K}b_j^\nu\le K^{\nu-r}M.
\]
Thus an averaged tail argument survives. Reopening the block route requires
an actual evolution estimate with its linear term or slack, followed by an
averaged bad-block estimate. These scalar tests alone do not construct a
Cameron potential or prove a wave evolution bound.

\subsection{Concentrating temporal controls restrict integrability}
\label{S:nogo-27}

Compactness of a temporal mean map has a natural upper endpoint. A
concentrating control detects both excessive integrability and failure of
compactness at that endpoint.

\setcounter{nogo}{26}
\begin{nogo}[The temporal concentration threshold]
  \label{P:nogo-27}
  Let $I=(0,1)$, $0<a<1$, and let $H_a$ be the completion of smooth tests for
  $\|f\|_{H_a}^2=\int_I\int_I f(t)f(s)|t-s|^{-a}\ud t\ud s$.
  On smooth tests set $C_af(t)=\int_I|t-s|^{-a}f(s)\ud s$.
  The map $C_a$ is not bounded from $H_a$ to $L^r(I)$ when $r>2/a$,
  including $r=\infty$, and it is not compact into $L^{2/a}(I)$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Choose $t_0\in I$ and a nonzero nonnegative
  $\phi\in C_c^\infty(-1,1)$. Put
  \[
    v_\phi=\int\!\int\phi(u)\phi(v)|u-v|^{-a}\ud u\ud v\in(0,\infty),
    \qquad
    f_\epsilon(t)=\frac{\epsilon^{a/2-1}}{\sqrt{v_\phi}}
    \phi((t-t_0)/\epsilon).
  \]
  For sufficiently small $\epsilon$, its support lies in $I$. Changing both
  variables shows $\|f_\epsilon\|_{H_a}=1$, and
  \[
    (C_af_\epsilon)(t_0+\epsilon y)
    =\frac{\epsilon^{-a/2}}{\sqrt{v_\phi}}
    \int|y-v|^{-a}\phi(v)\ud v.
  \]
  For $|y|\le1$, the last integral is at least $2^{-a}\int\phi>0$.
  Thus for finite $r$,
  $\|C_af_\epsilon\|_{L^r}\ge c_r\epsilon^{1/r-a/2}$, and the analogous
  supremum lower bound is $c\epsilon^{-a/2}$. This proves unboundedness above
  $2/a$.
  For every fixed $t\ne t_0$, on the other hand,
  $C_af_\epsilon(t)=O_t(\epsilon^{a/2})\to0$.
  At $r=2/a$ the norm lower bound stays strictly positive. If a subsequence
  converged strongly in this $L^r$ space, a further subsequence would converge
  almost everywhere to its strong limit. The limit would have to be zero,
  contradicting the norm lower bound. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-27}.
\end{proof}

The endpoint argument refutes compactness, not boundedness. Tensoring the
controls with one fixed nonzero spatial profile transfers this input
obstruction to a corresponding separated mean map. The corrected compact
range $2<r<2/a$ is consistent with the witness. A larger range requires
additional temporal regularity or restrictions that exclude concentration.

\subsection{Pointwise convergence does not pass through a supremum}
\label{S:nogo-30}

The Gaussian action cost must remain in a variational comparison. Even with
that cost, bounded near-maximizers can escape in infinitely many directions.

\setcounter{nogo}{29}
\begin{nogo}[Escaping variational scores]
  \label{P:nogo-30}
  There are continuous functions $J_n$ on the Hilbert space $\ell^2$ tending
  to zero at every point such that
  \[
    \sup_h\{J_n(h)-\tfrac12\|h\|^2\}\ge\tfrac12,
    \qquad
    \sup_h\{0-\tfrac12\|h\|^2\}=0.
  \]
  The witnesses for the first inequality have norm one.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Let $(e_n)$ be the standard orthonormal sequence and set
  $J_n(h)=(1-2\|h-e_n\|)_+$.
  For each fixed $h\in\ell^2$, its $n$th coordinate tends to zero, and hence
  $\|h-e_n\|^2=\|h\|^2+1-2\langle h,e_n\rangle\to\|h\|^2+1\ge1$.
  Thus $J_n(h)=0$ eventually. But $J_n(e_n)=1$, so its score at $e_n$ is
  $1/2$. The limiting score is nonpositive and equals zero at $h=0$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-30}.
\end{proof}

A bounded action budget alone supplies no strong compactness in $\ell^2$.
To pass to an exact constant one needs a variational upper-limit argument,
for example compactness of near-maximizers in a topology with the needed
upper semicontinuity. Pointwise convergence, or compactness proved only with
an additional fixed spatial cutoff, does not supply that argument by itself.

\subsection{A cutoff point feature can lie outside the raw-noise image}
\label{S:nogo-32}

Lemma~\ref{L:source-retaining-isometry} embeds the original noise into one
fixed larger Gaussian space. Its closed image retains all original raw tests.
It does not contain every positive-cutoff point feature. The following
explicit orthogonal vector explains the role of the auxiliary noise.

\setcounter{nogo}{31}
\begin{nogo}[Positive-cutoff point sources need auxiliary noise]
  \label{P:nogo-32}
  Suppose that $\Gamma\ne0$ satisfies Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, $t_0>0$,
  $L\in(0,\infty)$ and $\epsilon>0$. In the feature space
  \[
    \mathcal G=L^2((0,\infty)_\lambda\times\R_r,
    \nu_a(\ud\lambda)\ud r;H_\Gamma),\qquad
    \nu_a(\ud\lambda)=\lambda^{a-1}\ud\lambda/\Gamma_E(a),
  \]
  let $K=\Phi H_W$ be the closed image of
  Lemma~\ref{L:source-retaining-isometry}. Set $g=p_\epsilon(x-\cdot)$ and
  \[
    v(\lambda,r)=\mathbf1_{\{\lambda\le L\}}\sqrt{2\lambda}
    e^{-\lambda(t_0-r)}\mathbf1_{\{r\le t_0\}}g.
  \]
  Then $v\notin K$. On the fixed extension
  $\mathcal I(v)=W(\Phi^{-1}Pv)+J((I-P)v)$, where $P$ projects onto $K$
  and $J$ is independent of $W$, one has
  $\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal I(v)\mid\sigma(W))>0$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  The heat profile has finite strictly positive norm:
  \[
    \|g\|_{H_\Gamma}^2=\int p_{2\epsilon}(y)\Gamma(\ud y)>0.
  \]
  Finiteness follows from temperedness and Gaussian decay, and positivity from
  nonzero nonnegative $\Gamma$. Integrating in $r$ also gives
  $\|v\|_{\mathcal G}^2=\|g\|^2\nu_a((0,L])<\infty$.
  Choose nonnegative nonzero smooth bumps $u_A,u_B$ supported in compact
  intervals $A\subset(0,L)$ and $B\subset(L,\infty)$, respectively, and put
  \[
    p(\lambda)=\frac{u_A(\lambda)}{\int u_A\ud\nu_a}
    -\frac{u_B(\lambda)}{\int u_B\ud\nu_a}.
  \]
  Then $\int p\ud\nu_a=0$ and $\int_{(0,L]}p\ud\nu_a=1$.
  Choose $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(0,\infty)$ with $\varphi(t_0)=1$, and define
  \[
    q(\lambda,r)=\frac{p(\lambda)}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}
    (\varphi'(r)+\lambda\varphi(r))g.
  \]
  Its support in $\lambda$ stays away from zero and infinity. In particular,
  \[
    \|q\|_{\mathcal G}^2=\frac{\|g\|^2}{2}
    \left(\|\varphi'\|_2^2\int\frac{p^2}{\lambda}\ud\nu_a
    +\|\varphi\|_2^2\int\lambda p^2\ud\nu_a\right)<\infty,
  \]
  where the cross term vanishes because $\int\varphi'\varphi=0$.
  For a smooth compact raw tensor $F$ the isometry is
  \[
    \Phi F(\lambda,r)=\sqrt{2\lambda}
    \int_{t>0,\ t\ge r}e^{-\lambda(t-r)}F_t\ud t.
  \]
  The identity
  \[
    \int_{-\infty}^t e^{-\lambda(t-r)}
    (\varphi'(r)+\lambda\varphi(r))\ud r=\varphi(t)
  \]
  follows by differentiating $e^{-\lambda(t-r)}\varphi(r)$ in $r$.
  All integrals paired with $q$ are absolutely integrable because its two
  supports are compact and its $\lambda$ support stays away from zero.
  Consequently,
  \[
    \langle\Phi F,q\rangle_{\mathcal G}
    =\left(\int_{t>0}\langle F_t,g\rangle_{H_\Gamma}
    \varphi(t)\ud t\right)\int p\ud\nu_a=0.
  \]
  Density and continuity give $q\perp K$. The same calculation for the point
  feature instead gives
  \[
    \langle v,q\rangle_{\mathcal G}
    =\varphi(t_0)\|g\|^2\int_{(0,L]}p\ud\nu_a=\|g\|^2>0.
  \]
  Thus $(I-P)v\ne0$. Independence of $J$ and $W$ yields
  \[
    \operatorname{Var}(\mathcal I(v)\mid\sigma(W))
    =\|(I-P)v\|^2\ge\frac{|\langle v,q\rangle|^2}{\|q\|^2}>0.
  \]
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-32}.
\end{proof}

\input{notes/figures/nogo-feature-range}

The conclusion concerns positive finite cutoffs and $t_0>0$ in this fixed
extension. It neither treats time zero nor proves that a nonlinear canonical
limit retains an auxiliary component. Raw-test convergence can remove such
components in a limit. The fixed-extension source construction remains valid;
a claim that a particular nonlinear limit is measurable with respect to $W$
alone would need its own convergence argument.

\section{Topology, localization and limits of variational arguments}
\label{S:nogo-topology}

A useful approximation must be small in the topology in which the next
argument uses it. The obstructions below distinguish a local operator from a
Hilbert--Schmidt operator, a fixed spatial cutoff from a growing one, and
pointwise convergence from convergence of variational suprema. Throughout,
$a=\alpha_0\in(0,1)$ and $\Lambda=(1-\Delta)^{1/2}$, except that the positive
number $L$ denotes the temporal feature cutoff in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-32}.

\subsection{The first driver cannot always be Hilbert--Schmidt}
\label{S:nogo-21}

The one-dimensional operator construction suggests trying the same
Hilbert--Schmidt space in higher dimensions. A compact spatial observation
removes infinite volume, but it does not remove the following high-frequency
obstruction. The relevant compatibility is with the given noise, through its
bilinear first-driver tests.

\setcounter{nogo}{20}
\begin{nogo}[A same-noise Hilbert--Schmidt obstruction]
\label{P:nogo-21}
Suppose that $d\in\{2,3\}$, $s\in\R$, and $0<T\le1$. Put
$E_s=H^s(\R^d)\times H^{s-1}(\R^d)$ and let $S(t)$ be the massless wave
phase group. For every nonzero covariance satisfying Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog},
there are a nonnegative compact smooth physical coefficient mask $\zeta$ and
a compact smooth observation cutoff $\chi$, equal to one on a sufficiently
large ball, for which the localized Gaussian first-driver form has no
compatible realization in $L^2(\Omega;\operatorname{HS}(E_s,E_s))$.
Here compatibility means equality with the given $W$ for each fixed smooth
phase pair, as specified in the proof.
\end{nogo}

\begin{proof}
First consider a deterministic coefficient $h(u,x)=k(u)H(x)$, where
$H\in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$ is nonzero, even and nonnegative, and
$k\in W^{1,\infty}[0,T]$ satisfies $k\ge k_0>0$. Write
$B(b)(v,w)=(0,bv)$ and
\[
 A_h=\int_0^T S(-u)B(h(u))S(u)\ud u,\qquad
 S(u,\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}
 \cos(u|\xi|)&\sin(u|\xi|)/|\xi|\\
 -|\xi|\sin(u|\xi|)&\cos(u|\xi|)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The sine quotient has its continuous value at zero. The integral is a strong
operator integral on $E_s$: $S(u)$ is strongly continuous and uniformly
bounded on compact time intervals, and multiplication by $H$ is bounded on
$H^s$. For the latter assertion, the weighted Fourier kernel is bounded in
absolute value by a constant times
$\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{|s|}|\widehat H(\xi-\eta)|$, an integrable
convolution kernel. This proves boundedness for every fixed real $s$.

Normalize the Fourier coordinates by
$\operatorname{diag}(\langle\xi\rangle^s,\langle\xi\rangle^{s-1})$.
Using the unitary Fourier transform, the lower-left block of $A_h$ then has
kernel
\[
 K_s(\xi,\eta)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}
 \langle\xi\rangle^{s-1}\langle\eta\rangle^{-s}
 \widehat H(\xi-\eta)
 \int_0^T k(u)\cos(u|\xi|)\cos(u|\eta|)\ud u.
\]
This formula follows from both phase coordinates: before applying the weights,
the full product, with $r=|\xi|$, $\rho=|\eta|$, is
\[
 \widehat H(\xi-\eta)
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -\dfrac{\sin(ru)}r\cos(\rho u)&
 -\dfrac{\sin(ru)}r\dfrac{\sin(\rho u)}\rho\\
 \cos(ru)\cos(\rho u)&\cos(ru)\dfrac{\sin(\rho u)}\rho
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
All Fourier and time exchanges are justified on Schwartz pairs by the rapid
decay of $\widehat H$ and then by density.

Let $J_0=\int_0^T k>0$ and
$B_0=\|k\|_\infty+T\|k'\|_\infty$. Choose $\epsilon>0$ so that
$\epsilon\le1$, $\epsilon T\le1$ and
$\widehat H(z)\ge\widehat H(0)/2>0$ when $|z|\le\epsilon$.
If $|\xi-\eta|\le\epsilon$, then
\[
 \int_0^T k(u)\cos((r-\rho)u)\ud u\ge J_0/2,
 \qquad
 \left|\int_0^T k(u)\cos((r+\rho)u)\ud u\right|
 \le B_0/(r+\rho).
\]
The second bound is integration by parts in the absolutely continuous
function $k$. Product-to-sum therefore bounds the time integral in $K_s$
from below by $J_0/8$ when
$r\ge R_0:=\max(1,2\epsilon,4B_0/J_0)$.
On the same region,
\[
 \langle\xi\rangle^{s-1}\langle\eta\rangle^{-s}
 =\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}
 (\langle\xi\rangle/\langle\eta\rangle)^s
 \ge 2^{-|s|}/(\sqrt2 r).
\]
Consequently, $|K_s(\xi,\eta)|\ge c_s/|\xi|$ there, for $c_s>0$.
If $P_N$ cuts off Fourier frequencies to $B_N$, its truncated kernel is
square integrable and Parseval followed by Tonelli gives
\[
 \|P_NK_sP_N\|_{\operatorname{HS}}^2
 \ge c_s^2|B_\epsilon||\mathbb S^{d-1}|
 \int_{R_0}^{N-\epsilon}r^{d-3}\ud r\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Indeed Parseval in the second kernel variable identifies its square integral
with the sum of squared images of an orthonormal basis. If $A_h$ were
Hilbert--Schmidt, every block compression would have bounded
Hilbert--Schmidt norm. The displayed divergence proves otherwise. Real
Hilbert--Schmidt operators and their complexifications have the same norm,
so Fourier complexification has not changed the conclusion.

Suppose that $\operatorname{supp}H\subset B_A$, and let $\chi=1$ on
$B_{A+T+1}$. With $D_\chi$ denoting multiplication of both phase coordinates,
finite propagation gives
\[
 D_\chi A_h=A_h=A_hD_\chi.
\]
For completeness, the local wave energy on a shrinking ball has boundary
flux $v_t\partial_n v-(v_t^2+|\nabla v|^2)/2\le0$. Zero initial data
therefore remain zero in the interior cone; time reversal gives the same
support assertion for $S(-u)$. Apply this first to smooth data and then use
Sobolev continuity and distributional testing to pass to $E_s$. The
coefficient acts only in $B_A$, and the resulting output lies in
$B_{A+T}$. Thus the failure persists after this compact observation.

We next produce $h$ as an actual covariance mean. Choose $L_0\ge1$ with
$0<\Gamma(B_{L_0})<\infty$, and an even nonnegative $q\in C_c^\infty$
equal to at least one on $B_{2L_0+1}$. Set $U=\Gamma*q$. Local finiteness
of $\Gamma$ permits differentiation on every compact set, so $U$ is smooth,
even and nonnegative, with $U(0)>0$. Moreover,
\[
 Q=\int q(x)U(x)\ud x
 \ge |B_{L_0+1}|\Gamma(B_{L_0})>0.
\]
Choose an even nonnegative compact smooth $\zeta$, equal to one near zero,
and put $H=\zeta U$. Choose $f\in C_c^\infty(0,1)$ nonnegative and
nonzero, and set
\[
 Z=W(f\otimes q),\qquad
 k(u)=\int_0^1|u-v|^{-a}f(v)\ud v.
\]
For $u\in[0,1]$, $k(u)\ge\int f>0$. Moving the derivative to the smooth
zero extension of $f$ shows $k\in C^1[0,1]$, since $|\cdot|^{-a}$ is
locally integrable. Also $\E Z^2=Q\int_0^1 f(u)k(u)\ud u\in(0,\infty)$.
The mask is applied after the covariance convolution: $H=\zeta(\Gamma*q)$.

It remains to specify the compatible first-driver form with the correct
Sobolev adjoint. For real Schwartz phases $z,w$ put
\[
 v_u=[S(u)z]_1,\qquad
 G_w(u)=-\frac{\sin(u|D|)}{|D|}(\chi\Lambda^{2s}w_1)
       +\cos(u|D|)(\chi\Lambda^{2s-2}w_2),
\]
and define
$F_{z,w}(u,x)=\mathbf1_{(0,T)}(u)\zeta(x)v_u(x)G_w(u,x)$.
This test lies in the raw noise completion. In fact, on a fixed compact
spatial support $K$, its covariance norm is bounded by a constant times
$\|u\mapsto\|F(u)\|_\infty\|_{L^2(0,T)}$, by local Radon mass
$\Gamma(K-K)$ and the time Schur bound for $|u-v|^{-a}$. Smooth interior
time approximation gives membership. Define
$\ell_\chi(z,w)=W(F_{z,w})$ on the given noise space.
Direct transposition, with the Sobolev weights retained, gives
\[
 G_w(u)=\Lambda^{2s-2}
       \big((D_\chi S(-u))^{*_{E_s}}w\big)_2.
\]
In particular, multiplication by $\chi$ has not been treated as self-adjoint
in $E_s$. Absolute Fubini in the covariance pairing now gives
\[
 \E[Z\ell_\chi(z,w)]
 =\int_0^T\!\int k(u)H(x)v_u(x)G_w(u,x)\ud x\ud u
 =\langle D_\chi A_hz,w\rangle_{E_s}
 =\langle A_hz,w\rangle_{E_s}.
\]
All spatial factors in the raw tests have compact support and the temporal
singularity is integrable, which supplies the absolute Fubini bound.

If $X\in L^2(\Omega;\operatorname{HS}(E_s,E_s))$ satisfied
$\langle Xz,w\rangle_{E_s}=\ell_\chi(z,w)$ almost surely for every fixed
Schwartz pair, then Cauchy--Schwarz would make
$Y=\E[ZX]$ a Hilbert--Schmidt Bochner integral. Continuous matrix evaluation
would give $\langle Yz,w\rangle=\langle A_hz,w\rangle$ for all such pairs.
Density implies $Y=A_h$, a contradiction. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-21}.
\end{proof}

This result concerns the same-index phase spaces $E_s$ and a compatible
$L^2$ Hilbert--Schmidt realization. It does not concern every cutoff: the zero
cutoff, for example, has no such obstruction. It is not a nonexistence result
for the Stratonovich solution or its moments. A route using loss of regularity
between input and output, operator norms, or a different Schatten class must
supply its own estimates; changing the fixed index $s$ alone cannot reopen
this particular route.

\subsection{A physical mask may not act on the noise quotient}
\label{S:nogo-22}

A smooth physical mask is harmless on ordinary test functions. It need not
preserve the zero classes of a covariance seminorm. The following is the
obstruction already proved in Proposition~\ref{P:masked-quotient-obstruction};
we include its witness to explain why the auxiliary completion is needed.

\setcounter{nogo}{21}
\begin{nogo}[A mask that separates noise-null representatives]
\label{P:nogo-22}
For $d=2$, $\alpha=1$, $0<a<1$ and
$\Gamma=\delta_0(\ud x_1)\ud x_2$, there exist real compact smooth $F$ and
a nonnegative smooth square mask $\zeta$ for which
$\E W(F)^2=0$ but $\E W(\zeta F)^2>0$.
Thus multiplication by this mask does not descend to the original noise
quotient, and no finite uniform covariance-norm multiplier bound holds.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
Choose a nonnegative nonconstant compact smooth plateau function $\psi$ and
put $\chi=\psi^2$. The spatial covariance seminorm is
\[
 V(g)=\int_\R\left(\int_\R g(x_1,x_2)\ud x_2\right)^2\ud x_1.
\]
This also shows positive definiteness of the nonnegative even tempered
measure $\Gamma$; its scaling is $\Gamma(cA)=c\Gamma(A)$, as required
for $d=2$, $\alpha=1$. Put
$g(x)=\chi(x_1)\chi''(x_2)$ and
$\zeta(x)=\chi(x_1)\chi(x_2)=(\psi(x_1)\psi(x_2))^2$.
Then $\int\chi''=0$, whereas integration by parts gives
\[
 \int\zeta(x)g(x)\ud x_2=-\chi(x_1)^2\int(\chi')^2.
\]
Consequently, $V(g)=0$ and
$V(\zeta g)=\int\chi^4\,\big(\int(\chi')^2\big)^2>0$.
Tensor with a nonzero nonnegative $\tau\in C_c^\infty(0,T)$. Its temporal
variance is finite and strictly positive, so $F=\tau\otimes g$ has the
asserted full-noise variances. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-22}.
\end{proof}

This covariance is not two-dimensional spatial white noise. One can still
mask individual tests before taking an auxiliary completion. To reopen a
multiplier argument on the original quotient, one must first prove that the
chosen mask preserves its null space and then prove the required norm bound.

\subsection{Frequency decay does not give whole-space compactness}
\label{S:nogo-24}

The compact-cutoff estimates use spatial confinement as well as frequency
smoothing. Removing the former loses compactness even for a deterministic
Bessel multiplier; see Proposition~\ref{P:cutoff-boundary-tests}.

\setcounter{nogo}{23}
\begin{nogo}[Translations defeat whole-space compactness]
\label{P:nogo-24}
For every $d\ge1$ and $\gamma>0$, the operator $\Lambda^{-\gamma}$ on
$L^2(\R^d)$ is not compact.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
Take a nonzero Schwartz function $f$ with Gaussian Fourier transform and put
$g=\Lambda^{-\gamma}f$. Then $g$ is nonzero and Schwartz. For translations
$T_hg(x)=g(x-h)$, Plancherel gives
\[
 \langle T_hg,g\rangle
 =\int e^{-i\xi\cdot h}|\widehat g(\xi)|^2\ud\xi\longrightarrow0
 \quad (|h|\longrightarrow\infty).
\]
The limit follows, for example, by integration by parts in the Fourier
transform of the Schwartz function $|\widehat g|^2$.
Choose a sufficiently large spacing $b$ and $h_n=nb e_1$ so that distinct
images $T_{h_n}g$ have squared distance at least $\|g\|_2^2$.
The inputs $T_{h_n}f$ have constant norm and
$\Lambda^{-\gamma}T_{h_n}f=T_{h_n}g$. Their images have no Cauchy
subsequence. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-24}.
\end{proof}

Compact spatial cutoffs and the conditional Schatten estimates in the main
text survive. No necessity at a Schatten endpoint is asserted here. A
whole-space successor needs a tightness condition that prevents translation
of mass, or an explicitly different topology.

\subsection{Fixed projections do not control growing spatial masks}
\label{S:nogo-25}

At each fixed radius the Cameron mean map can be compact. Its finite-rank
approximations need not be uniform in that radius. The following witness uses
actual unit controls for one-dimensional spatial white noise. The fixed-radius
map and its compact approximation are those of
Proposition~\ref{P:cameron-full-mean}.

\setcounter{nogo}{24}
\begin{nogo}[Escape from every fixed smooth projection]
\label{P:nogo-25}
Let $\Gamma=\delta_0$ in $d=1$, and let $H_I$ be the closed raw-noise
space generated by tests supported in $I\times\R$, $I=(0,1)$. Let $P_J$
be any fixed finite-rank orthogonal projection in $H_I$ whose range is
spanned by smooth compact tests supported in $I\times\R$. For the physical masked mean multiplier $T_R$ on
$F=(H^{1/2}\times H^{-1/2})\oplus\R^2$, with the last coordinates
representing constant displacement and velocity, one has, for every finite
$r\ge1$,
\[
 \sup_R\|T_R(I-P_J)\|_{H_I\to L^r((0,1);\mathcal K(F))}\ge c_a>0,
\]
where $c_a$ can be chosen independently of $J$. Here the smooth masks $m_R$
equal one on $[-R,R]$. For a smooth control $\Psi$ with covariance mean
$h_\Psi$, the operator sends $(v,w,c,d)$ to
$(0,m_Rh_\Psi(s)(v+c),0,0)$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
Use the temporal equilibrium density $f_a$ of
Lemma~\ref{L:temporal-equilibrium}, so that
$\int_0^1|s-t|^{-a}f_a(t)\ud t=1$ and
$\|f_a\|_{H_a}^2=M_a>0$.
Choose nonnegative $q\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ with $\|q\|_2=1$, and put
$q_n(x)=q(x-x_n)$, where $x_n\to\infty$. The controls
\[
 \Psi_n=\frac{f_a\otimes q_n}{\sqrt{M_a}}
\]
have unit raw norm and time-independent covariance mean
$q_n/\sqrt{M_a}$. Membership follows by $L^2$ approximation of $f_a$ and
the Schur bound for the temporal kernel.
The finitely many tests spanning $P_J$ have common compact spatial support.
For all sufficiently large $n$, spatial whiteness gives
$P_J\Psi_n=0$ exactly. Choose $R_n$ to contain $\operatorname{supp}q_n$
in the plateau of $m_{R_n}$.

The constant-displacement input $e=(0,0,1,0)$ has norm one. The phase dual
\[
 w_n=(0,\Lambda q_n/\|q\|_{H^{1/2}},0,0)
\]
also has norm one. Indeed its velocity norm is $H^{-1/2}$, and translation
preserves $\|q\|_{H^{1/2}}$. The mean multiplier sends $e$ to the velocity
$q_n/\sqrt{M_a}$, so the $H^{-1/2}$ inner product gives, for every $s$,
\[
 \langle T_{R_n}\Psi_n(s)e,w_n\rangle_F
 =\frac{\|q_n\|_2^2}{\sqrt{M_a}\|q\|_{H^{1/2}}}
 =:c_a>0.
\]
Integrating over the unit time interval proves the bound for all $r$ stated.
This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-25}.
\end{proof}

\input{notes/figures/nogo-projection-escape}

For a fixed $R$, sufficiently distant translates miss its mask, so there is
no contradiction with fixed-radius compactness. A replacement must use
projections depending on the radius with quantitative control, or a different
localization argument. The witness does not exclude every such strategy.

\subsection{Two distinct limitations of block costs}
\label{S:nogo-26}

A nonlinear block argument must handle both small perturbations and rare large
blocks. A power larger than one misses the first effect; an averaged budget
alone does not bound the largest block.

\setcounter{nogo}{25}
\begin{nogo}[A pure Young power and a maximum from an average]
\label{P:nogo-26}
With $\theta=2/3$ and $\nu=1/\theta=3/2$, no finite constant $C>0$ gives
$\log U(1)\le C\|D\|_{C^\theta}^{\nu}$ for all scalar paths starting at zero
and solving $\ud U=U\ud D$, $U(0)=1$, when
$\|D\|_{C^\theta}=\|D\|_\infty+[D]_{C^\theta}$.
Moreover, a bound on $N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N b_j^r$, $r>0$, does not give a
bound independent of $N$ on $\max_j b_j$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
For $D(t)=xt$, $x>0$, the solution is $U(t)=e^{xt}$ and
$\|D\|_{C^{2/3}}=2x$. Taking $x=1/(16C^2)$ gives
$C(2x)^{3/2}=x/\sqrt2<x=\log U(1)$.
For the second assertion, take $b_1=N^{1/r}$ and $b_j=0$ for $j>1$.
Their averaged $r$th power is one while their maximum tends to infinity.
This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-26}.
\end{proof}

A linear-plus-power cost is not refuted. For any $\tau>0$ and $\nu>1$,
$x\le\tau+\tau^{1-\nu}x^\nu$ gives an explicit alternative with slack.
Also, if $0<\nu<r$ and $N^{-1}\sum b_j^r\le M$, then H\"older gives
$N^{-1}\sum b_j^\nu\le M^{\nu/r}$, and
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{b_j>K}b_j^\nu\le K^{\nu-r}M.
\]
Thus an averaged tail argument survives. Reopening the block route requires
an actual evolution estimate with its linear term or slack, followed by an
averaged bad-block estimate. These scalar tests alone do not construct a
Cameron potential or prove a wave evolution bound.

\subsection{Concentrating temporal controls restrict integrability}
\label{S:nogo-27}

Compactness of a temporal mean map has a natural upper endpoint. A
concentrating control detects both excessive integrability and failure of
compactness at that endpoint.

\setcounter{nogo}{26}
\begin{nogo}[The temporal concentration threshold]
\label{P:nogo-27}
Let $I=(0,1)$, $0<a<1$, and let $H_a$ be the completion of smooth tests for
$\|f\|_{H_a}^2=\int_I\int_I f(t)f(s)|t-s|^{-a}\ud t\ud s$.
On smooth tests set $C_af(t)=\int_I|t-s|^{-a}f(s)\ud s$.
The map $C_a$ is not bounded from $H_a$ to $L^r(I)$ when $r>2/a$,
including $r=\infty$, and it is not compact into $L^{2/a}(I)$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
Choose $t_0\in I$ and a nonzero nonnegative
$\phi\in C_c^\infty(-1,1)$. Put
\[
 v_\phi=\int\!\int\phi(u)\phi(v)|u-v|^{-a}\ud u\ud v\in(0,\infty),
 \qquad
 f_\epsilon(t)=\frac{\epsilon^{a/2-1}}{\sqrt{v_\phi}}
 \phi((t-t_0)/\epsilon).
\]
For sufficiently small $\epsilon$, its support lies in $I$. Changing both
variables shows $\|f_\epsilon\|_{H_a}=1$, and
\[
 (C_af_\epsilon)(t_0+\epsilon y)
 =\frac{\epsilon^{-a/2}}{\sqrt{v_\phi}}
 \int|y-v|^{-a}\phi(v)\ud v.
\]
For $|y|\le1$, the last integral is at least $2^{-a}\int\phi>0$.
Thus for finite $r$,
$\|C_af_\epsilon\|_{L^r}\ge c_r\epsilon^{1/r-a/2}$, and the analogous
supremum lower bound is $c\epsilon^{-a/2}$. This proves unboundedness above
$2/a$.
For every fixed $t\ne t_0$, on the other hand,
$C_af_\epsilon(t)=O_t(\epsilon^{a/2})\to0$.
At $r=2/a$ the norm lower bound stays strictly positive. If a subsequence
converged strongly in this $L^r$ space, a further subsequence would converge
almost everywhere to its strong limit. The limit would have to be zero,
contradicting the norm lower bound. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-27}.
\end{proof}

The endpoint argument refutes compactness, not boundedness. Tensoring the
controls with one fixed nonzero spatial profile transfers this input
obstruction to a corresponding separated mean map. The corrected compact
range $2<r<2/a$ is consistent with the witness. A larger range requires
additional temporal regularity or restrictions that exclude concentration.

\subsection{Pointwise convergence does not pass through a supremum}
\label{S:nogo-30}

The Gaussian action cost must remain in a variational comparison. Even with
that cost, bounded near-maximizers can escape in infinitely many directions.

\setcounter{nogo}{29}
\begin{nogo}[Escaping variational scores]
\label{P:nogo-30}
There are continuous functions $J_n$ on the Hilbert space $\ell^2$ tending
to zero at every point such that
\[
 \sup_h\{J_n(h)-\tfrac12\|h\|^2\}\ge\tfrac12,
 \qquad
 \sup_h\{0-\tfrac12\|h\|^2\}=0.
\]
The witnesses for the first inequality have norm one.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
Let $(e_n)$ be the standard orthonormal sequence and set
$J_n(h)=(1-2\|h-e_n\|)_+$.
For each fixed $h\in\ell^2$, its $n$th coordinate tends to zero, and hence
$\|h-e_n\|^2=\|h\|^2+1-2\langle h,e_n\rangle\to\|h\|^2+1\ge1$.
Thus $J_n(h)=0$ eventually. But $J_n(e_n)=1$, so its score at $e_n$ is
$1/2$. The limiting score is nonpositive and equals zero at $h=0$.
This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-30}.
\end{proof}

A bounded action budget alone supplies no strong compactness in $\ell^2$.
To pass to an exact constant one needs a variational upper-limit argument,
for example compactness of near-maximizers in a topology with the needed
upper semicontinuity. Pointwise convergence, or compactness proved only with
an additional fixed spatial cutoff, does not supply that argument by itself.

\subsection{A cutoff point feature can lie outside the raw-noise image}
\label{S:nogo-32}

Lemma~\ref{L:source-retaining-isometry} embeds the original noise into one
fixed larger Gaussian space. Its closed image retains all original raw tests.
It does not contain every positive-cutoff point feature. The following
explicit orthogonal vector explains the role of the auxiliary noise.

\setcounter{nogo}{31}
\begin{nogo}[Positive-cutoff point sources need auxiliary noise]
\label{P:nogo-32}
Suppose that $\Gamma\ne0$ satisfies Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, $t_0>0$,
$L\in(0,\infty)$ and $\epsilon>0$. In the feature space
\[
 \mathcal G=L^2((0,\infty)_\lambda\times\R_r,
       \nu_a(\ud\lambda)\ud r;H_\Gamma),\qquad
 \nu_a(\ud\lambda)=\lambda^{a-1}\ud\lambda/\Gamma_E(a),
\]
let $K=\Phi H_W$ be the closed image of
Lemma~\ref{L:source-retaining-isometry}. Set $g=p_\epsilon(x-\cdot)$ and
\[
 v(\lambda,r)=\mathbf1_{\{\lambda\le L\}}\sqrt{2\lambda}
 e^{-\lambda(t_0-r)}\mathbf1_{\{r\le t_0\}}g.
\]
Then $v\notin K$. On the fixed extension
$\mathcal I(v)=W(\Phi^{-1}Pv)+J((I-P)v)$, where $P$ projects onto $K$
and $J$ is independent of $W$, one has
$\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal I(v)\mid\sigma(W))>0$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
The heat profile has finite strictly positive norm:
\[
 \|g\|_{H_\Gamma}^2=\int p_{2\epsilon}(y)\Gamma(\ud y)>0.
\]
Finiteness follows from temperedness and Gaussian decay, and positivity from
nonzero nonnegative $\Gamma$. Integrating in $r$ also gives
$\|v\|_{\mathcal G}^2=\|g\|^2\nu_a((0,L])<\infty$.
Choose nonnegative nonzero smooth bumps $u_A,u_B$ supported in compact
intervals $A\subset(0,L)$ and $B\subset(L,\infty)$, respectively, and put
\[
 p(\lambda)=\frac{u_A(\lambda)}{\int u_A\ud\nu_a}
            -\frac{u_B(\lambda)}{\int u_B\ud\nu_a}.
\]
Then $\int p\ud\nu_a=0$ and $\int_{(0,L]}p\ud\nu_a=1$.
Choose $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(0,\infty)$ with $\varphi(t_0)=1$, and define
\[
 q(\lambda,r)=\frac{p(\lambda)}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}
              (\varphi'(r)+\lambda\varphi(r))g.
\]
Its support in $\lambda$ stays away from zero and infinity. In particular,
\[
 \|q\|_{\mathcal G}^2=\frac{\|g\|^2}{2}
 \left(\|\varphi'\|_2^2\int\frac{p^2}{\lambda}\ud\nu_a
       +\|\varphi\|_2^2\int\lambda p^2\ud\nu_a\right)<\infty,
\]
where the cross term vanishes because $\int\varphi'\varphi=0$.
For a smooth compact raw tensor $F$ the isometry is
\[
 \Phi F(\lambda,r)=\sqrt{2\lambda}
      \int_{t>0,\ t\ge r}e^{-\lambda(t-r)}F_t\ud t.
\]
The identity
\[
 \int_{-\infty}^t e^{-\lambda(t-r)}
       (\varphi'(r)+\lambda\varphi(r))\ud r=\varphi(t)
\]
follows by differentiating $e^{-\lambda(t-r)}\varphi(r)$ in $r$.
All integrals paired with $q$ are absolutely integrable because its two
supports are compact and its $\lambda$ support stays away from zero.
Consequently,
\[
 \langle\Phi F,q\rangle_{\mathcal G}
 =\left(\int_{t>0}\langle F_t,g\rangle_{H_\Gamma}
                      \varphi(t)\ud t\right)\int p\ud\nu_a=0.
\]
Density and continuity give $q\perp K$. The same calculation for the point
feature instead gives
\[
 \langle v,q\rangle_{\mathcal G}
 =\varphi(t_0)\|g\|^2\int_{(0,L]}p\ud\nu_a=\|g\|^2>0.
\]
Thus $(I-P)v\ne0$. Independence of $J$ and $W$ yields
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\mathcal I(v)\mid\sigma(W))
 =\|(I-P)v\|^2\ge\frac{|\langle v,q\rangle|^2}{\|q\|^2}>0.
\]
This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-32}.
\end{proof}

\section{The compact mean and Cameron map on time blocks}
\label{S:actual-cameron-mean}

Retain the nonzero original covariance in Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, its
amplitude, $a = \alpha_0$, and the real phases $E = H^{1/2}\times H^{-1/2}$ and
$F = E\oplus\R^2$. Write $\Lambda = (1-\Delta)^{1/2}$. The form $B_\Gamma$ and
constant $R_{\mathrm{sp}}$ are those of
Section~\ref{S:spatial-occupation-foundation}. Let $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ be the real
covariance-null completion of $C_c^\infty(\R^d)$ with inner product $B_\Gamma$,
and $H_t$ the analogous completion of $C_c^\infty(I)$, $I = (0,1)$, with time
kernel $|s-t|^{-a}$. Let $H = H_I$ be the completion of smooth tests supported
inside $I\times\R^d$. It embeds isometrically as a closed subspace of the given
$H_W$. We retain the given $W$ restricted to it.

Fix $R > 0$ and real even $\eta_R \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$ with $0 \le \eta_R \le
1$, equal to one on the radius-$R$ ball and supported in the closed radius-$2R$
ball. The physical mask is $m_R = \eta_R^2$. Every operator path below takes its
mask argument equal to $m_R$: at this fixed radius the base profile $\zeta(y) =
m_R(Ry)$ meets the earlier hypotheses. The root $\eta_R$ factors state tests; it
does not multiply an original noise class. All compactness conclusions here fix
$R$.

\subsection{Fractional products and the compact spatial map}

\begin{lemma}[Fractional covariance products]
  \label{L:cameron-fractional-products}
  For $\alpha/4 < s < 1/2$, products of real $H^s$ functions define elements of
  $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$, with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-fractional-product}
    \|fg\|_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}
    \le C_s^2\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{sp}}}\|f\|_{H^s}\|g\|_{H^s}.
  \end{equation}
  Their Hilbert pairings equal their physical integrals. In particular,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-fractional-absolute}
    \int|f_1(x)f_2(x)f_3(x-y)f_4(x-y)|\ud x\,\Gamma(\ud y)
    \le C_s^4R_{\mathrm{sp}}\prod_{j=1}^4\|f_j\|_{H^s}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  First approximate real $H^1$ factors by compact smooth ones. The absolute
  four-linear bound~\eqref{E:spatial-four-linear} and covariance PSD make their
  products Cauchy in $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$, with norm at most
  $\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{sp}}}N(f)N(g)$. Expanding product differences proves
  independence of approximation; physical continuity identifies all pairings.
  PSD for products also follows from $fg = ((f+g)^2-(f-g)^2)/4$.

  Use real radial Fourier bands $D_0 = 1_{\{|\xi|<1\}}$ and $D_j =
  1_{\{2^{j-1}\le|\xi|<2^j\}}$, $j \ge 1$. For $f \in H^s$ put $f_j = D_jf$.
  Each block is real and in $H^1$. Frequency support, Parseval and weighted
  Cauchy--Schwarz give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-dyadic-product}
    N(f_j)\le2^{j\alpha/4}\|f_j\|_2,\qquad
    \sum_{j\ge0}N(f_j)\le C_s\|f\|_{H^s}.
  \end{equation}
  Indeed, $\sum_j2^{-2j(s-\alpha/4)} < \infty$, while $\sum_j2^{2js}\|f_j\|_2^2
  \le 2^{2s}\|f\|_{H^s}^2$. The low block has factor one. No $L^p$ multiplier
  result is used. Also $\sum_j\|f_j\|_2 < \infty$ since $s > 0$. Thus,
  $\sum_j|f_j|$ lies in $L^2$ by Minkowski and monotone convergence, and the
  series is absolutely convergent a.e. with sum $f$.

  Apply the absolute $H^1$ four-linear estimate to each band quadruple. The sum
  of its absolute integrals is at most
  $R_{\mathrm{sp}}\prod_{\ell=1}^4\sum_jN((f_\ell)_j) < \infty$. Tonelli and the
  null-set transfer at both spatial positions in
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-physical-pair} identify the physical product series,
  proving~\eqref{E:cameron-fractional-absolute}. The double series
  $\sum_{j,k}f_jg_k$ is also absolutely convergent in $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ by the
  $H^1$ product bound. Its partial sums are products of $H^1$ functions. The
  absolute quadruple calculation identifies their limiting physical pairings and
  gives~\eqref{E:cameron-fractional-product}. Compact smooth functions are dense
  in $H^s$: approximate in Fourier space by real Schwartz functions, then cut
  each off compactly in $H^1$ and hence in $H^s$. This also proves uniqueness of
  the bilinear extension. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:cameron-fractional-products}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Compact spatial mean map]\label{P:cameron-spatial-map}
  The smooth-test map
  \[
    T_{\mathrm{sp}}q=iB_F(m_R(\Gamma*q))
  \]
  extends to a compact linear map $T_{\mathrm{sp}}:H_{\mathrm{sp}} \to
  \mathcal{K}(F)$, where $\mathcal{K}(F)$ has the operator norm. For $z =
  (z_1,z_2,c_1,c_2)$ and $w = (w_1,w_2,d_1,d_2)$ set $w' = \Lambda^{-1}w_2$. Its
  exact row identity is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-spatial-row}
    \langle T_{\mathrm{sp}}qz,w\rangle_F
    =\langle q,k(z,w)\rangle_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}},
    \qquad
    k(z,w)=(\eta_Rz_1+c_1\eta_R)(\eta_Rw').
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Fix $s$ with $\alpha/4 < s < 1/2$; this interval is nonempty because the
  original threshold implies $\alpha < 2$. For smooth compact $q$, $\Gamma*q$ is
  locally smooth by differentiation under the compactly restricted Radon
  integral. The masked potential is smooth compact. The velocity pairing in $E$
  has weight $\Lambda^{-1}$; hence physical Fubini
  gives~\eqref{E:cameron-spatial-row} on smooth states. Evenness of $\Gamma$
  changes $B_\Gamma(k,q)$ to $B_\Gamma(q,k)$. For general states the localized
  products are $L^1$, and $\Gamma*q$ is bounded on their compact support. $L^2$
  state approximation passes the physical integral to the limit, while
  Lemma~\ref{L:cameron-fractional-products} passes the $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$
  pairing. This proves the row.

  Multiplication by $\eta_R$ maps bounded $H^{1/2}$ sets to precompact $H^s$
  sets by Proposition~\ref{P:cutoff-singular-values}, with gain $1/2-s > 0$.
  This applies to $z_1$ and $w'$. The $c_1\eta_R$ family is finite dimensional
  and compact. The closures of both factor families for unit $z,w$ are compact,
  so their products have compact closure $\mathcal{L}$ in $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ by
  the fractional product bound. The row identity gives $\|T_{\mathrm{sp}}q\| \le
  \sup_{k\in\mathcal{L}}\|k\|\|q\|$. Null covariance classes therefore map to
  zero.

  For smooth $q$, multiplication by its potential is compact $H^{1/2} \to
  H^{-1/2}$ by the same whole-space cutoff result with gain one. Its extra $c_1$
  column is finite rank, and the instantaneous $c_2$ column is zero. Thus, the
  smooth images lie in $\mathcal{K}(F)$, and so does the bounded extension by
  operator-norm closure.

  Choose a complete smooth-test orthonormal basis of $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ by
  countable compact-support approximation and Gram--Schmidt, omitting null
  classes. Let $Q_n$ project onto its first $n$ terms. The row identity yields
  \[
    \|T_{\mathrm{sp}}(I-Q_n)\|
    \le\sup_{k\in\mathcal{L}}\|(I-Q_n)k\|_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}
    \longrightarrow0.
  \]
  Strong projection convergence is uniform on compact $\mathcal{L}$ by a
  finite-net argument and $\|I-Q_n\| \le 1$. Each $T_{\mathrm{sp}}Q_n$ has
  finite-dimensional range; their operator-norm limit is a compact map. This is
  compactness of the map, in addition to compact values. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:cameron-spatial-map}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The supported tensor and temporal mean}

\begin{lemma}[Supported original tensor space]\label{L:cameron-supported-tensor}
  Canonically $H = H_t\otimes H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ as real Hilbert spaces. It is
  separable and admits a complete orthonormal basis $e_j$ with smooth test
  representatives supported inside $I\times\R^d$. Projection $P_J$ onto its
  first $J$ elements is also an orthogonal projection on $H_W$ by the same
  finite formula.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  On separated smooth tests, absolute Fubini makes the original inner product
  exactly the tensor product after both null quotients. Finite sums are dense:
  approximate a smooth compact spacetime test uniformly by smooth time
  partitions times its spatial slices, on one common compact support inside
  $I\times\R^d$. A uniform error $\epsilon$ on spatial compact $A$ has squared
  original covariance norm at most
  \[
    \epsilon^2|A|\Gamma(A-A)
    \int_I\int_I|s-t|^{-a}\ud s\ud t<\infty.
  \]
  This also gives countable smooth-test density on exhausting compact sets, both
  here and in $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$. Gram--Schmidt skipping zeros preserves smooth
  compact support. The isometric embedding into $H_W$ has closed image by
  completeness; its finite orthonormal projection formula remains orthogonal
  there. It is not multiplication by a time indicator and gives no independence
  of disjoint time intervals. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:cameron-supported-tensor}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The compact temporal mean, without trace class]
  \label{P:cameron-temporal-map}
  Let $K$ on $L^2(I)$ have kernel $|s-t|^{-a}$. It is compact, self-adjoint,
  strictly positive and injective. The map $\iota:f\mapsto K^{1/2}f$ extends
  isometrically from $H_t$ onto $L^2(I)$, and the temporal mean
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-temporal-map}
    C_t=K^{1/2}\iota:H_t\longrightarrow L^r(I)
  \end{equation}
  is bounded and compact for every $2 < r < 2/a$. The displayed formula first
  has its $L^2(I)$ meaning; the same completed map is then shown to take values
  compactly in $L^r(I)$. The operator $K$ is not trace class and $C_t:H_t \to
  L^2(I)$ is not Hilbert--Schmidt.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Schur bounds $K$ by its finite row and column integrals. The Gamma
  representation of $|s-t|^{-a}$ and zero extension of $f \in L^2(I)$ give
  strict positivity directly: the kernel $e^{-\lambda|v|}$ has quadratic form
  \[
    \int_\R\frac{2\lambda}{\lambda^2+\xi^2}
    |\widehat{f}(\xi)|^2\ud\xi>0
    \quad(f\ne0,\ \lambda>0)
  \]
  in the unitary Fourier convention. Absolute Fubini is justified by Schur
  applied to $|f(s)f(t)|$. Integrating with positive weight
  $\lambda^{a-1}/\Gamma_E(a)$ proves positivity and injectivity. Symmetry gives
  self-adjointness. The deterministic truncated kernel $K_\epsilon =
  K1_{\{|s-t|\ge\epsilon\}}$ is square integrable, so finite separated-kernel
  approximations make it compact. The Schur norm of $K-K_\epsilon$ is at most
  $2\epsilon^{1-a}/(1-a)$, proving compactness of $K$. These truncations are not
  used as Gaussian covariances. Its positive square root is compact by the
  compact spectral theorem.

  On tests $\|K^{1/2}f\|_2 = \|f\|_{H_t}$. Smooth-test $L^2$ density and $\ker K
  = 0$ imply that its range is dense, so the isometry extends onto $L^2(I)$. On
  tests $C_tf = Kf$, and~\eqref{E:cameron-temporal-map} first gives compactness
  into $L^2$.

  Extend $K_a(v) = |v|^{-a}1_{\{|v|\le1\}}$ to the line. It lies in $L^{r/2}$.
  Scalar Young convolution gives
  \[
    \|g\|_{H_t}^2\le\|K_a\|_{r/2}\|g\|_{r'}^2,\qquad
    r'=r/(r-1).
  \]
  To verify the needed Young inequality, take $u = r/2$, $v = r'$, so $u,v < r$
  and $1/u+1/v = 1+1/r$. Split the absolute convolution integrand into
  $(|f|^u|h|^v)^{1/r}$, $|f|^{1-u/r}$ and $|h|^{1-v/r}$. H\"older with exponents
  $r$, $(1/u-1/r)^{-1}$ and $(1/v-1/r)^{-1}$, followed by integration and
  Tonelli, gives $\|f*h\|_r \le \|f\|_u\|h\|_v$. Covariance Cauchy--Schwarz and
  $L^r$ duality now imply $\|Kf\|_r \le C_{a,r}\|f\|_{H_t}$ on tests. Their $Kf$
  are bounded by the integrable-kernel row, making duality legitimate before
  completion. Density extends the estimate, with $L^2$ value equal
  to~\eqref{E:cameron-temporal-map}. Choose $r < r_2 < 2/a$. Compactness into
  $L^2$ and boundedness into $L^{r_2}$ give compactness into $L^r$ by $\|u\|_r
  \le \|u\|_2^\tau\|u\|_{r_2}^{1-\tau}$ with $1/r = \tau/2+(1-\tau)/r_2$.

  Finally, the orthonormal cell indicators $N^{1/2}1_{(j/N,(j+1)/N)}$, $0 \le j
  < N$, have $K$ quadratic forms summing to $2N^a/((1-a)(2-a)) \to \infty$.
  Thus, positive $K$ has infinite trace, and $K^{1/2}\iota$ is not
  Hilbert--Schmidt into $L^2$. No instantaneous $L^2$-valued Gaussian has been
  introduced. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:cameron-temporal-map}.
\end{proof}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-27} explains the upper endpoint of this compactness
range: temporal concentration makes the mean map unbounded above
$2/\alpha_0$ and noncompact at $2/\alpha_0$. The endpoint witness does not
refute boundedness there.


\begin{proposition}[The compact time-space mean]\label{P:cameron-full-mean}
  Fix one $2 < r < 2/a$ throughout this proposition. For finite tensors with
  orthonormal spatial labels define
  \[
    \mathcal{U}\left(\sum_j f_j\otimes q_j\right)(s)
    =\sum_j(Kf_j)(s)q_j.
  \]
  It extends to a bounded map $H \to L^r(I;H_{\mathrm{sp}})$ with dimension-free
  bound. The map
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-full-mean}
    T\Phi(s)=T_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathcal{U}\Phi(s))
    :H\longrightarrow L^r(I;\mathcal{K}(F))
  \end{equation}
  is compact. For smooth supported $\Phi$, it is the multiplier
  $iB_F(m_Rh_\Phi(s))$, where $h_\Phi = |\cdot|^{-a}*(\Gamma*\Phi)$. For the
  supported projections of Lemma~\ref{L:cameron-supported-tensor},
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-compact-tail}
    \epsilon_J:=\|T(I-P_J)\|_{H\to L^r(\mathcal{K}(F))}
    \longrightarrow0.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For a finite-dimensional spatial field $g$, positivity of the time kernel and
  scalar Young give
  \[
    \|g\|_{H_t\otimes H_{\mathrm{sp}}}^2
    \le\int_I\int_I|s-t|^{-a}\|g_s\|\|g_t\|\ud s\ud t
    \le C_{a,r}^2\|g\|_{L^{r'}(H_{\mathrm{sp}})}^2.
  \]
  Hilbert Cauchy--Schwarz and finite-dimensional $L^r$ duality give
  $\|\mathcal{U}\Phi\|_{L^r(H_{\mathrm{sp}})} \le C_{a,r}\|\Phi\|_H$. The
  duality can be checked by the pointwise direction
  $\|\mathcal{U}\Phi\|^{r-2}\mathcal{U}\Phi$ and smooth coefficient
  approximation in $L^{r'}$, controlled by the displayed estimate. No dimension
  factor occurs. Tensor density and completeness extend this to a strongly
  measurable Bochner field. This time-only map still contains spatial vectors in
  the covariance space. Spatial covariance is applied once, by
  $T_{\mathrm{sp}}$.

  The physical mean identity on separated tests follows
  from~\eqref{E:cameron-spatial-row}. For a smooth spacetime test use the
  separated compact-support approximations in the tensor proof. Their uniform
  errors give uniformly small masked mean errors, using the finite time row and
  locally finite spatial covariance on the relevant compact differences. The
  multiplier bound by $L^\infty+L^2$ then gives uniform operator convergence,
  identifying the $L^r$ mean in~\eqref{E:cameron-full-mean}. General $H$
  controls denote completed operator fields, without a pointwise-potential
  assertion.

  Let $Q_n$ be the spatial projections in the compact-map proof. The maps
  $T_n\Phi = T_{\mathrm{sp}}Q_n\mathcal{U}\Phi$ approximate $T$ in operator norm
  by the dimension-free bound and $\|T_{\mathrm{sp}}(I-Q_n)\| \to 0$. For fixed
  $n$, each spatial coordinate extraction $H \to H_t$ has norm at most one and
  its image under $C_t$ is precompact in $L^r$. Finite combination with the
  compact operators $T_{\mathrm{sp}}q_j$ makes $T_n$ compact. Hence, $T$ is
  compact. Scalar time compactness alone tensored with an infinite identity
  would not prove this.

  To prove~\eqref{E:cameron-compact-tail}, if it failed choose unit $v_J$ on a
  subsequence with $\|T(I-P_J)v_J\|$ bounded below. The vectors $(I-P_J)v_J$
  tend weakly to zero, since their pairings with fixed $h$ are bounded by
  $\|(I-P_J)h\|$. Compactness gives a norm-convergent image subsequence with
  weak limit zero, a contradiction. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:cameron-full-mean}.
\end{proof}

The radius remains fixed in this approximation. No-go~\ref{P:nogo-25}
shows that translated white-noise controls prevent these fixed smooth
projections from giving tails uniform over growing radii.


\subsection{The noise residual and its Cameron operator}

Choose integer $B \ge 1$ and $0 < \delta < \min(1,1/B)$. Put $N =
\lfloor1/(\delta B)\rfloor$, $s_m = m\delta B$, $\ell_m = B$ for $m < N$ and
$\ell_N = \delta^{-1}-NB \in [0,B)$. The intervals $I_m =
[s_m,s_m+\delta\ell_m]$ partition $[0,1]$ up to endpoints. With $Q_J = I-P_J$ on
$H_W$ and the explicit pullback~\eqref{E:raw-block-pullback}, define
\[
  Y_{m,J}(f)=W(Q_JT_{\delta,m}f),\qquad
  g_\delta=\delta^{(1-a)/2}.
\]
Fix parameters
\begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-block-parameters}
  \frac12<\theta<\min\left(1-\frac a2,\frac{3-a-\alpha}{2}\right),
  \qquad \frac1{1-\theta}<r<\frac2a,\qquad
  \frac1\theta\le p<2<r .
\end{equation}
These intervals are nonempty.

\begin{lemma}[Supported residual paths]\label{L:cameron-actual-residual}
  Gaussian transfer with mask $m_R$ and its $F$ extension constructs the
  centered Gaussian paths
  \[
    X_{m,J}(u)=g_\delta A_{m_R}(Y_{m,J})(\min(u,\ell_m)),
    \qquad 0\le u\le B.
  \]
  Their finite tuple lies in the separable space $\mathscr{B}_0^{N+1}$, where
  $\mathscr{B}_0 = h_0^\theta([0,B];\mathcal{K}(F))$. Every continuous linear
  statistic $\ell(X)$ is $W(k_\ell)$ for some $k_\ell \in H_I$. An empty last
  interval gives zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Isometry of the pullback and orthogonality of $Q_J$ give
  $\operatorname{Var}Y_{m,J}(f) \le \|f\|_{H_W}^2$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:source-gaussian-transfer} therefore applies. Its finite
  sums retain both deterministic localizations, and their limits retain the true
  $F$ extension and constant columns. Choose $\theta < \beta < \beta_2$ below
  the upper threshold in~\eqref{E:cameron-block-parameters}; read the
  operator-path construction at exponent $\beta$ with auxiliary $\beta_2$.
  Constant stopping preserves compact values and $C^\beta$ bounds, hence gives
  paths in the little $C^\theta$ closure by polygonal approximation. Its
  separability is Lemma~\ref{L:raw-block-inputs}.

  As in Lemma~\ref{L:raw-block-inputs}, every observed row's slow pullback
  belongs to $H_I$: its time support is in $[0,1]$, and approximating its
  indicator inside $I$ converges in original covariance norm by dominated
  convergence against the integrable time kernel and locally finite $\Gamma$ on
  the common compact support. This includes endpoints. Schwartz $E$ states and
  both true constant columns supply the identities, then boundedness extends
  them to $F$. Let $P_H$ be the orthogonal projection onto closed $H_I$.
  Transfer for the noise $f\mapsto W(P_HQ_JT_{\delta,m}f)$ leaves each observed
  row unchanged, since its residual slow test already lies in $H_I$. Countable
  states and rational times identify the same operator paths. This
  projected-transfer series has coefficient tests in $H_I$. Applying $\ell$
  gives an $L^2$ limit in the closed isonormal image $W(H_I)$, proving the
  assertion. Original global-basis tests were not assumed individually
  supported. No time-support independence or Hilbert-valued noise is used. This
  proves Lemma~\ref{L:cameron-actual-residual}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Cameron covariance and averaged bound]
  \label{P:cameron-actual-embedding}
  The covariance map $L_{\delta,J}\Phi = \E[XW(\Phi)]$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-actual-covariance}
    (L_{\delta,J}\Phi)_m(u)=\sqrt\delta
    \int_0^{\min(u,\ell_m)}
    S_F(-v)T(Q_J\Phi)(s_m+\delta v)S_F(v)\ud v ,
    \qquad \Phi\in H.
  \end{equation}
  This is a compact-operator Bochner integral. Define the exact norm and
  constants
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-averaged-norm}
    \|x\|_{\delta,p}
    =\left(\delta\sum_{m=0}^N\|x_m\|_{C^\theta}^p\right)^{1/p},
    \quad G_B=\sup_{|v|\le B}\|S_F(v)\|,
    \quad C_B=G_B^2[B^{1-1/r}+B^{1-1/r-\theta}].
  \end{equation}
  Then the Gaussian Cameron embedding satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-averaged-bound}
    \|L_{\delta,J}\Phi\|_{\delta,p}
    \le\sqrt\delta\,C_B(2/B)^{1/p-1/r}
    \epsilon_J\|\Phi\|_H .
  \end{equation}
  Convergence $\epsilon_J \to 0$ fixes $R$ and is independent of $\delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For smooth supported $\Phi$, $Q_J\Phi$ is another such test. The explicit
  pullback covariance~\eqref{E:raw-block-mean-jacobian} gives
  \[
    \E[Y_{m,J}(f)W(\Phi)]
    =\delta^{a/2}\int f(v,x)
    h_{Q_J\Phi}(s_m+\delta v,x)\ud x\ud v .
  \]
  Absolute Fubini also holds for signed compact tests. Multiplying by $g_\delta$
  gives $\sqrt\delta$. Explicit rows and~\eqref{E:cameron-spatial-row} give
  \eqref{E:cameron-actual-covariance}. The constant-velocity input acquires
  displacement $v$ under $S_F(v)$; the constant displacement remains one. Both
  columns are included.

  Both $S_F$ and its adjoint are strongly continuous on Hilbert $F$. In
  Bessel-unitary $E$ coordinates the Fourier matrix is
  \[
    \begin{pmatrix}
      \cos(v|\xi|)&\langle\xi\rangle\sin(v|\xi|)/|\xi|\\
      -|\xi|\sin(v|\xi|)/\langle\xi\rangle&\cos(v|\xi|)
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Its continuous zero quotient and common bound $C(1+B)$ apply also to its
  adjoint; dominated convergence proves the assertion. The constant block is
  finite dimensional. For compact $A$, conjugation $S_F(-v)AS_F(v)$ is norm
  continuous: strong convergence is uniform on compact images, and the right
  factor follows by adjoints. Joint norm continuity in $(v,A)$ and uniform
  bounds give Bochner measurability for the conjugated strongly measurable
  compact-operator field. No norm continuity of the free group itself is
  assumed.

  Write $M = TQ_J\Phi$. H\"older and the primitive increment estimate give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cameron-local-primitive-bound}
    \|(L_{\delta,J}\Phi)_m\|_{C^\theta}
    \le\sqrt\delta\,C_B\delta^{-1/r}\|M\|_{L^r(I_m)}.
  \end{equation}
  Its primitive exponent is $1-1/r > \theta$. Simple compact-operator integrand
  approximation in $L^r$ puts the primitive in $\mathscr{B}_0$; constant
  stopping does not increase the H\"older norm. Affine pullback preserves null
  sets. For fixed $\delta,J$ the covariance map is bounded by Bochner
  Cauchy--Schwarz and finite tuple $L^2$ moments. The integral is bounded by the
  displayed estimate, so equality on smooth controls extends by density to all
  $H$. Dense states and rational times make it a full operator-path identity.

  Let $H_{\mathrm{seen}}$ be the closed span of the $k_\ell$ from
  Lemma~\ref{L:cameron-actual-residual}. Then $\ell(L_{\delta,J}\Phi) = \langle
  k_\ell,\Phi\rangle_H$ and $\ker L_{\delta,J} = H_{\mathrm{seen}}^\perp$. The
  first Wiener chaos definition of the Cameron space is therefore
  $L_{\delta,J}(H_{\mathrm{seen}})$ with the minimum $H$ preimage norm. Extra
  directions are removed by orthogonal projection, without an inverse
  covariance. A bound in the original $H$ norm is thus the embedding bound.

  Put $t_m = \|M\|_{L^r(I_m)} \ge 0$ and $n = N+1 \ge 2$. Finite power mean at
  exponent $r/p > 1$, applied to $t_m^p$, gives
  \[
    \left(\sum_m t_m^p\right)^{1/p}
    \le n^{1/p-1/r}\left(\sum_m t_m^r\right)^{1/r}.
  \]
  The true partition gives $\sum_m t_m^r = \|M\|_{L^r(I)}^r$, including an empty
  last interval, and $n \le 2/(\delta B)$. Hence, the exact $\delta$-weighted
  norm gives
  \[
    \|L_{\delta,J}\Phi\|_{\delta,p}
    \le\sqrt\delta\,C_B(\delta n)^{1/p-1/r}\|M\|_{L^r}
    \le\sqrt\delta\,C_B(2/B)^{1/p-1/r}
    \epsilon_J\|\Phi\|_H.
  \]
  The $B$ factor remains explicit; covariance and mask occur once. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:cameron-actual-embedding}.
\end{proof}

These conclusions fix the radius and make no uniform-radius projection tail
assertion. No concentration estimate or original moment upper bound follows from
this section alone.

\section{The temporal equilibrium density}
\label{S:temporal-equilibrium}

The density in Lemma~\ref{L:temporal-equilibrium} is the classical interval
Riesz equilibrium density. Its probability-normalized form on $[-1,1]$ is
recalled by Calef and Hardin~\cite{calef.hardin:source2008:riesz}*{p.~1}.
We prove the potential identity with the normalization used here.

We use Euler's function $\Gamma_E(x) = \int_0^\infty u^{x-1}e^{-u}\ud u$, $x >
0$, and $\operatorname{Beta}(x,y) = \int_0^1t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}\ud t$, $x,y > 0$.
Both integrals are positive and finite.

\begin{lemma}[Gamma and Beta identities]\label{L:equilibrium-gamma-beta}
  For positive arguments,
  \begin{align*}
    \Gamma_E(x+1)&=x\Gamma_E(x),\\
    \operatorname{Beta}(x,y)
    &=\frac{\Gamma_E(x)\Gamma_E(y)}{\Gamma_E(x+y)},\\
    \Gamma_E(2x)
    &=\frac{2^{2x-1}\Gamma_E(x)\Gamma_E(x+1/2)}{\sqrt\pi}.
  \end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Integration by parts gives the recurrence, with vanishing boundary terms. In
  the positive product Gamma integral, use $(u,v) = (Rt,R(1-t))$, with Jacobian
  $R$; Tonelli gives the Beta--Gamma identity. For the last identity, symmetry,
  then $t = (1+v)/2$ and $v^2 = w$, give $\operatorname{Beta}(x,x) =
  2^{1-2x}\operatorname{Beta}(1/2,x)$. Also $\Gamma_E(1/2) = \int_\R e^{-z^2}\ud
  z = \sqrt\pi$: the substitution $u = z^2$ gives the first equality, and
  squaring the positive Gaussian integral and using polar coordinates gives
  $2\pi\int_0^\infty re^{-r^2}\ud r = \pi$. Substituting these into the
  Beta--Gamma identity proves duplication. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:equilibrium-gamma-beta}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact potential and mass]\label{L:temporal-equilibrium}
  For $0 < a < 1$ put $r = (1-a)/2$ and, for $0 < t < 1$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:temporal-equilibrium-density}
    f_a(t)=\frac{[t(1-t)]^{-r}}{\Gamma_E(r)\Gamma_E(1-r)}.
  \end{equation}
  This positive function belongs to $L^1(0,1)\cap L^2(0,1)$, and
  \begin{align}
    \int_0^1|s-t|^{-a}f_a(t)\ud t&=1
    &&\text{for every }s\in[0,1],\label{E:temporal-equilibrium-potential}\\
    M_a:=\int_0^1f_a(t)\ud t
    &=\frac{\Gamma_E((1+a)/2)}
    {\Gamma_E(1+a)\Gamma_E((1-a)/2)}>0.
    \label{E:temporal-equilibrium-mass}
  \end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The positive binomial series $(1-z)^{-p} = \sum_{n\ge0}(p)_nz^n/n!$, $p > 0$,
  $0 \le z < 1$, follows directly as follows. Its coefficient ratio gives
  uniform convergence on compact subintervals, including for its derivative.
  Termwise differentiation gives $(1-z)y' = py$, $y(0) = 1$; hence $(1-z)^py$ is
  constant one. Here $(p)_0 = 1$, $(p)_n = p(p+1)\cdots(p+n-1)$. All terms used
  below are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies.

  For $0 < s < 1$ split the integral of $|s-t|^{2r-1}[t(1-t)]^{-r}$ into left
  and right parts. In the left part, $t = su$ gives
  \begin{align*}
    L(s)
    &=s^r\int_0^1(1-u)^{2r-1}u^{-r}(1-su)^{-r}\ud u\\
    &=\operatorname{Beta}(1-r,2r)s^r
    \sum_{n\ge0}
    \frac{(r)_n(1-r)_ns^n}{(1+r)_n n!}.
  \end{align*}
  The Beta ratio follows from the Gamma recurrence. Since $(r)_n/(1+r)_n =
  r/(r+n)$, expanding the incomplete Beta integral gives
  \[
    L(s)=r\operatorname{Beta}(1-r,2r)B_s(r,r),\qquad
    B_s(r,r)=\int_0^s u^{r-1}(1-u)^{r-1}\ud u.
  \]
  Reflection makes the right part $L(1-s)$, while symmetry gives
  $B_s(r,r)+B_{1-s}(r,r) = \operatorname{Beta}(r,r)$. The full integral is
  therefore $r\operatorname{Beta}(1-r,2r)\operatorname{Beta}(r,r) =
  \Gamma_E(r)\Gamma_E(1-r)$. All integrals are finite because $-r > -1$ and
  $2r-1 > -1$. At $s = 0$ the integral is directly $\operatorname{Beta}(r,1-r) =
  \Gamma_E(r)\Gamma_E(1-r)$; $s = 1$ follows by reflection. This proves
  \eqref{E:temporal-equilibrium-potential}, including the endpoints without a
  limiting argument there.

  Integrability and square integrability follow from $-r > -1$ and $-2r = a-1 >
  -1$. Finally,
  \[
    M_a=
    \frac{\operatorname{Beta}(1-r,1-r)}
    {\Gamma_E(r)\Gamma_E(1-r)}
    =\frac{\Gamma_E(1-r)}{\Gamma_E(2-2r)\Gamma_E(r)},
  \]
  which is~\eqref{E:temporal-equilibrium-mass}. Its time mass has not been
  renormalized. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:temporal-equilibrium}.
\end{proof}

## 9. White controls obstruct uniform-R fixed smooth projections; scope

Here is a precise negative guard against an automatic growing-mask upgrade.
Take the admissible d1 white Gamma=delta0,alpha1, any0<a<1. From the scalar equilibrium identity/mass in damped-lower-endpoint-bridge.md Section2,
f_a in L2(I), K_a*f_a=1 and ||f_a||H_t²=M_a>0. Its H_t membership follows
by smooth L2 approximation and the Schur norm bound. Let q be a nonnegative
unit-L2 compact smooth bump and q_n(x)=q(x-x_n), centers escaping to infinity.
The raw H unit direction Phi_n=f_a tensor q_n/sqrt(M_a) has actual mean
q_n/sqrt(M_a), constant in time. This follows either directly from the
scalar identity or from the L2/mean completion in Sections5-6.

Fix J. The finitely many smooth compact basis tests spanning P_J have a
common compact spatial support. Choose x_n outside that support by more
than the radius of q. White spatial covariance gives P_J Phi_n=0 exactly,
regardless of temporal correlation. Choose R_n large enough that m_(R_n)=1
on supp q_n. Use F input e_disp=(0,0,1,0) in this section's four-entry
notation, norm1, and output dual w_n=(0,Lambda q_n/||q||H1/2,0,0), norm1.
The completed multiplier has the constant-in-time coordinate

 <T_(R_n)Phi_n(s)e_disp,w_n>F=1/[sqrt(M_a)||q||H1/2]>0.       (16)

Indeed the E velocity inner product has weight Lambda^-1, and q_n is unit
L2 with translation-invariant H1/2 norm. Thus for every fixed J,
 sup_R ||T_R(I-P_J)||H->Lr(K(F))>=1/[sqrt(M_a)||q||H1/2].
There is NO projection-tail convergence uniform in R for this fixed family.
This refutes ONLY that proposed extension, not fixed-R C1-C3, not all
projection/localization strategies and not the original sharp-upper goal.
For fixed R, sufficiently far white-noise q_n misses its mask. The negative
guard uses a bounded phase functional, no Sobolev point trace. It is the
exact control proposed separately in globalization-guard-v0.md.
