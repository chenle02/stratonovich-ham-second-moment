\section{Phase propagation and Gaussian controls}\label{S:nogo-phase}

A wave equation transports displacement and velocity together. Its scalar
solution value is not a state variable for composition. Likewise, a
Gaussian change of law transports an entire random solution, not merely
the solution evaluated at the mean of the new noise. These distinctions
explain the failures in this section. The scalar examples are diagnostics
for generic proof principles; their scope is stated separately from the
homogeneous stochastic wave model.

\subsection{NG-14: scalar moments do not compose across blocks}
\label{S:nogo-14}

Independent static time blocks look favorable for multiplying second
moments. Independence does remove correlations between their potentials,
but it does not reset the velocity to zero at a block boundary. The
original record computed formal coefficients only. We include the
integrable-series argument needed to turn that diagnostic into a
small-variance counterexample.

\setcounter{nogo}{13}
\begin{nogo}[Two independent wave blocks do not multiply scalar moments]
  \label{P:nogo-14}
  Let $Z_1,Z_2$ be independent centered Gaussians of variance $A$. Solve
  $y''=Z_1y$ on $[0,1]$ and $y''=Z_2y$ on $[1,2]$, with continuous phase
  at time one and initial phase $(y(0),y'(0))=(1,0)$. Then, as
  $A\downarrow0$,
  \[
    \E y(1)^2=1+\frac A3+O(A^2),\qquad
    \E y(2)^2=1+3A+O(A^2).
  \]
  In particular, $\E y(2)^2>(\E y(1)^2)^2$ for all sufficiently small
  positive $A$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Define the entire functions
  \[
    c(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{(2n)!},\qquad
    s(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{(2n+1)!}.
  \]
  The unit-time phase propagator and the terminal displacement are
  \[
    M(z)=\begin{pmatrix}c(z)&s(z)\\zs(z)&c(z)\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    y(2)=c(Z_2)c(Z_1)+s(Z_2)Z_1s(Z_1).
  \]
  Expanding through total degree two in $(z,w)$ gives
  \[
    c(w)c(z)+s(w)zs(z)
    =1+\frac32z+\frac12w
    +\frac5{24}z^2+\frac5{12}zw+\frac1{24}w^2
    +\text{terms of degree at least three}.
  \]
  After squaring, the coefficients of $z^2$ and $w^2$ are respectively
  $8/3$ and $1/3$. Independence and centering eliminate the $zw$ term.
  Similarly, the coefficient of $z^2$ in $c(z)^2$ is $1/3$.

  For completeness, these expansions admit the stated remainders after
  Gaussian expectation. The series of $c$ and $s$ have nonnegative
  coefficients, and
  \[
    |c(z)|+|s(z)|\le2e^{\sqrt{|z|}}.
  \]
  Consequently, the absolute series for the square of the displayed
  two-block displacement is bounded by
  $C(1+|z|)^2e^{2\sqrt{|z|}+2\sqrt{|w|}}$.
  This is integrable at any fixed Gaussian variance $A_1>0$. Absolute
  integration allows termwise expectation. Odd powers in either variable
  vanish; every remaining term has even total degree and a nonnegative
  coefficient. Thus, the expectation is a convergent series
  $\sum_{j\ge0}b_jA^j$ with $b_j\ge0$ at $A_1$. For $0\le A\le A_1$,
  \[
    \sum_{j\ge2}b_jA^j
    \le (A/A_1)^2\sum_{j\ge2}b_jA_1^j=O(A^2).
  \]
  The same argument applies to the one-block moment. Its square equals
  $1+2A/3+O(A^2)$, whereas the two-block moment equals
  $1+3A+O(A^2)$. Their difference is $7A/3+O(A^2)$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-14}.
\end{proof}

This scalar model has spatially constant noise and independent static
blocks, outside Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}. It excludes the unrestricted
scalar multiplication rule, not a phase-space operator estimate. Corollary~\ref{C:static-interaction} gives a valid replacement for independent
successive blocks: it controls both phase coordinates and includes explicit

\subsection{NG-15: the missing second-order mean term}\label{S:nogo-15}

Keeping the first random Duhamel term gives a nonnegative squared norm.
It is not the complete coefficient of the variance in the expected phase
energy. The mean of the second Duhamel term contributes at the same order.

\setcounter{nogo}{14}
\begin{nogo}[The first variance coefficient need not be nonnegative]
  \label{P:nogo-15}
  For the scalar phase propagator $M$ in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-14} and a
  standard Gaussian $Z$, write
  \[
    \E\|M(\sigma Z)x\|^2=\|M(0)x\|^2+\sigma^2x^{\mathsf T}Kx
    +O(\sigma^4).
  \]
  Then
  \[
    K=\begin{pmatrix}4/3&4/5\\4/5&17/45\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    (1,-2)K(1,-2)^{\mathsf T}=-\frac{16}{45}.
  \]
  The matrix $K$ is the full first coefficient in the variance parameter,
  not a covariance matrix. Omitting the second-order mean term also
  underestimates this coefficient for $x=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  The coefficients of $M(z)=M_0+zM_1+z^2M_2+\cdots$ are
  \[
    M_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
    M_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/6\\1&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad
    M_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/24&1/120\\1/6&1/24\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Gaussian expectation of the squared phase norm gives
  \[
    K=M_1^{\mathsf T}M_1+M_0^{\mathsf T}M_2+M_2^{\mathsf T}M_0.
  \]
  Matrix multiplication gives the stated entries and negative value.
  For $e_1=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$, the first random contribution is
  $\|M_1e_1\|^2=5/4$, whereas $e_1^{\mathsf T}Ke_1=4/3$.
  Thus, it is not valid even as an upper bound for the full coefficient.

  The entire series are absolutely Gaussian integrable, including their
  squares, by the bound in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-14} with the additional
  polynomial factor from $zs(z)$. Absolute summation of the even-degree
  terms at a fixed nonzero $\sigma_1$ bounds the tail of degree at least
  four by $C\sigma^4$ for $|\sigma|\le|\sigma_1|$. Therefore, for
  $x=(1,-2)^{\mathsf T}$ the actual expected energy satisfies
  \[
    \E\|M(\sigma Z)x\|^2=5-\frac{16}{45}\sigma^2+O(\sigma^4).
  \]
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-15}.
\end{proof}

The example refutes arbitrary-phase covariance monotonicity for this
scalar flow. It says nothing against monotonicity of the original
solution-value moment with initial data $(1,0)$ in the homogeneous model.
Section~\ref{S:static-phase} retains both contributions to the coefficient.
The all-order estimate of Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-all-orders} and
the interaction bound of Corollary~\ref{C:static-interaction} retain these
terms. Positivity of the first random variance does not control the cross
term with the mean.

\subsection{NG-16: replacing a tilted mean by a deterministic value}
\label{S:nogo-16}

The Gaussian density cost is exact. A loss may instead enter when the
random solution under the tilted law is replaced by a deterministic
surrogate. The following two calculations identify the loss without
asserting an obstruction to Gaussian shifts themselves.

\setcounter{nogo}{15}
\begin{nogo}[A deterministic surrogate can lose the annealed factor]
  \label{P:nogo-16}
  Let $A>0$, $c>0$ and $0<r<2$, and define
  \[
    J_c=\sup_{H\ge0}\{cAH^r-H^2/2\}.
  \]
  Then $J_c=c^{2/(2-r)}J_1$, whereas
  \[
    \sup_{H\ge0}\{2AH^r-H^2\}=2J_1<J_2.
  \]
  Moreover, for $Y=e^{\sigma Z}$, $Z$ standard Gaussian and $\sigma>0$,
  the full tilted-mean Cauchy--Schwarz bound recovers $\E Y^2$ exactly,
  while substituting $Y(h)=e^{\sigma h}$ loses a factor in its logarithm.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Substitute $H=c^{1/(2-r)}K$ in the definition of $J_c$. Both terms
  acquire the factor $c^{2/(2-r)}$. The supremum $J_1$ is finite, since
  $r<2$, and positive, since for small positive $H$ the positive term
  dominates. Factoring two from the surrogate score proves the strict
  comparison because $2/(2-r)>1$.

  Let $Q_h$ be the Gaussian shift with density
  $L_h=e^{hZ-h^2/2}$ relative to the standard Gaussian law. Then
  $\E L_h^2=e^{h^2}$, so Cauchy--Schwarz gives
  \[
    \E Y^2\ge(\E_{Q_h}Y)^2e^{-h^2}.
  \]
  The exact tilted mean is $\E_{Q_h}Y=e^{\sigma^2/2+\sigma h}$.
  Maximizing its resulting exponent
  $\sigma^2+2\sigma h-h^2$ at $h=\sigma$ gives
  $e^{2\sigma^2}=\E Y^2$. Substitution of the deterministic value
  $Y(h)$ instead gives the maximum $e^{\sigma^2}$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-16}.
\end{proof}

These are generic scalar score calculations, not an SPDE no-go. They
identify which term is lost and show that retaining the full tilted mean
can avoid the loss. A deterministic surrogate remains useful if one
proves that its error is negligible on the intended exponential scale;
without such an estimate it cannot identify the annealed constant.

\subsection{NG-20: covariance profiles need not separate}
\label{S:nogo-20}

A fixed spatial profile times a scalar temporal factor is easier to
propagate than a general spacetime control. Such a product family is
legitimate as a restriction, but it does not represent every positive
control even in the white spatial model.

\setcounter{nogo}{19}
\begin{nogo}[A positive control need not have proportional covariance profiles]
  \label{P:nogo-20}
  Fix $0<a<1$ and one-dimensional spatial covariance
  $\Gamma=c_\Gamma\delta_0$, $c_\Gamma>0$. There is a nonnegative smooth
  compactly supported spacetime test $\psi$ for which
  \[
    h_\sigma(x)=c_\Gamma\int_0^1|\sigma-u|^{-a}\psi(u,x)\ud u,
    \qquad 0\le\sigma\le1,
  \]
  cannot be represented as $b(\sigma)U(x)$ with one fixed spatial $U$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Choose a nonzero nonnegative smooth $f_1$ supported in $(1/8,1/4)$,
  let $f_2(u)=f_1(1-u)$, and choose nonzero nonnegative smooth bumps
  $q_1,q_2$ supported respectively in $(-2,-1)$ and $(1,2)$. Set
  $\psi(u,x)=f_1(u)q_1(x)+f_2(u)q_2(x)$. Put
  \[
    A=\int_0^1u^{-a}f_1(u)\ud u,\qquad
    B=\int_0^1(1-u)^{-a}f_1(u)\ud u.
  \]
  Since $u<1-u$ on the support of $f_1$, we have $A>B>0$.
  Reflection in time gives
  \[
    h_0=c_\Gamma(Aq_1+Bq_2),\qquad
    h_1=c_\Gamma(Bq_1+Aq_2).
  \]
  Choose $x_i$ with $q_i(x_i)>0$. Disjoint supports imply
  \[
    h_0(x_1)h_1(x_2)-h_1(x_1)h_0(x_2)
    =c_\Gamma^2(A^2-B^2)q_1(x_1)q_2(x_2)>0.
  \]
  A representation by one $U$ would make this determinant zero.
  The white covariance has $\alpha=1$, so $1+a<2$ verifies the model
  parameter condition. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-20}.
\end{proof}

This is an exact representation obstruction. It does not prove that
separable controls fail to attain a variational supremum; a theorem
reducing optimizers to a smaller family would be a different statement.
A useful weaker fact survives: because $|\sigma-u|^{-a}\ge1$ on the
unit time square, every nonnegative test has
$h_\sigma\ge\Gamma*\int_0^1\psi(u,\cdot)\ud u$.
This common lower profile does not by itself prove a phase-propagation
comparison. Reopening an exact product reduction requires extra structure
on the control, while an optimizer reduction requires a variational proof.

\subsection{NG-31: initial phase and the backward cone}\label{S:nogo-31}

Two seemingly small generalizations remove essential hypotheses: replacing
the specified initial phase by an arbitrary one, and checking agreement
of spatial cutoffs only on the terminal observation set. Simple
deterministic waves already distinguish these statements.

\setcounter{nogo}{30}
\begin{nogo}[Growth does not give every-phase lower bounds or local cutoff consistency]
  \label{P:nogo-31}
  The growth rate of a wave phase propagator need not be a lower growth
  rate for every nonzero initial phase. Also, two smooth initial-data
  masks that agree on an observation interval can give different free-wave
  displacements throughout that interval at a later time, even when their
  initial displacements agree everywhere.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  For the first assertion, let
  $B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
  Its eigenvectors $(1,1)^{\mathsf T}$ and $(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}$ have
  eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$. Hence, $\|e^{tB}\|=e^t$ for $t\ge0$,
  but $e^{tB}(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}=e^{-t}(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}$.

  For the second assertion take the one-dimensional free wave with zero
  initial displacement and nonnegative smooth velocity $v$ supported in
  $(5,6)$, normalized by $\int v=1$. Choose smooth masks $\chi_s,\chi_l$
  between zero and one, with $\chi_s=1$ on $[-2,2]$ and support in
  $[-4,4]$, and $\chi_l=1$ on $[-14,14]$. Both masks agree on the
  observation interval $[-2,2]$. The masked velocities are nevertheless
  $\chi_sv=0$ and $\chi_lv=v$. D'Alembert's formula at time nine gives
  \[
    u_s(9,x)=0,\qquad
    u_l(9,x)=\frac12\int_{x-9}^{x+9}v(y)\ud y=\frac12,
    \qquad -2\le x\le2.
  \]
  Indeed, every interval $[x-9,x+9]$ in question contains $(5,6)$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-31}.
\end{proof}

The first example does not refute a terminal lower bound for the original
phase $(1,0)$. The second requires agreement of both displacement and
velocity on the full backward cone, not only on the observer. Finite
propagation with that guard survives. Thus, a successor must retain the
specified phase, or prove a suitable nonvanishing unstable component, and
must impose cutoff agreement on the relevant cone for both coordinates.

  \[
    \sum_{j\ge2}b_jA^j
    \le (A/A_1)^2\sum_{j\ge2}b_jA_1^j=O(A^2).
  \]
  The same argument applies to the one-block moment. Its square equals
  $1+2A/3+O(A^2)$, whereas the two-block moment equals
  $1+3A+O(A^2)$. Their difference is $7A/3+O(A^2)$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-14}.
\end{proof}

This scalar model has spatially constant noise and independent static
blocks, outside Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}. It excludes the unrestricted
scalar multiplication rule, not a phase-space operator estimate. Corollary~\ref{C:static-interaction} gives a valid replacement for independent
successive blocks: it controls both phase coordinates and includes explicit
block losses whose dyadic sum is finite. Its stated scope does not identify
products across overlapping levels with the original evolution.

\subsection{NG-15: the missing second-order mean term}\label{S:nogo-15}

Keeping the first random Duhamel term gives a nonnegative squared norm.
It is not the complete coefficient of the variance in the expected phase
energy. The mean of the second Duhamel term contributes at the same order.

\setcounter{nogo}{14}
\begin{nogo}[The first variance coefficient need not be nonnegative]
  \label{P:nogo-15}
  For the scalar phase propagator $M$ in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-14} and a
  standard Gaussian $Z$, write
  \[
    \E\|M(\sigma Z)x\|^2=\|M(0)x\|^2+\sigma^2x^{\mathsf T}Kx
    +O(\sigma^4).
  \]
  Then
  \[
    K=\begin{pmatrix}4/3&4/5\\4/5&17/45\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    (1,-2)K(1,-2)^{\mathsf T}=-\frac{16}{45}.
  \]
  The matrix $K$ is the full first coefficient in the variance parameter,
  not a covariance matrix. Omitting the second-order mean term also
  underestimates this coefficient for $x=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  We compute the complete first variance matrix, including the second-order
  mean terms. We then use the integrable series remainder to obtain an actual
  phase whose expected energy decreases at small coupling.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We retain all terms of the first variance matrix.
  The coefficients of $M(z)=M_0+zM_1+z^2M_2+\cdots$ are
  \[
    M_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
    M_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/6\\1&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad
    M_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/24&1/120\\1/6&1/24\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Gaussian expectation of the squared phase norm gives
  \[
    K=M_1^{\mathsf T}M_1+M_0^{\mathsf T}M_2+M_2^{\mathsf T}M_0.
  \]
  Matrix multiplication gives the stated entries and negative value.
  For $e_1=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$, the first random contribution is
  $\|M_1e_1\|^2=5/4$, whereas $e_1^{\mathsf T}Ke_1=4/3$.
  Thus, it is not valid even as an upper bound for the full coefficient.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We justify the negative small-coupling energy
  correction.
  The entire series are absolutely Gaussian integrable, including their
  squares, by the bound in No-go~\ref{P:nogo-14} with the additional
  polynomial factor from $zs(z)$. Absolute summation of the even-degree
  terms at a fixed nonzero $\sigma_1$ bounds the tail of degree at least
  four by $C\sigma^4$ for $|\sigma|\le|\sigma_1|$. Therefore, for
  $x=(1,-2)^{\mathsf T}$ the actual expected energy satisfies
  \[
    \E\|M(\sigma Z)x\|^2=5-\frac{16}{45}\sigma^2+O(\sigma^4).
  \]
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-15}.
\end{proof}

The example refutes arbitrary-phase covariance monotonicity for this
scalar flow. It says nothing against monotonicity of the original
solution-value moment with initial data $(1,0)$ in the homogeneous model.
Section~\ref{S:static-phase} retains both contributions to the coefficient.
The all-order estimate of Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-all-orders} and
the interaction bound of Corollary~\ref{C:static-interaction} retain these
terms. Positivity of the first random variance does not control the cross
term with the mean.

\subsection{NG-16: replacing a tilted mean by a deterministic value}
\label{S:nogo-16}

The Gaussian density cost is exact. A loss may instead enter when the
random solution under the tilted law is replaced by a deterministic
surrogate. The following two calculations identify the loss without
asserting an obstruction to Gaussian shifts themselves.

\setcounter{nogo}{15}
\begin{nogo}[A deterministic surrogate can lose the annealed factor]
  \label{P:nogo-16}
  Let $A>0$, $c>0$ and $0<r<2$, and define
  \[
    J_c=\sup_{H\ge0}\{cAH^r-H^2/2\}.
  \]
  Then $J_c=c^{2/(2-r)}J_1$, whereas
  \[
    \sup_{H\ge0}\{2AH^r-H^2\}=2J_1<J_2.
  \]
  Moreover, for $Y=e^{\sigma Z}$, $Z$ standard Gaussian and $\sigma>0$,
  the full tilted-mean Cauchy--Schwarz bound recovers $\E Y^2$ exactly,
  while substituting $Y(h)=e^{\sigma h}$ loses a factor in its logarithm.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Substitute $H=c^{1/(2-r)}K$ in the definition of $J_c$. Both terms
  acquire the factor $c^{2/(2-r)}$. The supremum $J_1$ is finite, since
  $r<2$, and positive, since for small positive $H$ the positive term
  dominates. Factoring two from the surrogate score proves the strict
  comparison because $2/(2-r)>1$.

  Let $Q_h$ be the Gaussian shift with density
  $L_h=e^{hZ-h^2/2}$ relative to the standard Gaussian law. Then
  $\E L_h^2=e^{h^2}$, so Cauchy--Schwarz gives
  \[
    \E Y^2\ge(\E_{Q_h}Y)^2e^{-h^2}.
  \]
  The exact tilted mean is $\E_{Q_h}Y=e^{\sigma^2/2+\sigma h}$.
  Maximizing its resulting exponent
  $\sigma^2+2\sigma h-h^2$ at $h=\sigma$ gives
  $e^{2\sigma^2}=\E Y^2$. Substitution of the deterministic value
  $Y(h)$ instead gives the maximum $e^{\sigma^2}$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-16}.
\end{proof}

These are generic scalar score calculations, not an SPDE no-go. They
identify which term is lost and show that retaining the full tilted mean
can avoid the loss. A deterministic surrogate remains useful if one
proves that its error is negligible on the intended exponential scale;
without such an estimate it cannot identify the annealed constant.

\subsection{NG-20: covariance profiles need not separate}
\label{S:nogo-20}

A fixed spatial profile times a scalar temporal factor is easier to
propagate than a general spacetime control. Such a product family is
legitimate as a restriction, but it does not represent every positive
control even in the white spatial model.

\setcounter{nogo}{19}
\begin{nogo}[A positive control need not have proportional covariance profiles]
  \label{P:nogo-20}
  Fix $0<a<1$ and one-dimensional spatial covariance
  $\Gamma=c_\Gamma\delta_0$, $c_\Gamma>0$. There is a nonnegative smooth
  compactly supported spacetime test $\psi$ for which
  \[
    h_\sigma(x)=c_\Gamma\int_0^1|\sigma-u|^{-a}\psi(u,x)\ud u,
    \qquad 0\le\sigma\le1,
  \]
  cannot be represented as $b(\sigma)U(x)$ with one fixed spatial $U$.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  Choose a nonzero nonnegative smooth $f_1$ supported in $(1/8,1/4)$,
  let $f_2(u)=f_1(1-u)$, and choose nonzero nonnegative smooth bumps
  $q_1,q_2$ supported respectively in $(-2,-1)$ and $(1,2)$. Set
  $\psi(u,x)=f_1(u)q_1(x)+f_2(u)q_2(x)$. Put
  \[
    A=\int_0^1u^{-a}f_1(u)\ud u,\qquad
    B=\int_0^1(1-u)^{-a}f_1(u)\ud u.
  \]
  Since $u<1-u$ on the support of $f_1$, we have $A>B>0$.
  Reflection in time gives
  \[
    h_0=c_\Gamma(Aq_1+Bq_2),\qquad
    h_1=c_\Gamma(Bq_1+Aq_2).
  \]
  Choose $x_i$ with $q_i(x_i)>0$. Disjoint supports imply
  \[
    h_0(x_1)h_1(x_2)-h_1(x_1)h_0(x_2)
    =c_\Gamma^2(A^2-B^2)q_1(x_1)q_2(x_2)>0.
  \]
  A representation by one $U$ would make this determinant zero.
  The white covariance has $\alpha=1$, so $1+a<2$ verifies the model
  parameter condition. This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-20}.
\end{proof}

This is an exact representation obstruction. It does not prove that
separable controls fail to attain a variational supremum; a theorem
reducing optimizers to a smaller family would be a different statement.
A useful weaker fact survives: because $|\sigma-u|^{-a}\ge1$ on the
unit time square, every nonnegative test has
$h_\sigma\ge\Gamma*\int_0^1\psi(u,\cdot)\ud u$.
This common lower profile does not by itself prove a phase-propagation
comparison. Reopening an exact product reduction requires extra structure
on the control, while an optimizer reduction requires a variational proof.

\subsection{NG-31: initial phase and the backward cone}\label{S:nogo-31}

Two seemingly small generalizations remove essential hypotheses: replacing
the specified initial phase by an arbitrary one, and checking agreement
of spatial cutoffs only on the terminal observation set. Simple
deterministic waves already distinguish these statements.

\setcounter{nogo}{30}
\begin{nogo}[Growth does not give every-phase lower bounds or local cutoff consistency]
  \label{P:nogo-31}
  The growth rate of a wave phase propagator need not be a lower growth
  rate for every nonzero initial phase. Also, two smooth initial-data
  masks that agree on an observation interval can give different free-wave
  displacements throughout that interval at a later time, even when their
  initial displacements agree everywhere.
\end{nogo}
\begin{proof}
  For the first assertion, let
  $B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
  Its eigenvectors $(1,1)^{\mathsf T}$ and $(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}$ have
  eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$. Hence, $\|e^{tB}\|=e^t$ for $t\ge0$,
  but $e^{tB}(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}=e^{-t}(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}$.

  For the second assertion take the one-dimensional free wave with zero
  initial displacement and nonnegative smooth velocity $v$ supported in
  $(5,6)$, normalized by $\int v=1$. Choose smooth masks $\chi_s,\chi_l$
  between zero and one, with $\chi_s=1$ on $[-2,2]$ and support in
  $[-4,4]$, and $\chi_l=1$ on $[-14,14]$. Both masks agree on the
  observation interval $[-2,2]$. The masked velocities are nevertheless
  $\chi_sv=0$ and $\chi_lv=v$. D'Alembert's formula at time nine gives
  \[
    u_s(9,x)=0,\qquad
    u_l(9,x)=\frac12\int_{x-9}^{x+9}v(y)\ud y=\frac12,
    \qquad -2\le x\le2.
  \]
  Indeed, every interval $[x-9,x+9]$ in question contains $(5,6)$.
  This proves No-go~\ref{P:nogo-31}.
\end{proof}

The first example does not refute a terminal lower bound for the original
phase $(1,0)$. The second requires agreement of both displacement and
velocity on the full backward cone, not only on the observer. Finite
propagation with that guard survives. Thus, a successor must retain the
specified phase, or prove a suitable nonvanishing unstable component, and
must impose cutoff agreement on the relevant cone for both coordinates.

\section{Model and standing assumptions}\label{S:model}

We study the hyperbolic Anderson model
\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(1.1)}
\begin{equation}\label{E:HAM}
  \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\W(t,x)u(t,x),
  \qquad u(0,x)=1,\quad \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=0,
\end{equation}
for $(t,x) \in \R_+\times\R^d$, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, interpreted in the
Stratonovich sense (limit of classical wave equations driven by smoothed noises
$\W_{\epsilon,\delta}$). The noise is centered Gaussian with
\begin{equation}\label{E:cov}
  \operatorname{Cov}\big(\W(s,x),\W(t,y)\big)=|s-t|^{-\alpha_0}\gamma(x-y),
  \qquad 0<\alpha_0<1,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma \ge 0$ is nonnegative definite with spectral measure $\mu$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Homogeneous covariance]\label{H:homog}
  Let $d \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$. The spatial covariance is a
  nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon measure $\Gamma$ on
  $\R^d$, with positive even tempered Radon spectral measure $\mu$ in the
  convention
  \[
    \Gamma=\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu(\ud\xi)
    \quad\text{in the sense of tempered distributions}.
  \]
  The symbol $\gamma$ in~\eqref{E:cov} denotes this covariance as a function
  when one exists, and as a distribution otherwise. For every Borel set $A$ and
  $c > 0$, $\Gamma(cA) = c^{d-\alpha}\Gamma(A)$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:threshold}
    \alpha_0+\alpha<2,
  \end{equation}
  with $0 < \alpha < d$, or $\alpha = d = 1$. In particular, the last case
  includes spatial white noise $\Gamma = \delta_0$.
\end{hypothesis}

\paragraph{Zero covariance.}
Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} allows $\Gamma = 0$ by design. In this case the
centered Gaussian noise is $\W = 0$, and the solution with initial data
$(1,0)$ is $u(t,x) = 1$. Thus $\E|u(t,x)|^p = 1$ for every $p > 0$, and
all normalized logarithmic moment rates are zero. Results asserting strict
positivity of covariance-dependent quantities impose $\Gamma \ne 0$ locally.

Throughout we write
\begin{equation}\label{E:kappa}
  \LeanEqMarker{E:kappa}
  \kappa:=\frac{4-\alpha-\alpha_0}{3-\alpha}\in(1,2).
\end{equation}

\section{All static orders with arbitrary initial phase}
\label{S:static-all-orders}

We now seek all static Duhamel orders, retaining the full spatial covariance
class of Section~\ref{S:static-phase}. The temporal covariance is not included
in this section. Static estimates alone do not identify the evolution for
overlapping intervals; that step is treated in the next section.

We use one external operator theorem. From Crouzeix--Palencia
\cite{crouzeix.palencia:source2017:numerical}*{author preprint v1, (3), p.~1,
and Theorem~6, pp.~5--6}, if $T$ is a bounded operator on a complex Hilbert
space and $F$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline{W(T)}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{E:cp-input}
  \|F(T)\|\le(1+\sqrt2)\sup_{w\in W(T)}|F(w)|,\qquad
  W(T)=\{\langle Tu,u\rangle:\|u\|=1\}.
\end{equation}
The theorem is imported, not proved here. All operators to which we apply it
below are bounded; no unbounded-operator extension is assumed.

\LeanMarker{L:complex-phase}
\begin{lemma}[A complex phase bound]\label{L:complex-phase}
  Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space, $A_0$ a bounded nonnegative selfadjoint
  operator, and $B$ a bounded selfadjoint operator. Write $A_\rho = \rho^2+A_0$.
  Suppose that, for $0 < a < 1$ and $d > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:abstract-sandwich}
    \|A_\rho^{-1/2}BA_\rho^{-1/2}\|\le d\rho^{a-2},
    \qquad \rho>0.
  \end{equation}
  Put
  \[
    I_a=\int_0^\infty s^{-1/2}(1+s)^{(a-3)/2}\ud s,\qquad
    \delta_a=\min\{1/4,\pi/(8I_a)\}.
  \]
  For $\lambda > 0$ and $|z| \le \delta_a\lambda^{2-a}/d$, let $L = A_0-zB$. The
  phase propagator of $u''+Lu = 0$ satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:complex-phase-bound}
    \|\mathcal{M}_z(h)\|_{X_\lambda\to X_\lambda}
    \le100e^{2\lambda h},\qquad
    \|(f,g)\|_{X_\lambda}^2
    =\|f\|^2+\|A_\lambda^{-1/2}g\|^2,\quad h\ge0.
  \end{equation}
  The constant is independent of $H$, $\|A_0\|$ and $\|B\|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Set $\delta = \delta_a$ and $L_\lambda = L+\lambda^2$. For $\kappa =
  (\lambda^2+t)^{1/2}$,
  \[
    L_\lambda+t
    =A_\kappa^{1/2}(1-zK_\kappa)A_\kappa^{1/2},\qquad
    K_\kappa=A_\kappa^{-1/2}BA_\kappa^{-1/2}.
  \]
  Since $a-2 < 0$, $\|zK_\kappa\| \le \delta$. Thus, these operators are
  invertible and
  \[
    \|A_\kappa^{1/2}(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}\|
    \le\frac1{(1-\delta)\kappa}.
  \]
  Also $\operatorname{Re}\langle L_\lambda u,u\rangle \ge (1-\delta)\langle
  A_\lambda u,u\rangle$.

  Principal inverse square roots are defined on the right half-plane. The scalar
  identity $w^{-1/2} = \pi^{-1}\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}(w+t)^{-1}\ud t$, and
  bounded holomorphic calculus, give
  \[
    Q_z-1
    =\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2}
    \big[(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}-(A_\lambda+t)^{-1}\big]\ud t,
    \qquad Q_z=A_\lambda^{1/2}L_\lambda^{-1/2}.
  \]
  The integrals converge in operator norm. Indeed, the resolvent identity,
  $\|A_\lambda^{1/2}A_\kappa^{-1/2}\| \le 1$, and the following ordered
  factorization give the required integrand bound:
  \[
    \begin{split}
      &A_\lambda^{1/2}
      \big[(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}-A_\kappa^{-1}\big]\\
      &\qquad=A_\lambda^{1/2}A_\kappa^{-1/2}
      (1-zK_\kappa)^{-1}zK_\kappa A_\kappa^{-1/2}.
    \end{split}
  \]
  The four factors have norms at most $1$, $(1-\delta)^{-1}$, $|z|d\kappa^{a-2}$
  and $\kappa^{-1}$, respectively. Thus, the integrand without $t^{-1/2}/\pi$ is
  bounded by
  \[
    \frac{|z|d}{1-\delta}\kappa^{a-3}.
  \]
  Consequently,
  \[
    \|Q_z-1\|
    \le\frac{|z|d\lambda^{a-2}I_a}{\pi(1-\delta)}
    \le\frac16.
  \]
  In particular, $Q_z$ and $Q_z^{-1}$ have norm at most two. Apply the same
  argument to $L^* = A_0-\overline zB$ and take adjoints. It follows that all
  four operators
  \begin{equation}\label{E:phase-root-comparisons}
    A_\lambda^{1/2}L_\lambda^{-1/2},\quad
    L_\lambda^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2},\quad
    L_\lambda^{1/2}A_\lambda^{-1/2},\quad
    A_\lambda^{-1/2}L_\lambda^{1/2}
  \end{equation}
  have norm at most two. This is a quantitative comparison at the bounded
  cutoffs, not an assumed square-root domain identity.

  We next control the numerical range. For $\|u\| = 1$ put $s = \|A_0^{1/2}u\|$.
  Optimizing~\eqref{E:abstract-sandwich} at $\rho = s$ gives $|\langle
  Bu,u\rangle| \le 2ds^a$. If $s = 0$, the same conclusion follows by letting
  $\rho\downarrow0$. Separately, evaluating~\eqref{E:abstract-sandwich} at $\rho
  = \lambda$ gives
  \[
    |\langle Bu,u\rangle|
    \le d\lambda^{a-2}(s^2+\lambda^2),\qquad
    |z\langle Bu,u\rangle|\le\delta(s^2+\lambda^2).
  \]
  This is the estimate for the real part; the optimized estimate controls the
  imaginary part. Thus, for $w = x+iy = \langle Lu,u\rangle$,
  \[
    x+\lambda^2\ge(1-\delta)(s^2+\lambda^2),\qquad
    |y|\le2\delta\lambda^{2-a}s^a.
  \]
  Since $s^{2a}\lambda^{2-2a} \le s^2+\lambda^2$,
  \[
    y^2\le\frac{4\delta^2}{1-\delta}\lambda^2(x+\lambda^2)
    \le\frac13\lambda^2(x+\lambda^2),\qquad
    x\ge-\lambda^2/4.
  \]
  Hence, $|w| \le x+2\lambda^2$, and either square root of $w$ has
  $|\operatorname{Im}\sqrt w| \le \lambda$.

  The scalar functions
  \[
    C_h(w)=\cos(h\sqrt w),\qquad
    F_h(w)=\sqrt{w+\lambda^2}\frac{\sin(h\sqrt w)}{\sqrt w},\qquad
    H_h(w)=\frac{w}{\sqrt{w+\lambda^2}}
    \frac{\sin(h\sqrt w)}{\sqrt w}
  \]
  are holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline{W(L)}$. The values at $w = 0$
  are removable; the only square-root branch required is that of $w+\lambda^2$,
  whose real part is positive. On the numerical range,
  \[
    |C_h(w)|\le e^{\lambda h},\qquad
    |F_h(w)|\le2(1+\lambda h)e^{\lambda h},\qquad
    |H_h(w)|\le2e^{\lambda h}.
  \]
  Indeed, $|\operatorname{Im}\sqrt w| \le \lambda$ gives $|\cos(h\sqrt
  w)|,|\sin(h\sqrt w)| \le e^{\lambda h}$. The entire identity $\sin(h\sqrt
  w)/\sqrt w = \int_0^h\cos(r\sqrt w)\ud r$ also gives the bound $he^{\lambda
  h}$, including $w = 0$. If $|w| \le \lambda^2/2$, then
  \[
    |F_h(w)|\le\sqrt{3/2}\,\lambda h e^{\lambda h}.
  \]
  If $|w| > \lambda^2/2$, then $|w+\lambda^2|/|w| \le 3$, so $|F_h(w)| \le
  \sqrt3 e^{\lambda h}$. These bounds imply the displayed estimate for $F_h$.
  Finally,
  \[
    \frac{|w|}{|w+\lambda^2|}
    \le1+\frac{\lambda^2}{|w+\lambda^2|}
    \le1+(1-\delta)^{-1}\le7/3,
  \]
  and $H_h(w) = \sqrt w\sin(h\sqrt w)/\sqrt{w+\lambda^2}$ gives its stated
  bound. All identities have their removable values at zero. Applying the
  imported~\eqref{E:cp-input} gives bounds $3e^{\lambda h}$, $6(1+\lambda
  h)e^{\lambda h}$ and $6e^{\lambda h}$ for these three operator functions.

  Under the isometry $(u,v)\mapsto(u,A_\lambda^{-1/2}v)$, the four phase blocks
  are
  \[
    C_h(L),\quad
    F_h(L)L_\lambda^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2},\quad
    -A_\lambda^{-1/2}L_\lambda^{1/2}H_h(L),\quad
    A_\lambda^{-1/2}L_\lambda^{1/2}C_h(L)
    L_\lambda^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2}.
  \]
  This is the usual matrix
  $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}C_h(L)&G_h(L)\\-LG_h(L)&C_h(L)\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
  with $G_h(w) = \sin(h\sqrt w)/\sqrt w$, after weighting. In the order
  displayed, the four block norms are at most
  \[
    3e^{\lambda h},\qquad
    12(1+\lambda h)e^{\lambda h},\qquad
    12e^{\lambda h},\qquad12e^{\lambda h}.
  \]
  For example, the last block has two root-comparison factors of norm at most
  two, with $C_h(L)$ between them. Only functions of $L$ commute; no commutation
  with $A_\lambda$ is used in these ordered products. A block operator on
  $H\oplus H$ has norm at most the sum of its four block norms, by the triangle
  inequality and the norm-one coordinate inclusions and projections. Therefore,
  its norm is at most
  \[
    (39+12\lambda h)e^{\lambda h}
    \le39(1+\lambda h)e^{\lambda h}\le100e^{2\lambda h}.
  \]
  The phase operator itself is the exponential of the bounded block generator
  with rows $(0,1)$ and $(-L,0)$. It is entire in $z$; the intermediate square
  roots are used to estimate it, not to impose a branch-dependent definition of
  that entire operator. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:complex-phase}.
\end{proof}

We use the standard Gaussian creation and annihilation algebra; see
Janson~\cite{janson:97:gaussian}*{Section~13.2, Theorem~13.7, pp.~200--201}.
The following calculation records the normalization used for the finite
occupation spaces in this argument.

\LeanMarker{L:fock-form}
\begin{lemma}[Gaussian cutoff form bound]\label{L:fock-form}
  Retain the covariance and $R_\alpha$ of Section~\ref{S:static-phase}. Fix
  $\epsilon > 0$. Represent the smoothed real field on independent standard
  Gaussian coordinates as $W_\epsilon(x) = \sum_{i\ge1}g_i e_i(x)$ in
  $L^2(\Omega)$. Let $P_N$ be projection onto Gaussian Hermite degrees at most
  $N$, and $S_b$ spatial Fourier projection onto $|\eta| \le b$. For finite $m$,
  on their joint range set
  \[
    B_{N,m,b}=P_NS_b\Big(\sum_{i=1}^m g_i M_{e_i}\Big)S_bP_N,\qquad
    A_\rho=\rho^2-\Delta .
  \]
  These are bounded selfadjoint operators and, uniformly in $\epsilon,m,b$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:fock-sandwich}
    \|A_\rho^{-1/2}B_{N,m,b}A_\rho^{-1/2}\|
    \le d_N\rho^{\alpha/2-2},\qquad
    d_N=2\sqrt{N+1}\sqrt{R_\alpha}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Choose an orthonormal basis of the real isonormal space, which is separable,
  and write $e_i(x) = \E[W_\epsilon(x)g_i]$. Then $\sum_i e_i(x)^2 =
  \Gamma_\epsilon(0) = :v_\epsilon$, and $|e_i(x)| \le \sqrt{v_\epsilon}$. Thus,
  each multiplication $M_{e_i}$ is bounded and selfadjoint. Put $H_i =
  A_\rho^{-1/2}M_{e_i}A_\rho^{-1/2}$ on spatial $L^2$. Gaussian isometry and
  spatial Plancherel give
  \[
    \sum_i\|H_i f\|_2^2
    =\frac1{(2\pi)^3}\int
    \frac{|\widehat{f}(\eta)|^2}{\rho^2+|\eta|^2}
    J_{\rho,\epsilon}(\eta)\ud\eta,\qquad
    J_{\rho,\epsilon}(\eta)
    =\int\frac{e^{-\epsilon|\xi|^2}\mu(\ud\xi)}
    {\rho^2+|\eta+\xi|^2}.
  \]
  This is the covariance calculation for $A_\rho^{-1/2}W_\epsilon
  A_\rho^{-1/2}f$. By Lemma~\ref{L:phase-contractions}, $J_{\rho,\epsilon} \le
  R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}$. Therefore, $\sum_i H_i^2 \le
  R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-4}I$ in quadratic forms. Finite sums have the same upper
  bound. Compression to $S_b$ preserves it: for selfadjoint $H_i$,
  $(S_bH_iS_b)^2 \le S_bH_i^2S_b$ on its range. Both row and column variances
  are the same, since these operators are selfadjoint.

  For completeness, use normalized Hermite polynomials, so multiplication by
  $g_i$ is $a_i^*+a_i$, with $g_i h_k =
  \sqrt{k_i+1}\,h_{k+e_i}+\sqrt{k_i}\,h_{k-e_i}$. On degree $k$, the row map
  $(v_i)\mapsto\sum_i a_i^*v_i$ has norm $\sqrt{k+1}$: its product with its
  adjoint is $\sum_i a_i^*a_i = (k+1)I$ on degree $k+1$. Composing with the
  column of spatial operators $S_bH_iS_b$ bounds the creation part by
  $\sqrt{k+1}\sqrt{R_\alpha}\rho^{(\alpha-4)/2}$. Distinct input degrees have
  orthogonal output degrees. The annihilation part is its adjoint, with the same
  bound after truncation. Adding them on degrees at most $N$ proves
  \eqref{E:fock-sandwich}. Boundedness of $-\Delta$ on $S_b$ is explicit; no
  infinite-frequency operator theorem was used. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:fock-form}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite Gaussian products and their degree cutoffs}

Lemma~\ref{L:fock-form} bounds the field only on Hermite degrees at most $N$.
We now check that this cutoff does not change the finite words that we need.

\LeanMarker{L:finite-word-cutoff}
\begin{lemma}[Exact cutoffs for finite Gaussian words]\label{L:finite-word-cutoff}
  Let $g_{ij}$, $1 \le i \le M$, $1 \le j \le m_i$, be independent standard real
  Gaussian coordinates, grouped by color $i$, and let $\mathscr{X}$ be a complex
  Hilbert space. On Gaussian polynomials with coefficients in $\mathscr{X}$ set
  \[
    J_i=\sum_{j=1}^{m_i}M_{g_{ij}}\otimes K_{ij},
  \]
  where each $K_{ij}$ is bounded on $\mathscr{X}$. Let $F_0,\ldots,F_n$ be
  bounded operators acting only on $\mathscr{X}$. Fix a word $i_1,\ldots,i_n$,
  and let $r_i$ be the number of its occurrences of color $i$. If $N_i \ge r_i$
  and $P$ projects each color onto total Hermite degree at most $N_i$, then, for
  every $v \in \mathscr{X}$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:finite-word-cutoff}
    \begin{split}
      &(I\otimes F_n)(PJ_{i_n}P)(I\otimes F_{n-1})\cdots
      (PJ_{i_1}P)(I\otimes F_0)(1\otimes v)\\
      &\qquad=(I\otimes F_n)J_{i_n}(I\otimes F_{n-1})\cdots
      J_{i_1}(I\otimes F_0)(1\otimes v).
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  The equality holds for finite sums and for Bochner integrals of such words
  whenever the displayed vector integrals exist.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  In normalized Hermite coordinates, multiplication by $g_{ij}$ maps
  $h_{\boldsymbol a}$ to $\sqrt{a_{ij}+1}\,h_{\boldsymbol a+e_{ij}}
  +\sqrt{a_{ij}}\,h_{\boldsymbol a-e_{ij}}$; the second term is zero if $a_{ij}
  = 0$. Thus, it changes the total degree of color $i$ by one and leaves all
  other color degrees unchanged. The operators $I\otimes F_l$ leave every
  Gaussian degree unchanged. After the first $l$ insertions, the uncompressed
  word therefore has color-$i$ degree at most $r_i(l) = |\{j\le l:i_j=i\}| \le
  r_i \le N_i$. This is an induction starting from degree zero in every color.
  Consequently, $P$ is the identity both before and after every insertion along
  that word. Induction on $l$ now proves \eqref{E:finite-word-cutoff}. The
  lowering term has been retained: the assertion includes creation followed by
  annihilation and all resulting self-contractions. Linearity and equality of
  the vector integrands prove the last assertion. This is an equality of
  selected finite words, not of full propagators at a fixed cutoff. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:finite-word-cutoff}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform bounds for all orders}

Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-variance} controls only the first variance
coefficient. We now combine Lemmas~\ref{L:complex-phase}, \ref{L:fock-form}
and~\ref{L:finite-word-cutoff} to bound every static Duhamel order.

\LeanMarker{P:static-phase-all-orders}
\begin{proposition}[All regularized static phase orders]
  \label{P:static-phase-all-orders}
  Retain the full covariance class of Section~\ref{S:static-phase}. For real
  Schwartz $f,g$, let $Z_n^\epsilon(h)$ be the $n$th regularized static Duhamel
  phase, with the recursion \eqref{E:phase-duhamel} for every $n \ge 0$. This is
  an ordinary Gaussian polynomial of degree at most $n$, including
  self-contractions; it is not a pure Wick chaos. There is an explicit finite
  constant
  \[
    A_\alpha=\frac{400\sqrt2\,e^2}{\delta_{\alpha/2}}
  \]
  such that, for $n \ge 1$, uniformly in $\epsilon > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:all-static-phase-orders}
    \|Z_n^\epsilon(h)\|_{L^2(\Omega;X_{1/h})}
    \le
    \frac{(A_\alpha\sqrt{R_\alpha})^n
    h^{(4-\alpha)n/2}}{(n!)^{(3-\alpha)/2}}
    \|(f,g)\|_{1/h}.
  \end{equation}
  At every fixed positive smoothing and real coupling $\sigma$,
  $\sum_{n\ge0}\sigma^nZ_n^\epsilon(h)$ converges in $L^2$ phase. Its squared
  norm is at most
  \begin{equation}\label{E:all-static-phase-sum}
    (\sqrt e+2)^2
    \exp\left\{6A_\alpha^{2/(3-\alpha)}
    (|\sigma|^2R_\alpha)^{1/(3-\alpha)}
    h^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}\right\}\|(f,g)\|_{1/h}^2.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  If $R_\alpha = 0$, spectral homogeneity gives $\mu = 0$, and every
  positive-order term is zero. Assume $R_\alpha > 0$. First work at finite
  Gaussian cutoff $m$, degree cutoff $N$ and spatial band $b$, with initial
  phase $S_b(f,g)$ times the Gaussian vacuum $1$. All relevant operators are
  bounded. Lemmas~\ref{L:fock-form} and~\ref{L:complex-phase}, with $a =
  \alpha/2$, give a bound $100e^{2\lambda h}$ on the complex coupling circle of
  radius $\delta_{\alpha/2}\lambda^{2-\alpha/2}/d_N$.

  The four phase entries are entire in the coupling, by the power series for
  $\cos(h\sqrt L)$ and $\sin(h\sqrt L)/\sqrt L$ of a bounded $L$. Cauchy's
  formula for their operator-valued series bounds the $n$th coefficient by
  \[
    100e^{2\lambda h}
    \left(\frac{d_N}
    {\delta_{\alpha/2}\lambda^{2-\alpha/2}}\right)^n
    \|S_b(f,g)\|_\lambda .
  \]
  The coefficient is exactly the $n$th Duhamel phase from the vacuum. Apply
  Lemma~\ref{L:finite-word-cutoff} with one color, $N = n$, the spatial phase
  Hilbert space as $\mathscr{X}$, and its free wave operators as the $F_l$. It
  identifies each cutoff Duhamel word exactly with its uncompressed word,
  including self-contractions. Dominated time integration preserves this
  identity.

  Choose $\lambda = n/h$. For any phase $z$, $\|z\|_{1/h} \le n\|z\|_{n/h}$ and
  $\|(f,g)\|_{n/h} \le \|(f,g)\|_{1/h}$. Also $d_n \le
  2\sqrt{2n}\sqrt{R_\alpha}$. The preceding bound is therefore at most
  \[
    100n\left(\frac{2\sqrt2 e^2}{\delta_{\alpha/2}}\right)^n
    R_\alpha^{n/2}h^{(4-\alpha)n/2}
    n^{-(3-\alpha)n/2}\|(f,g)\|_{1/h}.
  \]
  Since $100n \le 200^n$ and $n! \le n^n$, this gives
  \eqref{E:all-static-phase-orders} at the cutoffs.

  We justify removal of those cutoffs at fixed $\epsilon$ and $n$. On Gaussian
  degrees at most $N$, the unweighted field multiplication has norm at most
  $2\sqrt{N+1}\sqrt{v_\epsilon}$, uniformly in $m$, by the same
  creation/annihilation argument applied pointwise in $x$. It follows that the
  noise phase insertion $(u,v)\mapsto(0,W_\epsilon u)$, between the required
  finite degree levels, is bounded on $X_\lambda$ by this constant divided by
  $\lambda$. Free phase evolution is bounded by $e^{\lambda h/2}$. For finite
  $m$, $S_b \to I$ strongly and commutes with the free propagator. Each fixed
  Duhamel word therefore converges strongly as $b \to \infty$, by dominated time
  integration.

  Next let $m \to \infty$ on the full Gaussian space. The truncated field
  multiplications converge strongly between finite degree levels. To see this,
  first take a vector depending on finitely many Gaussian coordinates. The
  annihilation tail is then zero, while the creation tail is bounded by a
  constant times
  \[
    \left(\int\sum_{i>m}e_i(x)^2\|F(x)\|^2\ud x\right)^{1/2},
  \]
  which tends to zero by dominated convergence, since the sum is bounded by
  $v_\epsilon$. Such vectors are dense; the uniform multiplication bound extends
  strong convergence to all vectors in those degree levels. Dominated time
  integration again gives convergence of every fixed word. The limit is
  precisely $Z_n^\epsilon$, so the estimate survives both limits. We assert no
  zero-smoothing phase limit in this argument.

  Finally, put $\beta = (3-\alpha)/2 \in (1/2,3/2)$ and $x =
  A_\alpha|\sigma|\sqrt{R_\alpha}h^{(4-\alpha)/2}$. The elementary series
  satisfies
  \[
    \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{x^n}{(n!)^\beta}
    \le2e^{3x^{1/\beta}}.
  \]
  For $\beta \ge 1$, this follows from $\sum a_n^\beta \le (\sum a_n)^\beta$
  with $a_n = x^{n/\beta}/n!$. For $\beta < 1$, apply H\"older to
  $((2x^{1/\beta})^n/n!)^\beta (2^{-\beta n/(1-\beta)})^{1-\beta}$. The second
  geometric sum is at most two. Since $\|Z_0(h)\|_{1/h} \le \sqrt
  e\|(f,g)\|_{1/h}$, absolute $L^2$ summation and squaring give
  \eqref{E:all-static-phase-sum}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-all-orders}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{C:static-interaction}
\begin{corollary}[Summable static interaction bounds]
  \label{C:static-interaction}
  Let $\mathcal{S}_0(t)$ be free phase evolution and, for $\lambda h \le 1$,
  define the static interaction map by its convergent series
  \[
    \mathcal{I}^\epsilon_\sigma(h)(f,g)
    =\mathcal{S}_0(-h)\sum_{n\ge0}\sigma^n Z_n^\epsilon(h).
  \]
  Put $\gamma = 3-\alpha$, $B_\alpha = 3eA_\alpha^2$ and
  \[
    \begin{split}
      L_\alpha(h,\lambda)
      &=4eR_\alpha h^2\lambda^{\alpha-2}
      +\frac{4\sqrt eD_\Gamma}{(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}
      \frac{h^{3-\alpha}}{\lambda},\\
      x&=B_\alpha\sigma^2R_\alpha h^{4-\alpha},\\
      \mathcal{E}(h,\sigma,\lambda)
      &=\sigma^2L_\alpha(h,\lambda)
      +(\lambda h)^{-2}x^2e^{\gamma x^{1/\gamma}}.
    \end{split}
  \]
  Uniformly in $\epsilon > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-interaction}
    \E\|\mathcal{I}^\epsilon_\sigma(h)(f,g)\|_\lambda^2
    \le[1+\mathcal{E}(h,\sigma,\lambda)]\|(f,g)\|_\lambda^2.
  \end{equation}
  If $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$ and $\alpha+\alpha_0 < 2$, then, for $h_j =
  2^{-j}/\lambda$, $\sigma_j^2 = h_j^{-\alpha_0}$ and $T > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-block-series}
    \sum_{j\ge0}\frac{T}{h_j}\mathcal{E}(h_j,\sigma_j,\lambda)<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  For a finite single partition, let $\mathcal{M}_j(h_j)$ be the static phase
  maps with mutually independent copies of the smoothed field, real couplings
  $\sigma_j$, and $\lambda h_j \le 1$. They have continuous $L^2$ extensions to
  independent random initial phases. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:independent-static-blocks}
    \E\|\mathcal{M}_K(h_K)\cdots\mathcal{M}_1(h_1)(f,g)\|_\lambda^2
    \le
    \exp\left\{\lambda\sum_{j=1}^K h_j+
    \sum_{j=1}^K\mathcal{E}(h_j,\sigma_j,\lambda)\right\}
    \|(f,g)\|_\lambda^2.
  \end{equation}
  This concerns independent successive blocks on one partition.
  Formula~\eqref{E:static-block-series} only sums individual upper bounds across
  dyadic levels; it does not identify a product with an evolution driven by
  overlapping or repeated fields.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Write $N = \|(f,g)\|_\lambda$ and $Y_n = \mathcal{S}_0(-h)Z_n^\epsilon(h)$.
  The free estimate from Section~\ref{S:static-phase} holds for both time
  directions, since its differentiated energy was bounded in absolute value.
  Thus, $\|\mathcal{S}_0(-h)\|_\lambda \le \sqrt e$. Since $Y_0 = (f,g)$,
  Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-variance} bounds the complete first variance
  coefficient of $\E\|\sum\sigma^nY_n\|_\lambda^2$ in absolute value by
  $L_\alpha(h,\lambda)N^2$. In detail, its first random squared norm is bounded
  by $e$ times~\eqref{E:phase-born}, and its second-order mean cross term by
  $2N\sqrt e$ times~\eqref{E:phase-mean}.

  For $n \ge 1$, the norm comparisons when $\lambda h \le 1$
  and~\eqref{E:all-static-phase-orders} give
  \[
    \|Y_n\|_{L^2(\Omega;X_\lambda)}
    \le\frac{\sqrt e}{\lambda h}
    \frac{(A_\alpha\sqrt{R_\alpha})^n h^{(4-\alpha)n/2}}
    {(n!)^{\gamma/2}}N.
  \]
  Gaussian symmetry makes $\E\langle Y_r,Y_s\rangle_\lambda = 0$ if $r+s$ is
  odd. All double series below converge absolutely by these bounds. For $r+s =
  2k$, the elementary factorial inequality $r!s! \ge (k!)^2$ shows that the sum
  of absolute inner products is at most
  \[
    \frac{e(2k+1)}{(\lambda h)^2}
    \frac{(A_\alpha^2R_\alpha)^k h^{(4-\alpha)k}}
    {(k!)^\gamma}N^2,\qquad k\ge1.
  \]
  This includes $r = 0$ or $s = 0$, since $\lambda h \le 1$ and $\|Y_0\| = N$.
  The bound $e(2k+1) \le (3e)^k$ therefore controls all orders $k \ge 2$ by
  \[
    \frac1{(\lambda h)^2}
    \sum_{k\ge2}\frac{x^k}{(k!)^\gamma}N^2
    \le\frac{x^2e^{\gamma x^{1/\gamma}}}{(\lambda h)^2}N^2.
  \]
  Here $\gamma > 1$, and $\sum_{j\ge0}x^j/(j!)^\gamma \le e^{\gamma
  x^{1/\gamma}}$. Keeping the sharper first coefficient separately proves
  \eqref{E:static-interaction}.

  Finally, set $\beta = 4-\alpha-\alpha_0 > 2$. After multiplication by $T/h_j$,
  the two first-coefficient terms in~\eqref{E:static-block-series} are constant
  multiples of
  \[
    T\lambda^{\alpha-2}h_j^{1-\alpha_0},\qquad
    \frac T\lambda h_j^{2-\alpha-\alpha_0}.
  \]
  The higher-order term is
  \[
    \frac{T(B_\alpha R_\alpha)^2}{\lambda^2}
    h_j^{2\beta-3}
    \exp\{\gamma(B_\alpha R_\alpha)^{1/\gamma}h_j^{\beta/\gamma}\}.
  \]
  The exponential factor is bounded by its value at $h_0$. All three powers are
  positive, so their geometric series converge. For the finite partition, write
  $\mathcal{M}_j(h_j) = \mathcal{S}_0(h_j)\mathcal{I}_j(h_j)$.
  Bound~\eqref{E:static-interaction} and $\|\mathcal{S}_0(h_j)\|_\lambda^2 \le
  e^{\lambda h_j}$ give a bounded linear map from deterministic $X_\lambda$ to
  $L^2(\Omega_j;X_\lambda)$, initially on Schwartz phases and then by density.
  Tensoring with the past probability space extends this bound to random initial
  phases independent of the $j$th field. Iteration gives the product of
  $e^{\lambda h_j}(1+\mathcal{E}(h_j,\sigma_j,\lambda))$. The inequality $1+y
  \le e^y$ for $y \ge 0$ proves \eqref{E:independent-static-blocks}. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:static-interaction}.
\end{proof}

The independent-block estimate does not use log-convexity in additive
covariance amplitudes. That unrestricted interpolation principle already
fails for a scalar Gaussian wave; see No-go~\ref{P:nogo-11}.


These estimates concern individual static phase and interaction maps. Their
summability alone does not identify the evolution driven by overlapping interval
fields. The coefficient argument in Section~\ref{S:interval-upper} supplies the
separate estimate needed for that evolution.

\section{Young evolution with constant initial data}
\label{S:constant-phase-young}

We use the fixed $R,T,E,\gamma,\theta,\beta$ of~\eqref{E:driver-parameters}, and
choose the allowed even mask $m = \zeta_R$ with $0 \le m \le 1$. All constants
in this section can depend on these fixed data, the model and the auxiliary
physical cutoffs. There is no uniform estimate as $R$ or $T$ grows.

\subsection{The distributional phase and its free evolution}

Define the real Hilbert space
\begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-space}
  F=E\oplus\R^2,\qquad
  \|(e,c_1,c_2)\|_F^2=\|e\|_E^2+c_1^2+c_2^2,\qquad
  e_0=(0,1,0).
\end{equation}
Its distributional interpretation is $(e,c_1,c_2)\mapsto(e_1+c_1,e_2+c_2)$. Let
$i:E \to F$ denote $e\mapsto(e,0,0)$ and let $\pi_C:F \to \R^2$ be the
constant-coordinate projection.

\begin{lemma}[Constants and finite propagation]\label{L:constant-phase-free}
  The distributional interpretation of $F$ is continuous and injective. The
  space is complete and separable, and $iE$ is closed and proper. For every real
  $\psi \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-cutoff}
    L_\psi(e,c_1,c_2)=\psi e+(c_1\psi,c_2\psi)
  \end{equation}
  is bounded from $F$ to $E$. The free wave group on $F$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-free}
    S_F(t)(e,c_1,c_2)=(S(t)e,c_1+tc_2,c_2).
  \end{equation}
  It is strongly continuous and bounded on finite horizons and has finite
  propagation for the total distributional data, including local cancellation
  between $e$ and the constants.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  A nonzero constant has Fourier transform a nonzero multiple of $\delta_0$.
  Every $H^\sigma$ Fourier transform is locally $L^2$ near zero, since its
  weight is bounded above and below there. A locally $L^2$ function $f$ cannot
  represent $\delta_0$: testing against $\varphi(\xi/\varepsilon)$, with
  $\varphi(0) = 1$ and $\varphi$ compactly supported in $B_1$, bounds its
  integral by
  \[
    \|f\|_{L^2(B_\varepsilon)}
    \varepsilon^{d/2}\|\varphi\|_2\longrightarrow0,
  \]
  whereas $\delta_0$ gives one. Thus, constants lie in neither $H^{1/2}$ nor
  $H^{-1/2}$; the interpretation is injective. Sobolev duality and the two test
  integrals prove continuity into distributions, uniformly on bounded families
  of compact tests. Completeness, separability and the closed proper inclusion
  follow from the Hilbert direct sum.

  Multiplication by $\psi$ is bounded on both Sobolev entries. Indeed, the
  Peetre inequalities give $\langle\xi\rangle^\sigma\langle\eta\rangle^{-\sigma}
  \le 2^{|\sigma|/2}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{|\sigma|}$. In the unitary Fourier
  convention its weighted kernel is therefore bounded by convolution with the
  $L^1$ function $2^{|\sigma|/2}c_d|\widehat{\psi}(q)|\langle
  q\rangle^{|\sigma|}$, where $c_d = (2\pi)^{-d/2}$. The $L^1*L^2$ bound applies
  at $\sigma = \pm1/2$; the two constant columns are also in $E$. This proves
  boundedness of~\eqref{E:constant-phase-cutoff}.

  The finite-dimensional block in~\eqref{E:constant-phase-free} and the strong,
  finite-horizon bound for $S(t)$ from Lemma~\ref{L:driver-gain-localization}
  prove the group assertions. To verify its physical meaning, for $t > 0$
  consider the matrix of compactly supported distributions
  \[
    \begin{pmatrix}
      \partial_tG_d(t)&G_d(t)\\
      \Delta G_d(t)&\partial_tG_d(t)
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  The source Green convention gives $\widehat{G}_d(t)(\xi) =
  c_d\sin(t|\xi|)/|\xi|$; hence its convolution multiplier is the sine quotient,
  and the differentiated multipliers are $\cos(t|\xi|)$ and
  $-|\xi|\sin(t|\xi|)$. The matrix agrees with $S(t)$ on Schwartz phases.
  Convolution by a compact distribution is continuous on tempered distributions:
  its transpose convolves a Schwartz test with the reflected compact
  distribution, continuously in all Schwartz seminorms. Schwartz approximation
  in $E$ extends the identity to $E$. The masses of the four entries are
  $1,t,0,1$, so constants give exactly~\eqref{E:constant-phase-free}. Time
  reversal handles $t < 0$. Each entry is supported in $B_{|t|}$, also for the
  three-dimensional sphere kernel. If the total initial distribution vanishes on
  an enlarged region, each translated kernel tests zero there, so the evolved
  total distribution vanishes on the smaller cone. This argument permits
  cancellation between its Sobolev and constant parts. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:constant-phase-free}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two columns for constant initial data}

Choose real $\chi \in C_c^\infty$, equal to one on $B_{2R+T+1}$ and supported in
$B_{2R+T+2}$, and choose real compact smooth $\psi = 1$ on a neighborhood of
$\operatorname{supp}\chi$. Write $M_E = M_R$ and $M_E^\rho = M_{Y_\rho}$ for the
paths already constructed, with the same original deterministic coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[Extended operator path]\label{P:constant-phase-driver}
  As identities of continuous operator paths,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-localization}
    D_\chi M_E=M_E=M_ED_\chi,
  \end{equation}
  and the same holds for each fixed $\rho$. Define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-phase-driver}
    A_E=M_EL_\psi,\qquad A=iA_E,\qquad
    A_E^\rho=M_E^\rho L_\psi,\qquad A^\rho=iA_E^\rho.
  \end{equation}
  These are independent of $\psi$, have compact values, and satisfy $Ai = iM_E$,
  $\pi_CA = 0$ and their source counterparts. In every finite $L^pC^\theta$,
  $A^\rho \to A$ at arbitrary relative source-width rates. Their norms have
  uniform $\sqrt p$ moment bounds and a common finite square-exponential moment.
  The original $A$ is measurable with respect to the given $W$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Lemma~\ref{L:driver-gain-localization} gives both localizations for every
  deterministic $A_j(0,t)$. The original finite sums retain them.
  Proposition~\ref{P:source-gaussian-transfer} constructs the source finite sums
  with exactly these coefficients and correlated $Y_\rho(f_j)$. Their $C^\theta$
  limits preserve both identities. A uniformly convergent subsequence, or
  rational times and continuous paths,
  gives~\eqref{E:constant-phase-localization} for all $t$ on one event. Use a
  separate event for each fixed $\rho$.

  For two choices $\psi,\psi'$, $(L_\psi-L_{\psi'})z \in E$ and
  $D_\chi(L_\psi-L_{\psi'})z = 0$. The right localization kills this difference.
  For $z = ie$, $D_\chi D_\psi e = D_\chi e$ and hence $Ai = iM_E$. The constant
  columns are precisely $iM_E(\psi,0)$ and $iM_E(0,\psi)$; they are retained.
  Bounded composition preserves compactness and transfers
  \eqref{E:source-driver-limit} and the Gaussian norm bounds
  to~\eqref{E:constant-phase-driver}. If $\|X\|_{L^p} \le C\sqrt p$, then $\E
  X^{2n} \le C^{2n}(2n)^n$. Since $n! \ge (n/e)^n$, the exponential series gives
  $\E e^{cX^2} < \infty$ uniformly when $2ecC^2 < 1$. Finally, $A$ is a
  deterministic bounded transform of the original $W$-measurable operator path.
  This proves Proposition~\ref{P:constant-phase-driver}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ordinary response on all of $F$]\label{L:constant-source-response}
  For fixed $\rho$ put $b(r) = mY_\rho(r)$ and define
  \[
    B_F(b)(e,c_1,c_2)=(0,b(e_1+c_1))\in E.
  \]
  Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-source-response}
    A_E^\rho(t)z=\int_0^tS(-r)B_F(b(r))S_F(r)z\ud r
  \end{equation}
  as a statewise strong integral. Its primitive is Lipschitz in
  $\mathcal{B}(F,E)$ and the identity holds for all states and times on one
  event for this fixed $\rho$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The source regularity gives one compact spatial support for $b$, continuity in
  $L^\infty$ and $L^2$, and bounded spatial derivatives on the horizon. Since
  $e_1 \in L^2$ and $\|\cdot\|_{H^{-1/2}} \le \|\cdot\|_2$,
  \[
    \|B_F(b)\|_{\mathcal{B}(F,E)}
    \le\|b\|_\infty+\|b\|_2.
  \]
  Strong continuity and finite-horizon bounds of both free groups make the
  integrand strongly continuous on each state and uniformly bounded as an
  operator. Integrating its bound proves operator-norm Lipschitz increments
  without assuming a Banach-valued operator derivative.

  The total initial distributions of $z$ and $iL_\psi z$ agree on the entire
  enlarged ball where $\psi = 1$. Lemma~\ref{L:constant-phase-free} gives
  equality of the free displacements near $\operatorname{supp}m$ for $0 \le r
  \le T$. Multiplication by $b(r)$ kills the difference. Apply
  Proposition~\ref{P:source-ordinary-response} to the $E$ state $L_\psi z$ to
  obtain~\eqref{E:constant-source-response}. Separability and rational times,
  followed by boundedness and continuity, give its path identity. This proves
  the constant columns directly; no density of $E$ in $F$ is used. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-response}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact linear Young equation}

We use Young's integration theory~\cite{young:36:inequality} in its
Banach-space form. The estimate is stated by
Friz and Hairer~\cite{friz.hairer:20:course}*{Sections~1.4--1.5, 4.1, (4.3), pp.~13, 61--62}.
For $D,V$ with exponent $\theta > 1/2$ and bounded bilinear multiplication, left
composition gives
\begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-estimate}
  \left\|\int_s^t\ud D(r)V(r)-D(s,t)V(s)\right\|
  \le C_\theta[D]_\theta[V]_\theta|t-s|^{2\theta}.
\end{equation}
The Banach spaces here are $\mathcal{B}(F)$, and multiplication is bounded with
constant one. We derive the linear solution and stability estimates below rather
than invoke a nonlinear bounded-vector-field theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Linear Young evolution]\label{P:constant-young-linear}
  For every $D \in C^\theta([0,T];\mathcal{B}(F))$ with $D(0) = 0$ there is a
  unique $C^\theta$ solution, in the terminal variable, of
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-equation}
    U_D(t,s)=I+\int_s^t\ud D(r)U_D(r,s).
  \end{equation}
  It has the composition law, invertible values and joint operator-norm
  continuity on $0 \le s \le t \le T$. The map $D\mapsto U_D$ is locally
  Lipschitz into the uniform time-triangle norm, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-growth}
    \sup_{s\le t\le T}\|U_D(t,s)\|
    \le C_T\exp\big(C_T(1+\|D\|_{C^\theta})^{1/\theta}\big).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  On an interval $[a,a+\ell]$ use the complete scaled norm $N_\ell(V) =
  \sup\|V\|+\ell^\theta[V]_\theta$ and put $I_D(V)(t) = \int_a^t\ud D\,V$.
  Applying~\eqref{E:constant-young-estimate} to every subinterval gives
  \begin{align*}
    \sup\|I_D(V)\|
    &\le[D]_\theta\ell^\theta\sup\|V\|
    +C_\theta[D]_\theta\ell^{2\theta}[V]_\theta,\\
    [I_D(V)]_\theta
    &\le[D]_\theta\sup\|V\|
    +C_\theta[D]_\theta\ell^\theta[V]_\theta.
  \end{align*}
  Thus, with $c_\theta = 2\max(1,C_\theta)$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-local}
    N_\ell(I_D(V))
    \le c_\theta[D]_\theta\ell^\theta N_\ell(V).
  \end{equation}
  Take $K \ge 1$, $[D]_\theta \le K$, and choose $\ell$ with $c_\theta
  K\ell^\theta \le 1/4$. The map $V\mapsto V_a+I_D(V)$ is a contraction on the
  complete affine space of paths starting at $V_a$. Its solution satisfies
  $N_\ell(V) \le (4/3)\|V_a\| \le 2\|V_a\|$.

  To compare $D,D'$, require both seminorms at most $K$ and write $\delta =
  \|D-D'\|_{C^\theta}$, $W = V-V'$. Subtracting gives $W =
  W_a+I_D(W)+I_{D-D'}(V')$. Absorbing the factor $1/4$ yields
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-young-difference}
    N_\ell(W)\le2\|W_a\|
    +2c_\theta\ell^\theta\delta N_\ell(V').
  \end{equation}
  Cover $[s,T]$ by at most $n_{K,T}$ such pieces. If the second initial norm is
  $B$, its norm on piece $j$, indexed from zero, is at most $2^{j+1}B$. With
  $E_j$ the difference at its left endpoint, induction gives
  \[
    E_{j+1}\le2E_j+2^{j+2}c_\theta\ell^\theta\delta B,\qquad
    E_j\le2^jE_0+j2^{j+1}c_\theta\ell^\theta\delta B.
  \]
  Equation~\eqref{E:constant-young-difference} gives the same bound inside each
  piece; a shorter final piece only reduces the coefficient. For evolutions,
  $E_0 = 0$ and $B = 1$. The number of pieces is uniform in $s,t$, proving local
  Lipschitz dependence in the full triangle norm.

  Now choose $K = 1+\|D\|_{C^\theta}$. At most
  $C_T(1+\|D\|_{C^\theta}^{1/\theta})$ pieces suffice. Each multiplies the norm
  by at most two, giving \eqref{E:constant-young-growth}. Uniqueness patches the
  solutions; right multiplication by the preceding evolution gives the
  composition law. On a small piece~\eqref{E:constant-young-local} gives
  $\|U_D-I\|_\infty \le 1/3$, so a Neumann series proves local invertibility,
  then products give global invertibility. Finally, $U_D(t,s) =
  U_D(t,0)U_D(s,0)^{-1}$ proves joint norm continuity, since $U_D(\cdot,0)$ is
  continuous and inversion is continuous on invertible operators. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-young-linear}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{P:constant-young-linear} concerns one deterministic operator
path. We now apply it to the random paths $A$ and $A^\rho$
of~\eqref{E:constant-phase-driver} and pass to the physical flow on $F$.

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian Young and physical flows]
  \label{P:constant-gaussian-flow}
  Apply Proposition~\ref{P:constant-young-linear} to $A,A^\rho$. The resulting
  flows are strongly measurable in uniform triangle norm, have every finite norm
  moment and satisfy
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-flow-limit}
    U_{A^\rho}\longrightarrow U_A
    \quad\hbox{in every finite }L^p
    \left(\sup_{0\le s\le t\le T}\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}(F)}\right).
  \end{equation}
  They preserve the interaction constant coordinates:
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-flow-range}
    \pi_CU_A(t,s)=\pi_C,\qquad
    (U_A(t,s)-I)F\subset iE,
  \end{equation}
  and likewise for the source. The physical flow is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-physical-flow}
    P_A(t,s)=S_F(t)U_A(t,s)S_F(-s).
  \end{equation}
  It has composition and strong continuity on states, physical constant block
  $(c_1,c_2)\mapsto(c_1+(t-s)c_2,c_2)$, all finite moments and the analogous
  uniform $L^p$ source convergence. Its difference from the free flow has range
  in $iE$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The original paths lie in separable closed path spans by their Gaussian
  construction. Bounded composition in~\eqref{E:constant-phase-driver} and the
  continuous Young map preserve strong measurability into
  $C(\Delta_T;\mathcal{B}(F))$, where $\Delta_T = \{(t,s):0\le s\le t\le T\}$
  has the uniform norm. Indeed, the continuous image of the separable space of
  operator paths has separable closure in this triangle space. Applying $\pi_C$
  to Young Riemann sums gives the first identity
  in~\eqref{E:constant-flow-range}. Each increment applied to any state lies in
  $iE$; closedness of this subspace passes the property through sums and limits,
  giving the second identity.

  Write $X_\rho = \|A^\rho\|_{C^\theta}$ and $X_0 = \|A\|_{C^\theta}$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-phase-driver} supplies $c_0 > 0$ and $H_0 <
  \infty$ such that
  \[
    \sup_\rho\E e^{c_0X_\rho^2}\le H_0,\qquad
    \E e^{c_0X_0^2}\le H_0.
  \]
  For $K \ge 1$, let $L_K$ be the Lipschitz constant of
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-young-linear} on the ball of operator paths of
  radius $K$, in the whole time-triangle norm. On $\{X_\rho\le K,\ X_0\le K\}$
  it gives
  \[
    \|U_{A^\rho}-U_A\|_\infty
    \le L_K\|A^\rho-A\|_{C^\theta}.
  \]
  Consequently, for $\varepsilon > 0$ and any finite $q \ge 1$, Markov and the
  union bound give
  \[
    \mathbb{P}(\|U_{A^\rho}-U_A\|_\infty>\varepsilon)
    \le2H_0e^{-c_0K^2}
    +(L_K/\varepsilon)^q\E\|A^\rho-A\|_{C^\theta}^q.
  \]
  At fixed $K$ the second term tends to zero at arbitrary relative source-width
  rates. Only then let $K \to \infty$ to obtain convergence in probability. No
  independence of the original, source and residual operator paths is required.

  Put $\nu = 1/\theta < 2$. For each finite $r \ge 1$ and $c > 0$, there is
  $C_{r,c} < \infty$ such that
  \[
    rC_T(1+x)^\nu\le cx^2+C_{r,c},\qquad x\ge0.
  \]
  This follows because $(1+x)^\nu/x^2 \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$, while the
  remainder is bounded on compact intervals. Use $c \le c_0$
  and~\eqref{E:constant-young-growth} to obtain
  \[
    \sup_\rho\E\|U_{A^\rho}\|_\infty^r<\infty,\qquad
    \E\|U_A\|_\infty^r<\infty.
  \]
  In particular, for fixed finite $p \ge 1$ and $V_\rho =
  \|U_{A^\rho}-U_A\|_\infty$, the $2p$ moments of $V_\rho$ are uniformly
  bounded. For every $\varepsilon > 0$,
  \[
    \E V_\rho^p
    \le\varepsilon^p+
    (\E V_\rho^{2p})^{1/2}
    \mathbb{P}(V_\rho>\varepsilon)^{1/2}.
  \]
  The second term tends to zero by convergence in probability; then let
  $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This proves \eqref{E:constant-flow-limit}. The
  square-exponential bound used here is an operator path bound; no
  square-exponential flow bound is needed.

  The free group is strongly continuous and bounded on the horizon. Define
  $\mathcal{B}_\infty(\Delta_T;\mathcal{B}(F))$ to be the Banach space of all
  bounded operator families on $\Delta_T$. The deterministic map
  \[
    \mathcal{L}(V)(t,s)=S_F(t)V(t,s)S_F(-s)
  \]
  is bounded linear from $C(\Delta_T;\mathcal{B}(F))$ into this bounded-family
  space, with norm at most $C_T^2$, where $C_T \ge \sup_{|r|\le T}\|S_F(r)\|$.
  Hence, it preserves strong measurability on the separable ranges and gives, in
  particular,
  \[
    \|P_{A^\rho}-P_A\|_\infty
    \le C_T^2\|U_{A^\rho}-U_A\|_\infty.
  \]
  All finite moments and uniform $L^p$ convergence therefore transfer to the
  physical flows. This does not place those flows in the norm-continuous
  triangle space or assert operator-norm continuity of the free group. For each
  fixed state, joint continuity follows by splitting a physical-flow difference
  into the norm-continuous $U$ difference and two strong free-group differences,
  using the uniform group bound. Group cancellation gives composition. The
  finite constant block $J_t(c_1,c_2) = (c_1+tc_2,c_2)$ satisfies $J_tJ_{-s} =
  J_{t-s}$, which gives the asserted physical constant coordinates from
  $\pi_CU_A = \pi_C$. Finally,
  \[
    P_A(t,s)-S_F(t-s)
    =S_F(t)(U_A(t,s)-I)S_F(-s)
  \]
  has range in $iE$, since $S_F(t)iE = iE$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-gaussian-flow}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Ordinary source solutions and the original tested observable}

So far $P_{A^\rho}$ is defined through Young integrals, not through the source
equation of Proposition~\ref{P:masked-source-moments}. We now connect the two
at a fixed source width $\rho$.

\begin{lemma}[Identification with the ordinary source equation]
  \label{L:constant-source-solution}
  For fixed $\rho$, the displacement distribution of $P_{A^\rho}(t,0)e_0$ is the
  locally continuous ordinary source solution $u_\rho^m(t,\cdot)$ from
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-source-moments}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  In~\eqref{E:constant-source-response} let $C(r) = S(-r)B_F(b(r))S_F(r)$. The
  primitive is $A_E^\rho$. For left endpoints $r_j = t_j$, the Young sums
  applied to a state $v$ are
  \[
    \sum_j\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}iC(r)U_{A^\rho}(r_j,s)v\ud r.
  \]
  Uniform continuity of $U$ and the uniform bound for $C$ make these converge to
  the strong integral with $U(r,s)v$. Thus, the Young equation is precisely the
  ordinary interaction integral on states. Multiplying by free groups gives the
  Duhamel equation for displacement and velocity. Its unique $C([0,T];F)$
  solution follows by the bounded-coefficient Picard argument with the strongly
  continuous, bounded free group. No operator-norm derivative of $S_F$ is used.

  More explicitly, put $W_0(t) = e_0$ and, for $n \ge 1$,
  \[
    W_n(t)=\int_0^t S_F(t-r)iB_F(b(r))W_{n-1}(r)\ud r.
  \]
  If $B_T = \sup_r\|B_F(b(r))\|_{\mathcal{B}(F,E)}$, induction on the time
  simplex gives $\sup_t\|W_n(t)\|_F \le C_T^{n+1}B_T^nT^n/n!$. We take $C_T \ge
  \max(1,\sup_{|t|\le T}\|S_F(t)\|)$, which also supplies the bound at order
  zero. The series converges in $C([0,T];F)$. The Duhamel map there has norm at
  most $C_TB_TT$, so its sum solves the same integral equation and equals the
  ordinary phase by uniqueness.

  Let $X_0(t,x) = 1$ and $X_n(t) = \int_0^tG_d(t-r)*(b(r)X_{n-1}(r))\ud r$. At
  fixed $n$ these are continuous in time and smooth in space. Derivatives fall
  on the smooth compact factor $bX_{n-1}$; Green mass $t-r$ bounds the
  integrals, while the unit-time measure representation gives time continuity.
  This is induction using only finitely many bounded derivatives for each fixed
  derivative order. At $n = 0$ the phase displacement is $X_0$. If the equality
  holds at $n-1$, multiplication by smooth $b$ identifies the forcing with
  $(0,f_r)$, where $f_r = b(r)X_{n-1}(r)$ is smooth and compactly supported. For
  a compact smooth test $\varphi$, continuous distributional testing of the
  phase integral yields
  \[
    \langle[W_n(t)]_1,\varphi\rangle
    =\int_0^t\int f_r(y)
    \int G_d(t-r,\ud z)\varphi(y+z)\ud y\ud r
    =\langle X_n(t),\varphi\rangle.
  \]
  Absolute Fubini follows from $\|\varphi\|_\infty\|f_r\|_1(t-r)$, integrable on
  the horizon because $f_r$ has one compact support and continuous $L^1$ norm.
  This also covers sphere measures in $d = 3$. Iterating the scalar recursion
  gives exactly $X_{\rho,n}^m$, with chronological order and all physical mask
  factors. The scalar compact-cone factorial bound gives locally uniform
  convergence to $u_\rho^m$, whereas the phase series converges in $F$ and hence
  in distributions. Their displacement limits agree. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-solution}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-solution} holds at each fixed $\rho$. To reach
the original noise we test against a fixed $\phi$ and use the source
limit~\eqref{E:constant-flow-limit}.

\begin{proposition}[Original average and original point moment]
  \label{P:constant-raw-average}
  Let real $\phi \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$ be nonnegative with integral one. The
  bounded functional and original random variable
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-raw-average}
    O_\phi(e,c_1,c_2)=\langle e_1,\phi\rangle+c_1,\qquad
    Z_m(t,\phi)=O_\phi P_A(t,0)e_0
  \end{equation}
  are well defined, and $Z_m$ is $W$-measurable. At arbitrary relative
  source-width rates, for every finite $p$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-tested-limit}
    \int\phi(x)u_\rho^m(t,x)\ud x
    \longrightarrow Z_m(t,\phi)\quad\text{in }L^p.
  \end{equation}
  In particular,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:constant-original-point-transfer}
    \E|Z_m(t,\phi)|^2\le\E|u(t,0)|^2.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Sobolev duality gives $\|O_\phi\| \le (\|\phi\|_{H^{-1/2}}^2+1)^{1/2}$. The
  original operator path is $W$-measurable, and the Young and deterministic maps
  above are continuous or bounded measurable on their separable ranges. Thus,
  $Z_m$ is $W$-measurable and does not depend on the auxiliary $J$ used in
  source realizations. At each fixed $\rho$,
  Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-solution} identifies the displacement as a
  distribution with the ordinary source solution. Testing by this same $\phi$,
  with $\int\phi = 1$, therefore gives the almost sure identity
  \[
    O_\phi P_{A^\rho}(t,0)e_0
    =C_\phi:=\int\phi(x)u_\rho^m(t,x)\ud x.
  \]
  This uses the displacement pairing and its constant displacement coordinate
  in~\eqref{E:constant-raw-average}; it takes no point trace of an $H^{1/2}$
  state. All identities here are at one fixed $\rho$, so no uncountable
  intersection of source-version events is used.

  We verify that $C_\phi$ equals the Bochner $L^2$ average $B_\phi$ from
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-source-moments}. That proposition gives the
  spatially uniform bound $\|u_\rho^m(t,x)\|_2 \le Q_t$, with $Q_t$ also
  independent of source widths. Scalar Fubini gives
  \[
    \E\int|\phi(x)u_\rho^m(t,x)|\ud x
    \le Q_t\|\phi\|_1<\infty.
  \]
  Thus, the ordinary spatial integral exists almost surely. Weighted
  Cauchy--Schwarz and scalar Fubini also give
  \[
    \E|C_\phi|^2
    \le\|\phi\|_1\int|\phi(x)|\E|u_\rho^m(t,x)|^2\ud x
    \le Q_t^2\|\phi\|_1^2.
  \]
  For every bounded real random variable $\Xi$ on the fixed source space,
  $V\mapsto\E[\Xi V]$ is a continuous functional on $L^2$. It therefore commutes
  with the Bochner integral, while scalar Fubini is justified by $\|\Xi\|_\infty
  Q_t\|\phi\|_1$. Consequently,
  \[
    \E[\Xi B_\phi]
    =\int\phi(x)\E[\Xi u_\rho^m(t,x)]\ud x
    =\E[\Xi C_\phi].
  \]
  Both averages are real $L^2$ variables. Taking $\Xi =
  \operatorname{sign}(B_\phi-C_\phi)$ yields $\E|B_\phi-C_\phi| = 0$, so the two
  averages agree almost surely.

  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-gaussian-flow} now gives, for every finite $p$,
  \[
    \|B_\phi-Z_m(t,\phi)\|_{L^p}
    \le\|O_\phi\|\|e_0\|
    \big\|\|P_{A^\rho}-P_A\|_\infty\big\|_{L^p}
    \longrightarrow0.
  \]
  This is~\eqref{E:constant-tested-limit}, including its $L^2$ case, for the
  masked Bochner average, with arbitrary relative source-width rates. It
  supplies the conditional convergence premise of
  Proposition~\ref{P:masked-canonical-transfer} at the same fixed mask, horizon,
  original covariance and observable. Since $\phi \ge 0$ and $\|\phi\|_1 = 1$,
  that proposition gives \eqref{E:constant-original-point-transfer}. Zero
  covariance gives the free constant solution and equality. All source limits
  are at fixed mask and horizon, before time asymptotics. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-raw-average}.
\end{proof}

So far the operator path comes from the noise alone.
Section~\ref{S:compact-mean-family} also needs a deterministic potential.

\begin{proposition}[Compact deterministic base]\label{P:constant-compact-base}
  Let $h \in C([0,T];C_c^\infty(K))$ be real for one fixed compact $K$, with all
  spatial derivatives continuous on that support. The statewise primitive
  \[
    A_h(t)=\int_0^t iS(-r)B_F(h(r))S_F(r)\ud r
  \]
  is Lipschitz in $\mathcal{B}(F)$, takes values with range in $iE$, and has a
  bound $C_T\sup_r(\|h(r)\|_\infty+\|h(r)\|_2)$. The flow conclusions above,
  including moments, source convergence and ordinary-source identification, hold
  for $A_h+gA$ and $A_h+gA^\rho$, uniformly on bounded displayed deterministic
  bounds and bounded real $g$. No Cameron-shift identity is asserted here.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The estimate for $B_F$ and the strong free groups prove the primitive and its
  Lipschitz bound exactly as in Lemma~\ref{L:constant-source-response}. If
  $\|A_h\|_{C^\theta} \le H$, $|g| \le G$ and $X = \|A\|_{C^\theta}$, then
  $\|A_h+gA\|_{C^\theta} \le H+GX$. If $\E e^{cX^2}$ is bounded, choose $c' \le
  c/(2(1+G^2))$ to obtain
  \[
    \E e^{c'\|A_h+gA\|_{C^\theta}^2}
    \le e^{2c'H^2}\E e^{2c'G^2X^2}<\infty.
  \]
  Alternatively $pC(1+H+Gx)^{1/\theta} \le cx^2+C_{p,H,G,c}$ transfers the Young
  growth estimate directly to all finite moments. Source operator-path
  convergence, local Young stability and uniform integrability give the same
  flow convergence. The state-integral and Picard-to-chain arguments apply to
  $h+gmY_\rho$, whose spatial support lies in one fixed compact union. These are
  individual estimates and versions, without an uncountable common-parameter
  event. Only $h = 0$, $g = 1$ enters
  \eqref{E:constant-original-point-transfer}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:constant-compact-base}.
\end{proof}

\section{Compact mean families and the spectral gap for wave evolution}
\label{S:compact-mean-family}

Let $I = [0,1]$, let $K_x \subset \R^d$ be compact, and let $\sigma\mapsto
V_\sigma$ be continuous from $I$ into $C_c^\infty(K_x)$, real and nonnegative.
The index $\sigma$ is a family parameter, distinct from physical propagation
time $t$. Assume that one real $w \in H^1(\R^d)$ with $\|w\|_2 = 1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{E:compact-common-trial}
  \|\nabla w\|_2^2-\int V_\sigma w^2\le-\varepsilon
  \quad\hbox{for all }\sigma\in I,\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\end{equation}
Put $M = \sup_I\|V_\sigma\|_\infty$ and $H_\sigma = -\Delta-V_\sigma$. The
phases $E,F$, inclusion $i$, data $e_0$ and observable $O_\phi$ are exactly
those of Section~\ref{S:constant-phase-young}.
Proposition~\ref{P:constant-compact-base}, with deterministic coefficient $h =
V_\sigma$ and noise amplitude zero, gives the ordinary physical flow
$P_\sigma(t)$ on $F$. Its $E$ restriction is $P_\sigma^E(t)$. Uniqueness of the
Duhamel solution makes finite-horizon constructions agree on overlaps; integer
horizons and autonomy give the same semigroup for every $t \ge 0$.

We import the following statements from the \emph{second edition}
of~\cite{teschl:14:mathematical}: Theorem~10.2, p.~258, gives the self-adjoint
$H^2$ operator, lower bound, essential spectrum $[0,\infty)$ and compact smooth
core for real compact smooth potentials in the dimensions considered here.
Theorem~10.13, p.~273, gives a simple positive ground state \emph{if the
spectral bottom is an eigenvalue}. Theorem~4.12 and Corollary~4.13,
pp.~140--141, give max--min and form monotonicity, with the essential threshold
used after discrete eigenvalues end and with the quadratic-form domain allowed.
Uniformity, fractional norms and all applications to $F$ are proved below.

\subsection{The ground family and the global phase}

\begin{proposition}[Ground states and a uniform rate gap]
  \label{P:compact-ground-family}
  The common form domain of $H_\sigma$ is $H^1$ with form $q_\sigma(u) =
  \|\nabla u\|_2^2-\int V_\sigma|u|^2$. Its bottom $E_1(\sigma)$ is a simple
  eigenvalue with a unique real positive-a.e. normalized ground state
  $p_\sigma$. Let $E_2(\sigma)$ be the second max--min level, set to zero when
  there is no second negative eigenvalue. Then
  \begin{align}
    -M&\le E_1(\sigma)\le-\varepsilon,\qquad
    E_1(\sigma)<E_2(\sigma)\le0,\label{E:compact-energy-levels}\\
    |E_j(\sigma)-E_j(\tau)|
    &\le\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty,\qquad j=1,2.
    \label{E:compact-level-continuity}
  \end{align}
  The map $\sigma\mapsto p_\sigma$ is continuous into $H^2$ and bounded there.
  With $\lambda_\sigma = \sqrt{-E_1(\sigma)}$ and $\mu_\sigma =
  \sqrt{-E_2(\sigma)}$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-rate-gap}
    \sqrt\varepsilon\le\lambda_\sigma\le\sqrt M,\qquad
    d_0:=\inf_I(\lambda_\sigma-\mu_\sigma)>0.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The imported Theorem~10.2 applies to $-V_\sigma$, which is real, compactly
  supported and in $L^2$ for $d \le 3$. It gives the stated self-adjoint
  operator on $H^2$ and essential threshold zero. Write $c = 1+M$ and $A_\sigma
  = H_\sigma+c \ge 1$. On $H^2$, integration by parts and the spectral theorem
  give
  \[
    \|A_\sigma^{1/2}u\|_2^2
    =q_\sigma(u)+c\|u\|_2^2,\qquad
    \|u\|_{H^1}^2\le\|A_\sigma^{1/2}u\|_2^2
    \le c\|u\|_{H^1}^2.
  \]
  Spectral cutoffs $1_{[1,n]}(A_\sigma)u$ show that $H^2$ is a core for the
  half-power form norm. Fourier cutoffs show it is dense in $H^1$. The two
  equivalent completions are therefore $D(A_\sigma^{1/2})$ and $H^1$. Both norms
  dominate $L^2$ and give the same $L^2$ limit, so these are equal as subspaces,
  with the displayed form. This verifies the form domain used below.

  The form lower bound and~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial} give $-M \le E_1 \le
  -\varepsilon$ by the imported form max--min principle. The bottom belongs to
  the spectrum and lies below the essential threshold, so it is an isolated
  eigenvalue of finite multiplicity. Only now apply the imported Theorem~10.13:
  it is simple with a positive ground state. Conjugation preserves the real
  operator; positivity and unit $L^2$ norm fix its real representative.

  Simplicity, isolation and threshold zero give $E_1 < E_2 \le 0$. For every
  common unit form test, $|q_\sigma(u)-q_\tau(u)| \le
  \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty$. Taking the same infima over orthogonal
  complements and suprema in the source max--min formula proves
  \eqref{E:compact-level-continuity}, including passage of $E_2$ to the
  threshold. Compactness gives $\Delta_*: = \min_I(E_2-E_1) > 0$. Thus,
  \[
    \lambda_\sigma-\mu_\sigma
    =\frac{E_2(\sigma)-E_1(\sigma)}
    {\lambda_\sigma+\mu_\sigma}
    \ge\frac{\Delta_*}{2\sqrt M}>0.
  \]
  Here $M \ge \varepsilon > 0$, and no division by $\mu_\sigma$ occurs.

  Put $d_V = \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty$ and decompose $p_\tau = a p_\sigma+r$,
  with $r\perp p_\sigma$ and $0 < a \le 1$. The variational bound gives
  $q_\sigma(p_\tau) \le E_1(\sigma)+2d_V$, whereas the complementary spectral
  bound gives $q_\sigma(p_\tau) \ge E_1(\sigma)+\Delta_*\|r\|_2^2$. Hence,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-ground-continuity}
    \|p_\tau-p_\sigma\|_2^2
    =2(1-a)\le2(1-a^2)\le4d_V/\Delta_*.
  \end{equation}
  The eigenfunction equation is
  \[
    (1-\Delta)p_\sigma
    =(1-\lambda_\sigma^2+V_\sigma)p_\sigma.
  \]
  Its right side is continuous in $L^2$ and bounded there by $1+2M$. The inverse
  of $1-\Delta$ is an isometry from $L^2$ to the Bessel $H^2$ space, proving the
  asserted $H^2$ continuity and bound. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-ground-family}.
\end{proof}

The rate gap~\eqref{E:compact-rate-gap} is a statement about the spectrum of
$H_\sigma$. The wave on $E$ is measured in the fixed norm of $E$, so we first
compare the two.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform fractional phase norms]\label{L:compact-fractional-phase}
  Let $B = 1-\Delta$, $c = 1+M$ and $A_\sigma = H_\sigma+c$. The quarter-power
  domain is $H^{1/2}$ and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-quarter-power}
    \|B^{1/4}u\|_2\le\|A_\sigma^{1/4}u\|_2
    \le c^{1/4}\|B^{1/4}u\|_2.
  \end{equation}
  The norm
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-spectral-phase-norm}
    \|(u,z)\|_{E_\sigma}^2
    =\|A_\sigma^{1/4}u\|_2^2
    +\|A_\sigma^{-1/4}z\|_2^2
  \end{equation}
  is uniformly equivalent to the fixed norm on $E = H^{1/2}\times H^{-1/2}$,
  interpreting the negative entry by completion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  On the common form domain $H^1$, $B \le A_\sigma \le cB$ and all three
  operators are at least one. For positive self-adjoint $T,S$ with common form
  domain, $T \le S$ implies $(T+t)^{-1} \ge (S+t)^{-1}$ for $t > 0$. Indeed,
  \[
    \langle f,(T+t)^{-1}f\rangle
    =\sup_u\{2\operatorname{Re}\langle f,u\rangle
    -q_T(u)-t\|u\|_2^2\};
  \]
  completing the square in the closed form norm proves the identity, and
  comparison of the suprema proves the order. Consequently, $I-t(T+t)^{-1} \le
  I-t(S+t)^{-1}$. The scalar identity
  \[
    \sqrt x=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty
    t^{-1/2}\frac{x}{x+t}\ud t
  \]
  follows by $t = xr^2$ and $\int_0^\infty(1+r^2)^{-1}\ud r = \pi/2$. Spectral
  integration and positive Tonelli, first over finite intervals and then by
  monotone convergence, give $T^{1/2} \le S^{1/2}$ as forms, including domain
  inclusions. Apply this to $B \le A_\sigma \le cB$ to obtain
  \eqref{E:compact-quarter-power} and equality of the quarter-power domains with
  $H^{1/2}$.

  Under the $L^2$ pairing the dual norm of $\|A_\sigma^{1/4}u\|_2$ at $z \in
  L^2$ is $\|A_\sigma^{-1/4}z\|_2$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives the upper bound, and a
  scalar multiple of $A_\sigma^{-1/2}z$ attains it. Completion
  and~\eqref{E:compact-quarter-power} identify this negative space with
  $H^{-1/2}$, uniformly in $\sigma$. Adding the squared norms proves the claim.
  This proves Lemma~\ref{L:compact-fractional-phase}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The complementary wave on $E$]\label{P:compact-E-wave}
  Put
  \[
    e_\sigma^E=(p_\sigma,\lambda_\sigma p_\sigma),\qquad
    \ell_\sigma^E(u,z)
    =\frac{\langle p_\sigma,u\rangle+
    \langle p_\sigma,z\rangle/\lambda_\sigma}{2},
    \qquad \Pi_\sigma^E=e_\sigma^E\ell_\sigma^E.
  \]
  These are bounded real objects, continuous in their respective norms, with
  $\ell_\sigma^E(e_\sigma^E) = 1$, and
  \begin{align}
    P_\sigma^E(t)e_\sigma^E
    &=e^{\lambda_\sigma t}e_\sigma^E,\qquad
    \ell_\sigma^EP_\sigma^E(t)
    =e^{\lambda_\sigma t}\ell_\sigma^E,\label{E:compact-E-eigen}\\
    \|P_\sigma^E(t)(I-\Pi_\sigma^E)\|_{\mathcal{B}(E)}
    &\le C(1+t)e^{\mu_\sigma t},\qquad t\ge0.
    \label{E:compact-E-complement}
  \end{align}
  The constant is uniform over $\sigma \in I$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Define real spectral functions $C_t(h),S_t(h)$ by $\cos(t\sqrt h),\sin(t\sqrt
  h)/\sqrt h$ for $h > 0$, by $1,t$ at zero, and by
  $\cosh(t\sqrt{-h}),\sinh(t\sqrt{-h})/\sqrt{-h}$ for $h < 0$. The spectral wave
  matrix is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-spectral-wave}
    \begin{pmatrix}
      C_t(H_\sigma)&S_t(H_\sigma)\\
      -H_\sigma S_t(H_\sigma)&C_t(H_\sigma)
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \end{equation}
  Real potentials, their resolvents and spectral resolutions commute with
  conjugation. All these real functions and positive $A_\sigma$ powers preserve
  the real phases.

  The spatial projection $Q_\sigma^{\mathrm{sp}} = I-|p_\sigma\rangle\langle
  p_\sigma|$ commutes with $H_\sigma,A_\sigma$ and is contractive on both
  entries of~\eqref{E:compact-spectral-phase-norm}. On its support $h \ge E_2 =
  -\mu_\sigma^2$, the phase isometry gives scalar matrix entries
  \[
    C_t(h),\quad \sqrt{h+c}\,S_t(h),\quad
    -hS_t(h)/\sqrt{h+c},\quad C_t(h).
  \]
  For $h \ge 0$, use $|S_t(h)| \le \min(t,h^{-1/2})$ and split $h \le 1$ from $h
  \ge 1$; the lower off-diagonal entry is at most one. For $-\mu_\sigma^2 \le h
  < 0$, use $|C_t(h)| \le e^{\mu_\sigma t}$ and $|S_t(h)| \le t e^{\mu_\sigma
  t}$, with $|h| \le M$ and $1 \le h+c \le c$. Thus, the complementary spatial
  matrix has norm at most $C(1+t)e^{\mu_\sigma t}$, including $\mu_\sigma = 0$.

  The two spatial ground branches are $(p_\sigma,\pm\lambda_\sigma p_\sigma)$.
  Besides $\Pi_\sigma^E$, their projections include the decaying branch
  \[
    (p_\sigma,-\lambda_\sigma p_\sigma)
    \frac{\langle p_\sigma,u\rangle
    -\langle p_\sigma,z\rangle/\lambda_\sigma}{2}.
  \]
  Their sum is the spatial ground projection on both phase entries. Both
  projection norms are uniformly bounded in the spectral phase norm because
  $\sqrt\varepsilon \le \lambda_\sigma \le \sqrt M$ and $1 \le
  c-\lambda_\sigma^2 \le c$. The decaying branch has factor $e^{-\lambda_\sigma
  t}$ and remains in $I-\Pi_\sigma^E$. The fractional norm equivalence now
  proves the stated complement estimate for the spectral matrix.

  We identify this matrix with the physical flow. The same scalar bounds at
  graph levels $(A_\sigma u,A_\sigma^{1/2}z)$ give continuous solutions in
  $H^2\times H^1$. These graph norms are uniformly equivalent to the Sobolev
  norms: the $H^1$ case was proved above, and $A_\sigma-B = M-V_\sigma$ is
  bounded by $M$ while $A_\sigma,B \ge 1$. Spectral cutoffs give $u_t = z$ in
  $H^1$ and $z_t = -H_\sigma u$ in $L^2$, with continuous derivatives there. The
  free Fourier group on $H^1\times L^2$ has, after its Bessel isometry, the same
  bounded weighted entries and is strongly continuous by dominated Fourier
  convergence. Its generator is $(z,\Delta u)$ on $H^2\times H^1$, as the
  Fourier multipliers show. For compact smooth initial data, differentiating the
  free interaction picture at these graph levels gives Duhamel with force
  $(0,V_\sigma u)$. The identity also holds in $E$. Multiplication by $V_\sigma$
  maps $H^{1/2}$ into $L^2$ and then $H^{-1/2}$ with bound $M$, so Picard
  uniqueness identifies the spectral solution with the ordinary $E$ flow on
  these data. Density within $E$ and finite-horizon boundedness extend equality
  to all $E$. No density of $E$ in $F$ is used. Finally, the ground branches
  give~\eqref{E:compact-E-eigen}; their pairings are continuous because
  $p_\sigma \in H^2$. Continuity and uniform bounds of the displayed vectors and
  duals follow from Proposition~\ref{P:compact-ground-family}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-E-wave}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The growing projection and its complementary wave evolution}

For the constant coordinates write $C(t)(c_1,c_2) = (c_1+tc_2,c_2)$ and
$D_\sigma(c_1,c_2) = (0,V_\sigma c_1) \in E$.
The data $e_0 = (0,1,0)$ lie entirely in these constant coordinates, outside
$iE$. So we follow the flow on all of $F$, where $D_\sigma$ couples the
constants to $E$.

\begin{proposition}[The growing projection on the wave phase space]
  \label{P:compact-F-projection}
  The physical flow satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-F-block}
    P_\sigma(t)(e,c)
    =\left(P_\sigma^E(t)e+
    \int_0^tP_\sigma^E(t-r)D_\sigma C(r)c\ud r,\ C(t)c\right).
  \end{equation}
  Set
  \begin{align}
    f_\sigma&=\frac{\int V_\sigma p_\sigma}{2\lambda_\sigma}>0,
    &\ell_\sigma^C(c)&=
    f_\sigma\left(\frac{c_1}{\lambda_\sigma}
    +\frac{c_2}{\lambda_\sigma^2}\right),
    \label{E:compact-constant-dual}\\
    e_\sigma&=ie_\sigma^E,
    &\ell_\sigma(e,c)&=\ell_\sigma^E(e)+\ell_\sigma^C(c),\qquad
    \Pi_\sigma=e_\sigma\ell_\sigma,\quad Q_\sigma=I-\Pi_\sigma.
    \label{E:compact-F-projection}
  \end{align}
  These vectors, duals and rank-one projections are norm-continuous and
  uniformly bounded, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-F-eigen}
    \ell_\sigma(e_\sigma)=1,\qquad
    P_\sigma(t)\Pi_\sigma=\Pi_\sigma P_\sigma(t)
    =e^{\lambda_\sigma t}\Pi_\sigma.
  \end{equation}
  For every fixed nonnegative smooth compact $\phi$ of integral one,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-positive-margins}
    \inf_I\ell_\sigma(e_0)>0,\qquad
    \inf_I O_\phi(e_\sigma)>0.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The constant coordinates obey $C(t)$ because the potential force has range in
  $iE$. To justify variation of constants on the $E$ component, let $g$ be a
  continuous $E$ force and substitute the ordinary free-Duhamel equation for
  $P_\sigma^E(t-r)g(r)$ in its time integral. With $B_\sigma^E(u,z) =
  (0,V_\sigma u)$, strong $E$-valued Fubini gives
  \begin{align*}
    &\int_0^t\int_r^t S(t-s)B_\sigma^E
    P_\sigma^E(s-r)g(r)\ud s\ud r\\
    &\qquad=\int_0^tS(t-s)B_\sigma^E
    \left[\int_0^sP_\sigma^E(s-r)g(r)\ud r\right]\ud s.
  \end{align*}
  All norms are bounded on the finite triangle, so this exchange is absolute in
  $E$. Thus, $Y(t) = P_\sigma^E(t)e+\int_0^tP_\sigma^E(t-r)g(r)\ud r$ solves
  free Duhamel with force $B_\sigma^EY+g$. Take $g(r) = D_\sigma C(r)c$ and
  adjoin the constant block. This is the same $F$ Duhamel equation as the
  ordinary flow; uniqueness proves~\eqref{E:compact-F-block}. Its finite-horizon
  bounds are uniform since the family has bounded $L^\infty$ and $L^2$ norms on
  one compact support.

  The integral defining $f_\sigma$ is finite by $L^2$ Cauchy--Schwarz and
  positive because $V_\sigma$ is nonzero by the common trial and $p_\sigma > 0$
  a.e. Its coefficients are continuous and uniformly bounded by the $H^2$
  continuity and positive lower bound for the rate; no $p_\sigma \in L^1$
  assumption is used. Directly,
  \begin{align*}
    \ell_\sigma^ED_\sigma C(r)c&=f_\sigma(c_1+rc_2),\\
    \ell_\sigma^C(c)
    &=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda_\sigma r}
    f_\sigma(c_1+rc_2)\ud r,\\
    \ell_\sigma^C(C(t)c)
    &=\int_t^\infty e^{\lambda_\sigma(t-r)}
    f_\sigma(c_1+rc_2)\ud r.
  \end{align*}
  These integrals converge absolutely. Applying $\ell_\sigma$
  to~\eqref{E:compact-F-block}, the finite integral and this tail combine to
  give $\ell_\sigma P_\sigma(t) = e^{\lambda_\sigma t}\ell_\sigma$. The ground
  mode also gives $P_\sigma(t)e_\sigma = e^{\lambda_\sigma t}e_\sigma$ and
  $\ell_\sigma(e_\sigma) = 1$, proving~\eqref{E:compact-F-eigen}. Bounded
  bilinear multiplication and the preceding continuous coefficients prove norm
  continuity of all objects. Finally, $\ell_\sigma(e_0) =
  f_\sigma/\lambda_\sigma > 0$ and $O_\phi(e_\sigma) = \int\phi p_\sigma > 0$.
  Both are continuous on compact $I$, proving
  \eqref{E:compact-positive-margins}. Positivity is in integrals, without a
  point trace or a one-dimensional ODE. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-F-projection}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{P:compact-F-projection} isolates the growing part
$\Pi_\sigma$. It remains to bound the flow on the complement $Q_\sigma$. The
proof of Lemma~\ref{L:raw-block-indexed-cone} uses the normalized form of this
bound.

\begin{proposition}[Complementary wave evolution and parameter dependence]
  \label{P:compact-F-complement}
  Uniformly over $\sigma$ and $t \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-F-complement}
    \|P_\sigma(t)Q_\sigma\|_{\mathcal{B}(F)}
    \le C(1+t)^3e^{\mu_\sigma t}.
  \end{equation}
  For every fixed $0 < \eta < d_0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-normalized-complement}
    \|e^{-\lambda_\sigma t}P_\sigma(t)Q_\sigma\|
    \le C_\eta e^{-\eta t}.
  \end{equation}
  For every finite $T_0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-parameter-flow}
    \sup_{0\le t\le T_0}\|P_\sigma(t)-P_\tau(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(F)}
    \le C_{T_0}
    \big(\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty+\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_2\big).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Write $Q_\sigma^E = I-\Pi_\sigma^E$. Split $D_\sigma$ into its growing and
  complementary parts in~\eqref{E:compact-F-block} and use the two tail
  integrals in the preceding proof. The exact $E$ component of
  $P_\sigma(t)Q_\sigma(e,c)$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-complement-forcing}
    P_\sigma^E(t)Q_\sigma^Ee
    +\int_0^tP_\sigma^E(t-r)Q_\sigma^ED_\sigma C(r)c\ud r
    -e_\sigma^E\ell_\sigma^C(C(t)c),
  \end{equation}
  and the constant component is $C(t)c$. The final subtraction retains the
  growing response to constants in the projection. Since $\|Q_\sigma^ED_\sigma
  C(r)\| \le C(1+r)$, the integral in~\eqref{E:compact-complement-forcing} has
  norm at most
  \[
    Ce^{\mu_\sigma t}|c|
    \int_0^t(1+t-r)(1+r)\ud r
    =Ce^{\mu_\sigma t}(t+t^2+t^3/6)|c|.
  \]
  The first term is bounded by $C(1+t)e^{\mu_\sigma t}\|e\|_E$; the final term
  and constant component by $C(1+t)|c|$. Since $\mu_\sigma \ge 0$ this proves
  \eqref{E:compact-F-complement}. The boundedness of $(1+t)^3e^{-(d_0-\eta)t}$
  proves \eqref{E:compact-normalized-complement}. In particular, the
  constant-velocity polynomial is included in this estimate.

  Put $\mathcal{B}_\sigma = iB_F(V_\sigma)$ and $d_{\sigma,\tau} =
  \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty+ \|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_2$. The bound for $B_F$ gives
  $\|\mathcal{B}_\sigma-\mathcal{B}_\tau\| \le d_{\sigma,\tau}$. Subtract the
  two free-Duhamel equations on states. Their difference is the sum of the
  integral with $S_F(t-r)\mathcal{B}_\sigma(P_\sigma(r)-P_\tau(r))$ and that
  with $S_F(t-r)(\mathcal{B}_\sigma-\mathcal{B}_\tau)P_\tau(r)$. The uniform
  finite-horizon bounds and scalar Gronwall prove
  \eqref{E:compact-parameter-flow}. The operator norm is measurable:
  separability of $F$ expresses it as the supremum over a countable dense set of
  unit states, each with continuous image. Hence, Gronwall applies without a
  $\mathcal{B}(F)$-valued derivative or operator-norm continuity of the free
  group. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:compact-F-complement}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The original nonnegative control and a common trial}

So far $V_\sigma$ was any continuous family
satisfying~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial}. We now produce such a family from a
nonnegative test $\Psi$ and the original covariance $\Gamma$, in two steps.

\begin{lemma}[The original mean and variance]\label{L:compact-raw-profile}
  Let $\Gamma \ne 0$ satisfy Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, let $a = \alpha_0$, and
  let real nonnegative nonzero $\Psi \in C_c^\infty((0,1)\times\R^d)$ be
  arbitrary, without a separability assumption. Define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-raw-mean}
    h_\sigma(x)=\int_0^1|\sigma-u|^{-a}
    (\Gamma*\Psi(u,\cdot))(x)\ud u,\qquad
    \overline{q}(x)=\int_0^1\Psi(u,x)\ud u,\qquad Z=W(\Psi).
  \end{equation}
  Then $h$ is smooth in time and space on compact sets, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-raw-cost}
    0<v_\Psi:=\E Z^2
    =\int_0^1\int\Psi(\sigma,x)h_\sigma(x)\ud x\ud\sigma<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  Against every smooth compact positive-time test $F_0$,
  \[
    \E[W(\Psi)W(F_0)]=\int F_0(s,x)h_s(x)\ud x\ud s.
  \]
  Moreover, on $I$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:compact-positive-mean-floor}
    h_\sigma\ge\Gamma*\overline{q},\qquad
    v_\Psi\ge Q(\overline{q})
    :=\int\Gamma(\ud z)\int\overline{q}(x)\overline{q}(x-z)\ud x>0.
  \end{equation}
  For every fixed mask, $V_\sigma = m_Rh_\sigma$ is a smooth compact family,
  Lipschitz in $\sigma$ in $L^\infty$ and $L^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Choose $A \ge 1$ containing the spatial support of $\Psi$ in $B_A$. The
  original covariance identity and absolute Fubini give the stated mean pairing:
  the two compact tests restrict spatial differences to a compact set of finite
  $\Gamma$ mass, and the time singularity is locally integrable since $a < 1$.
  This identifies the linear covariance mean without evaluating noise at a
  point. The same calculation gives~\eqref{E:compact-raw-cost}. Its spatial
  integrand is bounded in integral by
  $\|\Psi\|_\infty^2|B_A|\Gamma(\overline{B}_{2A})$, and
  $\int_0^1\int_0^1|\sigma-u|^{-a}\ud u\ud\sigma = 2/((1-a)(2-a))$, proving
  finiteness.

  Nonnegative nonzero smooth $\Psi$ has positive integral, so $\overline{q} \ne
  0$. Nonzero $\Gamma$ charges some ball, and homogeneity gives $\Gamma(B_1) =
  r^{\alpha-d}\Gamma(B_r) > 0$. Thus, every origin-centered ball $B_r$, $r > 0$,
  has positive finite mass. The continuous compact autocorrelation
  $\int\overline{q}(x)\overline{q}(x-z)\ud x$ is positive at zero and bounded
  below on some ball. This proves $0 < Q(\overline{q}) < \infty$. Since
  $|\sigma-u|^{-a} \ge 1$ on the unit time square, positive Tonelli gives both
  inequalities in~\eqref{E:compact-positive-mean-floor}.

  Extend $\Psi$ by zero in time and write
  \[
    h_s(x)=\int_\R |r|^{-a}
    \int\Gamma(\ud z)\Psi(s-r,x-z)\ud r.
  \]
  On compact $s,x$ sets the relevant $r,z$ range over fixed compact sets;
  $|r|^{-a}$ is integrable there. Every derivative falls on the smooth test, not
  on the singular time kernel. Dominated differentiation gives smoothness and
  continuity of all derivatives on compacts. Multiplication by $m_R$ now gives
  the claimed compact family and its fixed-$R$ Lipschitz bounds. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:compact-raw-profile}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:compact-raw-profile} gives a smooth compact family for every
fixed mask, but not yet the common negative
trial~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial}.

\begin{proposition}[Binding with the original control and cost fixed]
  \label{P:compact-original-common-trial}
  For every $\Psi$ in Lemma~\ref{L:compact-raw-profile}, a sufficiently large
  finite mask $m_R$ gives $V_\sigma = m_Rh_\sigma$ a common negative
  trial~\eqref{E:compact-common-trial}. Thus, all preceding ground, projection,
  positive-margin and complementary-wave conclusions apply. The rate is
  Lipschitz on $I$ and has $\lambda_0 > 0$. The original test $\Psi$, original
  variable $Z$ and variance $v_\Psi$ are unchanged by this choice. The mask can
  additionally cover any fixed compact variational test supports.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Choose a real unit $L^2$ function $\psi \in C_c^\infty(B_2)$ with $\psi \ge
  c_0 > 0$ on $B_1$, and put $\psi_L(x) = L^{-d/2}\psi(x/L)$, $D_\psi =
  \|\nabla\psi\|_2^2$, where $L \ge \max(1,2A)$. Choose the even mask with $R
  \ge 2L$, so $V_\sigma = h_\sigma$ on $\operatorname{supp}\psi_L$. This is the
  allowed real mask $0 \le m_R \le 1$, equal to one on $B_R$ and with support in
  $\overline{B}_{2R}$. Positive Tonelli
  and~\eqref{E:compact-positive-mean-floor} give
  \begin{align}
    \int V_\sigma\psi_L^2
    &\ge c_0^2L^{-d}\int_{B_L}(\Gamma*\overline{q})(x)\ud x\nonumber\\
    &=c_0^2L^{-d}\int\overline{q}(y)\Gamma(B_L-y)\ud y\nonumber\\
    &\ge c_0^2L^{-d}\Gamma(B_{L/2})\int\overline{q}
    =C_\Gamma L^{-\alpha},\label{E:compact-trial-potential}\\
    C_\Gamma&=c_0^22^{\alpha-d}\Gamma(B_1)\int\overline{q}>0.\nonumber
  \end{align}
  Here $B_{L/2} \subset B_L-y$ for $|y| \le A$ and $L \ge 2A$. All covariance
  masses in the argument are compact and finite. The kinetic energy is $D_\psi
  L^{-2}$. Since $\alpha < 2$, choose $L$ large enough that $\varepsilon_R: =
  C_\Gamma L^{-\alpha}-D_\psi L^{-2} > 0$. Then
  \[
    \|\nabla\psi_L\|_2^2-\int V_\sigma\psi_L^2
    \le-\varepsilon_R\quad\hbox{for every }\sigma\in I.
  \]
  Fix this finite $R$ before invoking Proposition~\ref{P:compact-ground-family}.
  Its level bound and the mean Lipschitz estimate give
  \[
    |\lambda_\sigma-\lambda_\tau|
    \le\frac{\|V_\sigma-V_\tau\|_\infty}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon_R}}
    \le C_R|\sigma-\tau|.
  \]
  The positive mean integrates the entire time test, so $\lambda_0 > 0$ even
  though $\Psi(0,\cdot) = 0$. Only the trial and mask have grown; the original
  test, its variable and~\eqref{E:compact-raw-cost} were fixed first. Enlarging
  $R$ to contain additional fixed compact test supports preserves the trial and
  gives equality of masked and unmasked potential energies on those supports.
  This includes singular invariant-direction covariances and the one-dimensional
  white case. It is not an all-strength binding assertion for one fixed compact
  potential. Masks are fixed before source or time asymptotics; a later supremum
  over controls must follow their individual bounds. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:compact-original-common-trial}.
\end{proof}
