\section{Upper bounds for spatial exponents at most one}\label{S:d1-upper}

The upper bound at scale $t^\kappa$ is proved for $0 < \alpha \le 1$ in
dimensions one, two and three under the standing assumptions. The separate
one-dimensional Riesz estimates retain their sharper constants and white
endpoint. Degree-one covariance in dimensions two and three does not mean full
spatial white noise. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper} treats the higher exponents
by a separate argument. The intrinsic constant is identified  in
Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.

We retain the two-chain expansion of Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, but estimate its
spatial and temporal factors in a different order. The spatial estimate keeps a
power of the number of pairs. After taking a common spatial maximum, we sum all
temporal pairings before using the ordered time domains.

Throughout this section $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$. We first work in dimension one with
either of the spatial covariances
\begin{equation}\label{E:d1-covariances}
  \begin{cases}
    \gamma(y)=c_\alpha|y|^{-\alpha},&0<\alpha<1,\quad c_\alpha>0,\\
    \gamma=c_\delta\delta_0,&\alpha=1,\quad c_\delta>0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Put $b = 2-\alpha$, $q = 3-\alpha$, $\beta = 4-\alpha-\alpha_0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{E:d1-constants}
  Z=\frac{2}{(1-\alpha_0)(2-\alpha_0)},\qquad
  K_{\rm sp}=
  \begin{cases}
    \displaystyle\frac{c_\alpha 2^{2-\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-\alpha}}
    {(2-\alpha)^{2-\alpha}},&0<\alpha<1,\\[2mm]
    c_\delta,&\alpha=1.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
These parameters satisfy Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, so the fixed-order
integrability and mixed expansion already established apply.

\subsection{A spatial bound retaining the ordered increments}

For $m+n = 2k$, label the two chains in block order as in~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram}. For each vertex $l$ write
$h_l = s_l-s_{p(l)} > 0$ and $y_l = x_l-x_{p(l)}$, with a separate root zero
in each chain. Thus, $x_l$ is the sum of increments in its own chain
up to label $l$. The spatial change of variables has determinant one.
In this section $x_l$ denotes a spatial position and $y_l$ its increment; this local
notation differs from the position variables $y_l$
in~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram}. The terminal evaluation point of $u(t,x)$
remains denoted by $x$.
Let $p_\epsilon$ be the centered Brownian Gaussian density of variance
$\epsilon$ and mass one, and set
$\gamma_\epsilon = p_\epsilon*\gamma$. Allow a separate positive
$\epsilon_e$ for every pair $e \in D$ and define
\[
  S_{D,\boldsymbol\epsilon}(h)
  =\int_{\R^{2k}}\prod_{l=1}^{2k}G(h_l,y_l)
  \prod_{(i,j)\in D}\gamma_{\epsilon_{i,j}}(x_j-x_i)\ud y,
  \qquad G(h,y)=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{|y|<h\}}.
\]
This is an ordinary nonnegative integral; no pointwise delta value is used.

\LeanMarker{L:d1-spatial}
\begin{lemma}[Spatial estimate]\label{L:d1-spatial}
  Suppose~\eqref{E:d1-covariances} holds, $k \ge 1$, and $D$ is any pairing of
  the two chains. Orient every pair $(i,j)$ by $i < j$ and let $O_D$ and $C_D$
  be its opening and closing label sets. Then, uniformly in
  $\boldsymbol\epsilon$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-spatial-product}
    S_{D,\boldsymbol\epsilon}(h)\le
    \begin{cases}
      \displaystyle\left(\frac{c_\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)^k
      \prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{1-\alpha},
      &0<\alpha<1,\\[2mm]
      \displaystyle\left(\frac{c_\delta}{2}\right)^k
      \prod_{i\in O_D}h_i,&\alpha=1.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  In particular, if $\sum_{l=1}^{2k}h_l \le 2t$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-spatial-max}
    S_{D,\boldsymbol\epsilon}(h)
    \le K_{\rm sp}^{\,k}\frac{t^{bk}}{k^{bk}}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We first record the convolution bounds. For $0 < \alpha < 1$, the
  integral of the even decreasing function $|y|^{-\alpha}$ over an
  interval of fixed length is maximal when the interval is centered
  at zero. Therefore,
  \[
    \sup_z\int_\R G(h,y)c_\alpha|y+z|^{-\alpha}\ud y
    =\frac{c_\alpha h^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.
  \]
  By Tonelli's theorem, the integral with $\gamma_\epsilon$ equals the
  $p_\epsilon(w)\ud w$ average of the unsmoothed integrals with shifts $z-w$.
  Its nonnegative mass-one average is therefore bounded by the same supremum. In
  the white case, $\gamma_\epsilon = c_\delta p_\epsilon$, so
  \[
    \sup_z\int_\R G(h,y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)\ud y
    \le\frac{c_\delta}{2}.
  \]
  Both estimates are uniform in $\epsilon > 0$.

  Now integrate \emph{all} spatial increments in descending label order
  $2k,2k-1,\ldots,1$. Before processing label $l$, the remaining covariance
  factors are exactly those pairs whose closing labels are at most $l$; the
  bounds from the removed pairs depend only on $h$. If $l \in C_D$, its pair is
  $(i,l)$ with $i < l$ and $x_l-x_i = y_l+z$, where $z$ is independent of $y_l$.
  Every other remaining pair has both endpoints smaller than $l$, so its two
  positions are independent of $y_l$: positions involve only earlier increments
  in their own block chain. Thus, the only covariance involving $y_l$ is its own
  pair, and the convolution bound removes it. If $l \in O_D$, its partner has a
  larger label and its covariance factor has already been removed. All remaining
  covariances have endpoints smaller than $l$, so integration of $y_l$ gives
  only $\int G(h_l,y_l)\ud y_l = h_l$. This also preserves the stated invariant
  for the next label. Tonelli's theorem and the nonnegativity of all factors
  justify each step. There are $k$ labels of each type,
  proving~\eqref{E:d1-spatial-product} for every split and pairing.

  For Riesz covariance, weighted arithmetic--geometric mean gives
  \[
    \prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{1-\alpha}
    \le\left(\frac{2t}{bk}\right)^{bk}
    (1-\alpha)^{(1-\alpha)k}.
  \]
  Indeed, the weights are one at the $k$ openings and $1-\alpha$ at the $k$
  closings, with total $bk$, and the sum of the increments is at most $2t$.
  Multiplication by $(c_\alpha/(1-\alpha))^k$ gives~\eqref{E:d1-spatial-max}
  with the stated $K_{\rm sp}$. In the white case, $\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i \le
  (2t/k)^k$; multiplying by $(c_\delta/2)^k$ gives the same conclusion with $b =
  1$ and $K_{\rm sp} = c_\delta$. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Summing temporal pairings before using time ordering}

Define the total-order coefficients
\[
  A_k(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\E[X_m(t,x)X_{2k-m}(t,x)],\qquad A_0(t)=1.
\]
They do not depend on $x$, by spatial stationarity. By
Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, $\E u^2(t,x) = \sum_{k\ge0}A_k(t)$.

\LeanMarker{P:d1-upper}
\begin{proposition}[One-dimensional upper bound]\label{P:d1-upper}
  Under~\eqref{E:d1-covariances}, for every $k \ge 1$ and $t > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-coefficient}
    A_k(t)\le
    \frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp})^k t^{\beta k}}{k!\,k^{bk}}
    \le\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp})^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-upper}
    \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp(C_{\rm dir}t^\kappa),\qquad
    C_{\rm dir}=q(2ZK_{\rm sp})^{1/q},\quad \kappa=\frac{\beta}{q}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We first work with the Gaussian spatial smoothing and temporal truncation
  in~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.9), (2.11), (2.23)--(2.24)}. Let
  $X_n^{\epsilon,\delta}$ be the smoothed Stratonovich term, using common
  parameters in every factor. Wick's formula expresses each mixed expectation of
  total order $2k$ as a sum over $D \in \Pi_k$. After the chain reversal in
  Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, its spatial factor is the one in
  Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}; its temporal factors are nonnegative and bounded by
  $|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$. For times in $[0,t]^m_ < \times[0,t]^{2k-m}_ <$, the
  increments sum to the two terminal times and therefore to at most $2t$.
  Extract~\eqref{E:d1-spatial-max}, which is independent of the time vector, the
  split, the pairing, and the smoothing parameters. The remaining temporal sum
  is
  \[
    F_k(s_1,\ldots,s_{2k})
    =\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}\prod_{(i,j)\in D}|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}.
  \]
  Every permutation of the $2k$ labels bijects $\Pi_k$, so $F_k$ is fully
  symmetric. Moreover,
  \[
    \int_{[0,t]^2}|s-r|^{-\alpha_0}\ud s\ud r
    =Zt^{2-\alpha_0},\qquad
    \int_{[0,t]^{2k}}F_k(s)\ud s
    =P_k(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k<\infty.
  \]
  The second equality uses the disjoint endpoints of every pairing. Partitioning
  the time cube according to the order within each block now gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-time-symmetry}
    \int_{[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^{2k-m}_<}F_k(s)\ud s
    =\frac{P_k(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k}{m!(2k-m)!}.
  \end{equation}
  Ties have measure zero. Crucially, this symmetry is applied to the \emph{sum}
  of pairings after taking the common spatial bound, not to an individual
  temporally weighted diagram.

  The number of pairings obeys $P_0 = 1$ and $P_k = (2k-1)P_{k-1}$, by choosing
  the partner of the smallest label. Hence, $P_k = (2k)!/(2^kk!)$. The binomial
  identity gives
  \[
    \sum_{m=0}^{2k}\frac1{m!(2k-m)!}=\frac{2^{2k}}{(2k)!}.
  \]
  Multiplying~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry} by the spatial bound and summing over
  $m$ yields
  \[
    \sum_{m=0}^{2k}\E[X_m^{\epsilon,\delta}X_{2k-m}^{\epsilon,\delta}]
    \le K_{\rm sp}^{\,k}\frac{t^{bk}}{k^{bk}}
    \frac{2^{2k}}{(2k)!}
    \frac{(2k)!}{2^kk!}(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k
    =\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp})^kt^{\beta k}}{k!\,k^{bk}}.
  \]
  The same proof covers empty chains, with $X_0^{\epsilon,\delta} = 1$.

  By Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}, each smoothed factor converges in $L^2$.
  Products converge in $L^1$ by Cauchy--Schwarz; for fixed $k$ the sum over
  splits is finite. The bound is uniform in $\epsilon,\delta$, so it passes to
  $A_k(t)$. Since $k! \le k^k$ and $b > 0$, we have $k!\,k^{bk} \ge (k!)^{b+1} =
  (k!)^q$, proving both inequalities in~\eqref{E:d1-coefficient}.

  Finally, for $z \ge 0$ and $q \ge 1$,
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^k}{(k!)^q}
    =\sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    \le\left(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    =\exp(qz^{1/q}).
  \]
  Apply this inequality with $z = 2ZK_{\rm sp}t^\beta$, adding the separately
  defined term $A_0 = 1$. Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} then
  gives~\eqref{E:d1-upper}. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectral bounds for $0 < \alpha \le 1$ in all three dimensions}

We now retain Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} with $d \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $0 < \alpha
\le 1$. The covariance is nonnegative and nonnegative definite as in
Section~\ref{S:model}; when it is a distribution, both these properties and its
homogeneity are understood distributionally. Let $\mu$ be its positive Radon
spectral measure in the convention $\gamma(x) = \int e^{i\xi\cdot
x}\mu(\ud\xi)$, and put $V = \mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le1\})$. No isotropy or spectral
density is assumed. Write $G_h$ for the positive wave-kernel measure of mass
$h$, and $G_{h,\eta} = p_\eta*G_h$ for $\eta > 0$; set $G_{h,0} = G_h$. The
measure notation includes the sphere kernel in dimension three. The mixed-moment
definition of $A_k(t)$ is now applied to the present $d$-dimensional solution.

\LeanMarker{L:low-alpha-convolution}
\begin{lemma}[Low-$\alpha$ convolution bound]\label{L:low-alpha-convolution}
  Under the preceding assumptions with $0 < \alpha < 1$, for every $R,h > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-radial}
    \mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le R\})=VR^\alpha,\qquad
    \int_{\R^d}\min\{h,|\xi|^{-1}\}\mu(\ud\xi)
    =\frac{V}{1-\alpha}h^{1-\alpha}.
  \end{equation}
  For $\epsilon > 0$ and $\eta \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-convolution}
    0\le\int_{\R^d}G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    \le\frac{V}{1-\alpha}h^{1-\alpha},
    \qquad z\in\R^d.
  \end{equation}
  The estimate is uniform in both smoothing parameters and in $z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The spectral measure of $\gamma(c\,\cdot)$ is the pushforward of $\mu$ under
  $\xi\mapsto c\xi$. Homogeneity and uniqueness of the Fourier transform on
  tempered distributions therefore give $\mu(A/c) = c^{-\alpha}\mu(A)$ for every
  Borel set $A$. Equivalently, $\mu(cA) = c^\alpha\mu(A)$. This proves the
  ball-mass formula. Since $\mu$ is Radon, $V < \infty$, and continuity from
  above gives $\mu(\{0\}) = \lim_{R\downarrow0}VR^\alpha = 0$. By the layer-cake
  identity and Tonelli's theorem,
  \[
    \int_{\R^d}\min\{h,|\xi|^{-1}\}\mu(\ud\xi)
    =\int_0^h\mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le u^{-1}\})\ud u
    =V\int_0^h u^{-\alpha}\ud u.
  \]
  The endpoint of each indicator has no effect on its $u$ integral. Since
  $\alpha < 1$, the last expression is the finite quantity
  in~\eqref{E:low-alpha-radial}. This computation uses ball masses, not a radial
  density for $\mu$.

  Gaussian weighting gives a finite measure, by the polynomial ball growth of
  $\mu$. Thus, Fourier Fubini is justified for $\gamma_\epsilon$ and the finite
  measure $G_{h,\eta}$. From the source's wave-kernel
  formulas~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.3)--(2.5), p.~7},
  \[
    \widehat{G}_h(\xi)=\frac{\sin(h|\xi|)}{|\xi|},\qquad
    G_h(\R^d)=h,
  \]
  with value $h$ in the Fourier formula at zero. The Fourier multipliers of
  $p_\epsilon$ and $p_\eta$ have absolute value at most one, with the latter
  multiplier equal to one when $\eta = 0$. Consequently,
  \[
    \left|\int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)\right|
    \le\int_{\R^d}\frac{|\sin(h|\xi|)|}{|\xi|}\mu(\ud\xi)
    \le\int_{\R^d}\min\{h,|\xi|^{-1}\}\mu(\ud\xi).
  \]
  Nonnegativity of $\gamma$ as a distribution implies $\gamma_\epsilon \ge 0$,
  since Gaussian translates are nonnegative test functions; $G_{h,\eta}$ is also
  nonnegative. This proves the lower bound in~\eqref{E:low-alpha-convolution}.
  Combining the preceding displays proves its upper bound. No positivity of the
  oscillatory Fourier integrand is asserted. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:low-alpha-convolution}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:alpha-one-convolution}
\begin{lemma}[Degree-one convolution bound]\label{L:alpha-one-convolution}
  Under the preceding covariance assumptions with $\alpha = 1$, for every $h >
  0$, $\epsilon > 0$, $\eta \ge 0$, and $z \in \R^d$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:alpha-one-convolution}
    0\le\int_{\R^d}G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    \le\frac{\pi V}{2}.
  \end{equation}
  No isotropy or angular density is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Fourier uniqueness, applied to the distributional homogeneity as above, gives
  $\mu(cA) = c\mu(A)$ and $\mu(\{0\}) = 0$. Define a finite measure on the unit
  sphere by
  \[
    \sigma(E)=\mu\{\xi:0<|\xi|\le1,\ \xi/|\xi|\in E\}.
  \]
  Its total mass is $V$. Homogeneity implies that the corresponding sector of
  radius $R$ has mass $R\sigma(E)$. Subtracting two such sectors shows that a
  polar rectangle $(r_1,r_2]\times E$ has mass $(r_2-r_1)\sigma(E)$. Uniqueness
  of measures on these generating rectangles therefore gives the polar
  decomposition $\mu(\ud\xi) = \ud r\,\sigma(\ud\theta)$, $r > 0$, $\theta \in
  \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$. The real stationary covariance is even; Fourier uniqueness
  makes $\mu$, and hence $\sigma$, even.

  Use the Brownian Gaussian convention, so the combined covariance and Green
  smoothing multiplier is $e^{-ar^2}$ with $a = (\epsilon+\eta)/2 > 0$. Gaussian
  weighting makes the spectral measure finite. Fourier Fubini and evenness give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:alpha-one-polar}
    \begin{split}
      \int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
      &=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}} I_a(h,\theta\cdot z)\sigma(\ud\theta),\\
      I_a(h,v)&=\int_0^\infty e^{-ar^2}
      \frac{\sin(hr)}r\cos(rv)\ud r.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  The radial integral is absolutely convergent: its integrand is bounded near
  zero and Gaussian-decaying at infinity. Passing from the complex exponential
  to cosine introduces no factor two, since both antipodal directions are
  already included in $\sigma$.

  Let $g_h(v) = \tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{|v|<h\}}$ and let $p_{2a}^{(1)}(v) = (4\pi
  a)^{-1/2}e^{-v^2/(4a)}$ be the one-dimensional Gaussian density. Their Fourier
  transforms are $\sin(hr)/r$ and $e^{-ar^2}$. The one-dimensional inversion
  factor is $1/(2\pi)$ in the source convention, so
  \[
    (g_h*p_{2a}^{(1)})(v)
    =\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R e^{irv}e^{-ar^2}
    \frac{\sin(hr)}r\ud r
    =\frac1\pi I_a(h,v).
  \]
  The sign in the inversion exponential is immaterial because the integrand
  pairs evenly under $r\mapsto-r$. Since $p_{2a}^{(1)}$ has mass one and $0 \le
  g_h \le 1/2$, we obtain $0 \le I_a(h,v) \le \pi/2$. Integrating against
  $\sigma$, whose mass is $V$, proves~\eqref{E:alpha-one-convolution}. This
  retains the oscillation until the one-dimensional integral is evaluated; the
  divergent absolute Fourier envelope at $\alpha = 1$ is not used. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{P:low-alpha-upper}
\begin{proposition}[Upper bound for $\alpha \le 1$]\label{P:low-alpha-upper}
  Suppose that Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} and the standing covariance positivity
  assumptions hold, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, $0 < \alpha \le 1$, and $V > 0$. Define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-constants}
    \begin{split}
      K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen}
      &=\begin{cases}
        \displaystyle\frac{V2^{2-\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-\alpha}}
        {(2-\alpha)^{2-\alpha}},&0<\alpha<1,\\[2mm]
        \pi V,&\alpha=1,
      \end{cases}\\
      C_{\rm dir}^{\rm gen}&=q(2ZK_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^{1/q}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  With the same $A_k$, $b$, $q$, $\beta$, and $Z$ as above, for
  $k \ge 1$ and $t > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-coefficient}
    A_k(t)\le\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^k t^{\beta k}}
    {k!\,k^{bk}}
    \le\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q},
    \qquad
    \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp(C_{\rm dir}^{\rm gen}t^\kappa).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  First smooth the noise as in~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.9), (2.11), (2.23)--(2.24)}.
  In dimension three also use $G_{h,\eta}$ with $\eta > 0$, as in the
  source's pp.~13--14. The increment change of variables is now a
  triangular map on vectors in $\R^d$, still of determinant one.
  The descending-label proof of Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial} depends only
  on which increments enter each position, not on their dimension.
  Its invariant therefore remains valid: at a closing label there
  is exactly one covariance depending on that vector increment, and
  at an opening label its pair has already been removed.
  If $0 < \alpha < 1$, Lemma~\ref{L:low-alpha-convolution} gives the
  closing bound $Mh_l^{1-\alpha}$ with $M = V/(1-\alpha)$.
  At $\alpha = 1$, Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution} gives the
  constant closing bound $M = \pi V/2$. Every opening gives mass
  $h_l$, uniformly in $\eta$ and all noise-smoothing parameters.
  For $\alpha < 1$, the same weighted arithmetic--geometric mean
  calculation gives
  \[
    M^k\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{1-\alpha}
    \le (K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^k\frac{t^{bk}}{k^{bk}}.
  \]
  At $\alpha = 1$, ordinary arithmetic--geometric mean on the $k$ opening
  increments gives $M^k\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i \le (2M)^kt^k/k^k$, which is the same
  bound with $b = 1$ and $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen} = \pi V$. Both cases use
  $\sum_lh_l \le 2t$. For $d = 1,2$ one may use the Green densities directly;
  their opening mass and closing convolution obey these same bounds.

  Replace the nonnegative truncated temporal factors by $|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$
  before taking their pairing sum. The temporal symmetry, cube integral and
  split count in~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry} do not involve the spatial
  dimension. Thus, exactly the multiplication of counts in the proof of
  Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper} gives the first bound
  in~\eqref{E:low-alpha-coefficient} for the regularized moments.

  We spell out the limit in dimension three. Fix every noise-smoothing parameter
  and first let $\eta\downarrow0$. At each time vector, the Gaussian moment
  formula is a finite sum of bounded continuous products of the smoothed
  covariance functions, integrated against products of the finite Green
  measures. These products of measures converge weakly as $\eta\downarrow0$,
  with the same masses $\prod_l h_l$. At fixed noise smoothing the Gaussian
  covariance factors are uniformly bounded, so a constant times $\prod_lh_l$
  dominates on each bounded time simplex. Dominated convergence applies also to
  the covariance between two approximations with different $\eta$ values. The
  resulting common joint covariance limit proves the $L^2$ Cauchy property of
  each fixed-order random integral. This is the inner limit used in the source's
  definition on pp.~13--14. Products of two factors converge in $L^1$ by
  Cauchy--Schwarz, so the moment inequality survives this limit. Next send the
  noise-smoothing parameters to zero using the fixed-order $L^2$ convergence of
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}, and again pass products in $L^1$. In
  dimensions one and two only the latter limit is required. The bounds at both
  stages are uniform.

  We have proved the first coefficient bound for $A_k(t)$. The factorial
  inequality and nonnegative-series argument in Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}
  apply unchanged, with $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen}$ in place of $K_{\rm sp}$ and $A_0
  = 1$. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the spectral estimates]\label{R:low-alpha-scope}
  If $V = 0$, spectral homogeneity gives $\mu(B_R) = 0$ for every $R$, hence
  $\mu = 0$ and $u = 1$; the noise-free conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, all
  angular and dimensional dependence is allowed in $V$. For $0 < \alpha < 1$,
  the example $\gamma(x) = c|x_1|^{-\alpha}$ in dimensions two or three has its
  spectral measure supported on an axis; a full-dimensional density is not
  required. The absolute Fourier envelope diverges at $\alpha = 1$, where the
  separate oscillatory calculation in Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution} is
  essential. The endpoint is not obtained by taking a limit in $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm
  gen}$ for $\alpha < 1$. For $d = 1$ and $\gamma = c_\delta\delta_0$, the
  source convention gives $\mu(\ud\xi) = c_\delta\ud\xi/(2\pi)$ and $V =
  c_\delta/\pi$. Hence, the endpoint constant $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen} = \pi V =
  c_\delta$ agrees exactly with Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}, with no extra
  antipodal factor. In dimensions two and three the endpoint covers degree-one
  homogeneous covariance, not full spatial white noise. No result for $\alpha >
  1$ follows from these estimates.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Checks on the scale, constants and scope}

\LeanMarker{C:d1-first-calibration}
\begin{corollary}[First-moment calibration]\label{C:d1-first-calibration}
  Let $K_*$ be $K_{\rm sp}$ under~\eqref{E:d1-covariances}, or $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm
  gen}$ under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}. Put
  $C_{2,*} = q(2ZK_*)^{1/q}$. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-first-calibration}
    \E u(t,x)\le\exp(C_{1,*}t^\kappa),\qquad
    C_{1,*}=q\left(\frac{ZK_*}{2^{3-\alpha}}\right)^{1/q}
    =\frac{C_{2,*}}{2^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}}.
  \end{equation}
  In either nonzero case $2C_{1,*} < C_{2,*}$. Conditionally on a first-moment
  lower rate at least $L_*$, $2L_* \le 2C_{1,*} < C_{2,*}$, consistently with
  Proposition~\ref{P:Jensen}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  For one chain of length $2k$, the increments sum to at most $t$. The spatial
  estimate in Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}, or in the proof of
  Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}, therefore applies with $t/2$ in place of
  $t$. The temporal pairing sum on one ordered simplex is
  $P_k(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k/(2k)!$. Using the same regularization and limit order
  as in the respective upper-bound proof gives
  \[
    \E X_{2k}(t,x)
    \le\frac{(ZK_*/2^{b+1})^k t^{\beta k}}{k!\,k^{bk}}
    \le\frac{(ZK_*/2^{3-\alpha})^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}.
  \]
  Every odd term has zero expectation. The $L^2$ expansion permits taking
  expectations of partial sums, and the resulting nonnegative even series is
  summed by the scalar inequality in the proof of Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}.
  This gives~\eqref{E:d1-first-calibration}. If the first-moment lower rate is
  at least $L_*$, \eqref{E:d1-first-calibration} implies $L_* \le C_{1,*}$. In
  the stated nonzero cases $K_* > 0$, hence $C_{1,*} > 0$; and
  $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha) > 1$ gives $2C_{1,*} < C_{2,*}$. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:d1-first-calibration}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Checks and comparison with the uniform pairing bound]\label{R:d1-upper-checks}
  The argument preserves the factor $k^{-(2-\alpha)k}$ from the spatial
  increment maximum. Replacing the spatial volume merely by a constant times
  $t^{(2-\alpha)k}$ would lose the factorial power needed
  in~\eqref{E:d1-coefficient}. This proof does not use the
  common-Laplace-parameter norm majorant of Proposition~\ref{P:envelope}, and it
  does not assert a stronger factorial estimate for every individual diagram.
  Instead, the temporal ordering is exploited after summing pairings
  in~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry}. Thus, the scoped G0 obstruction remains valid.
  For unit white covariance, the bound on the total-order-two coefficient is
  $2Z$, whereas the exact value from Lemma~\ref{L:white-test} is $2c_0$; their
  ratio is $2(3-\alpha_0) > 4$. At the static endpoint the exact order-four
  coefficient is $11/540$, while the first bound in~\eqref{E:d1-coefficient}
  gives $1/2$. These are checks of normalization, not evidence of sharp
  constants.
\end{remark}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-06} quantifies the loss avoided here: discarding the
spatial constraints before summation gives the wrong time exponent.


\begin{remark}[Boundary and literature comparisons]\label{R:d1-comparisons}
  As $\alpha_0\downarrow0$, the time exponent tends to $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$,
  the Chen--Hu static exponent cited in Remark~\ref{R:G1-scope}. This is an
  exponent calibration, not an interchange of the static and large-time limits.
  With the Riesz normalization $c_\alpha = (1-\alpha)/2$, the displayed $K_{\rm
  sp}$ tends to one as $\alpha\uparrow1$, matching unit white covariance. For
  comparison only, the Skorohod upper bound of
  Balan--Conus~\cite{balan.conus:16:intermittency}*{Theorem~2.1(b), (8), (10),
  p.~1492} has time exponent $(2H+2-a)/(3-a)$. Setting $H = 1-\alpha_0/2$ and $a
  = \alpha$ yields the same $\kappa$. Their covariance normalization contains
  $H(2H-1)$ and they use a Skorohod mild solution. No constants or solutions are
  identified, and their theorem is not an input to the present proof. The
  current coverage of this section is $0 < \alpha \le 1$ in $d = 1,2,3$,
  including the one-dimensional white case. It does not address $1 < \alpha <
  2-\alpha_0$ in dimensions two and three.
\end{remark}

\section{Higher spatial exponents under a convolution bound}
\label{S:higher-alpha}

The convolution assumption below is additional to homogeneity, and the concrete
applications cover only the stated Riesz ranges. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper}
establishes the full general upper scale by a separate argument. This
alternative route is not an input to Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.

We retain the standing covariance assumptions and take $d \in \{2,3\}$ and $1 <
\alpha < 2-\alpha_0$. Put $\delta = \alpha-1$, $q = 3-\alpha$, and $\beta =
4-\alpha-\alpha_0$. Here $\delta$ is an exponent, not a smoothing parameter.
Write $p_\epsilon$ for the centered Gaussian density with covariance $\epsilon
I$, $\gamma_\epsilon = p_\epsilon*\gamma$, and $G_{h,\eta} = p_\eta*G_h$, with
$G_{h,0} = G_h$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Spatial maximum]\label{H:spatial-maximum}
  There is $M > 0$ such that, for every $h > 0$, $z \in \R^d$, $\epsilon > 0$,
  and $\eta \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:higher-convolution}
    0\le\int_{\R^d}G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    \le M h^{-\delta}.
  \end{equation}
  The same $M$ works for all translations and both Gaussian widths. In
  particular, each pairing edge may have its own positive covariance width and
  each Green factor its own nonnegative smoothing width.
\end{hypothesis}

This uniform maximum is an additional assumption, not a consequence of
homogeneity. No-go~\ref{P:nogo-08} gives admissible covariances for which
the translated wave convolution diverges.


\begin{proposition}[Conditional upper bound]\label{P:higher-transfer}
  Suppose that Hypotheses~\ref{H:homog} and~\ref{H:spatial-maximum} hold in the
  range specified above. Choose and fix
  \[
    \frac1{1-\alpha_0}<r<\frac1\delta,\qquad
    r'=\frac r{r-1},\qquad \theta=\alpha_0r',\qquad
    a=1-\frac{\delta r}{2}.
  \]
  Define the explicit, nonsharp constants
  \begin{equation}\label{E:higher-constants}
    Z_\theta=\frac2{(1-\theta)(2-\theta)},\qquad
    D_a=e^2(e/a)^{2a},\qquad
    K=12M[\Gamma(1-\delta r)D_a]^{1/r}Z_\theta^{1/r'}.
  \end{equation}
  For the total-order coefficients $A_k(t)$ of Section~\ref{S:d1-upper},
  \begin{equation}\label{E:higher-bound}
    A_k(t)\le\frac{K^kt^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}\quad(k\ge1),
    \qquad \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp\{qK^{1/q}t^\kappa\}.
  \end{equation}
  These estimates hold for every $t > 0$ and $x \in \R^d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The interval for $r$ is nonempty because $\delta+\alpha_0 < 1$. Thus, $\delta
  r < 1$, $\theta < 1$, and $1/2 < a < 1$. We use the source's noise
  regularization from Chen~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.9),
  (2.11), (2.23)--(2.24)}, as in Section~\ref{S:d1-upper}. Its nonnegative
  temporal covariance is bounded by $|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$, and its spatial
  covariance on an edge $e$ is $\gamma_{\epsilon_e+\epsilon'_e}$, where
  $\epsilon_e,\epsilon'_e > 0$ are the two endpoint noise widths. Indeed,
  $p_{\epsilon_e}*p_{\epsilon'_e} = p_{\epsilon_e+\epsilon'_e}$; equal widths
  give the source's $\gamma_{2\epsilon}$. Every such positive covariance width
  is covered by Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum}. In dimension three first
  smooth the Green measures as prescribed on pp.~13--14 of that source.

  Fix $m+n = 2k$, $k \ge 1$, and put $\Omega_{m,n} = [0,t]^m_ < \times[0,t]^n_
  <$. Use the two separate roots and increments $h_l = s_l-s_{p(l)}$ of
  Section~\ref{S:d1-upper}. For $D \in \Pi_k$, orient its pairs by increasing
  block label and denote their opening and closing sets by $O_D,C_D$. Descending
  integration of the vector increments gives
  \[
    S_D(h)\le M^k\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{-\delta}
    \le M^k(2t/k)^k H_D(h),\qquad
    H_D(h)=\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{-\delta}.
  \]
  Indeed, before label $l$ is integrated, the remaining covariances have closing
  labels at most $l$. At a closing label exactly its own pair depends on that
  increment, as $\gamma_\epsilon(y_l+z)$; all other endpoints have smaller
  labels. Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum} removes this factor. At an opening
  its pair is already removed and the integral is the Green mass $h_l$. This
  proves the invariant in every dimension and for empty chains. Tonelli applies
  by positivity. Finally, the $k$ opening increments sum to at most $2t$, so
  ordinary arithmetic--geometric mean gives the second inequality. We have not
  applied the low-$\alpha$ conclusion of Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}.

  Set $T_D(s) = \prod_{(i,j)\in D}|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$. H\"older's inequality
  on counting measure on $\Pi_k$ times Lebesgue measure on $\Omega_{m,n}$ gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:higher-holder}
    \sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}H_DT_D\ud s
    \le\left(\sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}H_D^r\ud s\right)^{1/r}
    \left(\sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}T_D^{r'}\ud s\right)^{1/r'}.
  \end{equation}
  Both integrals are finite by the following computations. If the two chains
  contain $k_1,k_2$ closing labels, then $k_1+k_2 = k$ and the simplex change to
  increments has determinant one. The Dirichlet integral, including the unused
  terminal gap with exponent zero, gives
  \[
    \int_{\Omega_{m,n}}H_D^r\ud s
    =\frac{\Gamma(1-\delta r)^k t^{(2-\delta r)k}}
    {\Gamma(1+m-\delta r k_1)\Gamma(1+n-\delta r k_2)}.
  \]
  To see the normalization, in a chain of length $m$ the $k_1$ closing
  increments have Dirichlet parameter $1-\delta r$, the other increments have
  parameter one, and the terminal gap has parameter one. Scaling gives
  $t^{m-\delta r k_1}$. Iterating the beta integral gives the displayed Gamma
  quotient. Empty chains contribute $\Gamma(1) = 1$. The two denominator
  arguments are positive and have average $1+ak$. Log convexity of $\Gamma$
  therefore bounds their product below by $\Gamma(1+ak)^2$. An elementary lower
  bound makes the constant explicit:
  \[
    \Gamma(1+ak)\ge\int_{ak}^{ak+1}x^{ak}e^{-x}\ud x
    \ge e^{-1}(a/e)^{ak}k^{ak}
    \ge e^{-1}(a/e)^{ak}(k!)^a.
  \]
  Since $k \ge 1$, its square is at least $D_a^{-k}(k!)^{2-\delta r}$. Writing
  $P_k = |\Pi_k|$, we obtain
  \[
    \sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}H_D^r\ud s
    \le\frac{P_k[\Gamma(1-\delta r)D_a]^k
    t^{(2-\delta r)k}}{(k!)^{2-\delta r}}.
  \]
  The sum $\sum_DT_D^{r'}$ is symmetric in all $2k$ time labels, because a
  permutation bijects the pairings. On the full cube each pairing has integral
  $(Z_\theta t^{2-\theta})^k$. Therefore,
  \[
    \sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}T_D^{r'}\ud s
    =\frac{P_k(Z_\theta t^{2-\theta})^k}{m!n!}
    \le\frac{P_k(Z_\theta t^{2-\theta})^k}{(k!)^2}.
  \]
  Here $m!n! \ge (k!)^2$ since $m+n = 2k$. Symmetry is used only for the pairing
  sum, not for an individual diagram. Also, $P_k = \prod_{j=1}^k(2j-1) \le
  2^kk!$ and $k^k \ge k!$. Combining these bounds with~\eqref{E:higher-holder}
  and the opening estimate proves, uniformly in all regularization parameters,
  \[
    \sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}S_D T_D\ud s
    \le\frac{\{4M[\Gamma(1-\delta r)D_a]^{1/r}
    Z_\theta^{1/r'}\}^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}.
  \]
  Indeed, the time power is $1+(2-\delta r)/r+(2-\theta)/r' = 3-\delta-\alpha_0
  = \beta$; the factorial power is $1+(2-\delta r)/r+2/r'-1 = 2-\delta = q$.
  There are $2k+1 \le 3^k$ splits, giving the asserted $K$.

  We now pass to the solution using the independent fixed-order construction in
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}, which applies under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}.
  In dimension three, fix the noise smoothing first and send the Green smoothing
  to zero. Smoothed spatial covariances are bounded and continuous. Products of
  the finite Green measures converge weakly, with masses $\prod_lh_l$. At fixed
  noise smoothing all covariance factors are bounded, so a constant times
  $\prod_lh_l$ dominates the time integrals on bounded simplexes. The same
  argument for mixed Green approximations gives a common joint covariance limit,
  and hence the fixed-order $L^2$ Cauchy property. This is the inner limit of
  the source's pp.~13--14, detailed in Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}. Next
  remove the noise smoothing using Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}'s fixed-order
  $L^2$ convergence. In dimension two only this latter limit is needed. At each
  stage products converge in $L^1$, since
  \[
    \|YZ-Y'Z'\|_1
    \le\|Y-Y'\|_2\|Z\|_2+\|Y'\|_2\|Z-Z'\|_2.
  \]
  Each split sum is finite and the bound is uniform. Thus, it passes to
  $A_k(t)$; zero chains remain the constant one. These convergence inputs use
  the standing assumptions, not $\alpha \le 1$.

  Finally, Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} supplies the nonnegative full series under
  these same hypotheses. Since $q > 1$, for $z \ge 0$,
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^k}{(k!)^q}
    =\sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    \le\left(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    =e^{qz^{1/q}}.
  \]
  Take $z = Kt^\beta$ and use $A_0 = 1$ and $\beta/q = \kappa$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:higher-transfer}.
\end{proof}

% This lemma discharges H:spatial-maximum only for its explicitly stated
% isotropic Riesz ranges.
\LeanMarker{L:higher-riesz}
\begin{lemma}[Riesz spatial maxima]\label{L:higher-riesz}
  Let $\gamma(x) = c|x|^{-\alpha}$, $c > 0$. In dimension three, for $1 < \alpha
  < 2$, Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum} holds with the optimal uniform
  constant $M_3 = c2^{1-\alpha}/(2-\alpha)$. In dimension two, for $1 < \alpha <
  3/2$, it holds with
  \[
    M_2=c\left\{4^\alpha+
    \frac{2\alpha}{3/2-\alpha}4^{\alpha-3/2}\right\}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  From Chen~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.5), p.~7}, the wave
  measures are
  \[
    G_h^{(2)}(\ud y)=\frac{\mathbf1_{\{|y|<h\}}\ud y}
    {2\pi\sqrt{h^2-|y|^2}},\qquad
    G_h^{(3)}(\ud y)=\frac{\sigma_h(\ud y)}{4\pi h},
  \]
  where $\sigma_h$ is surface measure on the sphere of radius $h$. Both have
  mass $h$ and satisfy $G_h(hA) = hG_1(A)$. Thus, the unsmoothed convolution is
  $ch^{1-\alpha}F_d(z/h)$, where $F_d(w) = \int G_1^{(d)}(\ud
  y)|y+w|^{-\alpha}$. In dimension three spherical coordinates give, for $\rho =
  |w| > 0$,
  \[
    F_3(w)=\frac{(1+\rho)^v-|1-\rho|^v}{2v\rho},
    \qquad v=2-\alpha\in(0,1).
  \]
  For $0 < \rho < 1$ this is the average of the convex function $f(s) =
  (1+s)^{v-1}$ on $[-\rho,\rho]$. Its derivative has numerator
  $\rho[f(\rho)+f(-\rho)]-\int_{-\rho}^\rho f(s)\ud s \ge 0$, by the endpoint
  chord bound for a convex function. For $\rho > 1$, every distance
  $\sqrt{1+\rho^2+2\rho u}$, $-1 \le u \le 1$, increases with $\rho$, so its
  negative power decreases. The formula is continuous at $\rho = 1$ and at zero.
  Its maximum is therefore $F_3(e_1) = 2^{1-\alpha}/(2-\alpha)$.

  In dimension two we claim $G_1(B(w,R)) \le 2R^{3/2}$ for $0 < R \le 1/4$.
  Rotate $w = (A,0)$, $A \ge 0$. If $A < 1/2$, the ball lies in $|y| < 3/4$,
  where the density is at most $2/(\pi\sqrt7)$; its mass is at most
  $2R^2/\sqrt7$. If $A \ge 1/2$, write $y = (x,v)$. On the ball $|v| \le R$ and
  $x \ge 1/4$. Put $L = \sqrt{1-v^2}$. Inside the disk, $1-|y|^2 = (L-x)(L+x)$
  with $L+x > 1$. Each horizontal slice has length at most $2R$, so integrating
  $(L-x)^{-1/2}$ gives at most $2\sqrt{2R}$. The $v$ range has length at most
  $2R$, giving mass at most $(2\sqrt2/\pi)R^{3/2} \le 2R^{3/2}$, as claimed.
  Layer cake now gives
  \[
    F_2(w)\le4^\alpha+
    \alpha\int_0^{1/4}R^{-\alpha-1}G_1(B(-w,R))\ud R
    \le M_2/c.
  \]
  This includes centers outside the disk and is uniform in $w$.

  Finally, Tonelli and the Gaussian convolution rule give
  \[
    \int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    =\int p_{\epsilon+\eta}(w)(G_h*\gamma)(z-w)\ud w.
  \]
  Mass-one averaging preserves both supremum bounds, for all widths in
  Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum}. We justify the approximate identity used
  for optimality. For $f(x) = |x|^{-\lambda}$ in $\R^j$, $0 < \lambda < j$, fix
  $x \ne 0$. In the convolution $\int p_\epsilon(x-y)f(y)\ud y$, a small
  neighborhood of $x$ gives convergence by continuity. On $|y| < |x|/2$ the
  integral is bounded by a constant times
  $\epsilon^{-j/2}e^{-|x|^2/(8\epsilon)}$, since $f$ is integrable on that ball.
  On the remaining region outside the neighborhood of $x$, $f$ is bounded and
  the Gaussian tail tends to zero. Thus, $p_\epsilon*f(x) \to f(x)$ for every $x
  \ne 0$. In dimension three Fatou's lemma at a maximizing shift as
  $\epsilon\downarrow0$, $\eta = 0$, now recovers the unsmoothed value; the
  single singular point has zero sphere measure. Hence, no smaller uniform
  constant is possible. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:higher-riesz}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Riesz moment bounds]\label{C:higher-riesz}
  For isotropic Riesz covariance $c|x|^{-\alpha}$, the bounds
  in~\eqref{E:higher-bound} hold in dimension three for $1 < \alpha <
  2-\alpha_0$, and in dimension two for $1 < \alpha < \min\{3/2,2-\alpha_0\}$,
  with $M = M_d$ in~\eqref{E:higher-constants}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  These Riesz functions are nonnegative, homogeneous of degree $-\alpha$,
  locally integrable since $\alpha < d$, and nonnegative definite. For the last
  property, the identity
  \[
    |x|^{-\alpha}=\frac1{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}
    \int_0^\infty s^{\alpha/2-1}e^{-s|x|^2}\ud s
  \]
  represents the covariance as a positive mixture of positive definite
  Gaussians. To justify this distributionally, take a complex Schwartz function
  $\varphi$, put $\widetilde{\varphi}(x) = \overline{\varphi(-x)}$, and set
  $\psi = \varphi*\widetilde{\varphi}$. This is Schwartz and $\int
  |x|^{-\alpha}|\psi(x)|\ud x < \infty$: local integrability controls the origin
  and rapid decay controls infinity. Tonelli applied with $|\psi|$ therefore
  proves absolute integrability of the mixture against $\psi$, so Fubini
  applies. Each Gaussian has nonnegative integral against $\psi$, as follows
  from its positive Fourier transform and the factor $|\widehat{\varphi}|^2$.
  The mixture consequently has nonnegative integral against $\psi$. This proves
  nonnegative definiteness as a tempered distribution. The stated ranges satisfy
  $\alpha+\alpha_0 < 2$, and Lemma~\ref{L:higher-riesz} supplies the additional
  spatial hypothesis with all Gaussian uniformities.
  Proposition~\ref{P:higher-transfer} applies. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:higher-riesz}.
\end{proof}

Another possible reduction would merge two neighboring wave kernels.
No-go~\ref{P:nogo-10} explains why an unweighted convolution estimate
cannot simply discard the covariance linking a merged vertex to its remote
partner in a crossing diagram.


\begin{proposition}[Obstructions to the spatial hypothesis]
  \label{P:higher-obstructions}
  In dimension two, Riesz covariance with $3/2 \le \alpha < 2$ fails
  Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum}. For every $1 < \alpha < 2$ there are
  nonnegative, nonnegative definite homogeneous locally integrable covariance
  functions in both dimensions two and three that fail it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For the first claim set $h = 1$, $z = -e_1$, and $y =
  e_1+\rho(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)$. On the wedge $0 < \rho < 1/2$, $2\pi/3 \le \phi
  \le 4\pi/3$, we have $0 < 1-|y|^2 \le 2\rho$. Therefore,
  \[
    \int G_1^{(2)}(\ud y)c|y-e_1|^{-\alpha}
    \ge\frac{c}{3\sqrt2}\int_0^{1/2}\rho^{1/2-\alpha}\ud\rho
    =\infty.
  \]
  This includes logarithmic divergence at $\alpha = 3/2$. For the second claim
  choose $a_1 = (1+\max\{2-\alpha,\alpha-1\})/2$ and $a_2 = \alpha-a_1$. Then $0
  < a_1,a_2 < 1$ and $a_1+a_2/2 > 1$. The function $\gamma(x) =
  c|x_1|^{-a_1}|x_2|^{-a_2}$, independent of $x_3$ in dimension three, is
  locally integrable, tempered, and homogeneous of degree $-\alpha$. The
  Gaussian mixture identity in the preceding proof applied separately to each
  coordinate shows nonnegative definiteness; products of these Gaussians and the
  constant function in the third coordinate are positive definite. More
  explicitly, for the Schwartz autocorrelation $\psi$ used in the preceding
  proof, choose $N > 1$ and bound $|\psi(x)| \le
  C_N\prod_{j=1}^d(1+|x_j|)^{-N}$. Then $\int\gamma(x)|\psi(x)|\ud x < \infty$,
  by the product of one-dimensional integrals and $a_1,a_2 < 1$. Tonelli with
  $|\psi|$ and then Fubini justify the two Gaussian-mixture integrals against
  $\psi$. Their integrands are nonnegative quadratic forms, proving the
  assertion also for the covariance independent of $x_3$. With $u = 1-y_1$ and
  then $y_2 = \sqrt{2u-u^2}\,w$, Tonelli gives
  \[
    \int G_1^{(2)}(\ud y)\gamma(y-e_1)
    =\frac{c B((1-a_2)/2,1/2)}{2\pi}
    \int_0^2u^{-a_1}(2u-u^2)^{-a_2/2}\ud u=\infty.
  \]
  The beta factor is finite and the integral diverges at zero. The projection of
  $G_1^{(3)}$ onto its first two coordinates is $G_1^{(2)}$: the two sphere
  graphs each have surface Jacobian $(1-|y|^2)^{-1/2}$, divided by $4\pi$.
  Hence, the same divergence holds in dimension three. For radial Riesz
  covariance, the approximate-identity argument in Lemma~\ref{L:higher-riesz}
  applies. For the product covariance, Gaussian factorization and Tonelli give
  \[
    (p_\epsilon*\gamma)(x)
    =c\prod_{j=1}^2
    (p_\epsilon^{(1)}*|\,\cdot\,|^{-a_j})(x_j).
  \]
  In dimension three the third coordinate integrates to one. Each factor
  converges when $x_j \ne 0$ by the same argument with dimension one and
  exponent $a_j < 1$. This does not require the product kernel to be bounded at
  infinity; it is unbounded along its singular axes. The singular sets have zero
  shifted Green measure in both dimensions. Fatou with $\eta = 0$ implies that
  the smoothed convolution tends to infinity in lower limit as
  $\epsilon\downarrow0$. Even if both widths must be positive, take $\eta =
  \epsilon$ and use their sum in the Gaussian identity above. Thus, no uniform
  $M$ exists. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:higher-obstructions}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Calibrations and scope]\label{R:higher-calibrations}
  The printed first-moment statement and unconditional comparison are
  Theorem~\ref{T:first} and Proposition~\ref{P:Jensen}. Under the additional
  spatial hypothesis, Cauchy--Schwarz and Proposition~\ref{P:higher-transfer}
  give $\E u(t,x) \le \exp\{qK^{1/q}t^\kappa/2\}$, and hence $2L_* \le qK^{1/q}$
  if the intended first-moment lower bound holds. No sharp first- or
  second-moment constant is identified. As $\alpha_0\downarrow0$, the exponent
  tends to $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$, consistently with the Chen--Hu static
  calibration in Remark~\ref{R:d1-comparisons}; no interchange of limits is
  claimed. For the Riesz cases here, the Skorohod comparison of
  Balan--Conus~\cite{balan.conus:16:intermittency}*{Theorem~2.1(b), p.~1492;
  p.~1490} applies with $a = \alpha < d$, $\alpha < 2$, and $H = 1-\alpha_0/2 >
  1/2$. Its exponent $(2H+2-a)/(3-a)$ is $\kappa$. This comparison is restricted
  to those Riesz cases, with a different solution concept and covariance
  normalization; it is not an input here, and no equality with the
  Balan--Chen--Wang second-moment asymptotics is inferred. The obstructions
  above concern only the spatial supremum, not existence, moment finiteness, or
  failure of the target time scale. In particular, arbitrary homogeneous
  covariances remain outside this conditional argument, even when
  $\alpha+\alpha_0 < 2$.
\end{remark}

\section{Upper bounds for spatial exponents at most one}\label{S:d1-upper}

The upper bound at scale $t^\kappa$ is proved for $0 < \alpha \le 1$ in
dimensions one, two and three under the standing assumptions. The separate
one-dimensional Riesz estimates retain their sharper constants and white
endpoint. Degree-one covariance in dimensions two and three does not mean full
spatial white noise. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper} treats the higher exponents
by a separate argument. The intrinsic constant is identified  in
Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.

We retain the two-chain expansion of Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, but estimate its
spatial and temporal factors in a different order. The spatial estimate keeps a
power of the number of pairs. After taking a common spatial maximum, we sum all
temporal pairings before using the ordered time domains.

Throughout this section $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$. We first work in dimension one with
either of the spatial covariances
\begin{equation}\label{E:d1-covariances}
  \begin{cases}
    \gamma(y)=c_\alpha|y|^{-\alpha},&0<\alpha<1,\quad c_\alpha>0,\\
    \gamma=c_\delta\delta_0,&\alpha=1,\quad c_\delta>0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Put $b = 2-\alpha$, $q = 3-\alpha$, $\beta = 4-\alpha-\alpha_0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{E:d1-constants}
  Z=\frac{2}{(1-\alpha_0)(2-\alpha_0)},\qquad
  K_{\rm sp}=
  \begin{cases}
    \displaystyle\frac{c_\alpha 2^{2-\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-\alpha}}
    {(2-\alpha)^{2-\alpha}},&0<\alpha<1,\\[2mm]
    c_\delta,&\alpha=1.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
These parameters satisfy Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, so the fixed-order
integrability and mixed expansion already established apply.

\subsection{A spatial bound retaining the ordered increments}

For $m+n = 2k$, label the two chains in block order as in~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram}. For each vertex $l$ write
$h_l = s_l-s_{p(l)} > 0$ and $y_l = x_l-x_{p(l)}$, with a separate root zero
in each chain. Thus, $x_l$ is the sum of increments in its own chain
up to label $l$. The spatial change of variables has determinant one.
In this section $x_l$ denotes a spatial position and $y_l$ its increment; this local
notation differs from the position variables $y_l$
in~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram}. The terminal evaluation point of $u(t,x)$
remains denoted by $x$.
Let $p_\epsilon$ be the centered Brownian Gaussian density of variance
$\epsilon$ and mass one, and set
$\gamma_\epsilon = p_\epsilon*\gamma$. Allow a separate positive
$\epsilon_e$ for every pair $e \in D$ and define
\[
  S_{D,\boldsymbol\epsilon}(h)
  =\int_{\R^{2k}}\prod_{l=1}^{2k}G(h_l,y_l)
  \prod_{(i,j)\in D}\gamma_{\epsilon_{i,j}}(x_j-x_i)\ud y,
  \qquad G(h,y)=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{|y|<h\}}.
\]
This is an ordinary nonnegative integral; no pointwise delta value is used.

\LeanMarker{L:d1-spatial}
\begin{lemma}[Spatial estimate]\label{L:d1-spatial}
  Suppose~\eqref{E:d1-covariances} holds, $k \ge 1$, and $D$ is any pairing of
  the two chains. Orient every pair $(i,j)$ by $i < j$ and let $O_D$ and $C_D$
  be its opening and closing label sets. Then, uniformly in
  $\boldsymbol\epsilon$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-spatial-product}
    S_{D,\boldsymbol\epsilon}(h)\le
    \begin{cases}
      \displaystyle\left(\frac{c_\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)^k
      \prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{1-\alpha},
      &0<\alpha<1,\\[2mm]
      \displaystyle\left(\frac{c_\delta}{2}\right)^k
      \prod_{i\in O_D}h_i,&\alpha=1.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  In particular, if $\sum_{l=1}^{2k}h_l \le 2t$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-spatial-max}
    S_{D,\boldsymbol\epsilon}(h)
    \le K_{\rm sp}^{\,k}\frac{t^{bk}}{k^{bk}}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We first obtain uniform convolution bounds. Descending label integration
  then removes each closing covariance and opening mass in order, and an
  increment maximum gives the final spatial bound.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We bound the Riesz and white convolutions.
  We first record the convolution bounds. For $0 < \alpha < 1$, the
  integral of the even decreasing function $|y|^{-\alpha}$ over an
  interval of fixed length is maximal when the interval is centered
  at zero. Therefore,
  \[
    \sup_z\int_\R G(h,y)c_\alpha|y+z|^{-\alpha}\ud y
    =\frac{c_\alpha h^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.
  \]
  By Tonelli's theorem, the integral with $\gamma_\epsilon$ equals the
  $p_\epsilon(w)\ud w$ average of the unsmoothed integrals with shifts $z-w$.
  Its nonnegative mass-one average is therefore bounded by the same supremum. In
  the white case, $\gamma_\epsilon = c_\delta p_\epsilon$, so
  \[
    \sup_z\int_\R G(h,y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)\ud y
    \le\frac{c_\delta}{2}.
  \]
  Both estimates are uniform in $\epsilon > 0$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We integrate all increments in descending label
  order.
  Now integrate \emph{all} spatial increments in descending label order
  $2k,2k-1,\ldots,1$. Before processing label $l$, the remaining covariance
  factors are exactly those pairs whose closing labels are at most $l$; the
  bounds from the removed pairs depend only on $h$. If $l \in C_D$, its pair is
  $(i,l)$ with $i < l$ and $x_l-x_i = y_l+z$, where $z$ is independent of $y_l$.
  Every other remaining pair has both endpoints smaller than $l$, so its two
  positions are independent of $y_l$: positions involve only earlier increments
  in their own block chain. Thus, the only covariance involving $y_l$ is its own
  pair, and the convolution bound removes it. If $l \in O_D$, its partner has a
  larger label and its covariance factor has already been removed. All remaining
  covariances have endpoints smaller than $l$, so integration of $y_l$ gives
  only $\int G(h_l,y_l)\ud y_l = h_l$. This also preserves the stated invariant
  for the next label. Tonelli's theorem and the nonnegativity of all factors
  justify each step. There are $k$ labels of each type,
  proving~\eqref{E:d1-spatial-product} for every split and pairing.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We maximize the remaining increment product.
  For Riesz covariance, weighted arithmetic--geometric mean gives
  \[
    \prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{1-\alpha}
    \le\left(\frac{2t}{bk}\right)^{bk}
    (1-\alpha)^{(1-\alpha)k}.
  \]
  Indeed, the weights are one at the $k$ openings and $1-\alpha$ at the $k$
  closings, with total $bk$, and the sum of the increments is at most $2t$.
  Multiplication by $(c_\alpha/(1-\alpha))^k$ gives~\eqref{E:d1-spatial-max}
  with the stated $K_{\rm sp}$. In the white case, $\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i \le
  (2t/k)^k$; multiplying by $(c_\delta/2)^k$ gives the same conclusion with $b =
  1$ and $K_{\rm sp} = c_\delta$. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Summing temporal pairings before using time ordering}

Define the total-order coefficients
\[
  A_k(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\E[X_m(t,x)X_{2k-m}(t,x)],\qquad A_0(t)=1.
\]
They do not depend on $x$, by spatial stationarity. By
Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, $\E u^2(t,x) = \sum_{k\ge0}A_k(t)$.

\LeanMarker{P:d1-upper}
\begin{proposition}[One-dimensional upper bound]\label{P:d1-upper}
  Under~\eqref{E:d1-covariances}, for every $k \ge 1$ and $t > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-coefficient}
    A_k(t)\le
    \frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp})^k t^{\beta k}}{k!\,k^{bk}}
    \le\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp})^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-upper}
    \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp(C_{\rm dir}t^\kappa),\qquad
    C_{\rm dir}=q(2ZK_{\rm sp})^{1/q},\quad \kappa=\frac{\beta}{q}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We extract one spatial bound common to all pairings, then use symmetry of
  their temporal sum. We count all chain splits, pass the fixed-order limits
  and sum the factorial bound.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We separate the common spatial bound from the
  temporal pairing sum.
  We first work with the Gaussian spatial smoothing and temporal truncation
  in~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.9), (2.11), (2.23)--(2.24)}. Let
  $X_n^{\epsilon,\delta}$ be the smoothed Stratonovich term, using common
  parameters in every factor. Wick's formula expresses each mixed expectation of
  total order $2k$ as a sum over $D \in \Pi_k$. After the chain reversal in
  Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, its spatial factor is the one in
  Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}; its temporal factors are nonnegative and bounded by
  $|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$. For times in $[0,t]^m_ < \times[0,t]^{2k-m}_ <$, the
  increments sum to the two terminal times and therefore to at most $2t$.
  Extract~\eqref{E:d1-spatial-max}, which is independent of the time vector, the
  split, the pairing, and the smoothing parameters. The remaining temporal sum
  is
  \[
    F_k(s_1,\ldots,s_{2k})
    =\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}\prod_{(i,j)\in D}|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}.
  \]
  Every permutation of the $2k$ labels bijects $\Pi_k$, so $F_k$ is fully
  symmetric. Moreover,
  \[
    \int_{[0,t]^2}|s-r|^{-\alpha_0}\ud s\ud r
    =Zt^{2-\alpha_0},\qquad
    \int_{[0,t]^{2k}}F_k(s)\ud s
    =P_k(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k<\infty.
  \]
  The second equality uses the disjoint endpoints of every pairing. Partitioning
  the time cube according to the order within each block now gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-time-symmetry}
    \int_{[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^{2k-m}_<}F_k(s)\ud s
    =\frac{P_k(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k}{m!(2k-m)!}.
  \end{equation}
  Ties have measure zero. Crucially, this symmetry is applied to the \emph{sum}
  of pairings after taking the common spatial bound, not to an individual
  temporally weighted diagram.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We count pairings and splits.
  The number of pairings obeys $P_0 = 1$ and $P_k = (2k-1)P_{k-1}$, by choosing
  the partner of the smallest label. Hence, $P_k = (2k)!/(2^kk!)$. The binomial
  identity gives
  \[
    \sum_{m=0}^{2k}\frac1{m!(2k-m)!}=\frac{2^{2k}}{(2k)!}.
  \]
  Multiplying~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry} by the spatial bound and summing over
  $m$ yields
  \[
    \sum_{m=0}^{2k}\E[X_m^{\epsilon,\delta}X_{2k-m}^{\epsilon,\delta}]
    \le K_{\rm sp}^{\,k}\frac{t^{bk}}{k^{bk}}
    \frac{2^{2k}}{(2k)!}
    \frac{(2k)!}{2^kk!}(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k
    =\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp})^kt^{\beta k}}{k!\,k^{bk}}.
  \]
  The same proof covers empty chains, with $X_0^{\epsilon,\delta} = 1$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We remove regularization and sum the full series.
  By Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}, each smoothed factor converges in $L^2$.
  Products converge in $L^1$ by Cauchy--Schwarz; for fixed $k$ the sum over
  splits is finite. The bound is uniform in $\epsilon,\delta$, so it passes to
  $A_k(t)$. Since $k! \le k^k$ and $b > 0$, we have $k!\,k^{bk} \ge (k!)^{b+1} =
  (k!)^q$, proving both inequalities in~\eqref{E:d1-coefficient}.

  Finally, for $z \ge 0$ and $q \ge 1$,
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^k}{(k!)^q}
    =\sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    \le\left(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    =\exp(qz^{1/q}).
  \]
  Apply this inequality with $z = 2ZK_{\rm sp}t^\beta$, adding the separately
  defined term $A_0 = 1$. Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} then
  gives~\eqref{E:d1-upper}. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectral bounds for $0 < \alpha \le 1$ in all three dimensions}

We now retain Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} with $d \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $0 < \alpha
\le 1$. The covariance is nonnegative and nonnegative definite as in
Section~\ref{S:model}; when it is a distribution, both these properties and its
homogeneity are understood distributionally. Let $\mu$ be its positive Radon
spectral measure in the convention $\gamma(x) = \int e^{i\xi\cdot
x}\mu(\ud\xi)$, and put $V = \mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le1\})$. No isotropy or spectral
density is assumed. Write $G_h$ for the positive wave-kernel measure of mass
$h$, and $G_{h,\eta} = p_\eta*G_h$ for $\eta > 0$; set $G_{h,0} = G_h$. The
measure notation includes the sphere kernel in dimension three. The mixed-moment
definition of $A_k(t)$ is now applied to the present $d$-dimensional solution.

\LeanMarker{L:low-alpha-convolution}
\begin{lemma}[Low-$\alpha$ convolution bound]\label{L:low-alpha-convolution}
  Under the preceding assumptions with $0 < \alpha < 1$, for every $R,h > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-radial}
    \mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le R\})=VR^\alpha,\qquad
    \int_{\R^d}\min\{h,|\xi|^{-1}\}\mu(\ud\xi)
    =\frac{V}{1-\alpha}h^{1-\alpha}.
  \end{equation}
  For $\epsilon > 0$ and $\eta \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-convolution}
    0\le\int_{\R^d}G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    \le\frac{V}{1-\alpha}h^{1-\alpha},
    \qquad z\in\R^d.
  \end{equation}
  The estimate is uniform in both smoothing parameters and in $z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We compute the absolute Fourier envelope from spectral ball masses. We then
  use the finite Gaussian-weighted measure to justify Fourier integration and
  bound the translated convolution.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We compute the homogeneous spectral envelope.
  The spectral measure of $\gamma(c\,\cdot)$ is the pushforward of $\mu$ under
  $\xi\mapsto c\xi$. Homogeneity and uniqueness of the Fourier transform on
  tempered distributions therefore give $\mu(A/c) = c^{-\alpha}\mu(A)$ for every
  Borel set $A$. Equivalently, $\mu(cA) = c^\alpha\mu(A)$. This proves the
  ball-mass formula. Since $\mu$ is Radon, $V < \infty$, and continuity from
  above gives $\mu(\{0\}) = \lim_{R\downarrow0}VR^\alpha = 0$. By the layer-cake
  identity and Tonelli's theorem,
  \[
    \int_{\R^d}\min\{h,|\xi|^{-1}\}\mu(\ud\xi)
    =\int_0^h\mu(\{\xi:|\xi|\le u^{-1}\})\ud u
    =V\int_0^h u^{-\alpha}\ud u.
  \]
  The endpoint of each indicator has no effect on its $u$ integral. Since
  $\alpha < 1$, the last expression is the finite quantity
  in~\eqref{E:low-alpha-radial}. This computation uses ball masses, not a radial
  density for $\mu$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We obtain the smoothed convolution bound.
  Gaussian weighting gives a finite measure, by the polynomial ball growth of
  $\mu$. Thus, Fourier Fubini is justified for $\gamma_\epsilon$ and the finite
  measure $G_{h,\eta}$. From the source's wave-kernel
  formulas~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.3)--(2.5), p.~7},
  \[
    \widehat{G}_h(\xi)=\frac{\sin(h|\xi|)}{|\xi|},\qquad
    G_h(\R^d)=h,
  \]
  with value $h$ in the Fourier formula at zero. The Fourier multipliers of
  $p_\epsilon$ and $p_\eta$ have absolute value at most one, with the latter
  multiplier equal to one when $\eta = 0$. Consequently,
  \[
    \left|\int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)\right|
    \le\int_{\R^d}\frac{|\sin(h|\xi|)|}{|\xi|}\mu(\ud\xi)
    \le\int_{\R^d}\min\{h,|\xi|^{-1}\}\mu(\ud\xi).
  \]
  Nonnegativity of $\gamma$ as a distribution implies $\gamma_\epsilon \ge 0$,
  since Gaussian translates are nonnegative test functions; $G_{h,\eta}$ is also
  nonnegative. This proves the lower bound in~\eqref{E:low-alpha-convolution}.
  Combining the preceding displays proves its upper bound. No positivity of the
  oscillatory Fourier integrand is asserted. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:low-alpha-convolution}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:alpha-one-convolution}
\begin{lemma}[Degree-one convolution bound]\label{L:alpha-one-convolution}
  Under the preceding covariance assumptions with $\alpha = 1$, for every $h >
  0$, $\epsilon > 0$, $\eta \ge 0$, and $z \in \R^d$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:alpha-one-convolution}
    0\le\int_{\R^d}G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    \le\frac{\pi V}{2}.
  \end{equation}
  No isotropy or angular density is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We resolve the degree-one spectral measure into radial and angular parts. We
  retain the radial oscillation and evaluate it as a one-dimensional heat
  convolution, whose bound is uniform in direction.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We obtain the polar spectral decomposition.
  Fourier uniqueness, applied to the distributional homogeneity as above, gives
  $\mu(cA) = c\mu(A)$ and $\mu(\{0\}) = 0$. Define a finite measure on the unit
  sphere by
  \[
    \sigma(E)=\mu\{\xi:0<|\xi|\le1,\ \xi/|\xi|\in E\}.
  \]
  Its total mass is $V$. Homogeneity implies that the corresponding sector of
  radius $R$ has mass $R\sigma(E)$. Subtracting two such sectors shows that a
  polar rectangle $(r_1,r_2]\times E$ has mass $(r_2-r_1)\sigma(E)$. Uniqueness
  of measures on these generating rectangles therefore gives the polar
  decomposition $\mu(\ud\xi) = \ud r\,\sigma(\ud\theta)$, $r > 0$, $\theta \in
  \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$. The real stationary covariance is even; Fourier uniqueness
  makes $\mu$, and hence $\sigma$, even.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We express the convolution through its radial
  oscillatory integral.
  Use the Brownian Gaussian convention, so the combined covariance and Green
  smoothing multiplier is $e^{-ar^2}$ with $a = (\epsilon+\eta)/2 > 0$. Gaussian
  weighting makes the spectral measure finite. Fourier Fubini and evenness give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:alpha-one-polar}
    \begin{split}
      \int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
      &=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}} I_a(h,\theta\cdot z)\sigma(\ud\theta),\\
      I_a(h,v)&=\int_0^\infty e^{-ar^2}
      \frac{\sin(hr)}r\cos(rv)\ud r.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  The radial integral is absolutely convergent: its integrand is bounded near
  zero and Gaussian-decaying at infinity. Passing from the complex exponential
  to cosine introduces no factor two, since both antipodal directions are
  already included in $\sigma$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We identify that integral with a bounded
  one-dimensional convolution.
  Let $g_h(v) = \tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{|v|<h\}}$ and let $p_{2a}^{(1)}(v) = (4\pi
  a)^{-1/2}e^{-v^2/(4a)}$ be the one-dimensional Gaussian density. Their Fourier
  transforms are $\sin(hr)/r$ and $e^{-ar^2}$. The one-dimensional inversion
  factor is $1/(2\pi)$ in the source convention, so
  \[
    (g_h*p_{2a}^{(1)})(v)
    =\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R e^{irv}e^{-ar^2}
    \frac{\sin(hr)}r\ud r
    =\frac1\pi I_a(h,v).
  \]
  The sign in the inversion exponential is immaterial because the integrand
  pairs evenly under $r\mapsto-r$. Since $p_{2a}^{(1)}$ has mass one and $0 \le
  g_h \le 1/2$, we obtain $0 \le I_a(h,v) \le \pi/2$. Integrating against
  $\sigma$, whose mass is $V$, proves~\eqref{E:alpha-one-convolution}. This
  retains the oscillation until the one-dimensional integral is evaluated; the
  divergent absolute Fourier envelope at $\alpha = 1$ is not used. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{P:low-alpha-upper}
\begin{proposition}[Upper bound for $\alpha \le 1$]\label{P:low-alpha-upper}
  Suppose that Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} and the standing covariance positivity
  assumptions hold, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, $0 < \alpha \le 1$, and $V > 0$. Define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-constants}
    \begin{split}
      K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen}
      &=\begin{cases}
        \displaystyle\frac{V2^{2-\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-\alpha}}
        {(2-\alpha)^{2-\alpha}},&0<\alpha<1,\\[2mm]
        \pi V,&\alpha=1,
      \end{cases}\\
      C_{\rm dir}^{\rm gen}&=q(2ZK_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^{1/q}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  With the same $A_k$, $b$, $q$, $\beta$, and $Z$ as above, for
  $k \ge 1$ and $t > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:low-alpha-coefficient}
    A_k(t)\le\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^k t^{\beta k}}
    {k!\,k^{bk}}
    \le\frac{(2ZK_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q},
    \qquad
    \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp(C_{\rm dir}^{\rm gen}t^\kappa).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We apply the descending-label spatial argument in every dimension using the
  appropriate closing bound. We then sum temporal pairings and pass the Green
  and noise limits in their prescribed order.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We obtain a common spatial bound in each
  dimension.
  First smooth the noise as in~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.9), (2.11), (2.23)--(2.24)}.
  In dimension three also use $G_{h,\eta}$ with $\eta > 0$, as in the
  source's pp.~13--14. The increment change of variables is now a
  triangular map on vectors in $\R^d$, still of determinant one.
  The descending-label proof of Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial} depends only
  on which increments enter each position, not on their dimension.
  Its invariant therefore remains valid: at a closing label there
  is exactly one covariance depending on that vector increment, and
  at an opening label its pair has already been removed.
  If $0 < \alpha < 1$, Lemma~\ref{L:low-alpha-convolution} gives the
  closing bound $Mh_l^{1-\alpha}$ with $M = V/(1-\alpha)$.
  At $\alpha = 1$, Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution} gives the
  constant closing bound $M = \pi V/2$. Every opening gives mass
  $h_l$, uniformly in $\eta$ and all noise-smoothing parameters.
  For $\alpha < 1$, the same weighted arithmetic--geometric mean
  calculation gives
  \[
    M^k\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{1-\alpha}
    \le (K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen})^k\frac{t^{bk}}{k^{bk}}.
  \]
  At $\alpha = 1$, ordinary arithmetic--geometric mean on the $k$ opening
  increments gives $M^k\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i \le (2M)^kt^k/k^k$, which is the same
  bound with $b = 1$ and $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen} = \pi V$. Both cases use
  $\sum_lh_l \le 2t$. For $d = 1,2$ one may use the Green densities directly;
  their opening mass and closing convolution obey these same bounds.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We sum the temporal factors.
  Replace the nonnegative truncated temporal factors by $|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$
  before taking their pairing sum. The temporal symmetry, cube integral and
  split count in~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry} do not involve the spatial
  dimension. Thus, exactly the multiplication of counts in the proof of
  Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper} gives the first bound
  in~\eqref{E:low-alpha-coefficient} for the regularized moments.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We remove regularizations and sum the coefficient
  bounds.
  We spell out the limit in dimension three. Fix every noise-smoothing parameter
  and first let $\eta\downarrow0$. At each time vector, the Gaussian moment
  formula is a finite sum of bounded continuous products of the smoothed
  covariance functions, integrated against products of the finite Green
  measures. These products of measures converge weakly as $\eta\downarrow0$,
  with the same masses $\prod_l h_l$. At fixed noise smoothing the Gaussian
  covariance factors are uniformly bounded, so a constant times $\prod_lh_l$
  dominates on each bounded time simplex. Dominated convergence applies also to
  the covariance between two approximations with different $\eta$ values. The
  resulting common joint covariance limit proves the $L^2$ Cauchy property of
  each fixed-order random integral. This is the inner limit used in the source's
  definition on pp.~13--14. Products of two factors converge in $L^1$ by
  Cauchy--Schwarz, so the moment inequality survives this limit. Next send the
  noise-smoothing parameters to zero using the fixed-order $L^2$ convergence of
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}, and again pass products in $L^1$. In
  dimensions one and two only the latter limit is required. The bounds at both
  stages are uniform.

  We have proved the first coefficient bound for $A_k(t)$. The factorial
  inequality and nonnegative-series argument in Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}
  apply unchanged, with $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen}$ in place of $K_{\rm sp}$ and $A_0
  = 1$. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the spectral estimates]\label{R:low-alpha-scope}
  If $V = 0$, spectral homogeneity gives $\mu(B_R) = 0$ for every $R$, hence
  $\mu = 0$ and $u = 1$; the noise-free conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, all
  angular and dimensional dependence is allowed in $V$. For $0 < \alpha < 1$,
  the example $\gamma(x) = c|x_1|^{-\alpha}$ in dimensions two or three has its
  spectral measure supported on an axis; a full-dimensional density is not
  required. The absolute Fourier envelope diverges at $\alpha = 1$, where the
  separate oscillatory calculation in Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution} is
  essential. The endpoint is not obtained by taking a limit in $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm
  gen}$ for $\alpha < 1$. For $d = 1$ and $\gamma = c_\delta\delta_0$, the
  source convention gives $\mu(\ud\xi) = c_\delta\ud\xi/(2\pi)$ and $V =
  c_\delta/\pi$. Hence, the endpoint constant $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm gen} = \pi V =
  c_\delta$ agrees exactly with Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}, with no extra
  antipodal factor. In dimensions two and three the endpoint covers degree-one
  homogeneous covariance, not full spatial white noise. No result for $\alpha >
  1$ follows from these estimates.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Checks on the scale, constants and scope}

\LeanMarker{C:d1-first-calibration}
\begin{corollary}[First-moment calibration]\label{C:d1-first-calibration}
  Let $K_*$ be $K_{\rm sp}$ under~\eqref{E:d1-covariances}, or $K_{\rm sp}^{\rm
  gen}$ under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}. Put
  $C_{2,*} = q(2ZK_*)^{1/q}$. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:d1-first-calibration}
    \E u(t,x)\le\exp(C_{1,*}t^\kappa),\qquad
    C_{1,*}=q\left(\frac{ZK_*}{2^{3-\alpha}}\right)^{1/q}
    =\frac{C_{2,*}}{2^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}}.
  \end{equation}
  In either nonzero case $2C_{1,*} < C_{2,*}$. Conditionally on a first-moment
  lower rate at least $L_*$, $2L_* \le 2C_{1,*} < C_{2,*}$, consistently with
  Proposition~\ref{P:Jensen}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  For one chain of length $2k$, the increments sum to at most $t$. The spatial
  estimate in Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}, or in the proof of
  Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}, therefore applies with $t/2$ in place of
  $t$. The temporal pairing sum on one ordered simplex is
  $P_k(Zt^{2-\alpha_0})^k/(2k)!$. Using the same regularization and limit order
  as in the respective upper-bound proof gives
  \[
    \E X_{2k}(t,x)
    \le\frac{(ZK_*/2^{b+1})^k t^{\beta k}}{k!\,k^{bk}}
    \le\frac{(ZK_*/2^{3-\alpha})^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}.
  \]
  Every odd term has zero expectation. The $L^2$ expansion permits taking
  expectations of partial sums, and the resulting nonnegative even series is
  summed by the scalar inequality in the proof of Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper}.
  This gives~\eqref{E:d1-first-calibration}. If the first-moment lower rate is
  at least $L_*$, \eqref{E:d1-first-calibration} implies $L_* \le C_{1,*}$. In
  the stated nonzero cases $K_* > 0$, hence $C_{1,*} > 0$; and
  $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha) > 1$ gives $2C_{1,*} < C_{2,*}$. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:d1-first-calibration}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Checks and comparison with the uniform pairing bound]\label{R:d1-upper-checks}
  The argument preserves the factor $k^{-(2-\alpha)k}$ from the spatial
  increment maximum. Replacing the spatial volume merely by a constant times
  $t^{(2-\alpha)k}$ would lose the factorial power needed
  in~\eqref{E:d1-coefficient}. This proof does not use the
  common-Laplace-parameter norm majorant of Proposition~\ref{P:envelope}, and it
  does not assert a stronger factorial estimate for every individual diagram.
  Instead, the temporal ordering is exploited after summing pairings
  in~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry}. Thus, the scoped G0 obstruction remains valid.
  For unit white covariance, the bound on the total-order-two coefficient is
  $2Z$, whereas the exact value from Lemma~\ref{L:white-test} is $2c_0$; their
  ratio is $2(3-\alpha_0) > 4$. At the static endpoint the exact order-four
  coefficient is $11/540$, while the first bound in~\eqref{E:d1-coefficient}
  gives $1/2$. These are checks of normalization, not evidence of sharp
  constants.
\end{remark}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-06} quantifies the loss avoided here: discarding the
spatial constraints before summation gives the wrong time exponent.


\begin{remark}[Boundary and literature comparisons]\label{R:d1-comparisons}
  As $\alpha_0\downarrow0$, the time exponent tends to $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$,
  the Chen--Hu static exponent cited in Remark~\ref{R:G1-scope}. This is an
  exponent calibration, not an interchange of the static and large-time limits.
  With the Riesz normalization $c_\alpha = (1-\alpha)/2$, the displayed $K_{\rm
  sp}$ tends to one as $\alpha\uparrow1$, matching unit white covariance. For
  comparison only, the Skorohod upper bound of
  Balan--Conus~\cite{balan.conus:16:intermittency}*{Theorem~2.1(b), (8), (10),
  p.~1492} has time exponent $(2H+2-a)/(3-a)$. Setting $H = 1-\alpha_0/2$ and $a
  = \alpha$ yields the same $\kappa$. Their covariance normalization contains
  $H(2H-1)$ and they use a Skorohod mild solution. No constants or solutions are
  identified, and their theorem is not an input to the present proof. The
  current coverage of this section is $0 < \alpha \le 1$ in $d = 1,2,3$,
  including the one-dimensional white case. It does not address $1 < \alpha <
  2-\alpha_0$ in dimensions two and three.
\end{remark}

\section{Higher spatial exponents under a convolution bound}
\label{S:higher-alpha}

The convolution assumption below is additional to homogeneity, and the concrete
applications cover only the stated Riesz ranges. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper}
establishes the full general upper scale by a separate argument. This
alternative route is not an input to Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.

We retain the standing covariance assumptions and take $d \in \{2,3\}$ and $1 <
\alpha < 2-\alpha_0$. Put $\delta = \alpha-1$, $q = 3-\alpha$, and $\beta =
4-\alpha-\alpha_0$. Here $\delta$ is an exponent, not a smoothing parameter.
Write $p_\epsilon$ for the centered Gaussian density with covariance $\epsilon
I$, $\gamma_\epsilon = p_\epsilon*\gamma$, and $G_{h,\eta} = p_\eta*G_h$, with
$G_{h,0} = G_h$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Spatial maximum]\label{H:spatial-maximum}
  There is $M > 0$ such that, for every $h > 0$, $z \in \R^d$, $\epsilon > 0$,
  and $\eta \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:higher-convolution}
    0\le\int_{\R^d}G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    \le M h^{-\delta}.
  \end{equation}
  The same $M$ works for all translations and both Gaussian widths. In
  particular, each pairing edge may have its own positive covariance width and
  each Green factor its own nonnegative smoothing width.
\end{hypothesis}

This uniform maximum is an additional assumption, not a consequence of
homogeneity. No-go~\ref{P:nogo-08} gives admissible covariances for which
the translated wave convolution diverges.


\begin{proposition}[Conditional upper bound]\label{P:higher-transfer}
  Suppose that Hypotheses~\ref{H:homog} and~\ref{H:spatial-maximum} hold in the
  range specified above. Choose and fix
  \[
    \frac1{1-\alpha_0}<r<\frac1\delta,\qquad
    r'=\frac r{r-1},\qquad \theta=\alpha_0r',\qquad
    a=1-\frac{\delta r}{2}.
  \]
  Define the explicit, nonsharp constants
  \begin{equation}\label{E:higher-constants}
    Z_\theta=\frac2{(1-\theta)(2-\theta)},\qquad
    D_a=e^2(e/a)^{2a},\qquad
    K=12M[\Gamma(1-\delta r)D_a]^{1/r}Z_\theta^{1/r'}.
  \end{equation}
  For the total-order coefficients $A_k(t)$ of Section~\ref{S:d1-upper},
  \begin{equation}\label{E:higher-bound}
    A_k(t)\le\frac{K^kt^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}\quad(k\ge1),
    \qquad \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp\{qK^{1/q}t^\kappa\}.
  \end{equation}
  These estimates hold for every $t > 0$ and $x \in \R^d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We retain the singular closing increments instead of replacing them by a
  spatial maximum. Hölder on the common pairing-time measure separates those
  increments from the temporal factors. Dirichlet and symmetry estimates then
  recover the required factorial power before the regularization limits.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We verify parameters and perform the spatial
  elimination.
  The interval for $r$ is nonempty because $\delta+\alpha_0 < 1$. Thus, $\delta
  r < 1$, $\theta < 1$, and $1/2 < a < 1$. We use the source's noise
  regularization from Chen~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.9),
  (2.11), (2.23)--(2.24)}, as in Section~\ref{S:d1-upper}. Its nonnegative
  temporal covariance is bounded by $|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$, and its spatial
  covariance on an edge $e$ is $\gamma_{\epsilon_e+\epsilon'_e}$, where
  $\epsilon_e,\epsilon'_e > 0$ are the two endpoint noise widths. Indeed,
  $p_{\epsilon_e}*p_{\epsilon'_e} = p_{\epsilon_e+\epsilon'_e}$; equal widths
  give the source's $\gamma_{2\epsilon}$. Every such positive covariance width
  is covered by Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum}. In dimension three first
  smooth the Green measures as prescribed on pp.~13--14 of that source.

  Fix $m+n = 2k$, $k \ge 1$, and put $\Omega_{m,n} = [0,t]^m_ < \times[0,t]^n_
  <$. Use the two separate roots and increments $h_l = s_l-s_{p(l)}$ of
  Section~\ref{S:d1-upper}. For $D \in \Pi_k$, orient its pairs by increasing
  block label and denote their opening and closing sets by $O_D,C_D$. Descending
  integration of the vector increments gives
  \[
    S_D(h)\le M^k\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{-\delta}
    \le M^k(2t/k)^k H_D(h),\qquad
    H_D(h)=\prod_{j\in C_D}h_j^{-\delta}.
  \]
  Indeed, before label $l$ is integrated, the remaining covariances have closing
  labels at most $l$. At a closing label exactly its own pair depends on that
  increment, as $\gamma_\epsilon(y_l+z)$; all other endpoints have smaller
  labels. Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum} removes this factor. At an opening
  its pair is already removed and the integral is the Green mass $h_l$. This
  proves the invariant in every dimension and for empty chains. Tonelli applies
  by positivity. Finally, the $k$ opening increments sum to at most $2t$, so
  ordinary arithmetic--geometric mean gives the second inequality. We have not
  applied the low-$\alpha$ conclusion of Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We separate the spatial gap powers and temporal
  pairing factors.
  Set $T_D(s) = \prod_{(i,j)\in D}|s_i-s_j|^{-\alpha_0}$. H\"older's inequality
  on counting measure on $\Pi_k$ times Lebesgue measure on $\Omega_{m,n}$ gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:higher-holder}
    \sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}H_DT_D\ud s
    \le\left(\sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}H_D^r\ud s\right)^{1/r}
    \left(\sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}T_D^{r'}\ud s\right)^{1/r'}.
  \end{equation}
  Both integrals are finite by the following computations. If the two chains
  contain $k_1,k_2$ closing labels, then $k_1+k_2 = k$ and the simplex change to
  increments has determinant one. The Dirichlet integral, including the unused
  terminal gap with exponent zero, gives
  \[
    \int_{\Omega_{m,n}}H_D^r\ud s
    =\frac{\Gamma(1-\delta r)^k t^{(2-\delta r)k}}
    {\Gamma(1+m-\delta r k_1)\Gamma(1+n-\delta r k_2)}.
  \]
  To see the normalization, in a chain of length $m$ the $k_1$ closing
  increments have Dirichlet parameter $1-\delta r$, the other increments have
  parameter one, and the terminal gap has parameter one. Scaling gives
  $t^{m-\delta r k_1}$. Iterating the beta integral gives the displayed Gamma
  quotient. Empty chains contribute $\Gamma(1) = 1$. The two denominator
  arguments are positive and have average $1+ak$. Log convexity of $\Gamma$
  therefore bounds their product below by $\Gamma(1+ak)^2$. An elementary lower
  bound makes the constant explicit:
  \[
    \Gamma(1+ak)\ge\int_{ak}^{ak+1}x^{ak}e^{-x}\ud x
    \ge e^{-1}(a/e)^{ak}k^{ak}
    \ge e^{-1}(a/e)^{ak}(k!)^a.
  \]
  Since $k \ge 1$, its square is at least $D_a^{-k}(k!)^{2-\delta r}$. Writing
  $P_k = |\Pi_k|$, we obtain
  \[
    \sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}H_D^r\ud s
    \le\frac{P_k[\Gamma(1-\delta r)D_a]^k
    t^{(2-\delta r)k}}{(k!)^{2-\delta r}}.
  \]
  The sum $\sum_DT_D^{r'}$ is symmetric in all $2k$ time labels, because a
  permutation bijects the pairings. On the full cube each pairing has integral
  $(Z_\theta t^{2-\theta})^k$. Therefore,
  \[
    \sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}T_D^{r'}\ud s
    =\frac{P_k(Z_\theta t^{2-\theta})^k}{m!n!}
    \le\frac{P_k(Z_\theta t^{2-\theta})^k}{(k!)^2}.
  \]
  Here $m!n! \ge (k!)^2$ since $m+n = 2k$. Symmetry is used only for the pairing
  sum, not for an individual diagram. Also, $P_k = \prod_{j=1}^k(2j-1) \le
  2^kk!$ and $k^k \ge k!$. Combining these bounds with~\eqref{E:higher-holder}
  and the opening estimate proves, uniformly in all regularization parameters,
  \[
    \sum_D\int_{\Omega_{m,n}}S_D T_D\ud s
    \le\frac{\{4M[\Gamma(1-\delta r)D_a]^{1/r}
    Z_\theta^{1/r'}\}^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q}.
  \]
  Indeed, the time power is $1+(2-\delta r)/r+(2-\theta)/r' = 3-\delta-\alpha_0
  = \beta$; the factorial power is $1+(2-\delta r)/r+2/r'-1 = 2-\delta = q$.
  There are $2k+1 \le 3^k$ splits, giving the asserted $K$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We pass the fixed-order limits.
  We now pass to the solution using the independent fixed-order construction in
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}, which applies under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}.
  In dimension three, fix the noise smoothing first and send the Green smoothing
  to zero. Smoothed spatial covariances are bounded and continuous. Products of
  the finite Green measures converge weakly, with masses $\prod_lh_l$. At fixed
  noise smoothing all covariance factors are bounded, so a constant times
  $\prod_lh_l$ dominates the time integrals on bounded simplexes. The same
  argument for mixed Green approximations gives a common joint covariance limit,
  and hence the fixed-order $L^2$ Cauchy property. This is the inner limit of
  the source's pp.~13--14, detailed in Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}. Next
  remove the noise smoothing using Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}'s fixed-order
  $L^2$ convergence. In dimension two only this latter limit is needed. At each
  stage products converge in $L^1$, since
  \[
    \|YZ-Y'Z'\|_1
    \le\|Y-Y'\|_2\|Z\|_2+\|Y'\|_2\|Z-Z'\|_2.
  \]
  Each split sum is finite and the bound is uniform. Thus, it passes to
  $A_k(t)$; zero chains remain the constant one. These convergence inputs use
  the standing assumptions, not $\alpha \le 1$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We sum the full coefficient series.
  Finally, Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} supplies the nonnegative full series under
  these same hypotheses. Since $q > 1$, for $z \ge 0$,
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^k}{(k!)^q}
    =\sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    \le\left(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    =e^{qz^{1/q}}.
  \]
  Take $z = Kt^\beta$ and use $A_0 = 1$ and $\beta/q = \kappa$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:higher-transfer}.
\end{proof}

% This lemma discharges H:spatial-maximum only for its explicitly stated
% isotropic Riesz ranges.
\LeanMarker{L:higher-riesz}
\begin{lemma}[Riesz spatial maxima]\label{L:higher-riesz}
  Let $\gamma(x) = c|x|^{-\alpha}$, $c > 0$. In dimension three, for $1 < \alpha
  < 2$, Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum} holds with the optimal uniform
  constant $M_3 = c2^{1-\alpha}/(2-\alpha)$. In dimension two, for $1 < \alpha <
  3/2$, it holds with
  \[
    M_2=c\left\{4^\alpha+
    \frac{2\alpha}{3/2-\alpha}4^{\alpha-3/2}\right\}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We compute the spherical maximum in dimension three and bound small
  translated ball masses in dimension two. Gaussian averaging preserves those
  bounds, and an approximate-identity limit proves the stated optimality in
  dimension three.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We evaluate the three-dimensional spherical
  convolution.
  From Chen~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.5), p.~7}, the wave
  measures are
  \[
    G_h^{(2)}(\ud y)=\frac{\mathbf1_{\{|y|<h\}}\ud y}
    {2\pi\sqrt{h^2-|y|^2}},\qquad
    G_h^{(3)}(\ud y)=\frac{\sigma_h(\ud y)}{4\pi h},
  \]
  where $\sigma_h$ is surface measure on the sphere of radius $h$. Both have
  mass $h$ and satisfy $G_h(hA) = hG_1(A)$. Thus, the unsmoothed convolution is
  $ch^{1-\alpha}F_d(z/h)$, where $F_d(w) = \int G_1^{(d)}(\ud
  y)|y+w|^{-\alpha}$. In dimension three spherical coordinates give, for $\rho =
  |w| > 0$,
  \[
    F_3(w)=\frac{(1+\rho)^v-|1-\rho|^v}{2v\rho},
    \qquad v=2-\alpha\in(0,1).
  \]
  For $0 < \rho < 1$ this is the average of the convex function $f(s) =
  (1+s)^{v-1}$ on $[-\rho,\rho]$. Its derivative has numerator
  $\rho[f(\rho)+f(-\rho)]-\int_{-\rho}^\rho f(s)\ud s \ge 0$, by the endpoint
  chord bound for a convex function. For $\rho > 1$, every distance
  $\sqrt{1+\rho^2+2\rho u}$, $-1 \le u \le 1$, increases with $\rho$, so its
  negative power decreases. The formula is continuous at $\rho = 1$ and at zero.
  Its maximum is therefore $F_3(e_1) = 2^{1-\alpha}/(2-\alpha)$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We bound the planar convolution by local ball
  masses.
  In dimension two we claim $G_1(B(w,R)) \le 2R^{3/2}$ for $0 < R \le 1/4$.
  Rotate $w = (A,0)$, $A \ge 0$. If $A < 1/2$, the ball lies in $|y| < 3/4$,
  where the density is at most $2/(\pi\sqrt7)$; its mass is at most
  $2R^2/\sqrt7$. If $A \ge 1/2$, write $y = (x,v)$. On the ball $|v| \le R$ and
  $x \ge 1/4$. Put $L = \sqrt{1-v^2}$. Inside the disk, $1-|y|^2 = (L-x)(L+x)$
  with $L+x > 1$. Each horizontal slice has length at most $2R$, so integrating
  $(L-x)^{-1/2}$ gives at most $2\sqrt{2R}$. The $v$ range has length at most
  $2R$, giving mass at most $(2\sqrt2/\pi)R^{3/2} \le 2R^{3/2}$, as claimed.
  Layer cake now gives
  \[
    F_2(w)\le4^\alpha+
    \alpha\int_0^{1/4}R^{-\alpha-1}G_1(B(-w,R))\ud R
    \le M_2/c.
  \]
  This includes centers outside the disk and is uniform in $w$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We retain smoothing uniformity and prove the
  optimality claim.
  Finally, Tonelli and the Gaussian convolution rule give
  \[
    \int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)\gamma_\epsilon(y+z)
    =\int p_{\epsilon+\eta}(w)(G_h*\gamma)(z-w)\ud w.
  \]
  Mass-one averaging preserves both supremum bounds, for all widths in
  Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum}. We justify the approximate identity used
  for optimality. For $f(x) = |x|^{-\lambda}$ in $\R^j$, $0 < \lambda < j$, fix
  $x \ne 0$. In the convolution $\int p_\epsilon(x-y)f(y)\ud y$, a small
  neighborhood of $x$ gives convergence by continuity. On $|y| < |x|/2$ the
  integral is bounded by a constant times
  $\epsilon^{-j/2}e^{-|x|^2/(8\epsilon)}$, since $f$ is integrable on that ball.
  On the remaining region outside the neighborhood of $x$, $f$ is bounded and
  the Gaussian tail tends to zero. Thus, $p_\epsilon*f(x) \to f(x)$ for every $x
  \ne 0$. In dimension three Fatou's lemma at a maximizing shift as
  $\epsilon\downarrow0$, $\eta = 0$, now recovers the unsmoothed value; the
  single singular point has zero sphere measure. Hence, no smaller uniform
  constant is possible. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:higher-riesz}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Riesz moment bounds]\label{C:higher-riesz}
  For isotropic Riesz covariance $c|x|^{-\alpha}$, the bounds
  in~\eqref{E:higher-bound} hold in dimension three for $1 < \alpha <
  2-\alpha_0$, and in dimension two for $1 < \alpha < \min\{3/2,2-\alpha_0\}$,
  with $M = M_d$ in~\eqref{E:higher-constants}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  These Riesz functions are nonnegative, homogeneous of degree $-\alpha$,
  locally integrable since $\alpha < d$, and nonnegative definite. For the last
  property, the identity
  \[
    |x|^{-\alpha}=\frac1{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}
    \int_0^\infty s^{\alpha/2-1}e^{-s|x|^2}\ud s
  \]
  represents the covariance as a positive mixture of positive definite
  Gaussians. To justify this distributionally, take a complex Schwartz function
  $\varphi$, put $\widetilde{\varphi}(x) = \overline{\varphi(-x)}$, and set
  $\psi = \varphi*\widetilde{\varphi}$. This is Schwartz and $\int
  |x|^{-\alpha}|\psi(x)|\ud x < \infty$: local integrability controls the origin
  and rapid decay controls infinity. Tonelli applied with $|\psi|$ therefore
  proves absolute integrability of the mixture against $\psi$, so Fubini
  applies. Each Gaussian has nonnegative integral against $\psi$, as follows
  from its positive Fourier transform and the factor $|\widehat{\varphi}|^2$.
  The mixture consequently has nonnegative integral against $\psi$. This proves
  nonnegative definiteness as a tempered distribution. The stated ranges satisfy
  $\alpha+\alpha_0 < 2$, and Lemma~\ref{L:higher-riesz} supplies the additional
  spatial hypothesis with all Gaussian uniformities.
  Proposition~\ref{P:higher-transfer} applies. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:higher-riesz}.
\end{proof}

Another possible reduction would merge two neighboring wave kernels.
No-go~\ref{P:nogo-10} explains why an unweighted convolution estimate
cannot simply discard the covariance linking a merged vertex to its remote
partner in a crossing diagram.


\begin{proposition}[Obstructions to the spatial hypothesis]
  \label{P:higher-obstructions}
  In dimension two, Riesz covariance with $3/2 \le \alpha < 2$ fails
  Hypothesis~\ref{H:spatial-maximum}. For every $1 < \alpha < 2$ there are
  nonnegative, nonnegative definite homogeneous locally integrable covariance
  functions in both dimensions two and three that fail it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We exhibit a divergent planar Riesz convolution at the boundary, then
  construct an anisotropic product covariance with the same obstruction in
  both dimensions. Approximate-identity limits transfer these divergences to
  the smoothed uniform-bound question.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We identify the divergent boundary configurations.
  For the first claim set $h = 1$, $z = -e_1$, and $y =
  e_1+\rho(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)$. On the wedge $0 < \rho < 1/2$, $2\pi/3 \le \phi
  \le 4\pi/3$, we have $0 < 1-|y|^2 \le 2\rho$. Therefore,
  \[
    \int G_1^{(2)}(\ud y)c|y-e_1|^{-\alpha}
    \ge\frac{c}{3\sqrt2}\int_0^{1/2}\rho^{1/2-\alpha}\ud\rho
    =\infty.
  \]
  This includes logarithmic divergence at $\alpha = 3/2$. For the second claim
  choose $a_1 = (1+\max\{2-\alpha,\alpha-1\})/2$ and $a_2 = \alpha-a_1$. Then $0
  < a_1,a_2 < 1$ and $a_1+a_2/2 > 1$. The function $\gamma(x) =
  c|x_1|^{-a_1}|x_2|^{-a_2}$, independent of $x_3$ in dimension three, is
  locally integrable, tempered, and homogeneous of degree $-\alpha$. The
  Gaussian mixture identity in the preceding proof applied separately to each
  coordinate shows nonnegative definiteness; products of these Gaussians and the
  constant function in the third coordinate are positive definite. More
  explicitly, for the Schwartz autocorrelation $\psi$ used in the preceding
  proof, choose $N > 1$ and bound $|\psi(x)| \le
  C_N\prod_{j=1}^d(1+|x_j|)^{-N}$. Then $\int\gamma(x)|\psi(x)|\ud x < \infty$,
  by the product of one-dimensional integrals and $a_1,a_2 < 1$. Tonelli with
  $|\psi|$ and then Fubini justify the two Gaussian-mixture integrals against
  $\psi$. Their integrands are nonnegative quadratic forms, proving the
  assertion also for the covariance independent of $x_3$. With $u = 1-y_1$ and
  then $y_2 = \sqrt{2u-u^2}\,w$, Tonelli gives
  \[
    \int G_1^{(2)}(\ud y)\gamma(y-e_1)
    =\frac{c B((1-a_2)/2,1/2)}{2\pi}
    \int_0^2u^{-a_1}(2u-u^2)^{-a_2/2}\ud u=\infty.
  \]

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We transfer the product example to dimension three
  and remove smoothing.
  The beta factor is finite and the integral diverges at zero. The projection of
  $G_1^{(3)}$ onto its first two coordinates is $G_1^{(2)}$: the two sphere
  graphs each have surface Jacobian $(1-|y|^2)^{-1/2}$, divided by $4\pi$.
  Hence, the same divergence holds in dimension three. For radial Riesz
  covariance, the approximate-identity argument in Lemma~\ref{L:higher-riesz}
  applies. For the product covariance, Gaussian factorization and Tonelli give
  \[
    (p_\epsilon*\gamma)(x)
    =c\prod_{j=1}^2
    (p_\epsilon^{(1)}*|\,\cdot\,|^{-a_j})(x_j).
  \]
  In dimension three the third coordinate integrates to one. Each factor
  converges when $x_j \ne 0$ by the same argument with dimension one and
  exponent $a_j < 1$. This does not require the product kernel to be bounded at
  infinity; it is unbounded along its singular axes. The singular sets have zero
  shifted Green measure in both dimensions. Fatou with $\eta = 0$ implies that
  the smoothed convolution tends to infinity in lower limit as
  $\epsilon\downarrow0$. Even if both widths must be positive, take $\eta =
  \epsilon$ and use their sum in the Gaussian identity above. Thus, no uniform
  $M$ exists. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:higher-obstructions}.
\end{proof}

  The printed first-moment statement and unconditional comparison are
  Theorem~\ref{T:first} and Proposition~\ref{P:Jensen}. Under the additional
  spatial hypothesis, Cauchy--Schwarz and Proposition~\ref{P:higher-transfer}
  give $\E u(t,x) \le \exp\{qK^{1/q}t^\kappa/2\}$, and hence $2L_* \le qK^{1/q}$

  second-moment constant is identified. As $\alpha_0\downarrow0$, the exponent

  Balan--Conus~\cite{balan.conus:16:intermittency}*{Theorem~2.1(b), p.~1492;

  failure of the target time scale. In particular, arbitrary homogeneous

\end{remark}

\section{Model and standing assumptions}\label{S:model}

We study the hyperbolic Anderson model
\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(1.1)}
\begin{equation}\label{E:HAM}
  \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\W(t,x)u(t,x),
  \qquad u(0,x)=1,\quad \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=0,
\end{equation}
for $(t,x) \in \R_+\times\R^d$, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, interpreted in the
Stratonovich sense (limit of classical wave equations driven by smoothed noises
$\W_{\epsilon,\delta}$). The noise is centered Gaussian with
\begin{equation}\label{E:cov}
  \operatorname{Cov}\big(\W(s,x),\W(t,y)\big)=|s-t|^{-\alpha_0}\gamma(x-y),
  \qquad 0<\alpha_0<1,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma \ge 0$ is nonnegative definite with spectral measure $\mu$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Homogeneous covariance]\label{H:homog}
  Let $d \in \{1,2,3\}$ and $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$. The spatial covariance is a
  nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon measure $\Gamma$ on
  $\R^d$, with positive even tempered Radon spectral measure $\mu$ in the
  convention
  \[
    \Gamma=\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu(\ud\xi)
    \quad\text{in the sense of tempered distributions}.
  \]
  The symbol $\gamma$ in~\eqref{E:cov} denotes this covariance as a function
  when one exists, and as a distribution otherwise. For every Borel set $A$ and
  $c > 0$, $\Gamma(cA) = c^{d-\alpha}\Gamma(A)$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:threshold}
    \alpha_0+\alpha<2,
  \end{equation}
  with $0 < \alpha < d$, or $\alpha = d = 1$. In particular, the last case
  includes spatial white noise $\Gamma = \delta_0$.
\end{hypothesis}

\paragraph{Zero covariance.}
Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} allows $\Gamma = 0$ by design. In this case the
centered Gaussian noise is $\W = 0$, and the solution with initial data
$(1,0)$ is $u(t,x) = 1$. Thus $\E|u(t,x)|^p = 1$ for every $p > 0$, and
all normalized logarithmic moment rates are zero. Results asserting strict
positivity of covariance-dependent quantities impose $\Gamma \ne 0$ locally.

\begin{proposition}[Jensen lower bound]\label{P:Jensen}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, $\E u(t,x) \ge 1$ and $\E u^2(t,x) \ge (\E
  u(t,x))^2$. Consequently, for every real $\ell$,
  \[
    \liminf_{t\to\infty}t^{-\kappa}\log\E u(t,x)\ge\ell
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \liminf_{t\to\infty}t^{-\kappa}\log\E u^2(t,x)\ge2\ell.
  \]
  The specialization $\ell = L_*$ is conditional on the intended first-moment
  lower bound with that constant. It is not an unconditional adoption of the
  printed normalization in Theorem~\ref{T:first}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For each fixed $t,x$, Lemma~\ref{L:vertex-crude} below gives
  $\sum_n\|X_n(t,x)\|_2 < \infty$; its proof does not use the present
  proposition. Thus, $\sum_n\E|X_n(t,x)| < \infty$, and the mean of the
  canonical series is the sum of its means. By~\eqref{E:expansion}
  and~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.20)--(2.21)}, $\E u(t,x) =
  \sum_{n\ge0}\E S_{2n}(g_{2n}(\cdot,t,x)) \ge 1$, since every term is a pairing
  integral of a nonnegative integrand and $S_0 = 1$. By the Cauchy--Schwarz
  inequality, $\E u^2(t,x) \ge (\E u(t,x))^2$, so $t^{-\kappa}\log\E u^2(t,x)
  \ge 2\,t^{-\kappa}\log\E u(t,x)$. Taking lower limits proves the implication
  for every valid $\ell$. Substituting $L_*$ requires its stated first-moment
  premise. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:Jensen}.
\end{proof}


\section{Main results}\label{S:targets}

We state the two main results for the model in Section~\ref{S:model}. Throughout
this section, Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} holds, $u$ is the canonical Stratonovich
expansion~\eqref{E:expansion} with initial data $(1,0)$, and $\kappa$ is the
exponent in~\eqref{E:kappa}. The spectral measure $\mu$ has the normalization
specified in Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}. Write $B_1$ for the unit ball in
$\R^d$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{E:intro-spectral-mass}
  R_\alpha=\frac{\alpha\pi\mu(B_1)}{2\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Upper bound at the correct scale]\label{T:intro-upper}
  \label{G:upper}
  For every admissible dimension $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, there is a finite constant
  $C = C(\alpha,\alpha_0)$ such that, for all $T \ge 1$ and $x \in \R^d$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:intro-upper-bound}
    \E u^2(T,x)
    \le e(T+2)^d
    \exp\left\{C R_\alpha^{1/(3-\alpha)}T^\kappa\right\}.
  \end{equation}
  In particular, for every fixed $x \in \R^d$,
  \[
    \limsup_{T\to\infty}T^{-\kappa}\log\E u^2(T,x)<\infty.
  \]
\end{theorem}

The proof is given in Theorem~\ref{T:full-upper}. It establishes finiteness
independently of the variational identification below. The more explicit bounds
for $0 < \alpha \le 1$ are retained in Section~\ref{S:d1-upper}.

To state the limiting constant, let $I = (0,1)$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{E:intro-control-class}
  \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}
  =\left\{\Psi\in C_c^\infty(I\times\R^d;\R):
  \Psi\ge0,\ \Psi\ne0\right\}.
\end{equation}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-20} exhibits controls whose covariance profiles do not
separate into a scalar time factor and one spatial function. This does not
decide whether a smaller family suffices for optimization.

This class includes controls that do not separate into a function of time and
a function of space. For $\Psi \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$, its covariance
mean and variance are
\begin{align}
  h_{\Psi,s}(x)
  &=\int_I|s-t|^{-\alpha_0}
  \int_{\R^d}\Psi(t,x-y)\Gamma(\ud y)\ud t,
  \label{E:intro-control-mean}\\
  v_\Psi
  &=\int_{I\times\R^d}\Psi(s,x)h_{\Psi,s}(x)\ud x\ud s.
  \label{E:intro-control-variance}
\end{align}
For $s \in I$, define the nonnegative Rayleigh value
\begin{equation}\label{E:intro-control-rate}
  \mathcal{R}_\Psi(s)
  =\sup_{\substack{f\in H^1(\R^d;\R)\\\|f\|_2=1}}
  \left\{\int_{\R^d}h_{\Psi,s}(x)f(x)^2\ud x
  -\|\nabla f\|_2^2\right\}.
\end{equation}
Here $H^1(\R^d;\R)$ is the real Sobolev space of square-integrable functions
with square-integrable first weak derivatives, and $\|\cdot\|_2$ denotes the
spatial $L^2$ norm. The spatial
covariance retains its original amplitude in these definitions.

\begin{theorem}[Second-moment limit]\label{T:intro-second-moment}
  \label{G:sharp}
  For every fixed $x \in \R^d$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:intro-second-moment-limit}
    \lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-\kappa}\log\E u^2(T,x)
    =C_2(\Gamma,\alpha_0),
  \end{equation}
  where
  \begin{equation}\label{E:intro-second-moment-constant}
    C_2(\Gamma,\alpha_0)
    =\sup_{\Psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}}
    \left\{2\int_I\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_\Psi(s)}\ud s
    -\frac{v_\Psi}{2}\right\}.
  \end{equation}
  If $\Gamma \ne 0$, then $0 < C_2(\Gamma,\alpha_0) < \infty$.
  If $\Gamma = 0$, then $u(T,x) = 1$ and $C_2(0,\alpha_0) = 0$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is given in Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}. The variational
formula uses the full class of nonnegative smooth compact controls. Its kinetic
coefficient is one, and its Gaussian cost is one half of the original variance.
It does not identify an auxiliary variational value with the first-moment limit
or with the constants in Chen's conjecture.
Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-occupation-ceiling},
Corollary~\ref{C:intrinsic-spatial-bracket} and
Theorem~\ref{T:white-moment-bounds} give bounds for this same constant. In
particular, the explicit spatial white-noise energy yields moment bounds,
not a closed-form evaluation of the full supremum.

\section{The complete two-chain expansion}

For $m+n = 2k$, label the two chains by $I_1 = \{1,\ldots,m\}$ and $I_2 =
\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}$. A self-pairing has both endpoints in one chain; a
cross-pairing has one endpoint in each chain. Both are included in $\Pi_k$. Let
$p(l)$ denote the preceding vertex in its own chain; at each first vertex set
$(s_{p(l)},y_{p(l)}) = (0,0)$. Thus, the second chain has its own root, rather
than the last vertex of the first chain. For $D \in \Pi_k$, set
\begin{equation}\label{E:mixed-diagram}
  \begin{split}
    J_{m,n,D}(t)
    ={}&\int_{[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^n_<}\ud\mathbf{s}
    \int_{(\R^d)^{m+n}}\ud\mathbf{y}\,
    \prod_{\rho=1}^2\prod_{l\in I_\rho}
    G(s_l-s_{p(l)},y_l-y_{p(l)})\\
    &\hspace{20mm}\times
    \prod_{(j,h)\in D}|s_j-s_h|^{-\alpha_0}\gamma(y_j-y_h).
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
An empty chain contributes the factor one. For $d = 3$, the spatial Green
kernels are measures; for spatial white noise the covariance is a delta measure.
Integrals in~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram} use the generalized-kernel and
regularization conventions of the source, not pointwise values of these
measures. Lemmas~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures}--\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}
construct them with the convention
of~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{Remark~2.3, p.~11, and the Green
regularization on pp.~13--14}.

\begin{proposition}[Mixed terms]\label{P:mixed}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog},
  \begin{equation}\label{E:mixed-moment}
    \E[X_m(t,x)X_n(t,x)]
    =\begin{cases}
      \displaystyle\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}J_{m,n,D}(t),&m+n=2k,\\
      0,&m+n\text{ odd},
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  where the case $m = n = 0$ equals one. In particular,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:full-second}
    \E u^2(t,x)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\sum_{m=0}^{2k}
    \sum_{D\in\Pi_k}J_{m,2k-m,D}(t).
  \end{equation}
  This is a convergent series of nonnegative terms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order} below constructs each $X_n$ and each
  individual two-chain diagram at arbitrary vertexwise widths, including unequal
  and empty chains. Its proof uses the positive spatial-measure limit and
  Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope}; it uses neither the present
  proposition nor any infinite solution series.

  At positive noise widths Wick's formula gives zero at odd total order and the
  finite pairing sum at even total order. The Green-width limit is taken first,
  as in the construction preceding Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures}. At fixed
  $m,n$, joint $L^2$ convergence of the two factors gives product convergence in
  $L^1$:
  \[
    \|Y_mY_n-X_mX_n\|_1
    \le\|Y_m-X_m\|_2\|Y_n\|_2
    +\|X_m\|_2\|Y_n-X_n\|_2\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  A zero-order factor stays equal to one. Each individual Wick diagram also has
  the finite nonnegative limit in Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}.

  To identify its initial-chain form, for terminal variables in the first chain
  set
  \[
    s'_j=t-s_{m+1-j},\qquad y'_j=x-y_{m+1-j},\qquad1\le j\le m,
  \]
  and use the corresponding length-$n$ transformation on the second chain. Each
  transformed chain has initial root $(0,0)$. For example, $G(t-s_m,x-y_m)$
  becomes $G(s'_1,y'_1)$; every other factor becomes the corresponding
  successive increment. The differences in each covariance change sign, absorbed
  by evenness. The vertex involution is
  \[
    \rho(j)=m+1-j\ (1\le j\le m),\qquad
    \rho(m+j)=m+n+1-j\ (1\le j\le n).
  \]
  It induces the pairing bijection $D\mapsto\{\{\rho(a),\rho(b)\}:\{a,b\}\in
  D\}$. The same transformation holds before removing noise smoothing. Passing
  to the finitely many diagram limits proves \eqref{E:mixed-moment} with
  precisely~\eqref{E:mixed-diagram}.

  Lemmas~\ref{L:crude-corrected} and~\ref{L:vertex-crude} independently prove
  fixed-$t$ norm summability of the $X_n$, using the fixed-order construction
  just cited and Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope}.
  Proposition~\ref{P:mild-construction} identifies their series as a
  Stratonovich mild solution. None of those proofs uses the present mixed-term
  identity. Hence, $U_N = \sum_{n=0}^N X_n \to u$ in $L^2$, and
  \[
    |\E U_N^2-\E u^2|
    \le\|U_N-u\|_2(\|U_N\|_2+\|u\|_2)\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  The finite expansions of $\E U_N^2$ increase, since every mixed expectation
  computed above is nonnegative. Their limit is the full double sum, independent
  of its enumeration. Regrouping by total order gives~\eqref{E:full-second}.
  This proves Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}.
\end{proof}

The two chain orders must remain distinct: chronological concatenation can
violate wave support, and a difference of block labels need not measure
chronological separation. No-gos~\ref{P:nogo-05} and~\ref{P:nogo-07}
give explicit positive-measure witnesses.

Write $G_\theta(s,y) = e^{-\theta s}G(s,y)$ and $\widehat{G}_s(\xi) =
\sin(s|\xi|)/|\xi|$, with value $s$ at $\xi = 0$. We use the norms in the
source's (3.3)--(3.5), with the full covariance, but give their bounds directly.
Write $\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}$ for Euler's Gamma function in the following
definitions, to distinguish it from the covariance measure:
\begin{equation}\label{E:damped-norm-definitions}
  \begin{split}
    N_0(\theta)&=\int_0^\infty e^{-\theta s}G_s(\R^d)\ud s,\\
    N_1(\theta)&=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(\alpha_0)}
    \int_0^\infty\lambda^{\alpha_0-1}\ud\lambda
    \int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{(\theta+\lambda)^2+|\xi|^2},\\
    N_2(\theta)^2&=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\theta(s+t)}|s-t|^{-\alpha_0}
    \int\widehat{G}_s(\xi)\overline{\widehat{G}_t(\xi)}
    \mu(\ud\xi)\ud s\ud t.
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
The second expression uses the elementary wave Laplace transform $\int_0^\infty
e^{-as}\widehat{G}_s(\xi)\ud s = (a^2+|\xi|^2)^{-1}$. The last expression is the
space-time covariance quadratic form; in physical variables it pairs
$G_\theta(s,x)$ with $G_\theta(t,y)$. Thus, the second Green factor in the
printed source (3.5) is read with the independent variable $y$; see

Remark~\ref{R:source-case2-time-shift} for the independent justification and the

\LeanMarker{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}
\begin{lemma}[Homogeneous damped norms and temporal tails]
  \label{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, the integrals
  in~\eqref{E:damped-norm-definitions} are finite for every $\theta > 0$, with
  $N_2(\theta)^2 \ge 0$, and
  \[
    N_0(\theta)=\theta^{-2},\qquad
    N_1(\theta)=\theta^{\alpha+\alpha_0-2}N_1(1),\qquad
    N_2(\theta)^2=\theta^{-\beta}N_2(1)^2,
    \quad\beta=4-\alpha-\alpha_0.
  \]
  Let
  \[
    K_M(r)=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(\alpha_0)}
    \int_M^\infty e^{-\lambda|r|}\lambda^{\alpha_0-1}\ud\lambda.
  \]
  Denote by $N_1^{(M)}(1)$ the first norm with $\lambda > M$, and by
  $N_2^{(M)}(1)$ the second norm with $K_M(s-t)$ in place of
  $|s-t|^{-\alpha_0}$. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:damped-tail-norms}
    N_1^{(M)}(1)\longrightarrow0,\qquad
    N_2^{(M)}(1)\longrightarrow0\quad(M\to\infty).
  \end{equation}
  More precisely, let $m:\R^d\to[0,1]$ be measurable and let
  $S\subset S'\subset(0,\infty)$ be measurable. Define $N_{1,m,S}(\theta)$
  by replacing $\mu$ with $m\mu$ and restricting the $\lambda$ integral in
  $N_1$ to $S$. Define $N_{2,m,S}(\theta)^2$ by replacing $\mu$ with $m\mu$
  and the temporal kernel in $N_2^2$ with
  \[
    K_S(r)=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(\alpha_0)}
    \int_S e^{-\lambda|r|}\lambda^{\alpha_0-1}\ud\lambda.
  \]
  Then, for every $\theta>0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:damped-multiplier-comparison}
    \begin{split}
      0\le N_{1,m,S}(\theta)&\le N_{1,1,S'}(\theta),\\
      0\le N_{2,m,S}(\theta)^2&\le N_{2,1,S'}(\theta)^2.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  These are comparisons of spectral expressions, including full and tail
  domains. An arbitrary $m$ need not preserve Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}; the
  scaling identities above are asserted for the original covariance only.
  Spatial Gaussian smoothing and Gaussian smoothing of the Green kernel
  give multipliers in this class. The separate mass identity for $N_0$ is
  unchanged by Gaussian smoothing of the Green kernel.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Scaling the Fourier representation of $\Gamma$ and using Fourier uniqueness
  gives $\mu(cA) = c^\alpha\mu(A)$. Radon finiteness and $\alpha > 0$ then imply
  $\mu(\{0\}) = 0$. In particular, $\mu(B_r) = r^\alpha V$, where $V = \mu(B_1)
  < \infty$. Therefore, for $a > 0$,
  \[
    \int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{a^2+|\xi|^2}
    =\alpha V a^{\alpha-2}
    \int_0^\infty\frac{u^{\alpha-1}}{1+u^2}\ud u<\infty.
  \]
  The last integral is finite since $0 < \alpha < 2$. Substitution into $N_1$
  reduces its finiteness to
  $\int_0^\infty\lambda^{\alpha_0-1}(\theta+\lambda)^{\alpha-2} \ud\lambda <
  \infty$: the conditions at zero and infinity are $\alpha_0 > 0$ and
  $\alpha+\alpha_0 < 2$.

  For $N_2$ use $|\widehat{G}_s(\xi)| \le \min(s,|\xi|^{-1})$. On $|\xi| \le 1$,
  absolute integration is bounded by
  \[
    V\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\theta(s+t)}|s-t|^{-\alpha_0}st\ud s\ud t<\infty.
  \]
  On $|\xi| > 1$, use $\int_{|\xi|>1}|\xi|^{-2}\mu(\ud\xi) = \alpha
  V/(2-\alpha)$ and the same time integral without $st$. Both time integrals are
  finite by splitting into $s \le t$ and $t \le s$, then using $u = \min(s,t)$
  and $v = |s-t|$; the only singular factor is $v^{-\alpha_0}$, with $\alpha_0 <
  1$. This proves absolute integrability of the displayed spectral formula. For
  each $\xi$, its time integral is nonnegative because the temporal kernel is
  positive definite. Indeed, it is a positive Laplace mixture of the kernels
  $e^{-\lambda|s-t|}$. This proves $N_2(\theta)^2 \ge 0$.

  The mass identity $G_s(\R^d) = s$ proves the formula for $N_0$. In $N_1$
  change $\lambda = \theta u$ and $\xi = \theta\eta$. In $N_2$ change $s =
  u/\theta$, $t = v/\theta$ and $\xi = \theta\eta$, using
  $\widehat{G}_{u/\theta}(\theta\eta) = \theta^{-1}\widehat{G}_u(\eta)$. These
  changes give exactly the stated powers.

  The first tail in~\eqref{E:damped-tail-norms} is a tail of the finite
  nonnegative $N_1(1)$ integral. For the second, $0 \le K_M(r) \le
  |r|^{-\alpha_0}$ and $K_M(r) \to 0$ for $r \ne 0$. The absolute majorant just
  proved permits dominated convergence in $(s,t,\xi)$, giving $N_2^{(M)}(1)^2
  \to 0$. The tail kernel is positive definite by the same Laplace-mixture
  argument, so its quadratic form is nonnegative.

  For the multiplier comparisons, define
  \[
    B_\theta(\lambda,\xi)=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\theta(s+t)}e^{-\lambda|s-t|}
    \widehat G_s(\xi)\overline{\widehat G_t(\xi)}\ud s\ud t.
  \]
  For each $\lambda>0$ and $\xi$, the positive definiteness of
  $e^{-\lambda|s-t|}$ gives $B_\theta(\lambda,\xi)\ge0$.
  The absolute-integrability bound above, $0\le m\le1$ and
  $0\le K_S\le|\cdot|^{-\alpha_0}$ justify changing the integration order:
  \[
    N_{2,m,S}(\theta)^2
    =\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(\alpha_0)}
    \int_S\lambda^{\alpha_0-1}
    \int m(\xi)B_\theta(\lambda,\xi)\mu(\ud\xi)\ud\lambda.
  \]
  Nonnegativity now proves the second comparison
  in~\eqref{E:damped-multiplier-comparison}. It is used after time integration,
  not pointwise on the oscillating product of wave Fourier kernels. The first
  comparison follows directly from the nonnegative integrand defining $N_1$.
  The choices $S=(0,\infty)$ or $S=(M,\infty)$ give the full or tail bounds.
  Gaussian spatial and Green smoothing contribute products of multipliers in
  $[0,1]$ depending only on $\xi$. Gaussian Green smoothing also preserves
  $G_s(\R^d)=s$, proving its separate assertion for $N_0$. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Covariance pairings of translated kernels.}
Fix $d \in \{1,2,3\}$. Let $\mu$ be a finite positive Borel measure on $\R^d$
such that $\gamma(z) = \int e^{i\xi\cdot z}\mu(\ud\xi)$ is real and nonnegative
for every $z$. Thus, $\gamma$ is bounded, continuous and even, with $0 \le
\gamma(z) \le \mu(\R^d)$. Let $\nu$ be a positive Borel measure on $(0,\infty)$
with $\nu((0,L]) < \infty$ for every $L > 0$, and put
\[
  \kappa_L(t)=\int_{(0,L]}e^{-\lambda|t|}\nu(\ud\lambda),\qquad
  C_L((t,x),(s,y))=\kappa_L(t-s)\gamma(x-y).
\]
For complex $L^1(\R\times\R^d)$ functions $f,h$, define
\begin{equation}\label{E:translated-covariance-form}
  Q_L(f,h)=\int_{\R^2}\int_{\R^{2d}}
  f(t,x)\overline{h(s,y)}\kappa_L(t-s)\gamma(x-y)
  \ud x\ud y\ud t\ud s.
\end{equation}
This integral is absolutely convergent, with $|Q_L(f,h)| \le
\nu((0,L])\mu(\R^d)\|f\|_1\|h\|_1$. For nonnegative $f,h$, define $Q(f,h) =
\lim_{L\to\infty}Q_L(f,h)$ as an extended nonnegative number. This positive cap
limit also defines the pairing when the full temporal kernel is singular on the
diagonal. The spatial covariance is fixed throughout this definition. For a
nonnegative Borel function $G$ on $(0,\infty)\times\R^d$, let $G^0$ be its zero
extension to nonpositive times and put
\[
  N_0(G)=\int_0^\infty\int_{\R^d}G(t,x)\ud x\ud t,\qquad
  N_C(G)^2=Q(G^0,G^0).
\]
For $(A,a) \in \R\times\R^d$, write $T_{A,a}f(t,x) = f(t-A,x-a)$. All translated
pairings are over the full time line.

\begin{lemma}[Translated covariance Cauchy--Schwarz]
  \label{L:translated-covariance-CS}
  Under the preceding assumptions on $\mu$ and $\nu$, let $G_1,G_2$ be
  nonnegative Borel functions with $N_0(G_i),N_C(G_i) < \infty$, $i = 1,2$. For
  arbitrary origins $(A,a),(B,b) \in \R\times\R^d$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:translated-covariance-CS}
    0\le Q(T_{A,a}G_1^0,T_{B,b}G_2^0)\le N_C(G_1)N_C(G_2),
  \end{equation}
  and $Q(T_{A,a}G_i^0,T_{A,a}G_i^0) = N_C(G_i)^2$ for $i = 1,2$. The coefficient
  one is independent of the origins, dimension, cap and covariance masses. Zero
  norms and zero measures are included.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We first prove positivity and Cauchy--Schwarz for the stationary kernel $C_L$
  at fixed finite $L$. For $\lambda > 0$, define
  \[
    h_\lambda(t,r)=\sqrt{2\lambda}\,e^{-\lambda(t-r)}
    \mathbf1_{\{r\le t\}}.
  \]
  Direct integration gives
  \[
    \int_\R h_\lambda(t,r)h_\lambda(s,r)\ud r
    =2\lambda e^{-\lambda(t+s)}
    \int_{-\infty}^{\min(t,s)}e^{2\lambda r}\ud r
    =e^{-\lambda|t-s|}.
  \]
  In particular, $\int h_\lambda(t,r)^2\ud r = 1$. On the complex Hilbert space
  \[
    \mathcal{H}_L=L^2((0,L]\times\R^d\times\R,
    \nu(\ud\lambda)\mu(\ud\xi)\ud r),
  \]
  with inner product linear in its first entry, put $\Phi_L(t,x)(\lambda,\xi,r)
  = h_\lambda(t,r)e^{i\xi\cdot x}$. Then
  \[
    \|\Phi_L(t,x)\|^2=m_L:=\nu((0,L])\mu(\R^d),\qquad
    \langle\Phi_L(t,x),\Phi_L(s,y)\rangle=C_L((t,x),(s,y)).
  \]
  The scalar integrals are absolutely convergent: Cauchy--Schwarz in $r$ bounds
  $\int|h_\lambda(t,r)h_\lambda(s,r)|\ud r$ by one, and both remaining measures
  are finite on the cap.

  The map $(t,x)\mapsto\Phi_L(t,x)$ is norm-continuous, since its squared
  increment is $2m_L-2\operatorname{Re}C_L((t,x),(s,y))$. Continuity of $C_L$
  follows by dominated convergence against the finite measures. Thus, the map is
  strongly measurable, has separable range and is bounded. For $f \in L^1$, the
  Bochner integral $F_L(f) = \int f(t,x)\Phi_L(t,x)\ud t\ud x$ exists. Bochner
  Fubini, justified by $m_L\|f\|_1\|h\|_1$, gives $Q_L(f,h) = \langle
  F_L(f),F_L(h)\rangle$. Therefore,
  \[
    |Q_L(f,h)|\le Q_L(f,f)^{1/2}Q_L(h,h)^{1/2}.
  \]
  This uses the original stationary covariance, rather than positivity of an
  independently shifted cross kernel. The feature space is deterministic and
  introduces no new Gaussian field.

  Changing both integration variables in~\eqref{E:translated-covariance-form} by
  the same time and spatial translation gives
  \[
    Q_L(T_{A,a}f,T_{A,a}h)=Q_L(f,h).
  \]
  Both differences remain unchanged. The full time line and Lebesgue translation
  invariance justify this equality without an origin factor. Apply
  Cauchy--Schwarz to the two different translates and this equality separately
  to their self pairings. This proves the capped form
  of~\eqref{E:translated-covariance-CS}.

  To make the passage for possibly unbounded nonnegative kernels explicit, set
  \[
    G_{i,M}(t,x)=\min(G_i(t,x),M)
    \mathbf1_{\{0<t\le M,\ |x|\le M\}},\qquad M\in\N.
  \]
  These functions increase to $G_i$ almost everywhere. Their zero extensions and
  translates are bounded compactly supported $L^1$ functions. Since $C_L \ge 0$,
  monotone convergence passes the capped inequality and common-translation
  identity to $G_i$ as $M \to \infty$. Equivalently, the finite-cap passage
  follows from $L^1$ convergence and the absolute bound
  after~\eqref{E:translated-covariance-form}.

  Finally, positivity of $\nu$ and nonnegativity of $\gamma$ give
  \[
    Q_L(G_i^0,G_i^0)\uparrow N_C(G_i)^2,\qquad
    Q_L(T_{A,a}G_1^0,T_{B,b}G_2^0)
    \uparrow Q(T_{A,a}G_1^0,T_{B,b}G_2^0).
  \]
  The finite self norms bound the latter limit by $N_C(G_1)N_C(G_2)$. The
  common-translation identity passes to the limit in the same way. No division
  by a norm is used, so zero norms cause no exception. No spatial Gaussian
  mollifier is removed in this argument. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:translated-covariance-CS}.
\end{proof}

\section{A finite product bound for two ordered chains}

Fix $d \ge 1$, $0 < a < 1$ and a matching $D$ on $2n$ labels. For each edge $e
\in D$, let $\mu_e$ be finite and positive, with $\gamma_e(x) = \int
e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu_e(\ud\xi) \ge 0$. For a measurable $A_e \subset (0,\infty)$
put
\[
  \nu_e(\ud\lambda)=\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)^{-1}
  \mathbf1_{A_e}(\lambda)\lambda^{a-1}\ud\lambda,
  \qquad \kappa_e(t)=\int e^{-\lambda|t|}\nu_e(\ud\lambda).
\]
For nonnegative $G \in L^1((0,\infty)\times\R^d)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{E:pairing-edge-norms}
  \begin{split}
    N_0(G)&=\int G(t,x)\ud t\ud x,\\
    N_{1,e}(G)&=\int\left|\int e^{-\lambda t+i\xi\cdot x}
    G(t,x)\ud t\ud x\right|\nu_e(\ud\lambda)\mu_e(\ud\xi),\\
    N_{2,e}(G)^2&=\int G(s,x)G(t,y)
    \kappa_e(s-t)\gamma_e(x-y)\ud s\ud x\ud t\ud y.
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
Time integrations in these definitions are positive. Denote the inner
Laplace--Fourier transform by $\mathcal{L} G(\lambda,\xi)$. The last integral
means its nonnegative temporal cap limit, as in the preceding covariance
pairing. In particular, its spatial variables are independent.

Let $I_1 = \{1,\ldots,n_1\}$ and $I_2 = \{n_1+1,\ldots,2n\}$, where $n_1+n_2 =
2n$ and either chain may be empty. Each chain is ordered in its own time
variables. Write $p(l)$ for its predecessor, using $(s_{p(l)},x_{p(l)}) = (0,0)$
at the first vertex of each chain. Set
\begin{equation}\label{E:pairing-diagram-general}
  \begin{split}
    I_D(\boldsymbol G)=\int
    &\prod_{\rho=1}^2\prod_{l\in I_\rho}
    G_l(s_l-s_{p(l)},x_l-x_{p(l)})\\
    &\times\prod_{e=\{j,k\}\in D}
    \kappa_e(s_j-s_k)\gamma_e(x_j-x_k)
    \ud\boldsymbol s\ud\boldsymbol x .
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
The domain is the product of the two positive ordered time simplices and all
spatial variables.

Call $(Q,\iota)$ admissible if $Q_0,Q_1,Q_2$ partition the kernel labels, $|Q_0|
= |Q_1|$, and the map $\iota:Q_1\cup Q_2 \to D$ uses each edge once on $Q_1$ or
twice on $Q_2$, never both. There is no incidence requirement between $l$ and
$\iota(l)$. These assignments form a finite nonempty set $\mathcal{A}_D$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{E:pairing-product-general}
  \mathcal{P}_{Q,\iota}(\boldsymbol G)=
  \prod_{l\in Q_0}N_0(G_l)
  \prod_{l\in Q_1}2N_{1,\iota(l)}(G_l)
  \prod_{l\in Q_2}N_{2,\iota(l)}(G_l).
\end{equation}
Thus, $|Q_2|$ is even and $|Q_0|+|Q_2|/2 = n$.

\begin{lemma}[Pairing product envelope]\label{L:pairing-product-envelope}
  Suppose every $G_l$ is nonnegative and integrable, and
  $N_{1,e}(G_l),N_{2,e}(G_l) < \infty$ for every $l$ and $e \in D$. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:pairing-product-envelope}
    I_D(\boldsymbol G)\le
    \max_{(Q,\iota)\in\mathcal{A}_D}
    \mathcal{P}_{Q,\iota}(\boldsymbol G).
  \end{equation}
  A maximizing assignment may depend on the kernels and edge data. No single
  assignment valid for all kernel families is asserted. For $n = 0$, both sides
  are one. Zero kernels, measures and norms are allowed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  First cap every temporal domain at $L < \infty$; its measure is finite since
  $a > 0$. The stationary covariance pairing in
  Lemma~\ref{L:translated-covariance-CS} has norm $N_{2,e}$. Its feature
  $h_\lambda(t,r)e^{i\xi\cdot x}$ and its proof apply in any finite dimension:
  their only spatial input is the finite measure $\mu_e$. Hence, its
  coefficient-one Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and invariance under simultaneous
  time and space translation apply here. All Fourier and Bochner interchanges at
  this cap are justified by the finite covariance mass times the relevant $L^1$
  masses.

  For one-sided spacetime convolution $F = G*H$, positivity and the
  Laplace--Fourier product give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:pairing-convolution-norms}
    \begin{split}
      N_0(F)&=N_0(G)N_0(H),\\
      N_{1,e}(F)&\le N_0(G)N_{1,e}(H),\\
      N_{2,e}(F)&\le N_0(G)N_{2,e}(H).
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  Indeed, $|\mathcal{L} G(\lambda,\xi)| \le N_0(G)$ for $\lambda \ge 0$ proves
  the first-norm estimate. For the second, zero-extend $H$ in time. Its
  translates have the same covariance Hilbert norm. Express $G*H$ as their
  Bochner integral against $G$ and apply Minkowski. Thus, every fused kernel
  satisfies all remaining edge hypotheses. No division by a norm is used.

  We use induction on $n$, beginning with the empty product at $n = 0$. With two
  vertices on one chain the integral is at most $N_0(G_{\rm
  first})N_{1,e}(G_{\rm last})$; with one on each chain it is at most their two
  second norms by covariance Cauchy--Schwarz. These also verify both
  nonempty-chain orientations at $n = 1$. Assume the full bound at $n-1$.

  We first prove a half-bound on any region where an endpoint $v$, its
  predecessor $p$ and its partner $a$ satisfy $s_a \le s_p$. Require the region
  indicator to be independent of $(s_v,x_v)$. Write $e_0 = \{a,v\}$. Since
  $\kappa_{e_0}$ is decreasing on positive arguments, replace
  $\kappa_{e_0}(s_v-s_a)$ by $\kappa_{e_0}(s_v-s_p)$. Integrating the endpoint
  and taking the modulus of its spatial Fourier phase gives the factor
  \[
    \int |\mathcal{L} G_v(\lambda,\xi)|
    \nu_{e_0}(\ud\lambda)\mu_{e_0}(\ud\xi)=N_{1,e_0}(G_v).
  \]
  Now drop the region indicator by positivity. Remove $v$ and its edge. Its
  partner $a$ is unpaired. If $a$ is terminal in its remaining chain, integrate
  it to $N_0(G_a)$ and apply induction. The result is one half of an admissible
  product, adding $a$ to $Q_0$ and $v$ to $Q_1$ with edge $e_0$.

  Otherwise, let $b$ be the immediate successor of $a$ on its own chain.
  Integration at $a$ gives exactly $F = G_a*G_b$ at $b$, also when $a$ was the
  first vertex. Apply induction to the two reduced chains and
  $D\setminus\{e_0\}$. Mark the slot $i = 0,1,2$ occupied by $F$ in a maximizing
  reduced product. Let $R_0,R_1,R_2$ be its other slots and $B_{\rm rest}$ their
  product, retaining every first-slot factor two. The new slots can be chosen as
  follows:
  \[
    \begin{array}{c|ccc}
      i&Q_0&Q_1&Q_2\\ \hline
      0&R_0\cup\{a,b\}&R_1\cup\{v\}&R_2\\
      1&R_0\cup\{a\}&R_1\cup\{b,v\}&R_2\\
      2&R_0\cup\{a\}&R_1\cup\{v\}&R_2\cup\{b\}.
    \end{array}
  \]
  For $i = 0$, $|R_0|+1 = |R_1|$ and $|R_2|$ is even. Mass multiplicativity
  gives one half of the displayed new product. For $i = 1$, $|R_0| = |R_1|+1$
  and $|R_2|$ is even. If $e_1$ is the inherited edge, the term and its bound
  are
  \begin{align*}
    &N_{1,e_0}(G_v)\,[2N_{1,e_1}(F)]B_{\rm rest}\\
    &\qquad\le\tfrac12N_0(G_a)
    [2N_{1,e_1}(G_b)][2N_{1,e_0}(G_v)]B_{\rm rest}.
  \end{align*}
  The inherited edge stays first exactly once; $e_0$ is first once. No incidence
  condition is needed. For $i = 2$, $|R_0| = |R_1|$ and $|R_2|$ is odd; $e_1$
  already occurs once there. The last inequality
  in~\eqref{E:pairing-convolution-norms} puts its other second norm on $G_b$, a
  mass on $G_a$ and $2N_{1,e_0}(G_v)$ on $v$, again with a factor one half.
  Every old edge retains its type and multiplicity. The displayed counts give
  $|Q_0| = |Q_1|$ and even $|Q_2|$ in all three cases. This proves the regional
  half-bound, using only the full induction hypothesis, not an assumed stronger
  induction.

  If an endpoint is paired to a vertex in its own chain, its partner is at or
  before its predecessor. Hence, this half-bound applies on the entire domain.
  This also handles an empty chain. If both endpoints $u = n_1$ and $v = 2n$ are
  paired to each other, condition on their separate predecessors $(A,z),(B,w)$.
  The endpoint integral keeps the temporal argument $r-q+A-B$ in relative times.
  The original stationary covariance Cauchy--Schwarz inequality bounds it by
  $N_{2,e}(G_u)N_{2,e}(G_v)$; translate each self pairing separately. Induction
  and adding $u,v$ to $Q_2$ prove the full bound. There is no origin factor or
  discarded predecessor-time offset.

  It remains that both endpoint pairs are cross and distinct. Let $a$ be the
  partner of $v$, $b$ the partner of $u$, and put $p = u-1$, $q = v-1$. Then $a
  < u$ and $b < v$. On $\{s_a\le s_q\}$ remove $v$ by the regional half-bound.
  Its indicator is independent of $s_v,x_v$. On the complementary region the
  chain orders give $s_p \ge s_a > s_q \ge s_b$, so remove $u$ instead; that
  indicator is independent of $s_u,x_u$. The removed partner has a successor on
  its own chain in both cases. The two bounds are $P/2$ and $P'/2$ for possibly
  different admissible products of the original labels. Therefore, their sum is
  at most $(P+P')/2 \le \max(P,P')$. This
  proves~\eqref{E:pairing-product-envelope} at the finite cap.

  Finally, increase $L$ to infinity. The diagram integrands and all norm factors
  increase to their declared values, by positivity and monotone convergence.
  There are only finitely many admissible products, so their maximum converges
  to the maximum of their limits. No kernel-independent or measurable maximizing
  choice is needed. Zero norms and measures require no exception; the positive
  cap convention avoids any undefined product on a singular time diagonal. This
  proves Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope}.
\end{proof}

\section{Spatial Wick measures and fixed-order construction}

Put $\gamma_b = p_b*\Gamma$, where $p_b$ is the Brownian heat kernel. Fix $m+n =
2k \ge 2$, the two chains $I_1,I_2$ above and $D \in \Pi_k$. For a gap vector
$\boldsymbol r \in (0,\infty)^{2k}$, let $s_l$ be the sum of the gaps up to
vertex $l$ in its own chain. Similarly, for spatial increments $z_l$, let $x_l$
be their cumulative sum in that chain. For positive spatial widths
$\boldsymbol\epsilon$ define
\[
  J_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}(\boldsymbol r)
  =\int\prod_{l=1}^{2k}G_{r_l}(\ud z_l)
  \prod_{\{j,h\}\in D}
  \gamma_{\epsilon_j+\epsilon_h}(x_j-x_h),\qquad
  \rho_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}(\ud\boldsymbol r)
  =J_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}(\boldsymbol r)\ud\boldsymbol r.
\]
These are nonnegative measures. They are locally finite because the smoothed
covariances are bounded and the Green masses are $r_l$. Write
$\nu_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D} = e^{-\sum_l r_l} \rho_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}$,
and let $\ell_i$ be the sum of the gaps in chain $i$, with value zero for an
empty chain.

\begin{lemma}[Positive spatial Wick-measure limit]\label{L:spatial-wick-measures}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, the finite measures
  $\nu_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}$ converge weakly, as all spatial widths tend to
  zero at arbitrary relative rates, to a finite measure $\nu_D$. The locally
  finite measure $\rho_D = e^{\sum_l r_l}\nu_D$ satisfies
  \[
    \rho_D(cA)=c^{(4-\alpha)k}\rho_D(A),\qquad c>0.
  \]
  It gives zero mass to every face $r_l = 0$ and every face $\ell_i = T > 0$.
  Consequently, for every bounded continuous $\varphi$ and $T_1,T_2 > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-wick-limit}
    \int_{\ell_1<T_1,\,\ell_2<T_2}\varphi\ud\rho_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}
    \longrightarrow
    \int_{\ell_1<T_1,\,\ell_2<T_2}\varphi\ud\rho_D.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Orient each pair $e = \{j,h\}$ as $j < h$, and let $\eta_l(\boldsymbol\xi)$ be
  the coefficient of $z_l$ in $\sum_{e=\{j,h\}}\xi_e\cdot(x_j-x_h)$. Finite
  heat-weighted spectral measures and the finite damped Green masses justify
  Fourier and Laplace Fubini. The wave Laplace transform gives, for $\lambda_l
  \ge 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:spatial-positive-laplace}
    \begin{split}
      L_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}(\boldsymbol\lambda)
      &:=\int e^{-\sum_l\lambda_l r_l}\ud\nu_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}\\
      &=\int\exp\left\{-\frac12\sum_{e=\{j,h\}}
      (\epsilon_j+\epsilon_h)|\xi_e|^2\right\}
      \prod_{l=1}^{2k}\frac1{(1+\lambda_l)^2+|\eta_l(\boldsymbol\xi)|^2}
      \prod_{e\in D}\mu(\ud\xi_e).
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  The integrand is nonnegative. Thus, the transforms increase when any spatial
  width decreases. This is transform monotonicity, not pointwise monotonicity of
  the physical covariances.

  We prove a uniform bound on their total masses. With $V = \mu(B_1)$, Fourier
  triangle and radial homogeneity give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:construction-heat-bound}
    0\le(p_t*\gamma_b)(x)\le A t^{-\alpha/2},\qquad t,b>0,
    \quad A=\alpha V\int_0^\infty u^{\alpha-1}e^{-u^2/2}\ud u<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  Indeed, its Fourier multiplier is $e^{-(t+b)|\xi|^2/2}$, whose integral is
  $A(t+b)^{-\alpha/2} \le At^{-\alpha/2}$. For a Brownian motion $B$ put $S_b(T)
  = \int_0^T\int_0^T\gamma_b(B_s-B_t)\ud s\ud t$. For every integer $j \ge 1$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:construction-brownian-moments}
    \E_B S_b(T)^j
    \le\frac{(2j)![A\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-\alpha/2)]^j}
    {\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}((2-\alpha/2)j+1)}T^{(2-\alpha/2)j}.
  \end{equation}
  To verify this, order the $2j$ time variables in the moment integral.
  Condition on all but the last remaining Brownian vertex. Its pair factor has
  conditional mean at most $A$ times the last remaining time gap to the power
  $-\alpha/2$, by~\eqref{E:construction-heat-bound}. Remove this vertex and its
  partner. The remaining factors do not involve either removed vertex, and their
  marginal law is again the Brownian law at the remaining times. Repeat this
  procedure. The $j$ selected right endpoints are distinct; each selected gap is
  at least the original adjacent gap at its right endpoint. On each ordered
  simplex the resulting bound is therefore $A^j$ times a product of $j$ distinct
  adjacent gaps to the power $-\alpha/2$. Integration over the simplex gives
  $\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-\alpha/2)^j
  T^{(2-\alpha/2)j}/\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}((2-\alpha/2)j+1)$. There are $(2j)!$
  possible orders. This proves the bound uniformly in $b$; the zeroth moment is
  one.

  Let $\epsilon_* = \min_l\epsilon_l$. The positive formula
  \eqref{E:spatial-positive-laplace} bounds $L_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}(0)$ by
  the transform with every vertex width equal to $\epsilon_*$. The identity of
  finite positive measures
  \[
    \int_0^\infty e^{-at}G_t(\ud x)\ud t
    =\frac12\int_0^\infty e^{-a^2u/2}p_u(x)\ud u\ud x
  \]
  follows by their equal Fourier transforms $(a^2+|\xi|^2)^{-1}$. Apply it to
  the $2k$ Green factors at unit damping. This writes the common-width transform
  as $2^{-2k}$ times a two-Brownian-chain integral with gap weights $e^{-\sum_l
  u_l/2}$ and covariance $\gamma_{2\epsilon_*}$ on every pair. If $\tau_i$ is
  the last Brownian time in chain $i$, insert
  \[
    1=\frac14\int_{\tau_1}^\infty\int_{\tau_2}^\infty
    e^{-((a-\tau_1)+(b-\tau_2))/2}\ud a\ud b.
  \]
  This also holds for an empty chain with $\tau_i = 0$. Positivity permits
  extension of the ordered Brownian time domains to their full cubes. At fixed
  paths the time integrations then factor into one occupation integral per pair.
  For independent Brownian paths, spectral Cauchy--Schwarz gives
  \[
    0\le A_{12}:=\int_0^a\int_0^b
    \gamma_{2\epsilon_*}(B_1(s)-B_2(t))\ud s\ud t
    \le\sqrt{S_{2\epsilon_*}^{(1)}(a)S_{2\epsilon_*}^{(2)}(b)}.
  \]
  If the pairing has $v_1,v_2$ self-pairs and $w$ cross-pairs, with $v_1+v_2+w =
  k$, its occupation product is at most
  \[
    S_1^{v_1+w/2}S_2^{v_2+w/2}\le(1+S_1)^k(1+S_2)^k.
  \]
  By~\eqref{E:construction-brownian-moments}, each expected factor is bounded by
  a fixed finite sum $M_k$ of nonnegative powers of its horizon, independently
  of $\epsilon_*$. Independence of the two paths now gives
  \[
    \sup_{\boldsymbol\epsilon>0}L_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}(0)
    \le2^{-2k-2}\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-a/2}M_k(a)\ud a\right)^2<\infty.
  \]

  Monotone convergence in~\eqref{E:spatial-positive-laplace} yields finite
  limits $L_D(\boldsymbol\lambda)$ for all $\boldsymbol\lambda \ge 0$. The
  limiting integrand at zero is integrable, so dominated convergence shows
  continuity at zero. Here is the finite-measure Laplace argument, including
  possible loss of mass. Push the measures to $[0,1]^{2k}$ by $y_l = e^{-r_l}$.
  Uniformly bounded masses on this compact cube have weak cluster limits. Their
  integer monomial moments are the prescribed $L_D$ values, so polynomial
  density determines a unique cluster limit. For $c > 0$ the integral of
  $\prod_l y_l^c$ is $L_D(c,\ldots,c)$. As $c\downarrow0$, continuity at zero
  and monotone convergence show that the cluster limit has no mass on the faces
  where any $y_l = 0$. Closed neighborhoods of the $y$-zero faces have limiting
  mass tending to zero; the portmanteau inequality bounds the approximating tail
  masses by these same quantities. Pulling back therefore proves weak
  convergence on $[0,\infty)^{2k}$. Bounded continuous test functions are
  handled by cutting off a neighborhood of those zero faces and then using the
  vanishing tail mass. Every sequence of widths tending to zero has this same
  limit, so the convergence is joint and independent of their relative rates.

  Sending just $\lambda_l \to \infty$ in the limiting positive integral
  \eqref{E:spatial-positive-laplace} gives zero by domination by its integrable
  zero-parameter integrand. On the measure side this limit is $\nu_D\{r_l=0\}$.
  Thus, every coordinate-zero face has zero mass.

  Heat and wave scaling give, for Borel sets,
  \[
    \rho_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}(cA)
    =c^{(4-\alpha)k}\rho_{\boldsymbol\epsilon/c^2,D}(A).
  \]
  This follows from $G_{cr}(c\ud z) = cG_r(\ud z)$, $\gamma_b(cx) =
  c^{-\alpha}\gamma_{b/c^2}(x)$, and the $2k$ time Jacobians. Passing to vague
  limits proves the stated homogeneity of $\rho_D$. If a compact part $A$ of
  $\{\ell_i=T\}$, $T > 0$, had positive mass, its dilates $cA$ at distinct $c
  \in [1,2]$ would be disjoint, have a common positive lower mass bound, and lie
  in one compact set. This contradicts local finiteness. Exhausting the face by
  compacts proves it has zero mass. For an empty chain the positive-level face
  is empty. The finite-horizon region is bounded, and its boundary is contained
  in these horizon faces and the coordinate-zero faces. Weak convergence with a
  compact cutoff, and the bounded continuous multiplier $e^{\sum r_l}\varphi$
  there, proves \eqref{E:spatial-wick-limit}. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures} removes only the spatial widths. We now
remove the temporal widths too and construct each fixed-order term.

\begin{lemma}[Joint diagram limits and fixed-order construction]
  \label{L:joint-fixed-order}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, every two-chain diagram with $m+n = 2k \ge 2$
  has a finite joint limit as all its positive vertexwise spatial and temporal
  widths tend to zero, at arbitrary relative rates. This includes unequal and
  empty chains. For every $n \ge 1,t > 0,x$, $Y_n^{\boldsymbol a}(t,x)$ has a
  unique joint $L^2$ limit, denoted $X_n(t,x)$; it is the multiple Stratonovich
  integral of $g_n$. Green smoothing in dimension three is removed before noise
  smoothing.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Use the gap variables and spatial measures of
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures}. With $s_j = s_j(\boldsymbol r)$ define,
  on $\ell_1 < T_1,\ell_2 < T_2$,
  \[
    F_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,\boldsymbol\delta,D}
    =\int\prod_{\{j,h\}\in D}
    \gamma^0_{\delta_j\vee\delta_h}(s_j-s_h)
    \ud\rho_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,D}.
  \]
  At a common temporal width $\eta > 0$ the multiplier is bounded and
  continuous. Hence, the spatial limit exists at every $T_1,T_2 > 0$
  by~\eqref{E:spatial-wick-limit}.

  We next bound removal of that temporal cutoff uniformly in all spatial widths.
  Write $F_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,0,D}$ for the integral with full factors
  $|s_j-s_h|^{-\alpha_0}$. It is finite at positive spatial widths: bound the
  spatial covariances by their finite suprema and the Green masses by their
  horizon bounds, then integrate the disjoint-pair time factors on the full time
  cube. Each factor $|s-r|^{-\alpha_0}$ is integrable since $\alpha_0 < 1$. The
  elementary difference-of-products inequality bounds
  $F_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,0,D}-F_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,\eta,D}$ by a sum of $k$
  nonnegative integrals, each with one marked time covariance $K_{1/\eta}$ and
  all other time covariances full.

  Apply Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope} to the damped kernels
  $e^{-s}G_s$. At first smooth the Green kernels in dimension three and cap the
  temporal Laplace domains above. Each spatial edge measure is a finite
  Gaussian-weighted $\mu$ with nonnegative physical covariance, and each
  temporal domain is a Laplace interval or its restriction. These are exactly
  the hypotheses of that lemma. The finite-horizon exponential loss is at most
  $e^{T_1+T_2}$. Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms} bounds all unmarked
  norms independently of the smoothing parameters. The marked edge appears once
  in the first-norm product or twice in the second-norm product in every
  admissible assignment. Consequently,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:joint-temporal-tail}
    0\le F_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,0,D}
    -F_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,\eta,D}
    \le C_{k,T_1,T_2}
    \left(N_1^{(1/\eta)}(1)+N_2^{(1/\eta)}(1)^2\right),
  \end{equation}
  uniformly in all spatial widths. Constants may depend on the fixed covariance
  and $k,T_1,T_2$; no uniformity in these quantities is needed. Let the upper
  temporal caps tend to infinity by positivity, and then remove Green smoothing
  at these fixed spatial widths. For the latter passage, the bounded spatial
  covariance products, weak convergence of Green measures and the integrable
  time-cube majorant just used give convergence. The bound survives both
  passages. Its right side tends to zero as $\eta\downarrow0$.

  Denote the fixed-$\eta$ spatial limit by $b_D(\eta)$. These numbers increase
  as $\eta$ decreases, and~\eqref{E:joint-temporal-tail} makes them Cauchy with
  a finite limit $b_D$. If every $\delta_j < \eta$, positivity gives
  \[
    F_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,\eta,D}
    \le F_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,\boldsymbol\delta,D}
    \le F_{\boldsymbol\epsilon,0,D}.
  \]
  Fix $\eta$, pass to the spatial limit in the lower term, and use the uniform
  remainder in~\eqref{E:joint-temporal-tail}; then let $\eta\downarrow0$. This
  proves the asserted joint limit $b_D$ with arbitrary spatial/temporal rates.
  If one chain is empty its horizon contributes no variables, and the same proof
  applies.

  At positive widths Wick's formula gives the mixed second moments of two copies
  of $Y_n$ as the finite sum of these diagrams, after reversing both terminal
  chains at their common horizon $t$. Evenness of the covariances absorbs the
  sign changes. Every such mixed moment, including either diagonal moment, tends
  to the same finite sum $Q_n = \sum_{D\in\Pi_n}b_D$. Hence,
  \[
    \|Y_n^{\boldsymbol a}-Y_n^{\boldsymbol b}\|_2^2
    \longrightarrow Q_n+Q_n-2Q_n=0.
  \]
  Completeness of $L^2$ gives a unique limit independent of all width ratios. At
  fixed positive noise widths the Green-width limit was already taken in the
  terminal construction, so the result has the required Green-before-noise
  convention. Restricting to common noise widths gives precisely the multiple
  Stratonovich definition for $g_n$. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms} gives constants $C_0 < \infty$ and

\begin{lemma}[Corrected crude estimate]\label{L:crude-corrected}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, for suitable $C < \infty$ and $\theta_0 > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:crude-corrected}
    \E X_n(t,x)^2\le e^{2\theta_0t}\frac{C^nt^{2n}}{n!}
    \quad(n\ge1,t>0),\qquad
    \E u^2(t,x)\le2e^{2\theta_0t+2Ct^2}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  On the two finite chains, the product of the exponential weights telescopes to
  $e^{-\theta(s_n+s_{2n})} \ge e^{-2\theta t}$. Extend the nonnegative integral
  to the two infinite time simplices and apply
  Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope}. For every admissible product, put $q_i
  = \#Q_i$. Then $q_0 = q_1$ and $q_0+q_2/2 = n$. Hence,~\eqref{E:crude-norms}
  bounds each pairing by $e^{2\theta t}C^n\theta^{-2n}$. Summing the $P_n$
  pairings gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:theta-envelope}
    \E X_n(t,x)^2\le P_n e^{2\theta t}C^n\theta^{-2n},
    \qquad\theta\ge\theta_0.
  \end{equation}
  For the finite-spectral-measure hypothesis in
  Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope}, first apply it with the spatially
  smoothed covariance and the temporal truncations of (2.24). The smoothed
  spectral measure is finite: the Gaussian multiplier is bounded by a constant
  times $(1+|\xi|^2)^{-(2-\alpha_0)/2}$, whose integral is finite by (1.5). This
  argument requires no radial density for the spectral measure. The zero norm is
  unchanged; the first and second norms are bounded by
  Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}: their Gaussian multipliers can be
  removed in the upper bounds for the nonnegative transforms and covariance
  quadratic forms. The bound is uniform in these regularizations. In $d = 3$,
  first use $G_\eta = p_\eta*G$, as defined on pp.~13--14 of the source. Its
  spatial Fourier multiplier is bounded by one and its spatial mass equals that
  of $G$, so the same bounds are uniform in $\eta$ as well. For each fixed noise
  regularization $(\epsilon,\delta)$, first let $\eta\downarrow0$ in the
  source's $L^2$ definition. The squared moments converge, so the inequality
  survives this first limit. Then let $(\epsilon,\delta) \to 0$ using
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}. In $d = 1,2$ only this latter limit is
  needed. These limits transfer a uniform inequality; no pointwise delta
  covariance bound is being used.

  Choose $\theta = \theta_0+n/t$ in~\eqref{E:theta-envelope}. Since $(2n)! \le
  (2n)^{2n}$, we have $P_n n^{-2n} \le 2^n/n!$. Absorb $2^ne^{2n}$ in $C^n$ to
  obtain the first bound in~\eqref{E:crude-corrected}. Its square roots are
  summable for each fixed $t$, by the ratio test. Thus, the norm series
  converges independently of Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}. By Minkowski and
  Cauchy--Schwarz,
  \[
    \begin{split}
      \|u(t,x)\|_2
      &\le e^{\theta_0t}\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(\sqrt C t)^n}{\sqrt{n!}},\\
      \left(\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\right)^2
      &\le\left(\sum_{n\ge0}2^{-n}\right)
      \left(\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(2z^2)^n}{n!}\right)
      =2e^{2z^2}.
    \end{split}
  \]
  This proves Lemma~\ref{L:crude-corrected}.
\end{proof}

\section{The mild equation with the corrected bound}

Lemma~\ref{L:crude-corrected} states bounds for the limits $X_n$ and their
sum. We now bound the regularized terms uniformly in all positive vertexwise
widths; Proposition~\ref{P:mild-construction} uses this bound.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform vertexwise regularization bound]\label{L:vertex-crude}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, there are $C < \infty$ and $\theta_0 > 0$ such
  that, for all positive vertexwise widths and $n \ge 1$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:vertex-crude}
    \|Y_n^{\boldsymbol a}(t,x)\|_2\le q_n(t),\qquad
    q_n(t):=e^{\theta_0t}\frac{C^{n/2}t^n}{\sqrt{n!}}.
  \end{equation}
  The bound also holds for $X_n(t,x)$, with $q_0(t) = e^{\theta_0t} \ge 1$. For
  each $T < \infty$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:vertex-summability}
    \sum_{n\ge0}\sqrt{n+1}\,q_n(T)<\infty.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Wick's formula \cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.19), p.~12}, with
  the joint covariance (2.11), gives the square of $Y_n^{\boldsymbol a}$, with
  the widths copied to the second chain. Its edge covariances are
  \[
    \gamma^0_{\delta_j\vee\delta_l}(s_j-s_l)
    \gamma_{\epsilon_j+\epsilon_l}(y_j-y_l).
  \]
  On each terminal chain use $s'_j = t-s_{n+1-j}$ and $y'_j = x-y_{n+1-j}$. This
  reverses the chain and only permutes its vertex widths; covariance differences
  change sign and the kernels are even. For each pairing apply
  Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope} to $e^{-\theta r}G_r$, first with Green
  smoothing if $d = 3$. The lemma permits a separate finite spectral measure and
  temporal truncation on each edge. These hypotheses hold here: the spatial
  spectral measure on an edge is
  $e^{-(\epsilon_j+\epsilon_l)|\xi|^2/2}\mu(\ud\xi)$, which is finite by (1.5),
  and its temporal Laplace domain is $[0,(\delta_j\vee\delta_l)^{-1}]$.

  All edge norms obey~\eqref{E:crude-norms}, uniformly in their widths. For the
  first norm the Laplace--Fourier transform of the damped Green kernel is
  positive, and the spatial Gaussian multiplier and temporal truncation can only
  decrease its nonnegative spectral integral. For the second norm first enlarge
  the temporal covariance to $|s-r|^{-\alpha_0}$, using positivity of the Green
  kernels and the smoothed spatial covariance. Its space-time spectral
  expression is an integral of the squared absolute transform against
  nonnegative measures, so removal of the spatial Gaussian multiplier increases
  it. Green smoothing has multiplier at most one and unchanged mass, so the same
  bounds hold before its removal. These assertions follow from
  Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}, rather than an imported normalization
  or large-parameter formula in the source's Appendix. The constants are
  independent of all the vertex widths.

  Since $\#Q_0 = \#Q_1$ and $\#Q_0+\#Q_2/2 = n$ for every admissible product,
  exactly as in~\eqref{E:theta-envelope} we obtain the following bound. Indeed,
  put $D = \max\{1,2C_0\}$. The norm constants contribute at most
  $D^{\#Q_1+\#Q_2} \le D^{2n}$, so we may take $C_1 = D^2$:
  \[
    \|Y_n^{\boldsymbol a}(t,x)\|_2^2
    \le P_n e^{2\theta t}C_1^n\theta^{-2n},\qquad\theta\ge\theta_0.
  \]
  Let the Green width tend to zero at these fixed positive noise widths, and
  choose $\theta = \theta_0+n/t$. The inequality $P_n n^{-2n} \le 2^n/n!$ then
  proves \eqref{E:vertex-crude}, after increasing $C$.
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order} gives the joint noise-width limit $X_n$; the
  same bound passes to it. Finally,
  \[
    \frac{\sqrt{n+2}\,q_{n+1}(T)}
    {\sqrt{n+1}\,q_n(T)}
    =\sqrt C\,T\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{n+1}\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  The ratio test proves~\eqref{E:vertex-summability}. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:vertex-crude}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Mild construction from the fixed-order limits]
  \label{P:mild-construction}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, the series $u(t,x) = \sum_{n\ge0}X_n(t,x)$ is
  a mild solution in~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{Definition~2.1 and
  (2.12), pp.~9--10}. More precisely, for each $t > 0$ and $x \in \R^d$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:mild-construction}
    \lim_{\epsilon,\delta\downarrow0}
    \int_0^t\int_{\R^d}G(t-s,x-\ud y)
    u(s,y)W_{\epsilon,\delta}(s,y)\ud s
    =u(t,x)-1\quad\text{in }L^2(\Omega).
  \end{equation}
  The limit allows arbitrary relative rates of the two widths. In dimension
  three the Green-width limit precedes the noise-width limit. No uniqueness
  assertion is made.
\end{proposition}

For $d = 3$ we read the source's generalized-integrand definition using its
Green regularization, with the random factor held fixed. The proof below
establishes that limit for the product $uW_{\epsilon,\delta}$.

\begin{proof}
  Let $\mathscr{P}_{\le n}$ be the closed Gaussian polynomial space of total
  degree at most $n$, generated by all the coupled source fields. It includes
  all lower-degree contractions. The fixed-width terminal integrals lie in this
  space, by their Bochner construction; their fixed-order $L^2$ limits $X_n$ do
  too.

  We first record an elementary multiplication bound on these spaces. If $H$ is
  any centered linear Gaussian variable and $F \in \mathscr{P}_{\le n}$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:occupied-multiplication}
    \|HF\|_2\le2\sqrt{n+1}\,\|H\|_2\|F\|_2.
  \end{equation}
  If $H = 0$ the assertion is immediate. Otherwise take $H/\|H\|_2$ as the first
  coordinate of an orthonormal Gaussian basis. On normalized Hermite polynomials
  its creation and annihilation operators have norms at most $\sqrt{n+1}$ and
  $\sqrt n$ on total degrees at most $n$: the squared coefficient sums have
  respective weights $a_1+1 \le n+1$ and $a_1 \le n$. Their sum proves the bound
  for finite-coordinate polynomials. Approximation in the degree-closed space
  proves it for $F$ as well. Indeed, the products of an approximating sequence
  are $L^2$ Cauchy by this bound; subsequences converging almost surely identify
  their limit with $HF$. This also proves the closedness of multiplication
  needed below. The argument allows $F$ to depend on $H$.

  Lemma~\ref{L:vertex-crude} proves absolute $L^2$ convergence of the defining
  series for $u$, locally uniformly in time in the displayed norm bounds. All
  fields are strongly measurable as $L^2(\Omega)$ vectors. At positive widths,
  rescale the ordered times and Green increments to fixed unit-time domains. The
  Gaussian evaluations are jointly measurable, and their bounded moments and the
  finite Green masses give Bochner domination on bounded time intervals.
  Parameter integration therefore gives joint strong measurability in $(s,y)$.
  Pointwise $L^2$ limits along a fixed countable sequence of widths give this
  property for $X_n$, then for the series. For later Green limits we also need
  spatial continuity at each fixed time. Positive-width terminal terms are
  $L^2$-continuous in space, by continuity of their bounded smoothed covariance
  products and dominated integration against the finite Green measures. Joint
  spatial stationarity of the coupled regularizations shows that
  \[
    \|Y_n^{\boldsymbol b}(s,y)-X_n(s,y)\|_2
    =\|Y_n^{\boldsymbol b}(s,0)-X_n(s,0)\|_2.
  \]
  Thus, their convergence is uniform in $y$ for fixed $s$. Consequently,
  $X_n(s,\cdot)$, and by summability $u(s,\cdot)$, have continuous $L^2$
  representatives.

  Fix an external width $a = (\epsilon,\delta) > 0$ and write $W_a =
  W_{\epsilon,\delta}$, with variance $V_a =
  \gamma^0_\delta(0)\gamma_{2\epsilon}(0) < \infty$. For $n \ge 1$ define the
  Bochner integral
  \[
    I_{n,a}(t,x)=\int_0^t\int_{\R^d}G(t-s,x-\ud y)
    W_a(s,y)X_{n-1}(s,y)\ud s.
  \]
  It exists because~\eqref{E:occupied-multiplication} gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:fixed-external-domination}
    \|W_a(s,y)X_{n-1}(s,y)\|_2
    \le2\sqrt n\sqrt{V_a}\,q_{n-1}(T),\qquad0\le s\le T,
  \end{equation}
  and the spatial Green mass is $t-s$. At fixed $a$, multiplication of the inner
  regularizations by $W_a$ preserves their $L^2$ convergence, by the same bound
  on degree at most $n-1$. Their norms have the uniform domination just used.
  Dominated Bochner integration therefore gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:partial-vertex-limit}
    I_{n,a}(t,x)=\lim_{\boldsymbol b\downarrow0}
    Y_n^{(\boldsymbol b,a)}(t,x)\quad\text{in }L^2.
  \end{equation}
  Here $a$ is at the last, terminal vertex and $\boldsymbol b$ at the preceding
  $n-1$ vertices. At positive widths this identity before passage to the limit
  is ordinary terminal-kernel Fubini; its moment bound was established before
  Lemma~\ref{L:vertex-crude}. For $n = 1$ there are no inner widths and the
  identity is exact. In particular,~\eqref{E:vertex-crude} passes to this inner
  limit:
  \begin{equation}\label{E:mixed-vertex-bound}
    \sup_{a>0}\|I_{n,a}(t,x)\|_2\le q_n(t).
  \end{equation}

  We spell out the dimension-three convention in these steps. The product
  $W_a(s,y)X_{n-1}(s,y)$ is $L^2$-continuous in $y$.
  Apply~\eqref{E:occupied-multiplication} first to
  $W_a(s,y)(X_{n-1}(s,y)-X_{n-1}(s,y'))$ and then to
  $(W_a(s,y)-W_a(s,y'))X_{n-1}(s,y')$. The two factors tend to zero in the
  required norms, by the continuity just proved and the smoothed covariance of
  $W_a$. The bound~\eqref{E:fixed-external-domination} is uniform in $y$.
  Therefore, convolution against $p_\eta*G_{t-s}$ converges in $L^2$ to
  convolution against $G_{t-s}$: on a compact set approximate this continuous
  vector function by a finite continuous partition of unity times fixed vector
  values, use scalar weak convergence, and control the complement by tightness
  and the uniform bound. The mass $t-s$ and~\eqref{E:fixed-external-domination}
  permit dominated time integration. This proves the Green-width limit at fixed
  external widths. The same argument applies before the inner noise limit; all
  $Y_n^{(\boldsymbol b,a)}$ already use their Green limit at positive noise
  widths. Thus,~\eqref{E:partial-vertex-limit} does not interchange the source's
  Green and noise limits.

  For each fixed $n$, the joint fixed-order convergence in
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order} implies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:external-vertex-limit}
    \lim_{a\downarrow0}I_{n,a}(t,x)=X_n(t,x)\quad\text{in }L^2.
  \end{equation}
  To see the role of joint convergence, fix $\zeta > 0$ and choose $r > 0$ so
  that every vertex width below $r$ gives $\|Y_n^{(\boldsymbol b,a)}-X_n\|_2 <
  \zeta$. Fix any external pair $a$ below $r$ and let the inner widths tend to
  zero in~\eqref{E:partial-vertex-limit}. Then $\|I_{n,a}-X_n\|_2 \le \zeta$.
  This proves the asserted limit with arbitrary relative rates; it does not
  assume a partial-limit theorem.

  It remains to identify the full product integral, rather than only its
  finite-order terms. At fixed $a$, \eqref{E:occupied-multiplication}
  and~\eqref{E:vertex-summability} imply
  \[
    \sum_{n\ge1}\|W_a(s,y)X_{n-1}(s,y)\|_2
    \le2\sqrt{V_a}\sum_{n\ge1}\sqrt n\,q_{n-1}(T)<\infty,
    \qquad0\le s\le T.
  \]
  Multiplication is closed: partial sums of $u$ converge in $L^2$, and their
  products converge by this estimate, so an almost surely convergent subsequence
  identifies the product limit with $W_a u$. Dominated Green integration and
  norm summability now prove
  \[
    I_a(t,x):=\int_0^t\int_{\R^d}G(t-s,x-\ud y)
    W_a(s,y)u(s,y)\ud s
    =\sum_{n\ge1}I_{n,a}(t,x)\quad\text{in }L^2.
  \]
  The sum of the product integrands is spatially $L^2$-continuous by uniform
  summability, so its Green-width limit is justified in exactly the same way.
  Finally, for every $N$,
  \[
    \|I_a(t,x)-(u(t,x)-1)\|_2
    \le\sum_{n=1}^N\|I_{n,a}(t,x)-X_n(t,x)\|_2
    +2\sum_{n>N}q_n(t).
  \]
  First let $a\downarrow0$ using~\eqref{E:external-vertex-limit}, then let $N
  \to \infty$ using~\eqref{E:vertex-summability}. This
  proves~\eqref{E:mild-construction}, hence the mild equation in the stated
  definition. The source's (5.3)--(5.4) and its Section~5(ii) identification
  proof have not been used. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:mild-construction}.
\end{proof}

Thus, the exponent two enters through replacing the paired norm products by
$C\theta^{-2}$, followed by the pairing count and the order summation. The sum
over unequal chain lengths is not responsible for this loss: the same loss
already occurs in the diagonal estimate and Minkowski's inequality.

\section{What homogeneity recovers, and what the bound still loses}

Under homogeneity the norms scale exactly in $\theta$. We now use this
scaling in place of~\eqref{E:crude-norms}.

\begin{proposition}[The uniform pairing estimate]\label{P:envelope}
  Under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, there is $K > 0$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{E:homogeneous-envelope}
    \E[X_m(t,x)X_n(t,x)]
    \le P_k K^k e^{2\theta t}\theta^{-\beta k},
    \qquad m+n=2k\ge2,\quad\theta>0.
  \end{equation}
  Define the numerical majorant produced by applying this estimate separately at
  every total order by
  \begin{equation}\label{E:B-envelope}
    B_K(t)=1+\sum_{k\ge1}(2k+1)P_kK^k
    \inf_{\theta>0}\{e^{2\theta t}\theta^{-\beta k}\}.
  \end{equation}
  Then $\E u^2(t,x) \le B_K(t)$ and, for some $c,C > 0$ and all large $t$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:envelope-growth}
    ct^{\rho}\le\log B_K(t)\le Ct^{\rho},\qquad
    \rho=\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}
    =\frac{4-\alpha-\alpha_0}{3-\alpha-\alpha_0}>\kappa.
  \end{equation}
  The lower bound here concerns the numerical majorant $B_K$, not the second
  moment.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The wave kernel scales as $G(cs,c\ud y) = cG(s,\ud y)$, and the covariance has
  degree $-\alpha$. Changing all time and spatial variables by $\theta^{-1}$ in
  the norms yields
  \begin{equation}\label{E:homogeneous-norms}
    N_0(\theta)=\theta^{-2},\quad
    N_1(\theta)=N_1(1)\theta^{\alpha+\alpha_0-2},\quad
    N_2(\theta)^2=N_2(1)^2\theta^{-\beta}.
  \end{equation}
  All constants and scalings follow directly from
  Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}. The products $2N_0N_1$ and $N_2^2$
  therefore both have degree $-\beta$. Choose $K \ge \max\{2N_1(1),N_2(1)^2\}$,
  increasing it to a positive constant if needed.
  Lemma~\ref{L:pairing-product-envelope} and $q_0+q_2/2 = k$ give
  $K^k\theta^{-\beta k}$ for every admissible product. The finite horizons
  contribute at most $e^{2\theta t}$. Apply this first to regularized moments.
  In $d = 3$, fix $(\epsilon,\delta)$ and let $\eta\downarrow0$ in each factor's
  $L^2$ limit; Cauchy--Schwarz gives $L^1$ convergence of their product. Then
  let $(\epsilon,\delta) \to 0$ in the same way, using
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order} for each chain. In $d = 1,2$ only the noise
  limit is required. The estimates are uniform at both stages, by the multiplier
  bounds in Lemma~\ref{L:crude-corrected}. This
  proves~\eqref{E:homogeneous-envelope}. Empty-chain cases satisfy the same
  bound (using $e^{2\theta t}$ is harmless). Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} now gives
  the majorant.

  The infimum in~\eqref{E:B-envelope} is attained at $\theta = \beta k/(2t)$ and
  equals $e^{\beta k}(2t/(\beta k))^{\beta k}$. Put $q = \beta-1 > 1$. The
  elementary estimates
  \[
    k!\le P_k\le2^k k!,\qquad
    (k/e)^k\le k!\le k^k,\qquad 2k+1\le3^k
  \]
  show that, for fixed positive $a,b$ depending only on $K,\beta$,
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(at^\beta)^k}{(k!)^q}
    \le B_K(t)\le
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(bt^\beta)^k}{(k!)^q}.
  \]
  Indeed, the additional factor $k!^\beta/k^{\beta k}$ lies between $e^{-\beta
  k}$ and one and can be absorbed into $a^k,b^k$. For $z > 0$, the right-hand
  series is at most $(\sum_{k\ge0}z^{k/q}/k!)^q = e^{qz^{1/q}}$, by the
  elementary $\ell^q$ versus $\ell^1$ inequality for nonnegative sequences. For
  a lower bound choose $k = \lfloor z^{1/q}/2\rfloor$ and use $k! \le k^k$; its
  single summand is at least $2^{qk}$. These estimates
  prove~\eqref{E:envelope-growth}. Finally, $\beta-1 = 3-\alpha-\alpha_0 <
  3-\alpha$, so $\rho > \kappa$. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:envelope}.
\end{proof}

\section{A static phase estimate at first variance order}
\label{S:static-phase}

A time-block argument would have to carry both position and velocity. We
establish a uniform estimate for the first variance coefficient with arbitrary
deterministic initial phase. The covariance may be any member of the original
three-dimensional class; no endpoint representation is assumed. The estimate
below is not an all-order propagator bound.
No-go~\ref{P:nogo-14} shows why scalar solution-value moments cannot be
multiplied across blocks. No-go~\ref{P:nogo-15} shows why both the first
random term and the second-order mean enter the phase energy coefficient.



Let $0 < \alpha < 2$, and let $\Gamma$ be a nonnegative, even, positive-definite
tempered Radon covariance on $\R^3$, satisfying $\Gamma(rA) =
r^{3-\alpha}\Gamma(A)$. Its spectral convention is $\Gamma = \int e^{i\xi\cdot
x}\mu(\ud\xi)$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{E:phase-constants}
  \begin{split}
    V&=\mu(B_1),\qquad M=\Gamma(B_1),\qquad
    R_\alpha=\frac{\alpha\pi V}{2\sin(\pi\alpha/2)},\\
    c_\Gamma&=\frac{(3-\alpha)M}{4\pi},\qquad
    b_\alpha=2^\alpha+\frac{3^{2-\alpha}}{2-\alpha},\qquad
    D_\Gamma=c_\Gamma b_\alpha.
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $B_1$ is the open unit ball in the relevant variable. The physical mass $M$
and the spectral mass $V$ are not identified. Let $p_\epsilon$ be the Brownian
Gaussian density with covariance $\epsilon I$, and set $\Gamma_\epsilon =
p_{2\epsilon}*\Gamma$.

\LeanMarker{L:phase-contractions}
\begin{lemma}[Two static contraction bounds]\label{L:phase-contractions}
  For $\lambda > 0$, $q \in \R^3$, $a > 0$, and $\epsilon > 0$,
  \begin{align}
    \int_{\R^3}\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{\lambda^2+|q+\xi|^2}
    &\le R_\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-2},
    \label{E:phase-resolvent}\\
    \left|T_\epsilon(a,q)\right|
    &\le D_\Gamma a^{1-\alpha},
    \label{E:phase-trace}\\
    T_\epsilon(a,q)
    &:=\int_{\R^3}e^{iq\cdot x}\Gamma_\epsilon(x)G_a(\ud x)
    =\int_{\R^3}\widehat{G}_a(q+\xi)e^{-\epsilon|\xi|^2}
    \mu(\ud\xi).\nonumber
  \end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Homogeneity and Fourier uniqueness give $\mu(B_r) = Vr^\alpha$. In particular,
  there is no origin atom. For $s > 0$, Schwartz Fourier pairing yields
  \[
    \int e^{-s|q+\xi|^2}\mu(\ud\xi)
    =\int p_{2s}(x)e^{iq\cdot x}\Gamma(\ud x)
    \le\int p_{2s}(x)\Gamma(\ud x)
    =\int e^{-s|\xi|^2}\mu(\ud\xi).
  \]
  The integrals are finite. Evenness makes the middle integral real, and
  nonnegativity of $\Gamma$ gives the inequality. Multiply by $e^{-\lambda^2s}$
  and integrate in $s$. Nonnegative Tonelli on the spectral side gives the
  resolvent comparison. At $q = 0$ its value is
  \[
    \alpha V\int_0^\infty\frac{r^{\alpha-1}}{\lambda^2+r^2}\ud r
    =R_\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-2}.
  \]
  This proves~\eqref{E:phase-resolvent}.

  Since $\mu$ is tempered, its Gaussian weighting is finite. Fourier Fubini
  therefore gives the identity for $T_\epsilon$. The measure
  $\Gamma_\epsilon(x)G_a(\ud x)$ is finite and nonnegative, whence
  $|T_\epsilon(a,q)| \le T_\epsilon(a,0)$. The radial distribution function of
  $\Gamma$ is $\Gamma(B_r) = Mr^{3-\alpha}$. Thus, for every nonnegative radial
  Schwartz function $F$,
  \[
    \int F(x)\Gamma(\ud x)
    =(3-\alpha)M\int_0^\infty F(r)r^{2-\alpha}\ud r
    =c_\Gamma\int_{\R^3}F(x)|x|^{-\alpha}\ud x.
  \]
  Apply this to $F = G_a*p_{2\epsilon}$, which is radial and Schwartz.
  Nonnegative Fubini gives
  \[
    T_\epsilon(a,0)
    =c_\Gamma[p_{2\epsilon}*(G_a*|\cdot|^{-\alpha})](0)
    \le c_\Gamma\sup_z(G_a*|\cdot|^{-\alpha})(z).
  \]
  Write $r = |z|$. The spherical wave measure has mass $a$, and direct
  integration over its normal coordinate gives, for $r > 0$,
  \[
    (G_a*|\cdot|^{-\alpha})(z)
    =\frac{(a+r)^{2-\alpha}-|a-r|^{2-\alpha}}
    {2r(2-\alpha)}.
  \]
  For $r < a/2$, distances from $z$ to the sphere are at least $a/2$, giving the
  bound $2^\alpha a^{1-\alpha}$. For $r > 2a$, they are at least $a$, giving
  $a^{1-\alpha}$. On $a/2 \le r \le 2a$, the displayed formula is at most
  $3^{2-\alpha}a^{1-\alpha}/(2-\alpha)$. These bounds also cover the endpoints
  by continuity and give~\eqref{E:phase-trace}. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:phase-contractions}.
\end{proof}

Let $A_\lambda = \lambda^2-\Delta$, and equip deterministic phase pairs with the
norm
\begin{equation}\label{E:phase-norm}
  \|(f,g)\|_\lambda^2
  =\|f\|_2^2+\|A_\lambda^{-1/2}g\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Let $C_t$ be the Fourier multiplier $\cos(t|\xi|)$. On one static isonormal
Gaussian space with covariance $\Gamma$, use $W_\epsilon(x) =
W(p_\epsilon(x-\cdot))$. Its covariance is exactly $\Gamma_\epsilon(x-y)$, with
spectral measure $\mu_\epsilon(\ud\xi) = e^{-\epsilon|\xi|^2}\mu(\ud\xi)$. For
real Schwartz $f,g$, define the free position $w_0(t) = C_tf+G_t*g$ and its
phase $Z_0(t) = (w_0(t),\dot{w}_0(t))$. The next two regularized static Duhamel
phases are
\begin{equation}\label{E:phase-duhamel}
  Z_{j+1}(t)
  =\int_0^t
  \begin{pmatrix}
    G_{t-s}*(W_\epsilon Z_{j,u}(s))\\
    C_{t-s}(W_\epsilon Z_{j,u}(s))
  \end{pmatrix}\ud s,\qquad j=0,1,
\end{equation}
where $Z_{j,u}$ is the position component. At fixed $\epsilon > 0$ these are
Gaussian-polynomial Bochner integrals in the indicated $L^2$ phase space.

\begin{proposition}[First variance estimate for arbitrary phase]
  \label{P:static-phase-variance}
  Let $N = \|(f,g)\|_\lambda$. Uniformly in $\epsilon > 0$,
  \begin{align}
    \E\|Z_1(h)\|_\lambda^2
    &\le R_\alpha h^2\lambda^{\alpha-2}
    (1+\lambda^2h^2)^2N^2,\label{E:phase-born}\\
    \|\E Z_2(h)\|_\lambda
    &\le\frac{D_\Gamma(1+\lambda^2h^2)h^{3-\alpha}}
    {\lambda(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}N.
    \label{E:phase-mean}
  \end{align}
  In particular, put $\lambda = h^{-1}$ and let $K_\epsilon$ be the coefficient
  of $\sigma^2$ in the finite polynomial $\E\|Z_0(h)+\sigma
  Z_1(h)+\sigma^2Z_2(h)\|_\lambda^2$. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:phase-variance}
    |K_\epsilon|
    \le\left(4R_\alpha+
    \frac{4\sqrt e\,D_\Gamma}{(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}\right)
    h^{4-\alpha}\|(f,g)\|_{1/h}^2.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We use the unnormalized spatial Fourier transform and Plancherel measure
  $(2\pi)^{-3}\ud\eta$. At fixed positive smoothing, Gaussian second and fourth
  moments make~\eqref{E:phase-duhamel} integrable: $G_t$ has finite positive
  mass, $C_t$ is an $L^2$ contraction, and $\E|W_\epsilon(x)W_\epsilon(y)|^2 \le
  3\Gamma_\epsilon(0)^2$. These facts, Minkowski and time integration justify
  the Gaussian and Fourier Fubini steps below first for Schwartz data.

  Set $a_s(\eta) = \widehat{w}_0(s,\eta)$ and
  \[
    e_\lambda(\eta)=|\widehat{f}(\eta)|^2+
    \frac{|\widehat{g}(\eta)|^2}{\lambda^2+|\eta|^2}.
  \]
  Cauchy--Schwarz on the two initial coordinates gives
  \[
    |a_s(\eta)|^2\le(1+\lambda^2s^2)e_\lambda(\eta),
  \]
  because the squared norm of their coefficient vector is
  $\cos^2(sq)+(1+\lambda^2/q^2)\sin^2(sq) \le 1+\lambda^2s^2$, with the
  continuous value at $q = 0$. For the two-component output multiplier put
  \[
    k_\tau(r)=\left(\frac{\sin(\tau r)}r,
    \frac{\cos(\tau r)}{\sqrt{\lambda^2+r^2}}\right).
  \]
  Its Euclidean squared norm is
  \[
    |k_\tau(r)|^2
    =\frac{1+\lambda^2\sin^2(\tau r)/r^2}{\lambda^2+r^2}
    \le\frac{1+\lambda^2h^2}{\lambda^2+r^2},\qquad 0\le\tau\le h.
  \]
  Expanding the covariance of the two copies of $W_\epsilon$, followed by
  spatial Plancherel, gives exactly
  \[
    \E\|Z_1(h)\|_\lambda^2
    =\frac1{(2\pi)^3}\int\ud\eta\int\mu_\epsilon(\ud\xi)
    \left|\int_0^h k_{h-s}(|\eta+\xi|)a_s(\eta)\ud s\right|^2.
  \]
  Cauchy--Schwarz in time bounds the last squared norm by
  \[
    \frac{h^2(1+\lambda^2h^2)^2}
    {\lambda^2+|\eta+\xi|^2}e_\lambda(\eta).
  \]
  Now $\mu_\epsilon \le \mu$ and Lemma~\ref{L:phase-contractions}
  give~\eqref{E:phase-born}.

  The contraction inside the mean of $Z_2$ is the convolution operator with
  kernel $G_{s-r}(\ud x)\Gamma_\epsilon(x)$. Indeed, this follows directly from
  $\E[W_\epsilon(x)W_\epsilon(y)] = \Gamma_\epsilon(x-y)$ in the two Duhamel
  integrals. Its Fourier multiplier is $T_\epsilon(s-r,\eta)$, up to the
  immaterial even sign. Its $L^2$ operator norm is therefore at most
  $D_\Gamma(s-r)^{1-\alpha}$. Under the isometry
  $(u,v)\mapsto(u,A_\lambda^{-1/2}v)$, $\|k_\tau(|D|)\|_{L^2\to L^2\oplus L^2}
  \le \sqrt{1+\lambda^2h^2}/\lambda$ and $\|w_0(r)\|_2 \le
  \sqrt{1+\lambda^2h^2}N$. Minkowski and
  \[
    \int_0^h\ud s\int_0^s(s-r)^{1-\alpha}\ud r
    =\frac{h^{3-\alpha}}{(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}
  \]
  prove~\eqref{E:phase-mean}.

  Finally, the free phase obeys $\|Z_0(h)\|_\lambda \le e^{\lambda h/2}N$. To
  see this, for Schwartz free data differentiate its squared norm. The $-\Delta$
  terms cancel, leaving $2\lambda^2\operatorname{Re}\langle
  A_\lambda^{-1}\dot{w}_0,w_0\rangle$, whose absolute value is at most
  $\lambda\|Z_0\|_\lambda^2$. Gronwall gives the stated bound. The required
  coefficient is exactly
  \[
    K_\epsilon=\E\|Z_1(h)\|_\lambda^2+
    2\operatorname{Re}\langle Z_0(h),\E Z_2(h)\rangle_\lambda.
  \]
  Substituting $\lambda = h^{-1}$ into the two estimates proves
  \eqref{E:phase-variance}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-variance}.
\end{proof}

The second term in $K_\epsilon$ must be retained; neither its sign nor
monotonicity of the full phase energy in covariance is assumed. The estimates
control both terms at this one variance order. They do not control higher
Gaussian contractions, products of independent time blocks, overlapping noise
levels or the temporal regularization limit. Thus, they do not yet establish
Theorem~\ref{T:intro-upper}.

\section{All static orders with arbitrary initial phase}
\label{S:static-all-orders}

We now seek all static Duhamel orders, retaining the full spatial covariance
class of Section~\ref{S:static-phase}. The temporal covariance is not included
in this section. Static estimates alone do not identify the evolution for
overlapping intervals; that step is treated in the next section.

We use one external operator theorem. From Crouzeix--Palencia
\cite{crouzeix.palencia:source2017:numerical}*{author preprint v1, (3), p.~1,
and Theorem~6, pp.~5--6}, if $T$ is a bounded operator on a complex Hilbert
space and $F$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline{W(T)}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{E:cp-input}
  \|F(T)\|\le(1+\sqrt2)\sup_{w\in W(T)}|F(w)|,\qquad
  W(T)=\{\langle Tu,u\rangle:\|u\|=1\}.
\end{equation}
The theorem is imported, not proved here. All operators to which we apply it
below are bounded; no unbounded-operator extension is assumed.

\LeanMarker{L:complex-phase}
\begin{lemma}[A complex phase bound]\label{L:complex-phase}
  Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space, $A_0$ a bounded nonnegative selfadjoint
  operator, and $B$ a bounded selfadjoint operator. Write $A_\rho = \rho^2+A_0$.
  Suppose that, for $0 < a < 1$ and $d > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:abstract-sandwich}
    \|A_\rho^{-1/2}BA_\rho^{-1/2}\|\le d\rho^{a-2},
    \qquad \rho>0.
  \end{equation}
  Put
  \[
    I_a=\int_0^\infty s^{-1/2}(1+s)^{(a-3)/2}\ud s,\qquad
    \delta_a=\min\{1/4,\pi/(8I_a)\}.
  \]
  For $\lambda > 0$ and $|z| \le \delta_a\lambda^{2-a}/d$, let $L = A_0-zB$. The
  phase propagator of $u''+Lu = 0$ satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:complex-phase-bound}
    \|\mathcal{M}_z(h)\|_{X_\lambda\to X_\lambda}
    \le100e^{2\lambda h},\qquad
    \|(f,g)\|_{X_\lambda}^2
    =\|f\|^2+\|A_\lambda^{-1/2}g\|^2,\quad h\ge0.
  \end{equation}
  The constant is independent of $H$, $\|A_0\|$ and $\|B\|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Set $\delta = \delta_a$ and $L_\lambda = L+\lambda^2$. For $\kappa =
  (\lambda^2+t)^{1/2}$,
  \[
    L_\lambda+t
    =A_\kappa^{1/2}(1-zK_\kappa)A_\kappa^{1/2},\qquad
    K_\kappa=A_\kappa^{-1/2}BA_\kappa^{-1/2}.
  \]
  Since $a-2 < 0$, $\|zK_\kappa\| \le \delta$. Thus, these operators are
  invertible and
  \[
    \|A_\kappa^{1/2}(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}\|
    \le\frac1{(1-\delta)\kappa}.
  \]
  Also $\operatorname{Re}\langle L_\lambda u,u\rangle \ge (1-\delta)\langle
  A_\lambda u,u\rangle$.

  Principal inverse square roots are defined on the right half-plane. The scalar
  identity $w^{-1/2} = \pi^{-1}\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}(w+t)^{-1}\ud t$, and
  bounded holomorphic calculus, give
  \[
    Q_z-1
    =\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2}
    \big[(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}-(A_\lambda+t)^{-1}\big]\ud t,
    \qquad Q_z=A_\lambda^{1/2}L_\lambda^{-1/2}.
  \]
  The integrals converge in operator norm. Indeed, the resolvent identity,
  $\|A_\lambda^{1/2}A_\kappa^{-1/2}\| \le 1$, and the following ordered
  factorization give the required integrand bound:
  \[
    \begin{split}
      &A_\lambda^{1/2}
      \big[(L_\lambda+t)^{-1}-A_\kappa^{-1}\big]\\
      &\qquad=A_\lambda^{1/2}A_\kappa^{-1/2}
      (1-zK_\kappa)^{-1}zK_\kappa A_\kappa^{-1/2}.
    \end{split}
  \]
  The four factors have norms at most $1$, $(1-\delta)^{-1}$, $|z|d\kappa^{a-2}$
  and $\kappa^{-1}$, respectively. Thus, the integrand without $t^{-1/2}/\pi$ is
  bounded by
  \[
    \frac{|z|d}{1-\delta}\kappa^{a-3}.
  \]
  Consequently,
  \[
    \|Q_z-1\|
    \le\frac{|z|d\lambda^{a-2}I_a}{\pi(1-\delta)}
    \le\frac16.
  \]
  In particular, $Q_z$ and $Q_z^{-1}$ have norm at most two. Apply the same
  argument to $L^* = A_0-\overline zB$ and take adjoints. It follows that all
  four operators
  \begin{equation}\label{E:phase-root-comparisons}
    A_\lambda^{1/2}L_\lambda^{-1/2},\quad
    L_\lambda^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2},\quad
    L_\lambda^{1/2}A_\lambda^{-1/2},\quad
    A_\lambda^{-1/2}L_\lambda^{1/2}
  \end{equation}
  have norm at most two. This is a quantitative comparison at the bounded
  cutoffs, not an assumed square-root domain identity.

  We next control the numerical range. For $\|u\| = 1$ put $s = \|A_0^{1/2}u\|$.
  Optimizing~\eqref{E:abstract-sandwich} at $\rho = s$ gives $|\langle
  Bu,u\rangle| \le 2ds^a$. If $s = 0$, the same conclusion follows by letting
  $\rho\downarrow0$. Separately, evaluating~\eqref{E:abstract-sandwich} at $\rho
  = \lambda$ gives
  \[
    |\langle Bu,u\rangle|
    \le d\lambda^{a-2}(s^2+\lambda^2),\qquad
    |z\langle Bu,u\rangle|\le\delta(s^2+\lambda^2).
  \]
  This is the estimate for the real part; the optimized estimate controls the
  imaginary part. Thus, for $w = x+iy = \langle Lu,u\rangle$,
  \[
    x+\lambda^2\ge(1-\delta)(s^2+\lambda^2),\qquad
    |y|\le2\delta\lambda^{2-a}s^a.
  \]
  Since $s^{2a}\lambda^{2-2a} \le s^2+\lambda^2$,
  \[
    y^2\le\frac{4\delta^2}{1-\delta}\lambda^2(x+\lambda^2)
    \le\frac13\lambda^2(x+\lambda^2),\qquad
    x\ge-\lambda^2/4.
  \]
  Hence, $|w| \le x+2\lambda^2$, and either square root of $w$ has
  $|\operatorname{Im}\sqrt w| \le \lambda$.

  The scalar functions
  \[
    C_h(w)=\cos(h\sqrt w),\qquad
    F_h(w)=\sqrt{w+\lambda^2}\frac{\sin(h\sqrt w)}{\sqrt w},\qquad
    H_h(w)=\frac{w}{\sqrt{w+\lambda^2}}
    \frac{\sin(h\sqrt w)}{\sqrt w}
  \]
  are holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline{W(L)}$. The values at $w = 0$
  are removable; the only square-root branch required is that of $w+\lambda^2$,
  whose real part is positive. On the numerical range,
  \[
    |C_h(w)|\le e^{\lambda h},\qquad
    |F_h(w)|\le2(1+\lambda h)e^{\lambda h},\qquad
    |H_h(w)|\le2e^{\lambda h}.
  \]
  Indeed, $|\operatorname{Im}\sqrt w| \le \lambda$ gives $|\cos(h\sqrt
  w)|,|\sin(h\sqrt w)| \le e^{\lambda h}$. The entire identity $\sin(h\sqrt
  w)/\sqrt w = \int_0^h\cos(r\sqrt w)\ud r$ also gives the bound $he^{\lambda
  h}$, including $w = 0$. If $|w| \le \lambda^2/2$, then
  \[
    |F_h(w)|\le\sqrt{3/2}\,\lambda h e^{\lambda h}.
  \]
  If $|w| > \lambda^2/2$, then $|w+\lambda^2|/|w| \le 3$, so $|F_h(w)| \le
  \sqrt3 e^{\lambda h}$. These bounds imply the displayed estimate for $F_h$.
  Finally,
  \[
    \frac{|w|}{|w+\lambda^2|}
    \le1+\frac{\lambda^2}{|w+\lambda^2|}
    \le1+(1-\delta)^{-1}\le7/3,
  \]
  and $H_h(w) = \sqrt w\sin(h\sqrt w)/\sqrt{w+\lambda^2}$ gives its stated
  bound. All identities have their removable values at zero. Applying the
  imported~\eqref{E:cp-input} gives bounds $3e^{\lambda h}$, $6(1+\lambda
  h)e^{\lambda h}$ and $6e^{\lambda h}$ for these three operator functions.

  Under the isometry $(u,v)\mapsto(u,A_\lambda^{-1/2}v)$, the four phase blocks
  are
  \[
    C_h(L),\quad
    F_h(L)L_\lambda^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2},\quad
    -A_\lambda^{-1/2}L_\lambda^{1/2}H_h(L),\quad
    A_\lambda^{-1/2}L_\lambda^{1/2}C_h(L)
    L_\lambda^{-1/2}A_\lambda^{1/2}.
  \]
  This is the usual matrix
  $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}C_h(L)&G_h(L)\\-LG_h(L)&C_h(L)\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
  with $G_h(w) = \sin(h\sqrt w)/\sqrt w$, after weighting. In the order
  displayed, the four block norms are at most
  \[
    3e^{\lambda h},\qquad
    12(1+\lambda h)e^{\lambda h},\qquad
    12e^{\lambda h},\qquad12e^{\lambda h}.
  \]
  For example, the last block has two root-comparison factors of norm at most
  two, with $C_h(L)$ between them. Only functions of $L$ commute; no commutation
  with $A_\lambda$ is used in these ordered products. A block operator on
  $H\oplus H$ has norm at most the sum of its four block norms, by the triangle
  inequality and the norm-one coordinate inclusions and projections. Therefore,
  its norm is at most
  \[
    (39+12\lambda h)e^{\lambda h}
    \le39(1+\lambda h)e^{\lambda h}\le100e^{2\lambda h}.
  \]
  The phase operator itself is the exponential of the bounded block generator
  with rows $(0,1)$ and $(-L,0)$. It is entire in $z$; the intermediate square
  roots are used to estimate it, not to impose a branch-dependent definition of
  that entire operator. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:complex-phase}.
\end{proof}

We use the standard Gaussian creation and annihilation algebra; see
Janson~\cite{janson:97:gaussian}*{Section~13.2, Theorem~13.7, pp.~200--201}.
The following calculation records the normalization used for the finite
occupation spaces in this argument.

\LeanMarker{L:fock-form}
\begin{lemma}[Gaussian cutoff form bound]\label{L:fock-form}
  Retain the covariance and $R_\alpha$ of Section~\ref{S:static-phase}. Fix
  $\epsilon > 0$. Represent the smoothed real field on independent standard
  Gaussian coordinates as $W_\epsilon(x) = \sum_{i\ge1}g_i e_i(x)$ in
  $L^2(\Omega)$. Let $P_N$ be projection onto Gaussian Hermite degrees at most
  $N$, and $S_b$ spatial Fourier projection onto $|\eta| \le b$. For finite $m$,
  on their joint range set
  \[
    B_{N,m,b}=P_NS_b\Big(\sum_{i=1}^m g_i M_{e_i}\Big)S_bP_N,\qquad
    A_\rho=\rho^2-\Delta .
  \]
  These are bounded selfadjoint operators and, uniformly in $\epsilon,m,b$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:fock-sandwich}
    \|A_\rho^{-1/2}B_{N,m,b}A_\rho^{-1/2}\|
    \le d_N\rho^{\alpha/2-2},\qquad
    d_N=2\sqrt{N+1}\sqrt{R_\alpha}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Choose an orthonormal basis of the real isonormal space, which is separable,
  and write $e_i(x) = \E[W_\epsilon(x)g_i]$. Then $\sum_i e_i(x)^2 =
  \Gamma_\epsilon(0) = :v_\epsilon$, and $|e_i(x)| \le \sqrt{v_\epsilon}$. Thus,
  each multiplication $M_{e_i}$ is bounded and selfadjoint. Put $H_i =
  A_\rho^{-1/2}M_{e_i}A_\rho^{-1/2}$ on spatial $L^2$. Gaussian isometry and
  spatial Plancherel give
  \[
    \sum_i\|H_i f\|_2^2
    =\frac1{(2\pi)^3}\int
    \frac{|\widehat{f}(\eta)|^2}{\rho^2+|\eta|^2}
    J_{\rho,\epsilon}(\eta)\ud\eta,\qquad
    J_{\rho,\epsilon}(\eta)
    =\int\frac{e^{-\epsilon|\xi|^2}\mu(\ud\xi)}
    {\rho^2+|\eta+\xi|^2}.
  \]
  This is the covariance calculation for $A_\rho^{-1/2}W_\epsilon
  A_\rho^{-1/2}f$. By Lemma~\ref{L:phase-contractions}, $J_{\rho,\epsilon} \le
  R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}$. Therefore, $\sum_i H_i^2 \le
  R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-4}I$ in quadratic forms. Finite sums have the same upper
  bound. Compression to $S_b$ preserves it: for selfadjoint $H_i$,
  $(S_bH_iS_b)^2 \le S_bH_i^2S_b$ on its range. Both row and column variances
  are the same, since these operators are selfadjoint.

  For completeness, use normalized Hermite polynomials, so multiplication by
  $g_i$ is $a_i^*+a_i$, with $g_i h_k =
  \sqrt{k_i+1}\,h_{k+e_i}+\sqrt{k_i}\,h_{k-e_i}$. On degree $k$, the row map
  $(v_i)\mapsto\sum_i a_i^*v_i$ has norm $\sqrt{k+1}$: its product with its
  adjoint is $\sum_i a_i^*a_i = (k+1)I$ on degree $k+1$. Composing with the
  column of spatial operators $S_bH_iS_b$ bounds the creation part by
  $\sqrt{k+1}\sqrt{R_\alpha}\rho^{(\alpha-4)/2}$. Distinct input degrees have
  orthogonal output degrees. The annihilation part is its adjoint, with the same
  bound after truncation. Adding them on degrees at most $N$ proves
  \eqref{E:fock-sandwich}. Boundedness of $-\Delta$ on $S_b$ is explicit; no
  infinite-frequency operator theorem was used. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:fock-form}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite Gaussian products and their degree cutoffs}

Lemma~\ref{L:fock-form} bounds the field only on Hermite degrees at most $N$.
We now check that this cutoff does not change the finite words that we need.

\LeanMarker{L:finite-word-cutoff}
\begin{lemma}[Exact cutoffs for finite Gaussian words]\label{L:finite-word-cutoff}
  Let $g_{ij}$, $1 \le i \le M$, $1 \le j \le m_i$, be independent standard real
  Gaussian coordinates, grouped by color $i$, and let $\mathscr{X}$ be a complex
  Hilbert space. On Gaussian polynomials with coefficients in $\mathscr{X}$ set
  \[
    J_i=\sum_{j=1}^{m_i}M_{g_{ij}}\otimes K_{ij},
  \]
  where each $K_{ij}$ is bounded on $\mathscr{X}$. Let $F_0,\ldots,F_n$ be
  bounded operators acting only on $\mathscr{X}$. Fix a word $i_1,\ldots,i_n$,
  and let $r_i$ be the number of its occurrences of color $i$. If $N_i \ge r_i$
  and $P$ projects each color onto total Hermite degree at most $N_i$, then, for
  every $v \in \mathscr{X}$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:finite-word-cutoff}
    \begin{split}
      &(I\otimes F_n)(PJ_{i_n}P)(I\otimes F_{n-1})\cdots
      (PJ_{i_1}P)(I\otimes F_0)(1\otimes v)\\
      &\qquad=(I\otimes F_n)J_{i_n}(I\otimes F_{n-1})\cdots
      J_{i_1}(I\otimes F_0)(1\otimes v).
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  The equality holds for finite sums and for Bochner integrals of such words
  whenever the displayed vector integrals exist.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  In normalized Hermite coordinates, multiplication by $g_{ij}$ maps
  $h_{\boldsymbol a}$ to $\sqrt{a_{ij}+1}\,h_{\boldsymbol a+e_{ij}}
  +\sqrt{a_{ij}}\,h_{\boldsymbol a-e_{ij}}$; the second term is zero if $a_{ij}
  = 0$. Thus, it changes the total degree of color $i$ by one and leaves all
  other color degrees unchanged. The operators $I\otimes F_l$ leave every
  Gaussian degree unchanged. After the first $l$ insertions, the uncompressed
  word therefore has color-$i$ degree at most $r_i(l) = |\{j\le l:i_j=i\}| \le
  r_i \le N_i$. This is an induction starting from degree zero in every color.
  Consequently, $P$ is the identity both before and after every insertion along
  that word. Induction on $l$ now proves \eqref{E:finite-word-cutoff}. The
  lowering term has been retained: the assertion includes creation followed by
  annihilation and all resulting self-contractions. Linearity and equality of
  the vector integrands prove the last assertion. This is an equality of
  selected finite words, not of full propagators at a fixed cutoff. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:finite-word-cutoff}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform bounds for all orders}

Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-variance} controls only the first variance
coefficient. We now combine Lemmas~\ref{L:complex-phase}, \ref{L:fock-form}
and~\ref{L:finite-word-cutoff} to bound every static Duhamel order.

\LeanMarker{P:static-phase-all-orders}
\begin{proposition}[All regularized static phase orders]
  \label{P:static-phase-all-orders}
  Retain the full covariance class of Section~\ref{S:static-phase}. For real
  Schwartz $f,g$, let $Z_n^\epsilon(h)$ be the $n$th regularized static Duhamel
  phase, with the recursion \eqref{E:phase-duhamel} for every $n \ge 0$. This is
  an ordinary Gaussian polynomial of degree at most $n$, including
  self-contractions; it is not a pure Wick chaos. There is an explicit finite
  constant
  \[
    A_\alpha=\frac{400\sqrt2\,e^2}{\delta_{\alpha/2}}
  \]
  such that, for $n \ge 1$, uniformly in $\epsilon > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:all-static-phase-orders}
    \|Z_n^\epsilon(h)\|_{L^2(\Omega;X_{1/h})}
    \le
    \frac{(A_\alpha\sqrt{R_\alpha})^n
    h^{(4-\alpha)n/2}}{(n!)^{(3-\alpha)/2}}
    \|(f,g)\|_{1/h}.
  \end{equation}
  At every fixed positive smoothing and real coupling $\sigma$,
  $\sum_{n\ge0}\sigma^nZ_n^\epsilon(h)$ converges in $L^2$ phase. Its squared
  norm is at most
  \begin{equation}\label{E:all-static-phase-sum}
    (\sqrt e+2)^2
    \exp\left\{6A_\alpha^{2/(3-\alpha)}
    (|\sigma|^2R_\alpha)^{1/(3-\alpha)}
    h^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}\right\}\|(f,g)\|_{1/h}^2.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  If $R_\alpha = 0$, spectral homogeneity gives $\mu = 0$, and every
  positive-order term is zero. Assume $R_\alpha > 0$. First work at finite
  Gaussian cutoff $m$, degree cutoff $N$ and spatial band $b$, with initial
  phase $S_b(f,g)$ times the Gaussian vacuum $1$. All relevant operators are
  bounded. Lemmas~\ref{L:fock-form} and~\ref{L:complex-phase}, with $a =
  \alpha/2$, give a bound $100e^{2\lambda h}$ on the complex coupling circle of
  radius $\delta_{\alpha/2}\lambda^{2-\alpha/2}/d_N$.

  The four phase entries are entire in the coupling, by the power series for
  $\cos(h\sqrt L)$ and $\sin(h\sqrt L)/\sqrt L$ of a bounded $L$. Cauchy's
  formula for their operator-valued series bounds the $n$th coefficient by
  \[
    100e^{2\lambda h}
    \left(\frac{d_N}
    {\delta_{\alpha/2}\lambda^{2-\alpha/2}}\right)^n
    \|S_b(f,g)\|_\lambda .
  \]
  The coefficient is exactly the $n$th Duhamel phase from the vacuum. Apply
  Lemma~\ref{L:finite-word-cutoff} with one color, $N = n$, the spatial phase
  Hilbert space as $\mathscr{X}$, and its free wave operators as the $F_l$. It
  identifies each cutoff Duhamel word exactly with its uncompressed word,
  including self-contractions. Dominated time integration preserves this
  identity.

  Choose $\lambda = n/h$. For any phase $z$, $\|z\|_{1/h} \le n\|z\|_{n/h}$ and
  $\|(f,g)\|_{n/h} \le \|(f,g)\|_{1/h}$. Also $d_n \le
  2\sqrt{2n}\sqrt{R_\alpha}$. The preceding bound is therefore at most
  \[
    100n\left(\frac{2\sqrt2 e^2}{\delta_{\alpha/2}}\right)^n
    R_\alpha^{n/2}h^{(4-\alpha)n/2}
    n^{-(3-\alpha)n/2}\|(f,g)\|_{1/h}.
  \]
  Since $100n \le 200^n$ and $n! \le n^n$, this gives
  \eqref{E:all-static-phase-orders} at the cutoffs.

  We justify removal of those cutoffs at fixed $\epsilon$ and $n$. On Gaussian
  degrees at most $N$, the unweighted field multiplication has norm at most
  $2\sqrt{N+1}\sqrt{v_\epsilon}$, uniformly in $m$, by the same
  creation/annihilation argument applied pointwise in $x$. It follows that the
  noise phase insertion $(u,v)\mapsto(0,W_\epsilon u)$, between the required
  finite degree levels, is bounded on $X_\lambda$ by this constant divided by
  $\lambda$. Free phase evolution is bounded by $e^{\lambda h/2}$. For finite
  $m$, $S_b \to I$ strongly and commutes with the free propagator. Each fixed
  Duhamel word therefore converges strongly as $b \to \infty$, by dominated time
  integration.

  Next let $m \to \infty$ on the full Gaussian space. The truncated field
  multiplications converge strongly between finite degree levels. To see this,
  first take a vector depending on finitely many Gaussian coordinates. The
  annihilation tail is then zero, while the creation tail is bounded by a
  constant times
  \[
    \left(\int\sum_{i>m}e_i(x)^2\|F(x)\|^2\ud x\right)^{1/2},
  \]
  which tends to zero by dominated convergence, since the sum is bounded by
  $v_\epsilon$. Such vectors are dense; the uniform multiplication bound extends
  strong convergence to all vectors in those degree levels. Dominated time
  integration again gives convergence of every fixed word. The limit is
  precisely $Z_n^\epsilon$, so the estimate survives both limits. We assert no
  zero-smoothing phase limit in this argument.

  Finally, put $\beta = (3-\alpha)/2 \in (1/2,3/2)$ and $x =
  A_\alpha|\sigma|\sqrt{R_\alpha}h^{(4-\alpha)/2}$. The elementary series
  satisfies
  \[
    \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{x^n}{(n!)^\beta}
    \le2e^{3x^{1/\beta}}.
  \]
  For $\beta \ge 1$, this follows from $\sum a_n^\beta \le (\sum a_n)^\beta$
  with $a_n = x^{n/\beta}/n!$. For $\beta < 1$, apply H\"older to
  $((2x^{1/\beta})^n/n!)^\beta (2^{-\beta n/(1-\beta)})^{1-\beta}$. The second
  geometric sum is at most two. Since $\|Z_0(h)\|_{1/h} \le \sqrt
  e\|(f,g)\|_{1/h}$, absolute $L^2$ summation and squaring give
  \eqref{E:all-static-phase-sum}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-all-orders}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{C:static-interaction}
\begin{corollary}[Summable static interaction bounds]
  \label{C:static-interaction}
  Let $\mathcal{S}_0(t)$ be free phase evolution and, for $\lambda h \le 1$,
  define the static interaction map by its convergent series
  \[
    \mathcal{I}^\epsilon_\sigma(h)(f,g)
    =\mathcal{S}_0(-h)\sum_{n\ge0}\sigma^n Z_n^\epsilon(h).
  \]
  Put $\gamma = 3-\alpha$, $B_\alpha = 3eA_\alpha^2$ and
  \[
    \begin{split}
      L_\alpha(h,\lambda)
      &=4eR_\alpha h^2\lambda^{\alpha-2}
      +\frac{4\sqrt eD_\Gamma}{(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}
      \frac{h^{3-\alpha}}{\lambda},\\
      x&=B_\alpha\sigma^2R_\alpha h^{4-\alpha},\\
      \mathcal{E}(h,\sigma,\lambda)
      &=\sigma^2L_\alpha(h,\lambda)
      +(\lambda h)^{-2}x^2e^{\gamma x^{1/\gamma}}.
    \end{split}
  \]
  Uniformly in $\epsilon > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-interaction}
    \E\|\mathcal{I}^\epsilon_\sigma(h)(f,g)\|_\lambda^2
    \le[1+\mathcal{E}(h,\sigma,\lambda)]\|(f,g)\|_\lambda^2.
  \end{equation}
  If $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$ and $\alpha+\alpha_0 < 2$, then, for $h_j =
  2^{-j}/\lambda$, $\sigma_j^2 = h_j^{-\alpha_0}$ and $T > 0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-block-series}
    \sum_{j\ge0}\frac{T}{h_j}\mathcal{E}(h_j,\sigma_j,\lambda)<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  For a finite single partition, let $\mathcal{M}_j(h_j)$ be the static phase
  maps with mutually independent copies of the smoothed field, real couplings
  $\sigma_j$, and $\lambda h_j \le 1$. They have continuous $L^2$ extensions to
  independent random initial phases. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:independent-static-blocks}
    \E\|\mathcal{M}_K(h_K)\cdots\mathcal{M}_1(h_1)(f,g)\|_\lambda^2
    \le
    \exp\left\{\lambda\sum_{j=1}^K h_j+
    \sum_{j=1}^K\mathcal{E}(h_j,\sigma_j,\lambda)\right\}
    \|(f,g)\|_\lambda^2.
  \end{equation}
  This concerns independent successive blocks on one partition.
  Formula~\eqref{E:static-block-series} only sums individual upper bounds across
  dyadic levels; it does not identify a product with an evolution driven by
  overlapping or repeated fields.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Write $N = \|(f,g)\|_\lambda$ and $Y_n = \mathcal{S}_0(-h)Z_n^\epsilon(h)$.
  The free estimate from Section~\ref{S:static-phase} holds for both time
  directions, since its differentiated energy was bounded in absolute value.
  Thus, $\|\mathcal{S}_0(-h)\|_\lambda \le \sqrt e$. Since $Y_0 = (f,g)$,
  Proposition~\ref{P:static-phase-variance} bounds the complete first variance
  coefficient of $\E\|\sum\sigma^nY_n\|_\lambda^2$ in absolute value by
  $L_\alpha(h,\lambda)N^2$. In detail, its first random squared norm is bounded
  by $e$ times~\eqref{E:phase-born}, and its second-order mean cross term by
  $2N\sqrt e$ times~\eqref{E:phase-mean}.

  For $n \ge 1$, the norm comparisons when $\lambda h \le 1$
  and~\eqref{E:all-static-phase-orders} give
  \[
    \|Y_n\|_{L^2(\Omega;X_\lambda)}
    \le\frac{\sqrt e}{\lambda h}
    \frac{(A_\alpha\sqrt{R_\alpha})^n h^{(4-\alpha)n/2}}
    {(n!)^{\gamma/2}}N.
  \]
  Gaussian symmetry makes $\E\langle Y_r,Y_s\rangle_\lambda = 0$ if $r+s$ is
  odd. All double series below converge absolutely by these bounds. For $r+s =
  2k$, the elementary factorial inequality $r!s! \ge (k!)^2$ shows that the sum
  of absolute inner products is at most
  \[
    \frac{e(2k+1)}{(\lambda h)^2}
    \frac{(A_\alpha^2R_\alpha)^k h^{(4-\alpha)k}}
    {(k!)^\gamma}N^2,\qquad k\ge1.
  \]
  This includes $r = 0$ or $s = 0$, since $\lambda h \le 1$ and $\|Y_0\| = N$.
  The bound $e(2k+1) \le (3e)^k$ therefore controls all orders $k \ge 2$ by
  \[
    \frac1{(\lambda h)^2}
    \sum_{k\ge2}\frac{x^k}{(k!)^\gamma}N^2
    \le\frac{x^2e^{\gamma x^{1/\gamma}}}{(\lambda h)^2}N^2.
  \]
  Here $\gamma > 1$, and $\sum_{j\ge0}x^j/(j!)^\gamma \le e^{\gamma
  x^{1/\gamma}}$. Keeping the sharper first coefficient separately proves
  \eqref{E:static-interaction}.

  Finally, set $\beta = 4-\alpha-\alpha_0 > 2$. After multiplication by $T/h_j$,
  the two first-coefficient terms in~\eqref{E:static-block-series} are constant
  multiples of
  \[
    T\lambda^{\alpha-2}h_j^{1-\alpha_0},\qquad
    \frac T\lambda h_j^{2-\alpha-\alpha_0}.
  \]
  The higher-order term is
  \[
    \frac{T(B_\alpha R_\alpha)^2}{\lambda^2}
    h_j^{2\beta-3}
    \exp\{\gamma(B_\alpha R_\alpha)^{1/\gamma}h_j^{\beta/\gamma}\}.
  \]
  The exponential factor is bounded by its value at $h_0$. All three powers are
  positive, so their geometric series converge. For the finite partition, write
  $\mathcal{M}_j(h_j) = \mathcal{S}_0(h_j)\mathcal{I}_j(h_j)$.
  Bound~\eqref{E:static-interaction} and $\|\mathcal{S}_0(h_j)\|_\lambda^2 \le
  e^{\lambda h_j}$ give a bounded linear map from deterministic $X_\lambda$ to
  $L^2(\Omega_j;X_\lambda)$, initially on Schwartz phases and then by density.
  Tensoring with the past probability space extends this bound to random initial
  phases independent of the $j$th field. Iteration gives the product of
  $e^{\lambda h_j}(1+\mathcal{E}(h_j,\sigma_j,\lambda))$. The inequality $1+y
  \le e^y$ for $y \ge 0$ proves \eqref{E:independent-static-blocks}. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:static-interaction}.
\end{proof}

The independent-block estimate does not use log-convexity in additive
covariance amplitudes. That unrestricted interpolation principle already
fails for a scalar Gaussian wave; see No-go~\ref{P:nogo-11}.


These estimates concern individual static phase and interaction maps. Their
summability alone does not identify the evolution driven by overlapping interval
fields. The coefficient argument in Section~\ref{S:interval-upper} supplies the
separate estimate needed for that evolution.

\section{Interval fields and the full upper bound}
\label{S:interval-upper}

The intrinsic constant is identified independently in
Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.

Write
\[
  \nu=3-\alpha>1,\qquad
  \beta=4-\alpha-\alpha_0>2,\qquad p=2-\alpha_0>1.
\]
Thus, $\beta-\nu = 1-\alpha_0 > 0$. In this section the spatial dimension is any
admissible $d \in \{1,2,3\}$. The spatial covariance is the nonnegative, even,
positive-definite tempered Radon measure $\Gamma$, with scaling $\Gamma(rA) =
r^{d-\alpha}\Gamma(A)$ and spectral convention $\Gamma = \int e^{i\xi\cdot
x}\mu(\ud\xi)$. This includes $\Gamma = \delta_0$ when $d = \alpha = 1$. Set
\[
  R_\alpha=\frac{\alpha\pi\mu(B_1)}{2\sin(\pi\alpha/2)},\qquad
  A_\rho=\rho^2-\Delta,\qquad
  \|(f,g)\|_\rho^2=\|f\|_2^2+\|A_\rho^{-1/2}g\|_2^2.
\]
Complex Hilbert inner products are linear in the first slot.

\subsection{Contractions and complex phase estimates}

The resolvent estimate in Lemma~\ref{L:phase-contractions} and the Gaussian
cutoff bound in Lemma~\ref{L:fock-form} were stated for a covariance on
$\R^3$. We now derive the needed column and finite-degree bounds for every
admissible $d$.

\LeanMarker{L:interval-contractions}
\begin{lemma}[Dimension-independent Gaussian contractions]
  \label{L:interval-contractions}
  Uniformly in positive spatial smoothing and in finite Gaussian-mode and
  spatial Fourier-band cutoffs,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:interval-column}
    \sum_m\|A_\rho^{-1/2}M_{e_m}f\|_2^2
    \le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\|f\|_2^2.
  \end{equation}
  Here $W_\epsilon(x) = \sum_m g_me_m(x)$ is an orthonormal-coordinate
  representation of $W(p_\epsilon(x-\cdot))$, whose covariance is
  $\Gamma_\epsilon = p_{2\epsilon}*\Gamma$. Consequently, on a Gaussian color
  that is initially in its vacuum, the phase insertion
  $(u,v)\mapsto(0,W_\epsilon u)$ has norm at most
  $\sqrt{R_\alpha}\rho^{(\alpha-2)/2}$ into its Gaussian $L^2$ phase. With
  Hermite degree of this color truncated at $N$, its sandwiched field has
  operator norm at most
  \begin{equation}\label{E:interval-fock}
    2\sqrt{N+1}\sqrt{R_\alpha}\rho^{\alpha/2-2}.
  \end{equation}
  Both statements remain valid tensoring with any other Hilbert factor.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Fourier uniqueness gives $\mu(B_r) = r^\alpha\mu(B_1)$. The Gaussian
  comparison in Lemma~\ref{L:phase-contractions} uses no dimension-specific
  identity:
  \[
    \int e^{-s|\eta+\xi|^2}\mu(\ud\xi)
    =\int p_{2s}^{(d)}(x)e^{i\eta\cdot x}\Gamma(\ud x)
    \le\int e^{-s|\xi|^2}\mu(\ud\xi).
  \]
  Evenness gives a real integral, and physical nonnegativity gives the
  inequality. Integrating against $e^{-\rho^2s}\ud s$ and using radial spectral
  homogeneity yields
  \[
    \sup_\eta\int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{\rho^2+|\eta+\xi|^2}
    \le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}.
  \]
  No exclusion of a physical origin atom is used.

  Gaussian isometry and unnormalized spatial Plancherel give
  \[
    \sum_m\|A_\rho^{-1/2}M_{e_m}f\|_2^2
    =\frac1{(2\pi)^d}\int|\widehat{f}(\eta)|^2
    \left(\int\frac{e^{-\epsilon|\xi|^2}\mu(\ud\xi)}
    {\rho^2+|\eta+\xi|^2}\right)\ud\eta .
  \]
  This proves~\eqref{E:interval-column}. A finite mode sum is a positive
  sub-sum. For a Fourier-band projection $S_b$, which commutes with $A_\rho$,
  use $S_bA_\rho^{-1}S_b \le A_\rho^{-1}$ to obtain the same inequality for
  $S_bM_{e_m}S_b$. The phase assertion on a new color follows because
  $\sum_mg_m(0,M_{e_m}u)$ has squared norm given by the left side
  of~\eqref{E:interval-column}, and its input is independent of this color.

  Substituting $A_\rho^{-1/2}f$ gives
  \[
    \sum_m(A_\rho^{-1/2}M_{e_m}A_\rho^{-1/2})^2
    \le R_\alpha\rho^{\alpha-4}I.
  \]
  These spatial sandwiches are selfadjoint, so their row and column variances
  agree. The normalized Hermite creation-row norm from degree $j$ to degree
  $j+1$ is $\sqrt{j+1}$, and annihilation is its adjoint, as proved in
  Lemma~\ref{L:fock-form}. Composition with the spatial column and addition of
  the two parts prove~\eqref{E:interval-fock}. The same positive operator
  identities hold with an identity on every other Hilbert factor. The
  calculation used only the radial mass $\mu(B_1)$, homogeneity and physical
  nonnegativity. Thus, $R_\alpha$ is the only covariance quantity in these
  estimates. No angular bound or unsmoothed multiplication norm has been
  absorbed into a constant. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:interval-contractions}.
\end{proof}

  \label{G:sharp}
  For every fixed $x \in \R^d$,

  $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$; see~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{Theorem~1.2, (1.9),

  Skorohod expansion, the rows with self-pairings are present here and cannot be
  dropped: already $M_2 = \E X_1^2+2\E X_2$. This is a structural comparison
  only, not a Skorohod result. Finally, any fixed truncation is a polynomial in
  $t^\beta$ with nonnegative coefficients, so its logarithm divided by
  $t^\kappa$ tends to zero. Orders two and four can falsify a normalization or
  an incorrect pairing rule, but cannot determine an all-order factorial power
  or prove either principal goal.
\end{remark}

\section{A variational formula for the second-moment limit}
\label{S:intrinsic-second-moment}

Retain every original covariance and amplitude. Write $a = \alpha_0$, $I =
(0,1)$, $K(s,t) = |s-t|^{-a}$ and $H_I = H_t\otimes H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ for the
original covariance. Keep the uniform $E$ mean $\mathcal{T}_R = T_R(C_t\otimes
I)$ and $C_{\rm mean} = M_0\|C_t\|$ of
Section~\ref{S:uniform-physical-products}, with exactly the fixed $r$ chosen
there. In particular, $2 < r < 2/a$. To distinguish it from the map below, write
$J_R = T_R$. For a bounded multiplier $V:H^{1/2} \to H^{-1/2}$ write
$B_E(V)(u,v) = (0,Vu)$, the $E$ operator of Lemma~\ref{L:uniform-E-lift}. The
physical mask is $m_R = \eta(\cdot/R)^2$, used once. Let
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$ be the full nonseparable class
in~\eqref{E:variational-positive-class}. For $\Psi$ in that class, retain its
unmasked mean $h_{\Psi,s}$, original variance $v_\Psi$, integrated full-gradient
rate $I_\Psi$ and variational values for nonnegative controls
$S_c^{\mathrm{pos}}$ from Section~\ref{S:unmasked-variational}. The value with
coefficient one is not identified with a first-moment limit or a constant in the
cited source. We assume $\Gamma \ne 0$ through the following comparison lemmas.
The theorem treats zero covariance directly.

\LeanMarker{L:positive-raw-embedding}
\begin{lemma}[Inclusion into the covariance space and the smooth mean]
  \label{L:positive-raw-embedding}
  For any real Hilbert space $G$ there is a bounded map $J_G:L^2(I;G) \to
  H_t\otimes G$ with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-bochner-inclusion}
    \|J_GF\|^2
    =\int_{I^2}K(s,t)\langle F(s),F(t)\rangle_G\ud s\ud t
    \le K_0\|F\|_{L^2(I;G)}^2,\qquad K_0=\frac{2^a}{1-a}.
  \end{equation}
  The natural inclusion puts every $\Psi \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$ in
  $H_I$, with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-raw-mean}
    \|\Psi\|_{H_I}^2=v_\Psi,\qquad
    \mathcal{T}_R\Psi(s)=B_E(m_Rh_{\Psi,s})\quad\hbox{a.e. }s.
  \end{equation}
  For each fixed smooth $\Psi$ the right side gives a canonical version for
  every $R \ge 1$ and every $s \in I$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Direct integration and concavity of $x^{1-a}$ give
  \[
    \int_I K(s,t)\ud t
    =\frac{s^{1-a}+(1-s)^{1-a}}{1-a}\le K_0 .
  \]
  For scalar $f \ge 0$, Cauchy--Schwarz against $K(s,t)\ud t$ and then Tonelli
  give $\|Kf\|_2 \le K_0\|f\|_2$. The same estimate for absolute values bounds
  the bilinear integral by $K_0\|f\|_2\|g\|_2$. Approximate scalar $L^2(I)$
  functions by real $C_c^\infty(I)$ functions. This bound makes their original
  test-null classes Cauchy in $H_t$, defines the bounded inclusion, and
  identifies its pairing with the integral. Finite $G$-valued tensors then give
  \eqref{E:positive-bochner-inclusion}. Simple fields are dense in Bochner
  $L^2(I;G)$; the same bound on differences extends $J_G$. Absolute bilinear
  continuity extends the integral identity as well.

  For $G = H_{\mathrm{sp}}$, the Bochner integral $\int_IK(s,t)F(t)\ud t$ exists
  almost everywhere: its norm is bounded by $K\|F\|(s)$, which belongs to $L^2$.
  Simple-field approximation and the Schur estimate give a strongly measurable
  $L^2$ version and a bounded map of norm at most $K_0$. On smooth finite
  tensors it agrees with $(C_t\otimes I)J_G$ by the covariance definition.
  Approximation by such tensors converges in $L^2(I;G)$. The integral converges
  there by Schur; the completed tensor map converges in $L^r(I;G)$ by
  Lemma~\ref{L:uniform-mean-tensor} and~\eqref{E:positive-bochner-inclusion},
  hence also in $L^2$. Uniqueness of the $L^2$ limit identifies these versions.
  In particular, the integral belongs to $L^r$.

  Choose a closed ball $B$ containing all spatial supports of $\Psi$. For real
  smooth $q$ supported there, the physical covariance gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-local-spatial-bound}
    \|q\|_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}^2
    \le |B|\,\Gamma(B-B)\|q\|_\infty^2.
  \end{equation}
  Indeed, in $\int\ud x\int\Gamma(\ud y)q(x)q(x-y)$ only $x \in B,y \in B-B$
  contribute. Absolute values and local finiteness prove the bound, including
  white and singular Radon covariances. Thus, $t\mapsto\Psi_t$ is continuous,
  bounded and compactly supported as an $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$-valued field, and
  belongs to $L^2$. Its image under $J_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}$ is the original test
  class of $\Psi$. Indeed, the separated compact smooth approximants in
  Lemma~\ref{L:cameron-supported-tensor} converge uniformly on one common
  compact support. The local bound above and the temporal Schur estimate give
  convergence both in $L^2(I;H_{\mathrm{sp}})$ and in the original covariance
  norm. Their finite tensor identifications therefore have this same limit.
  Formula~\eqref{E:positive-bochner-inclusion} and absolute Fubini give
  \[
    \|\Psi\|_{H_I}^2
    =\int_{I^2}K(s,t)\langle\Psi_s,\Psi_t\rangle_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}
    \ud s\ud t
    =\int_{I\times\R^d}\Psi(s,x)h_{\Psi,s}(x)\ud x\ud s=v_\Psi.
  \]
  The compact spatial supports and~\eqref{E:positive-local-spatial-bound} make
  all these absolute integrals finite.

  Test the spatial row~\eqref{E:uniform-mean-row} first with displacement $f$
  and dual second component $\Lambda g$, with $f,g$ compact smooth. The row
  product is $m_Rfg$, so the preceding temporal identity gives
  \[
    \left\langle\int_IK(s,t)\Psi_t\ud t,m_Rfg\right\rangle_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}
    =\int m_Rh_{\Psi,s}fg .
  \]
  This Fubini step has two fixed compact supports and integrable time kernel.
  Both resulting operators are bounded on $E$: the left by the mean theorem, the
  right by the bounded smooth $h_{\Psi,s}$ and compact smooth mask. Dense states
  extend the identity to $E$. For this fixed $\Psi$ the temporal integral exists
  at every $s$, with norm at most $K_0\sup_t\|\Psi_t\|_{H_{\mathrm{sp}}}$.
  Applying $J_R$ gives the explicit version in~\eqref{E:positive-raw-mean} for
  every $R,s$. For each $R$ it agrees with the completed $L^r$ mean class. This
  makes no simultaneous-version assertion over all rough controls. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-raw-embedding}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{P:original-signed-point-upper} bounds the original point
limsup by a limsup of the signed values $\mathcal{S}_R$.
Proposition~\ref{P:variational-positive-scores} bounds the original point
liminf below by $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$. To close this gap in
Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment} we compare $\mathcal{S}_R$ with
$S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$. We start with finite Gram matrices, with and without the
mask.

\LeanMarker{L:positive-physical-gram}
\begin{lemma}[Finite covariance vectors and entrywise order]
  \label{L:positive-physical-gram}
  Take a finite partition of $I$ with nonempty cells $I_i$, lengths $\ell_i > 0$
  and $\sum_i\ell_i = 1$, and real $f_i \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)$ with $\|f_i\|_2 =
  1$. Put
  \[
    h_i=\mathbf1_{I_i}/\ell_i,\quad b_i=\|\nabla f_i\|_2^2,\quad
    k_i^R=h_i\otimes(m_Rf_i^2),\quad
    k_i^\infty=h_i\otimes f_i^2 .
  \]
  These are elements of $H_I$. If $v_L$ is the multiplier form
  of~\eqref{E:uniform-E-pairing}, then for every $\Phi \in H_I$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-label-row}
    \langle\Phi,k_i^R\rangle_{H_I}
    =\ell_i^{-1}\int_{I_i}v_{\mathcal{T}_R\Phi(s)}(f_i,f_i)\ud s .
  \end{equation}
  The Grams $C^R_{ij} = \langle k_i^R,k_j^R\rangle$ and $D_{ij} = \langle
  k_i^\infty,k_j^\infty\rangle$ satisfy $0 \le C^R_{ij} \le D_{ij}$ entrywise.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-raw-embedding} puts $h_i$ in $H_t$. The compact smooth
  spatial factors lie in $H_{\mathrm{sp}}$ by its test definition
  and~\eqref{E:positive-local-spatial-bound}. On a smooth finite tensor,
  use~\eqref{E:uniform-mean-row} with $z = (f_i,0)$ and $w = (0,\Lambda f_i)$.
  Its row is $(\eta_Rf_i)(\eta_R\Lambda^{-1}\Lambda f_i) = m_Rf_i^2$. Temporal
  integration gives the pairing with $h_i$. This
  proves~\eqref{E:positive-label-row} on dense tensors. Its right side is a
  bounded original functional, with bound $\ell_i^{-1/r}C_{\rm
  mean}\|\Phi\|\|f_i\|_{H^{1/2}}^2$ by Hölder
  and~\eqref{E:uniform-completed-mean}. The left side is bounded by Hilbert
  Cauchy--Schwarz. Thus, the identity extends to the completed $H_I$. The form
  is the displacement form by Lemma~\ref{L:uniform-E-lift}.

  Each Gram is symmetric positive semidefinite. Its entries factor into the
  temporal pairing of nonnegative $h_i,h_j$ and the physical spatial covariance
  pairing. The kernels $K,\Gamma$ and $f_i^2,f_j^2$ are nonnegative; $0 \le m_R
  \le 1$. Tonelli therefore gives the entrywise order. The physical pairing
  already contains $\Gamma$, and the row cancellation introduces no additional
  Fourier factor. Zero labels are permitted. All hypotheses of
  Proposition~\ref{P:gram-entrywise-comparison} are met, without assuming
  $D-C^R$ is positive semidefinite. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-physical-gram}.
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{P:gram-positive-optimizer}, the unmasked finite problem has
a maximizer built from the $k_i^\infty$. Their time factors $h_i$ are scaled
indicators, so this maximizer is in general not a test in
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$. We also need a bound on the controls that holds
uniformly in $R$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform optimizer norm and positive smoothing]
  \label{L:positive-uniform-optimizer}
  Put $M = 4C_{\rm mean}+4$. The supremum defining $\mathcal{S}_R$ may be
  restricted to $\|\Phi\| \le M$, uniformly in $R$. For every finite trial
  family in Lemma~\ref{L:positive-physical-gram}, the unmasked finite
  variational expression has a maximizer $\Phi_D$ with $\|\Phi_D\| \le M$. It is
  zero or is a positive conic combination of the $k_i^\infty$. In the latter
  case it is approximable in $H_I$ by tests in $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Put $C = C_{\rm mean}$ and $q = \|\Phi\|$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-rate-averaging} gives
  \[
    2\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Phi)-q^2/2
    \le2+2Cq-q^2/2<2-2q<0\quad(q>M).
  \]
  The zero control gives zero, proving the restriction.

  Denote the finite unmasked objective by $\mathfrak{F}_D$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:gram-positive-optimizer} supplies an attained maximum at
  zero or a positive conic sum $\Phi_D$. Choose a finite $R' \ge 1$ whose
  plateau contains all supports of $f_i$. Then $k_i^{R'} = k_i^\infty$. Write
  $W_i = \ell_i^{-1}\int_{I_i}\mathcal{T}_{R'}\Phi(s)\ud s$. The row identity
  and real Rayleigh formula imply, for every $\Phi$,
  \begin{align*}
    \mathfrak{F}_D(\Phi)
    &\le2\sum_i\ell_i\lambda(W_i)-q^2/2\\
    &\le2+2\sum_i\ell_i\|W_i\|-q^2/2
    \le2+2Cq-q^2/2 .
  \end{align*}
  For fixed $f_i$, the map $L\mapsto v_L(f_i,f_i)$ is bounded linear; hence the
  form of the Bochner average is the average form. Here $\lambda(L) \le
  \max(1,\|L\|) \le 1+\|L\|$, the norm of an average is bounded by the average
  norm, and the $L^1$ mean norm is at most its $L^r$ norm. No time-averaging
  error or concavity of the positive-part square root enters this estimate.
  Since the maximum is nonnegative, its optimizer lies in the same ball of
  radius $M$, independently of the partition, profiles and $R'$.

  On a nonzero branch write $\Phi_D = \sum_{i\in A}w_i h_i\otimes f_i^2$, with
  $w_i > 0$. Extend $h_i$ by zero, convolve with a nonnegative compact smooth
  approximate identity, and multiply by smooth cutoffs $0 \le \chi_j \le 1$
  compactly supported in $I$, tending pointwise to one. The resulting
  nonnegative $h_{i,j} \in C_c^\infty(I)$ converge to $h_i$ in $L^2$: the error
  is bounded by the convolution error plus $\|(\chi_j-1)h_i\|_2$, which tends to
  zero by domination. Set $\Psi_j = \sum_{i\in A}w_i h_{i,j}f_i^2$. Finite
  tensor estimates and~\eqref{E:positive-bochner-inclusion} give $\Psi_j \to
  \Phi_D$ in $H_I$. The positive conic sum has positive squared norm, since at
  least one eligible diagonal is positive and all Gram entries are nonnegative.
  Thus, $\Psi_j \ne 0$ eventually, so $\Psi_j \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$.
  Costs converge. The integrated rate continuity proved in
  Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-rate-averaging} gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-rate-continuity}
    |\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Psi_j)-\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Phi_D)|
    \le\sqrt{3+C\|\Psi_j\|+C\|\Phi_D\|}
    \sqrt{C\|\Psi_j-\Phi_D\|}\longrightarrow0 .
  \end{equation}
  This estimate is uniform in $R'$. It asserts no uniform derivative bound on
  the smooth approximants. The zero branch will use $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}} \ge 0$
  directly. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:positive-uniform-optimizer}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{L:positive-physical-gram} orders the masked and unmasked Grams
entrywise. Lemma~\ref{L:positive-uniform-optimizer} keeps the unmasked optimizer
in a fixed ball and, when it is nonzero, approximates it by nonnegative tests.
The time averaging of Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-rate-averaging} links these
finite problems to the rate.

\begin{proposition}[Signed variational values bounded by the variational value over nonnegative controls]
  \label{P:positive-signed-comparison}
  For every $R \ge 1$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-signed-comparison}
    \mathcal{S}_R\le S_2^{\mathrm{pos}} .
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Use uniform partitions with $n \ge 2$ cells of length $1/n$. They are the $B =
  1,\delta = 1/n$ partitions in Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-rate-averaging}, with
  the empty last cell omitted. That proposition gives $e_n \to 0$, independent
  of $R$, such that
  \[
    \left|\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Phi)
    -\sum_i\ell_i\lambda\left(\ell_i^{-1}
    \int_{I_i}\mathcal{T}_R\Phi(s)\ud s\right)\right|\le e_n
    \quad(\|\Phi\|\le M).
  \]
  Fix $R,\Phi,n$, with $\|\Phi\| \le M$, and denote these averages by $W_i$.
  Real compact smooth unit trials are dense in the real $H^1$ unit sphere:
  truncate by cutoffs tending to one, whose gradient errors tend to zero, then
  mollify in $H^1$ and normalize in $L^2$. Continuity of the multiplier form in
  $H^{1/2}$ and hence $H^1$ proves
  \[
    \lambda(W_i)=
    \sup_{\substack{f\in C_c^\infty(\R^d;\R)\\\|f\|_2=1}}
    \sqrt{(v_{W_i}(f,f)-\|\nabla f\|_2^2)_+}.
  \]
  The real Rayleigh supremum is nonnegative by
  Proposition~\ref{P:uniform-E-spectral}, so the positive part does not change
  its supremum. For $\varepsilon > 0$ choose one near-maximizing $f_i$ per cell
  with error at most $\varepsilon$ in the square root. This is a finite choice,
  not a measurable continuum optimizer. Since $\sum_i\ell_i = 1$, the weighted
  trial error contributes $2\varepsilon$ to the next inequality.
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-physical-gram} gives
  \[
    2\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Phi)-\|\Phi\|^2/2
    \le\mathfrak{F}_{C^R}(\Phi)+2e_n+2\varepsilon .
  \]
  Optimize this finite objective and use entrywise Gram comparison:
  \[
    \mathfrak{F}_{C^R}(\Phi)
    \le\mathscr{S}(C^R)\le\mathscr{S}(D)=\mathfrak{F}_D(\Phi_D).
  \]
  This compares optimized values, not the same individual control.
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-uniform-optimizer} proves $\|\Phi_D\| \le M$ before time
  averaging is used again. Choose $R'$ covering the finite supports as in that
  lemma. The label row, Rayleigh bound and the same uniform averaging estimate
  give
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-finite-optimizer-bound}
    \mathfrak{F}_D(\Phi_D)
    \le2\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Phi_D)-\|\Phi_D\|^2/2+2e_n .
  \end{equation}

  If $\Phi_D = 0$, its finite variational expression is zero. Otherwise use its
  positive smooth approximants $\Psi_j$. Lemma~\ref{L:positive-raw-embedding}
  identifies their mean with $B_E(m_{R'}h_{\Psi_j,s})$ and their Gaussian cost
  with $v_{\Psi_j}$. Since $h_{\Psi_j,s} \ge 0$ and $0 \le m_{R'} \le 1$, real
  $H^1$ form order gives $\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Psi_j) \le I_{\Psi_j}$.
  Thus, $2\operatorname{Rate}_{R'}(\Psi_j)-\|\Psi_j\|^2/2 \le
  S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$. Rate and cost convergence pass this inequality to
  $\Phi_D$. Together with~\eqref{E:positive-finite-optimizer-bound} and the
  separate zero branch this yields
  \[
    2\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Phi)-\|\Phi\|^2/2
    \le S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}+4e_n+2\varepsilon .
  \]
  All finite choices were made after fixing $n,R,\Phi$. The right side is
  independent of those choices. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, take the supremum
  over the original ball, use Lemma~\ref{L:positive-uniform-optimizer}, and then
  let $n \to \infty$. This proves~\eqref{E:positive-signed-comparison}.
  Finiteness of the variational value over nonnegative controls was already
  proved from the independent canonical upper bound in
  Proposition~\ref{P:variational-positive-scores}, not from this conclusion.
  This proves Proposition~\ref{P:positive-signed-comparison}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{P:positive-signed-comparison} bounds $\mathcal{S}_R$ above for
every $R$. For the limit we still need a lower bound as the mask is removed.

\begin{proposition}[Cutoff removal for the optimized variational expression]
  \label{P:positive-score-cutoff}
  The possibly nonmonotone mask family satisfies
  \[
    \lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{S}_R=S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}.
  \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Fix $\Psi \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$ before taking $R$ or moment limits.
  Use the explicit mean version of Lemma~\ref{L:positive-raw-embedding}. Its
  rate $\lambda_R(s)$ obeys $0 \le \lambda_R(s) \le \lambda_{\Psi,s}$ by real
  form order. For each real compact smooth unit $f$, $m_R = 1$ on its support
  for every sufficiently large $R$. The Rayleigh formula therefore gives
  \[
    \liminf_{R\to\infty}\lambda_R(s)^2
    \ge\int h_{\Psi,s}f^2-\|\nabla f\|_2^2.
  \]
  Compact trial density and~\eqref{E:variational-real-rayleigh} identify the
  supremum of the right side as $\lambda_{\Psi,s}^2$. Hence, $\lambda_R(s) \to
  \lambda_{\Psi,s}$ for these versions. The global smooth-source bound $H_\Psi =
  \sup_{s,x}h_{\Psi,s}(x) < \infty$ gives $\lambda_R(s) \le \sqrt{H_\Psi}$.
  Dominated convergence yields $\operatorname{Rate}_R(\Psi) \to I_\Psi$. Neither
  monotonicity of $m_R$ nor an unmasked ground eigenfunction was used.

  The original representative and cost $\|\Psi\|^2 = v_\Psi$ are independent of
  $R$. Thus, $\liminf_R\mathcal{S}_R \ge 2I_\Psi-v_\Psi/2$ for each fixed test.
  Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose one fixed test with variational expression
  greater than $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}-\varepsilon$; the finite supremum admits such
  a test. Take the liminf and then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:positive-signed-comparison} supplies the reverse limsup.
  This proves Proposition~\ref{P:positive-score-cutoff}.
\end{proof}

This passage from cutoff scores to their suprema needs the comparison
just proved. No-go~\ref{P:nogo-30} shows why pointwise convergence and
bounded control costs alone would not justify it.


\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic original second-moment constant]
  \label{T:intrinsic-second-moment}
  For every original admissible model with data $(1,0)$ and fixed $x$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:intrinsic-second-moment-limit}
    \lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-(4-\alpha-a)/(3-\alpha)}
    \log\E|u_\Gamma(T,x)|^2
    =C_2(\Gamma,a)=S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}
    =2^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}S_1^{\mathrm{pos}}.
  \end{equation}
  Its complete variational formula over nonnegative controls is
  \begin{align}
    C_2(\Gamma,a)
    &=\sup_{\Psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}}
    \left\{2\int_I
    \sqrt{\sup_{\substack{f\in H^1(\R^d;\R)\\\|f\|_2=1}}
    \left(\int h_{\Psi,s}f^2-\|\nabla f\|_2^2\right)}
    \ud s-\frac{v_\Psi}{2}\right\},
    \label{E:intrinsic-positive-formula}\\
    h_{\Psi,s}(x)
    &=\int_I|s-t|^{-a}(\Gamma*\Psi_t)(x)\ud t,\qquad
    v_\Psi=\int_{I\times\R^d}\Psi(s,x)h_{\Psi,s}(x)\ud x\ud s.
    \notag
  \end{align}
  Here $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pos}}$ consists of all nonzero nonnegative real
  $C_c^\infty(I\times\R^d)$ tests, including nonseparable ones. The covariance
  retains its amplitude, the kinetic term has coefficient one, and the Gaussian
  action is $v_\Psi/2$. For nonzero covariance $0 < C_2 < \infty$. For $\Gamma =
  0$ the constant and moment rate are zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Assume first $\Gamma \ne 0$. Proposition~\ref{P:original-signed-point-upper},
  followed by Proposition~\ref{P:positive-score-cutoff} with $R =
  \delta^{-1}+2$, bounds the original point limsup by $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:variational-positive-scores} bounds the original point
  liminf below by this same value. Both are for the same canonical Stratonovich
  solution, exponent, original covariance and variance-half action. Their with
  the regularization removed first identifications retain all mixed, unequal,
  self and empty chains and both phase components with initial data $(1,0)$. The
  original spatial stationarity proved in
  Proposition~\ref{P:raw-block-original-lower} extends both bounds to every
  fixed $x$. Thus, the limit exists and equals $S_2^{\mathrm{pos}}$. Its
  homogeneity, positivity and finiteness are
  Proposition~\ref{P:variational-positive-scores}.
  Formula~\eqref{E:variational-real-rayleigh} and the definition of the original
  variance give~\eqref{E:intrinsic-positive-formula}.

  If $\Gamma = 0$, $H_I = \{0\}$, all means, variances and rates vanish,
  $\mathcal{S}_R = S_c^{\mathrm{pos}} = 0$, and $u = 1$. Both formulas are
  direct zero identities. This proves Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.
\end{proof}

The variational value with coefficient one is auxiliary; the theorem does not
identify it with a first-moment limit or with Chen's printed constants. It
neither repairs a source energy normalization nor asserts a Jensen improvement.
The exponent formally tends to $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$ as $a\downarrow0$; no
interchange with the static moment limit is claimed. The explicit bounds and
full calibration are separate from this identification.

\begin{nogo}[No finite spatial-supremum pullout in the merge lens]
  \label{P:nogo-10}
  In $d=2$, take $\Gamma(z)=c|z|^{-\alpha}$, $c>0$,
  $0<\alpha_0<1/2$ and $3/2\le\alpha<2-\alpha_0$.
  For the root-forward $(0,4)$ diagram
  with pairs $(1,3),(2,4)$, merging vertex two requires the weighted integral
  \[
    W=c\int_a^b(T-v)^{-\alpha_0}
    \int_{\mathbb R^2}G_{v-a}(y-x)G_{b-v}(z-y)|w-y|^{-\alpha}
    \,\ud y\ud v,
    \qquad 0<a<b<T.
  \]
  Here the letters $a,b,T$ are vertex times, so the temporal exponent is
  written $\alpha_0$. The spatial weight has no finite uniform essential
  supremum on admissible merge lenses. Hence, pulling it out by a uniform
  supremum cannot reduce $W$ to an unweighted two-wave convolution.
\end{nogo}
