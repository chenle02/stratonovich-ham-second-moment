I have read the manuscript in full and cross-checked its principal citations against the frozen source texts (the current 64-page author manuscript, Chen–Hu 2025, Chen 2023, Crouzeix–Palencia v1, and Balan–Conus). I report below the things that caught my attention as a referee, in no particular order of importance.

**1. The genre and status of the document.** The title block reads "… Working notes," and the abstract says the document "fix[es] the model, record[s] exactly what is known (with source pinpoints), state[s] the targets, and list[s] the planned route" (lines 85–87). The structure is that of a research diary (Sections titled "Gate G0," "Gate G1," a final "Established steps and open questions" list, and two "Goals"), not a standard theorem-and-proof paper. A large fraction of the text is meta-commentary: diagnosis of another manuscript's internal problems, provenance notes, and disclaimers about what is and is not an "input." This is not disqualifying in itself, but it is the first thing that makes me hesitate about the document's suitability for a journal in its present form.

**3. A large block of the paper is explicitly not used by the main theorem.** Lines 146–152 state that Sections S:higher-alpha through S:energy-fidelity are "alternative covariance interpolation arguments" that are "not … inputs," and are "retained to record their separate hypotheses and limitations." That is a substantial fraction of a ~6000-line manuscript devoted to conditional results (Hypothesis H:spatial-maximum, Hypothesis H:three-endpoints) that are only discharged for special covariances (Riesz, planar, product, plane-supported), plus the energy-identification section. A referee would ask for this material to be sharply cut or moved to an appendix/companion note, since it neither supports the main theorem nor advances the stated open goals.

**4. Theorem T:first (line 277) is a quoted source statement whose constants the author himself flags.** The theorem is presented "as printed," with a boxed warning (line 270–272) that the "variational constants require normalization clarification," and throughout the paper any use of L₋ is "conditional on the intended first-moment lower bound" (e.g., lines 170–173, abstract lines 78–80). Presenting as a numbered "Theorem" of this paper a result whose constants are flagged as possibly misnormalized, while "no correction is adopted," is awkward and risks confusing the reader about what the paper actually establishes.

**5. Notation is heavily overloaded, to the point that the paper has to maintain a disambiguation paragraph.** Lines 156–173 warn that Γ is both a covariance measure and the Euler gamma function; that δ means α−1 in one section and a smoothing width elsewhere; that p means 2−α₀ in one section and the moment order elsewhere; that b = 2−α in one place and b_j are coordinate powers in another; and that β and ν have section-specific meanings. The fact that the author feels obliged to post these warnings is itself evidence of a readability problem. Renaming some of these would materially improve the paper.

**6. The interval-field proof (Section S:interval-upper) is a long tower of limit exchanges.** The proof of Theorem T:full-upper closes with an enumerated list (items (i)–(v)) explicitly ordering Gaussian-mode cutoffs, spatial-band cutoffs, local Hermite cutoffs (Nᵢ = 2kᵢ), interval depth J → ∞, Green width η → 0, noise widths ε,δ → 0, and partial order N → ∞. The technical heart, Lemma L:fixed-split (line 5034), runs for roughly 360 lines and combines vector Cauchy formulas on polydiscs, Dyson series, phase-norm metric factors, and Hermite creation/annihilation bounds. I did not find a specific error, but this is a region where a referee would have to invest very substantial effort and where a mistake could easily hide; the paper's own repeated insistence that certain objects are "not identified" with others (e.g., "no stochastic identity is assumed for L_k^{ε,η}") indicates the author is aware of the fragility. I would want this section independently checked with particular care.

**7. The dyadic "three grids" construction and the interval inventory bound.** In the proof of Theorem T:full-upper the paper asserts that the three grids with shifts (−1)ʲt, t = 0, 1/3, 2/3, are "nested … since 3t ∈ ℤ," and that at least one grid interval contains any pair s,r with 3|s−r| < h_j ≤ 6|s−r| (equation E:dyadic-time-majorant), giving the domination |s−r|^{−α₀} ≤ 6^{α₀} Σ_I |I|^{−α₀} 1_I(s)1_I(r). I checked this and it works (one merged-grid point can lie between s,r, and it belongs to only one of the three grids), and the cumulative-count bound h_i ≤ 78T/i is correct. But the reasoning is compressed to a few sentences for a construction that everything downstream depends on; some expansion would help.

**8. The main theorem's constant and the claim "C = C(α,α₀)."** The theorem (line 5771) states a bound of the form e(T+2)^d exp{C R_α^{1/(3−α)} T^κ} with "C = C(α,α₀)." The covariance enters only through R_α = απμ(B₁)/(2 sin(πα/2)). This is dimension-independent and, as far as I can check from Lemma L:interval-contractions, the right place for the only covariance datum used. Two small notes: (a) R_α blows up as α → 2 (through sin(πα/2)), so the constant is not uniform near α = 2, though α is fixed; (b) the paper is careful (lines 152–154) that this "full-class" constant is not numerically optimized, whereas "explicit constants" in the abstract refer only to Section S:d1-upper. That distinction is maintained consistently.

**9. The white-space test (Lemma L:white-test) is the cleanest concrete check and it is correct.** I verified E X₁² = c₀ t^{3−α₀} with c₀ = ((1−α₀)(2−α₀)(3−α₀))^{−1} and E X₂ = ½ c₀ t^{3−α₀}, giving total order-two contribution 2c₀ t^{3−α₀}. This genuinely contradicts the source's (5.4) with a constant uniform in t, so the paper's central diagnosis in Section S:G0 is sound. The exponent computations ρ = β/(β−1) > κ and ρ < 2, and the homogeneous norm scalings N₀(θ)=θ^{−2}, N₁(θ)≍θ^{α+α₀−2}, N₂(θ)²≍θ^{−β} in Proposition P:envelope, also check out.

**10. On the auxiliary finite-order computations (Section S:G1).** The order-two Riesz coefficients (Proposition P:riesz-small) and the white-noise order-four table (Proposition P:white-four) are lengthy and I did not re-derive them all; I found no evident slip in the ones I spot-checked (e.g., the Riesz two-point spatial integral and the static limit coefficient c_α 2^{3−α}/[(1−α)(2−α)(3−α)(4−α)] are consistent with the white-noise limit 1/3 under the normalization c_α = (1−α)/2). The paper itself flags that no closed form is asserted for the remaining order-four integrals and that these are "checks of normalization, not evidence of sharp constants" (Remark R:d1-upper-checks), which is appropriately scoped.

**11. A minor but persistent rhetorical habit.** The paper repeatedly pre-empts possible objections with phrases like "we do not assert," "no … is claimed," "this is not an input here," "it is not identified with." This defensiveness is a red flag about how much the author is carrying around in this document that a referee would normally ask to be removed or resolved rather than caveated.

**12. Dependency on the Crouzeix–Palencia result is accurately cited but is doing real work.** Equation E:cp-input states ‖F(T)‖ ≤ (1+√2) sup_{W(T)}|F|, and the paper cites "(3), p. 1, and Theorem 6, pp. 5–6" of the v1 preprint. I verified against the frozen text that (3) is exactly the (1+√2) numerical-range bound and Theorem 6 is the corresponding result; the citation is faithful. However, Lemma L:complex-phase and Lemma L:interval-complex rest on this theorem together with delicate square-root/root-comparison estimates in the phase space; the extension to non-selfadjoint V (Lemma L:interval-complex) is asserted to follow by "the identical sandwich bound" and adjoint symmetry. This is plausible but is another point a referee would scrutinize.

**13. The Jensen comparison (Proposition P:Jensen) is correct and properly conditional.** E u ≥ 1 from nonnegativity of the even-order pairing integrals, E u² ≥ (E u)² by Cauchy–Schwarz, and the implication liminf t^{−κ} log E u ≥ ℓ ⇒ liminf t^{−κ} log E u² ≥ 2ℓ are all correct; the paper correctly states that specializing ℓ = L₋ is conditional on the source's first-moment lower bound. No issue here, but it underscores how much of the "sharp constant" goal remains open: the conjectured second-moment constant 2^{(4−α)/(3−α)}L* is strictly larger than what Jensen alone gives.

**14. Balan–Conus comparison is cosmetic and correctly flagged as such.** The exponent match (2H+2−a)/(3−a) = κ with H = 1−α₀/2, a = α is arithmetically correct, and the paper repeatedly says the comparison concerns a different (Skorohod) solution concept and normalization and "is not an input." Fine, but I note the comparison is invoked in several places (Remarks R:d1-comparisons, R:higher-calibrations, R:static-holder-scope) without adding mathematical content to the main proof.

Overall, the paper's mathematical core — the corrected crude bound, the G0 diagnosis, and the interval-field upper bound at the target scale — is internally coherent and, in the parts I checked, correct; but the document's form (working notes), its heavy reliance on an unpublished source in whose Section 5 the paper has found a real error while still importing results proved in that Section, and the large amount of non-contributing auxiliary material are the features that would most make me hesitate to recommend acceptance in its current state.
