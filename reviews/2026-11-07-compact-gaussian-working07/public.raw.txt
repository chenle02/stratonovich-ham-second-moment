## 0. Falsification first

### 0.1 Rank-one rectangular families

**Row family.** \(n=16\), \(A_k=e_k^{\mathsf T}\) (a \(1\times n\) row). Then

\[
X=\sum_{k=1}^{16}g_k A_k=(g_1,\ldots,g_{16}),
\qquad
X^*X=\mathbf g\mathbf g^{\mathsf T},
\]

whose single nonzero eigenvalue is \(\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}\mathbf g=\sum g_k^2\). Hence

\[
\|X\|_{S_4}^4=\operatorname{Tr}((X^*X)^2)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{16}g_k^2\right)^{\!2},
\]

\[
\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_4}^4=\sum_k\mathbb Eg_k^4+\sum_{k\ne l}\mathbb Eg_k^2\,\mathbb Eg_l^2
=16\cdot3+16\cdot15=288.
\]

The variances are

\[
V_{\rm out}=\sum_k A_kA_k^*=\sum_k 1=16,\qquad
V_{\rm in}=\sum_k A_k^*A_k=\sum_k e_ke_k^{\mathsf T}=I_{16}.
\]

So \(\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^2=256\), \(\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^2=16\). An input-variance-only bound with \(p=4\) would give moment \(\le(p-1)^{p/2}\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^{p/2}=9\cdot16=144<288\), false. The actual two-variance average bound gives

\[
\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_4}^4\le(p-1)^{p/2}\frac{\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^2+\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^2}{2}
=9\cdot\frac{256+16}{2}=1224,
\]

and the maximum bound gives \(9\cdot256=2304\); both dominate \(288\).

**Transpose family.** \(A_k=e_k\) (an \(n\times1\) column). Then \(V_{\rm out}=I_{16}\), \(V_{\rm in}=16\), and the output-only variance bound again undercounts, while the two-variance average remains a genuine bound. Hence a Gaussian \(S_p\) estimate that is symmetric in the two directional variances is forced.

### 0.2 Noninteger/s=0 cutoff check (mechanism only)

Take \(d=1\), \(\sigma=0\), \(\chi\) a smooth cutoff on \([0,1]\), \(\gamma=\tfrac12\). Then \(K_{\chi,0,\gamma}=\chi\Lambda^{-1/2}\), with singular values squared equal to the eigenvalues of \(\chi\Lambda^{-1}\chi\). Its singular-value decay is \(s_j\le C j^{-\gamma/d}=Cj^{-1/2}\) (an upper bound, not a sharp asymptotic). Membership in \(S_p\) therefore holds iff \(p\gamma>d\), i.e. \(p>\tfrac{d}{\gamma}=2\). This is consistent with `P:cutoff-singular-values`; the source claims only the upper bound, which is all that is needed.

## 1. PROOF075 (`L:gaussian-scalar-powers`)

The four statements are elementary; I display only the two least obvious arguments with exact labels.

**`E:gaussian-weighted-power`.** \(u^\theta v^{1-\theta}\le\theta u+(1-\theta)v\). For \(v>0\) set \(t=u/v\): the function \(f(t)=\theta t+(1-\theta)-t^\theta\) has \(f'(t)=\theta(1-t^{\theta-1})\), negative for \(t<1\) (since \(\theta-1<0\)) and positive for \(t>1\), so it attains value \(0\) at \(t=1\), its minimum. Continuity gives \(t=0\); \(v=0\) is immediate.

**`E:gaussian-integer-power`.** Apply it with \(u=|x|^L\), \(v=|y|^L\), \(\theta=q/L\): \(|x|^q|y|^{L-q}=u^{q/L}v^{(L-q)/L}\le\frac qL|x|^L+\frac{L-q}L|y|^L\).

**Finite Hölder** and **power Jensen** \(\mathbb EZ^r\le(\mathbb EZ)^r\) when \(F=\mathbb EZ\): for \(F>0\), \((Z/F)^r\le r(Z/F)+(1-r)\) by the weighted power, integrate and multiply by \(F^r\). Done.

## 2. PROOF076 (`L:gaussian-trace-bounds`)

**`E:gaussian-trace-word`.** Diagonalize \(Y=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)\); \(B=(b_{ij})\) Hermitian, \(b_{ji}=\overline{b_{ij}}\). Then

\[
\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(BY^qBY^{L-q})
=\operatorname{Re}\sum_{i,j}|b_{ij}|^2\lambda_j^q\lambda_i^{L-q}
\le\sum_{i,j}|b_{ij}|^2|\lambda_j|^q|\lambda_i|^{L-q}.
\]

By `E:gaussian-integer-power`,

\[
|\lambda_j|^q|\lambda_i|^{L-q}
\le\frac qL|\lambda_i|^L+\frac{L-q}L|\lambda_j|^L.
\]

The first resulting sum is \(\sum_i(\sum_j|b_{ij}|^2)|\lambda_i|^L=\sum_i(B^2)_{ii}|\lambda_i|^L=\operatorname{Tr}(B^2|Y|^L)\). The second is \(\sum_j(\sum_i|b_{ij}|^2)|\lambda_j|^L\), and since \(|b_{ij}|=|b_{ji}|\), \(\sum_i|b_{ij}|^2=\sum_i|b_{ji}|^2=(B^2)_{jj}\), so it equals the same trace. The weights \(q/L+(L-q)/L=1\) close the bound. No commutator \(BY=YB\) is used.

**`E:gaussian-trace-holder`.** In the eigenbasis of \(Y\), \(d_i=D_{ii}\ge0\). The identity \(\operatorname{Tr}(D|Y|^{2m-2})=\sum_i d_i|\lambda_i|^{2m-2}\) holds because \(|Y|^{2m-2}\) is diagonal there. The necessary monotonicity of the \(d_i\) against \(\operatorname{Tr}D^m\) is Schur's majorization: with \(D=U\operatorname{diag}(\theta_j)U^*\), \(d_i=\sum_j|U_{ij}|^2\theta_j\) and the doubly stochastic weights give \(d_i^m\le\sum_j|U_{ij}|^2\theta_j^m\), so \(\sum_i d_i^m\le\operatorname{Tr}D^m\). The source's tangent-line inequality \(t^m\ge s^m+ms^{m-1}(t-s)\) is precisely the subgradient form of convexity of \(t^m\), \(m\ge2\), used for this majorization. Finite Hölder with exponent pair \((m,m/(m-1))\) then yields the displayed bound.

## 3. PROOF077 (`P:finite-gaussian-matrix`) — the full both-variance derivation

### 3.1 Hermitian case by Gaussian integration by parts

Let \(Y=\sum_{k=1}^N g_kB_k\) with \(B_k\) Hermitian, \(D=\sum_kB_k^2\). Set

\[
F_m=\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{2m}),\qquad m\ge2.
\]

First, by the common \(Y\),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{2m})=\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{2m-1}Y)
=\sum_k g_k\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{2m-1}B_k)
=\sum_k g_k\operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^{2m-1}),
\]

by trace cyclicity. For independent standard Gaussians,

\[
\mathbb E[g_kP(g)]=\mathbb E[\partial_kP(g)]
\]

(multivariate Stein's identity, valid for polynomials by componentwise integration by parts against the product Gaussian density; complex traces are handled through real and imaginary parts). Here \(P_k(g)=\operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^{2m-1})\), and the product rule over the \(2m-1\) factors of \(Y\) gives

\[
\partial_k\operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^{2m-1})
=\sum_{q=0}^{2m-2}\operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^qB_kY^{2m-2-q}).
\]

Thus, with \(L=2m-2\),

\[
F_m=\sum_{k=1}^N\sum_{q=0}^L\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^qB_kY^{L-q}).
\]

**Reality of each term.** Since \(B_k,Y\) are Hermitian,

\[
\overline{\operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^qB_kY^{L-q})}
=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((B_kY^qB_kY^{L-q})^*\bigr)
=\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{L-q}B_kY^qB_k)
=\operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^qB_kY^{L-q})
\]

by one cyclic rotation. So the trace is real and the real part is the trace itself.

Apply `E:gaussian-trace-word` term by term (it bounds the real part, which is the whole term), and note that the bound depends on \(k\) through \(B_k\) and on the common \(Y\). Summing over \(q=0,\ldots,L\) (that is \(L+1=2m-1\) terms),

\[
\sum_{q=0}^{L}\operatorname{Tr}(B_kY^qB_kY^{L-q})
\le(2m-1)\operatorname{Tr}(B_k^2|Y|^{2m-2}).
\]

Therefore,

\[
F_m\le(2m-1)\sum_k\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(B_k^2|Y|^{2m-2})
=(2m-1)\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Big(\textstyle\sum_kB_k^2\Big)|Y|^{2m-2}\right)
=(2m-1)\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(D|Y|^{2m-2}).
\]

By `E:gaussian-trace-holder`,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(D|Y|^{2m-2})
\le(\operatorname{Tr}D^m)^{1/m}\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}|Y|^{2m}\bigr)^{(m-1)/m}.
\]

For Hermitian \(Y\), \(\operatorname{Tr}|Y|^{2m}=\sum|\lambda_i|^{2m}=\sum\lambda_i^{2m}=\operatorname{Tr}Y^{2m}\). Power Jensen \(r=(m-1)/m\in(0,1)\) gives

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(D|Y|^{2m-2})
\le(\operatorname{Tr}D^m)^{1/m}F_m^{(m-1)/m}.
\]

Hence

\[
F_m\le(2m-1)(\operatorname{Tr}D^m)^{1/m}F_m^{(m-1)/m}.
\]

If \(F_m>0\), divide by the positive power and take the \(m\)-th power:

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{2m})\le(2m-1)^m\operatorname{Tr}D^m .
\]

For \(m=1\), independence gives equality:

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}Y^2=\sum_k\operatorname{Tr}B_k^2=\operatorname{Tr}D.
\]

### 3.2 Rectangular case by Hermitian dilation

For complex \(o\times n\) matrices \(A_k\), form

\[
B_k=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_k\\ A_k^*&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\ X^*&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D=\begin{pmatrix}V_{\rm out}&0\\0&V_{\rm in}\end{pmatrix},
\]

with \(V_{\rm out}=\sum_k A_kA_k^*\), \(V_{\rm in}=\sum_kA_k^*A_k\). Direct multiplication gives

\[
Y^2=\begin{pmatrix}XX^*&0\\0&X^*X\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Y^{2m}=\begin{pmatrix}(XX^*)^m&0\\0&(X^*X)^m\end{pmatrix}.
\]

By trace cyclicity,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}((XX^*)^m)=\operatorname{Tr}(X(X^*X)^{m-1}X^*)=\operatorname{Tr}((X^*X)^m),
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{2m})=2\operatorname{Tr}((X^*X)^m)=2\|X\|_{S_{2m}}^{2m}
\]

with \(S_p\)-norm \(\|X\|_{S_p}^p=\operatorname{Tr}((X^*X)^m)\), \(p=2m\). Also

\[
\operatorname{Tr}D^m=\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^m+\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^m.
\]

Substituting the Hermitian bound, dividing by \(2\), and taking \(p\)-th roots gives

\[
(\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_p}^p)^{1/p}
\le(p-1)^{1/2}\left(\frac{\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^m+\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^m}{2}\right)^{1/p}.
\]

Since the average of two nonnegative numbers is at most their maximum,

\[
\le\sqrt{p-1}\max\left((\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^m)^{1/p},(\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^m)^{1/p}\right),
\]

and \(( \operatorname{Tr}V^{m})^{1/p}=\|V^{1/2}\|_{S_p}\) because \(\|V^{1/2}\|_{S_p}^p=\operatorname{Tr}V^{p/2}=\operatorname{Tr}V^m\). At \(p=2\), \(\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_2}^2=\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}X^*X=\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}\), and \(\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}=\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}\), giving equality in `E:finite-gaussian-average`.

This completes `P:finite-gaussian-matrix` with constants independent of \(o,n,N\), and the rank-one example of §0.1 already establishes that dropping either variance direction is false.

## 4. PROOF078 (`L:cutoff-factorization`)

The input is the Peetre bound

\[
(\langle x\rangle/\langle y\rangle)^r\le2^{|r|/2}\langle x-y\rangle^{|r|},
\]

for real \(r\), and the uniform lattice-sum bound

\[
\sup_y\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}\langle y-ak\rangle^{-N}<\infty\qquad(N>d),
\]

whose proof is the dyadic-shell argument in the source: for \(|k|\ge\sqrt d\), \(|y-ak|\ge a|k|/2\) after centering \(y\), and the shell \(2^{j}\le|k|<2^{j+1}\) contributes \(O(2^{jd}2^{-jN})\).

**Periodization kernel.** On a Schwartz \(u\), the \(k\)-th torus coefficient of \(\mathcal P_\psi u\) is

\[
(\mathcal P_\psi u)_k=L^{-d/2}\int_{\R^d}(\psi u)(x)e^{-i\kappa_k\cdot x}\,\mathrm dx
=L^{-d/2}\int_{\R^d}\widehat\psi(\kappa_k-\xi)\widehat u(\xi)\,\mathrm d\xi,
\]

the two \(c_d\)-factors cancel (verified: the whole-space Fourier convention is \(\widehat f(\xi)=c_d\int fe^{-i\xi x}\,\mathrm dx\), and Fourier inversion written with the same \(c_d\) makes the convolution kernel \(c_d\int\widehat\psi\widehat u=c_d\) times \(\int\widehat\psi(\kappa_k-\xi)\widehat u(\xi)\,\mathrm d\xi\), and the \(L^{-d/2}c_d^{-1}\) from the ordinary integral exactly cancels this \(c_d\)). Weighted by \(\langle\kappa_k\rangle^r\), the kernel is

\[
L^{-d/2}(\langle\kappa_k\rangle/\langle\xi\rangle)^r\widehat\psi(\kappa_k-\xi).
\]

**Extension kernel.** For a torus polynomial \(v=(v_k)\), \(\mathcal E_\chi v\) has normalized Fourier transform

\[
\langle\xi\rangle^r\widehat{\mathcal E_\chi v}(\xi)
=L^{-d/2}\sum_k(\langle\xi\rangle/\langle\kappa_k\rangle)^r\widehat\chi(\xi-\kappa_k)\,\langle\kappa_k\rangle^rv_k;
\]

this is the transposed/complex-conjugated structure, so the same kernel class is used.

Both kernels are, by Peetre, dominated by a fixed constant times \(\langle\text{frequency difference}\rangle^{|r|-N}\) for arbitrary \(N>|r|+d\), times the rapidly decaying \(|\widehat\psi|\) or \(|\widehat\chi|\). The mixed Schur norm for the Lebesgue/counting measures is bounded by the product of an \(L^1(\R^d)\) norm of the difference kernel (uniform in \(k\)) and a uniformly translated lattice sum (uniform in \(\xi\)), which is the stated lattice-bound. Hence both maps are bounded for all real \(r\), including negative \(r\).

The recovery identity \(\mathcal E_\chi\mathcal I_\gamma\mathcal P_\psi u=\chi u\) holds because \(\chi=\chi\psi\) and the periodic Fourier series of \(\psi u\) converges smoothly on the cube; multiplying by smooth compactly supported \(\chi\) recovers the whole-space function without alias. This uses polynomial (torus) density and smooth \(\R^d\) density, never pointwise Fourier sampling.

## 5. PROOF079 (`P:cutoff-singular-values`)

Define \(K_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma}=\Lambda^{\sigma}\chi\Lambda^{-\sigma-\gamma}\) on Schwartz functions. The factorization is

\[
K_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma}
=\Lambda^{\sigma}\mathcal E_\chi\mathcal I_\gamma\mathcal P_\psi\Lambda^{-\sigma-\gamma}.
\]

Let \(Q_n\) retain torus frequencies \(|k|_\infty\le n\); \(\operatorname{rank}Q_n=(2n+1)^d\), and

\[
\|(1-Q_n)\mathcal I_\gamma\|_{H^{\sigma+\gamma}(\T_L)\to H^{\sigma}(\T_L)}
=\sup_{|k|_\infty>n}\langle\kappa_k\rangle^{-\gamma}
=\bigl[1+(2\pi/L)^2(n+1)^2\bigr]^{-\gamma/2}\to0.
\]

Replacing \(\mathcal I_\gamma\) by \(Q_n\mathcal I_\gamma\) gives a bounded finite-rank \(K_n\) with \(\|K-K_n\|\to0\); finite-rank images have finite nets, and norm approximation transfers those nets to the limiting image of the unit ball, so \(K\) is compact.

For the decay, put

\[
M=\|\mathcal E_\chi:H^{\sigma}(\T_L)\to H^{\sigma}(\R^d)\|\cdot
\|\mathcal P_\psi:H^{\sigma+\gamma}(\R^d)\to H^{\sigma+\gamma}(\T_L)\|.
\]

For \(j\ge3^d\), take \(n=\lfloor j^{1/d}/4\rfloor\), so \((2n+1)^d<j\) and \(n+1> j^{1/d}/4\). Then

\[
\|K-K_n\|\le M(2\pi(n+1)/L)^{-\gamma}
<M(2L/\pi)^\gamma j^{-\gamma/d}.
\]

The Weyl–Courant–Fischer min-max estimate used for compact operators is

\[
s_j(K)=\inf_{\operatorname{rank}F<j}\|K-F\|:
\]

the right side is the standard min-max characterization, and the source supplies the direct argument (the first \(j\) right singular vectors give the unit vector in \(\ker F\)). Hence

\[
s_j(K)\le\|K-K_n\|\le M(2L/\pi)^\gamma j^{-\gamma/d}.
\]

For \(j<3^d\), \(s_j(K)\le\|K\|\le M\), and \(M\le M\cdot3^\gamma j^{-\gamma/d}\). Hence the stated constant

\[
C_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma,d}=M\max(3^\gamma,(2L/\pi)^\gamma)
\]

is admissible for all \(j\), so

\[
s_j(K)\le C_{\chi,\sigma,\gamma,d}\,j^{-\gamma/d},
\]

and \(K\in S_p\) iff \(p\ge1\) and \(p\gamma>d\). Only the upper bound, not sharpness, and no endpoint necessity, are asserted.

## 6. PROOF080 (`P:compact-two-envelopes`)

Let \(A_\sigma=\Lambda^{\sigma}\chi\Lambda^{-\sigma}\), which the Schur estimate in 078 proves bounded for every real \(\sigma\). On Schwartz functions, \(A_\sigma^*=A_{-\sigma}\) by self-adjointness of real multiplication and Bessel powers; density extends this to the actual adjoints. Then

\[
P_\chi=J_0D_\chi J_0^{-1},\qquad J_0=\operatorname{diag}(\Lambda^{1/2},\Lambda^{-1/2}),
\]

\[
P_\chi=\operatorname{diag}(A_{1/2},A_{-1/2}),\qquad
P_\chi^*=\operatorname{diag}(A_{-1/2},A_{1/2}),
\]

and the unitary swap \(U(v,w)=(w,v)\) gives \(P_\chi^*=UP_\chi U\). Since \(K_{\rm out}=P_\chi D_\gamma\), \(K_{\rm in}=P_\chi^*D_\gamma\), and \(U\) commutes with the diagonal \(D_\gamma=\operatorname{diag}(\Lambda^{-\gamma},\Lambda^{-\gamma})\),

\[
K_{\rm in}=UK_{\rm out}U.
\]

The two blocks of \(K_{\rm out}\) are \(K_{\chi,1/2,\gamma}\) and \(K_{\chi,-1/2,\gamma}\), each compact in \(S_p\) for \(p\gamma>d\); their direct sum has singular values equal to their multiset union, so

\[
\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p=\|K_{\chi,1/2,\gamma}\|_{S_p}^p+\|K_{\chi,-1/2,\gamma}\|_{S_p}^p,
\]

and unitary conjugation gives the same for \(K_{\rm in}\).

The envelope identities are

\[
B_{\rm out}=P_\chi D_\gamma^2P_\chi^*=K_{\rm out}K_{\rm out}^*,
\qquad
B_{\rm in}=P_\chi^* D_\gamma^2P_\chi=K_{\rm in}K_{\rm in}^*
\]

(the self-adjointness \(D_\gamma^*=D_\gamma\) supplies \((P_\chi D_\gamma)^*=D_\gamma P_\chi^*\); no commutation of \(P_\chi\) with \(D_\gamma\) is needed). For compact \(K\), the positive eigenvalues of \(KK^*\) and \(K^*K\) agree with multiplicity via the inverse-isometry map on each positive eigenspace; zeros do not contribute to traces. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Tr}B_{\rm out}^{p/2}
=\operatorname{Tr}(K_{\rm out}K_{\rm out}^*)^{p/2}
=\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p,
\]

and identically for the input side, giving `E:compact-envelope-traces`.

## 7. PROOF081 (`L:compact-positive-order`)

If \(0\le A\le B\) in the PSD (form) order and \(B\) is compact positive with finite spectral projections \(Q_n\) whose norms are the eigenvalue tails \(\lambda_{n+1}(B)\to0\), then positive congruence gives

\[
\|(1-Q_n)A^{1/2}\|^2=\|(1-Q_n)A(1-Q_n)\|\le\|(1-Q_n)B(1-Q_n)\|=\lambda_{n+1}(B)\to0.
\]

Hence \(A^{1/2}\) is a norm limit of finite ranks, compact, and so is \(A\). The max-min (Courant) formulas for ordered eigenvalues of compact positive operators, applied to the Rayleigh quotients of \(A\) and \(B\), give \(\lambda_j(A)\le\lambda_j(B)\) after zero padding. Then for \(t\ge1\), \(\lambda_j(A)^t\le\lambda_j(B)^t\) and summation in \(\ell^t\)-order gives \(\operatorname{Tr}A^t\le\operatorname{Tr}B^t\). The source draws no operator inequality \(A^t\le B^t\); only the scalar eigenvalue order is used.

## 8. PROOF082 (`P:localized-finite-variance`)

This proposition is **conditional**: its hypotheses `E:localized-finite-hypotheses` are

\[
P_\chi Z_\ell=Z_\ell=Z_\ell P_\chi,\qquad
V_{\rm out}=\sum_\ell Z_\ell Z_\ell^*\le C D_\gamma^2,\qquad
V_{\rm in}=\sum_\ell Z_\ell^*Z_\ell\le C D_\gamma^2,
\]

and no actual noise family has yet been shown to satisfy them. From \(Z_\ell=P_\chi Z_\ell\) and \(Z_\ell^*=P_\chi^*Z_\ell^*\), one gets \(Z_\ell Z_\ell^*=P_\chi(Z_\ell Z_\ell^*)P_\chi^*\), so \(V_{\rm out}=P_\chi V_{\rm out}P_\chi^*\), and similarly \(V_{\rm in}=P_\chi^*V_{\rm in}P_\chi\). Positive congruence then transfers the variance budgets to the compact envelopes:

\[
V_{\rm out}\le CP_\chi D_\gamma^2P_\chi^*=CB_{\rm out},\qquad
V_{\rm in}\le CP_\chi^*D_\gamma^2P_\chi=CB_{\rm in}.
\]

By `L:compact-positive-order` (with \(t=p/2\)), \(V_{\rm out},V_{\rm in}\) are compact in \(S_{p/2}\) with

\[
\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^{p/2},\ \operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm in}^{p/2}
\le C^{p/2}\operatorname{Tr}B_{\rm out}^{p/2}
=C^{p/2}\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p .
\]

For each \(\ell\), \(Z_\ell Z_\ell^*\le V_{\rm out}\) (a positive summand), so \(Z_\ell Z_\ell^*\) is compact by the same lemma; the tail argument \(\|(1-Q_n)Z_\ell\|^2=\|(1-Q_n)Z_\ell Z_\ell^*(1-Q_n)\|\to0\) gives compactness of \(Z_\ell\), and eigenvalue comparison gives \(Z_\ell\in S_p\) with \(\|Z_\ell\|_{S_p}^p\le\operatorname{Tr}V_{\rm out}^{p/2}\). The \(C=0\) case makes everything zero.

## 9. PROOF083 (`C:finite-gaussian-operators`) — the infinite-dimensional limit

Fix even \(p=2m\) with \(p\gamma>d\). Let \(\Pi_n\) be finite-rank orthogonal projections with \(\Pi_n\to I\) strongly, and set \(Z_{\ell,n}=\Pi_nZ_\ell\Pi_n\), \(X_n=\sum_\ell g_\ell Z_{\ell,n}\).

**Compressed variance order.** For positive compact \(V\),

\[
\lambda_j(\Pi_nV\Pi_n)\le\lambda_j(V)
\]

because the max-min sup in the formula for \(\lambda_j(\Pi_nV\Pi_n)\) runs over \(j\)-dimensional subspaces **inside** \(\operatorname{ran}\Pi_n\), a subset of all \(j\)-dimensional subspaces. The matrix output variance obeys

\[
\sum_\ell\Pi_nZ_\ell\Pi_nZ_\ell^*\Pi_n\le\Pi_nV_{\rm out}\Pi_n,
\]

since the difference is \(\sum_\ell\Pi_nZ_\ell(1-\Pi_n)Z_\ell^*\Pi_n\ge0\); the input side is identical with adjoints. Hence both finite-matrix trace powers are bounded by \(C^{p/2}\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p\).

**Finite matrix step.** Applying `P:finite-gaussian-matrix`,

\[
\mathbb E\|X_n\|_{S_p}^p
\le(p-1)^{p/2}\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\widehat V_{\rm out}^m+\operatorname{Tr}\widehat V_{\rm in}^m}{2}
\le(p-1)^{p/2}C^{p/2}\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p .
\]

**Norm and singular-value passage.** For each Gaussian coordinate vector \(\omega\), \(X(\omega)=\sum_\ell g_\ell(\omega)Z_\ell\) is a finite combination of compact operators, hence compact. Strong convergence of \(\Pi_n\) implies \(\Pi_nX(\omega)\Pi_n\to X(\omega)\) in operator norm: approximate \(X\) by finite-rank \(F\), use uniform strong convergence on the finite-dimensional ranges of \(F,F^*\), and control the remainder via \(\|\Pi_n\|\le1\). The singular value formula

\[
s_j(A)=\inf_{\dim L<j}\ \sup_{\begin{smallmatrix}v\in L^\perp\\\|v\|=1\end{smallmatrix}}\|Av\|
\]

makes singular values norm-Lipschitz:

\[
s_j(A)-s_j(B)\le\|A-B\|,\qquad
s_j(B)-s_j(A)\le\|A-B\|.
\]

So \(s_j(X_n(\omega))\to s_j(X(\omega))\) pointwise. Fatou for the nonnegative singular-value sums gives

\[
\|X\|_{S_p}^p=\sum_js_j(X)^p\le\liminf_n\sum_js_j(X_n)^p=\liminf_n\|X_n\|_{S_p}^p.
\]

Taking expectation and using ordinary Fatou,

\[
\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_p}^p\le\liminf_n\mathbb E\|X_n\|_{S_p}^p
\le(p-1)^{p/2}C^{p/2}\|K_{\rm out}\|_{S_p}^p ,
\]

so `E:finite-gaussian-operator-bound` follows, with almost-sure finiteness of \(\|X\|_{S_p}\) because the displayed expectation is finite.

**Measurability and the no-extra-assumption note.** The Gaussian coordinate map into bounded operators is continuous, each singular value is continuous in operator norm, and the norm power is their measurable sum; the bound yields almost-sure finiteness. No \(S_p\)-Bochner convergence is used: the compression is only a device to apply the finite matrix inequality and pass to the limit by Fatou. This is the infinite-dimensional limit actually needed by the later driver step; no interpolation theorem (Riesz–Thorin or similar) is invoked anywhere in 075–084 — all moments are even \(p\) by direct integration by parts, and all \(S_p\) facts used are the trace identities above plus compact positive order.

## 10. PROOF084 (`P:cutoff-boundary-tests`)

**Rank-one witness.** Take \(v,f\in C_c^\infty\) supported in the plateau where \(\chi=1\), and set

\[
\widetilde v=(\Lambda^{1/2}v,0),\qquad\widetilde w=(\Lambda^{-1/2}f,0).
\]

The critical cancellations are

\[
\Lambda^{-1/2}\Lambda^{1/2}v=v,\qquad\Lambda^{1/2}\Lambda^{-1/2}f=f,
\]

so the actual block formulas give \(P_\chi\widetilde v=\widetilde v\) and \(P_\chi^*\widetilde w=\widetilde w\)**after** those exact cancellations (the mask acts on the Schwartz preimage, not on the noncompactly-supported Bessel image). Therefore \(Zx=\langle x,\widetilde w\rangle\widetilde v\) satisfies \(P_\chi Z=Z=ZP_\chi\). Its variances are the rank-one operators

\[
ZZ^*=\|\widetilde w\|^2\,\widetilde v\otimes\widetilde v^*,
\qquad
Z^*Z=\|\widetilde v\|^2\,\widetilde w\otimes\widetilde w^*.
\]

For every \(x\in H\) and Schwartz \(a\),

\[
|\langle x,a\rangle|=|\langle D_\gamma x,D_\gamma^{-1}a\rangle|
\le\|D_\gamma x\|\,\|D_\gamma^{-1}a\|,
\]

and \(D_\gamma^{-1}a\) is applied only to Schwartz \(a\), so it belongs to \(H\). Hence the two variance budgets hold with the finite constant

\[
C=\max\left(\|\widetilde w\|^2\|D_\gamma^{-1}\widetilde v\|^2,
\|\widetilde v\|^2\|D_\gamma^{-1}\widetilde w\|^2\right).
\]

This is a valid nonzero witness on a plateau whenever \(\chi=1\) on a nonempty open set; it is not claimed for arbitrary compact cutoffs nor identified with an actual source family.

**Unlocalized noncompactness.** Let \(\gamma>0\), \(f\) with nonzero Gaussian Fourier transform, \(g=\Lambda^{-\gamma}f\in\mathcal S\). Translates of \(f\) map to translates of \(g\), and

\[
\langle T_hg,g\rangle=\int e^{-i\xi\cdot h}\widehat g(\xi)\overline{\widehat g(\xi)}\,\mathrm d\xi,
\]

which is \(c_d^{-1}\widehat{|\widehat g|^2}(h)\to0\) because \(|\widehat g|^2\) is Schwartz. Hence \(\|T_hg-g\|^2\to2\|g\|^2\), so for large spacing the image set has no Cauchy subsequence while the inputs have identical norm: \(\Lambda^{-\gamma}\) is not compact. Consequently \(\chi=1\) (i.e., no compact cutoff) cannot be substituted into the compact-cutoff argument.

## Remaining mathematical gaps

1. `P:localized-finite-variance` remains **conditional**: the localization identities \(P_\chi Z_\ell=Z_\ell=Z_\ell P_\chi\) and the two \(CD_\gamma^2\) variance budgets are hypotheses in 082, not theorems. 3. `C:finite-gaussian-operators` applies at even \(p\ge2\), \(p\gamma>d\), and gives a finite Gaussian operator bound, not an \(S_p\)-valued path, an infinite series, or a random bounded-operator time process; those are strictly later stages and are not implied here.
4. The non-integer/\(s=0\) and rank-one examples shown here are consistency checks; they verify the mechanism and the stated upper bounds, not sharp constants.
5. Everything in this section is at a fixed spatial cutoff and fixed single oscillation layer; no uniform-in-growing-radius, source-convergence, Young, sharp-constant, or tube result follows, and none was assumed.
