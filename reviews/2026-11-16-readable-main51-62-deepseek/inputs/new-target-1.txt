\begin{corollary}[Ordinary product and Riesz covariances in dimension three]
  \label{C:three-product-riesz}
  Let $d = 3$, $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$, and $1 < \alpha < 2-\alpha_0$. The
  coefficient and all-time moment estimates in~\eqref{E:three-transfer}, and the
  asymptotic estimate in~\eqref{E:three-variational-upper}, hold for either of
  the following spatial covariances, with $c > 0$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\gamma(x) = c\prod_{j=1}^3|x_j|^{-a_j}$, where
      $0 \le a_j < 1$ and $\sum_j a_j = \alpha$;
    \item $\gamma(x) = c|x|^{-\alpha}$.
  \end{enumerate}
  In the first case the constant depends only on $a_1,a_2,a_3,c,\alpha_0$; in
  the second it depends only on $\alpha,c,\alpha_0$. A zero coordinate exponent
  denotes a constant factor; the case $a_j = 1$ and any interpretation of it as
  coordinate white noise are excluded.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  In both cases choose the fixed midpoint $\vartheta = (\alpha_0+2-\alpha)/2$
  and define $b_0,\chi$ by~\eqref{E:three-parameters}. Then $\alpha_0 <
  \vartheta < 2-\alpha$, and all subsequent choices depend only on the data in
  the statement. For the product covariance, Lemma~\ref{L:product-endpoints}
  supplies $b,v$ by its explicit interval formula. Its same-amplitude endpoint
  densities $\gamma_b,\gamma_v$ are admissible covariance measures of exponents
  one and $\chi$. Their common-width Gaussian inequality is exactly
  Hypothesis~\ref{H:three-endpoints}.

  For the Riesz covariance take $\gamma_1(x) = c|x|^{-1}$ and $\gamma_\chi(x) =
  c|x|^{-\chi}$. Each degree $e \in \{1,\alpha,\chi\}$ is less than three, so
  the corresponding density is locally integrable and tempered. Positive
  definiteness follows from the Gaussian mixture
  \[
    c|x|^{-e}=\frac{c}{\Gamma(e/2)}
    \int_0^\infty s^{e/2-1}e^{-s|x|^2}\ud s.
  \]
  For a Schwartz autocorrelation, absolute integrability follows from $e < 3$
  near zero and rapid decay at infinity. Tonelli and Fubini therefore justify
  integrating the positive Gaussian quadratic forms. The affine identity $\alpha
  = \vartheta+(1-\vartheta)\chi$ gives $\gamma_\alpha =
  \gamma_1^\vartheta\gamma_\chi^{1-\vartheta}$ almost everywhere, with the same
  amplitude $c$. H\"older on the Gaussian probability measure proves the
  required common-width comparison for every $\epsilon,z$. Thus, in both cases
  all endpoint hypotheses are discharged. Proposition~\ref{P:three-transfer}
  applies. This proves Corollary~\ref{C:three-product-riesz}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite sums of linear images]\label{C:three-finite-images}
  Let $d = 3$, $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$, and $1 < \alpha < 2-\alpha_0$. Let
  $\Gamma_i$, $1 \le i \le N$, belong to one of the following classes, always

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item the ordinary products in Corollary~\ref{C:three-product-riesz};
    \item the isotropic Riesz covariances in that corollary;
    \item the plane-supported measures of Lemma~\ref{L:plane-endpoints},

  Lemma~\ref{L:product-endpoints} supplies the ordinary-product endpoints. For a

\section{Physical and spectral energy for measure covariances}
\label{S:energy-fidelity}

This section justifies the positive-measure energy used
in~\eqref{E:three-static-variational}. It does not assume
Hypothesis~\ref{H:three-endpoints} or the conditional moment bound. The imported
analytic input is the finiteness of the variation on the right of (1.5), stated
in the closing sentence of Chen~\cite{chen:23:exponential}*{Theorem~1.1, p.~2}.

This identification of variational functionals does not remove the additional
hypotheses of the separate moment argument. It does not resolve general
covariance factorization.

\begin{lemma}[Sobolev energy identification]\label{L:physical-spectral-energy}
  Let $\Xi$ be a nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon covariance
  on $\R^3$, with $\Xi(rA) = r^{3-\chi}\Xi(A)$ for $0 < \chi < 2$. Let $\mu_\Xi$
  be its positive even spectral measure in the convention $\Xi = \int
  e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi)$, understood distributionally, and use the
  Fourier transform $\widehat{h}(\xi) = \int e^{-i\xi\cdot x}h(x)\ud x$. For
  every real $g \in W^{1,2}(\R^3)$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:physical-spectral-energy}
    \mathcal{Q}_\Xi(g)
    :=\int_{\R^3}\int_{\R^3}g(y+z)^2g(y)^2\ud y\,\Xi(\ud z)
    =\int_{\R^3}|\widehat{g^2}(\xi)|^2\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi)<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  The physical expression is independent of the Borel representative of $g$.
  There is a finite $C_0 \ge 0$, depending on $\Xi$, such that
  \begin{equation}\label{E:energy-quartic-bound}
    \mathcal{Q}_\Xi(g)
    \le\frac12\|g\|_2^2\|\nabla g\|_2^2+C_0\|g\|_2^4.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We use the imported finiteness only on smooth profiles. Two distinct
  Cauchy--Schwarz bounds control the physical and spectral approximation
  errors. We then identify both strong limits and pass the quartic bound to
  the full Sobolev class.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We obtain the smooth-profile energy bound.
  Fourier uniqueness gives $\mu_\Xi(rA) = r^\chi\mu_\Xi(A)$. Since its unit-ball
  mass is finite, the dyadic estimate with $\sum_{j\ge0}2^{j(\chi-2)}$ verifies
  Dalang's condition. For a real Schwartz function $h$ put
  \[
    \mathscr{E}(h)=\int|\widehat{h}(\xi)|^2\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi),
    \qquad Q(f)=\mathscr{E}(f^2).
  \]
  Schwartz Fourier pairing identifies $Q(f)$ with its ordinary positive-measure
  integral. From the finiteness assertion in
  Chen~\cite{chen:23:exponential}*{Theorem~1.1, closing sentence for the
  variation in (1.5), p.~2}, restricted to real unit-$L^2$ Schwartz profiles,
  there is $C_0 \ge 0$ such that $Q(g) \le \frac12\|\nabla g\|_2^2+C_0$ for
  $\|g\|_2 = 1$. For $f \ne 0$, quartic homogeneity gives
  \[
    Q(f)=\|f\|_2^4Q(f/\|f\|_2)
    \le\frac12\|f\|_2^2\|\nabla f\|_2^2+C_0\|f\|_2^4.
  \]
  The same inequality is immediate for $f = 0$. Only Schwartz pairings have been
  used at this point, so this import presupposes no energy identification for
  nonsmooth profiles. If $N(f)^2 = \|f\|_2^2+\|\nabla f\|_2^2$, it follows that
  $Q(f)^{1/2} \le D N(f)^2$ for some finite $D$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We estimate the two types of bilinear pairing.
  We need two different Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities. For real Schwartz $f,h$,
  spectral Cauchy--Schwarz gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      0\le P(f,h)
      &:=\int\Xi(\ud z)\int f(y+z)^2h(y)^2\ud y\\
      &=\int\widehat{f^2}(\xi)
      \overline{\widehat{h^2}(\xi)}\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi)
      \le Q(f)^{1/2}Q(h)^{1/2}.
    \end{split}
  \]
  Evenness makes the Fourier sign convention in this real pairing immaterial. On
  the other hand, Cauchy--Schwarz on the positive measure $\ud y\,\Xi(\ud z)$
  gives
  \[
    \mathscr{E}(fh)
    =\int\Xi(\ud z)\int
    f(y+z)f(y)h(y+z)h(y)\ud y
    \le Q(f)^{1/2}Q(h)^{1/2}.
  \]
  All these are absolutely convergent Schwartz pairings. In particular,
  $\mathscr{E}(fh)$ is the quadratic energy of $fh$, not the quartic expression
  $Q(fh)$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We compare the physical and spectral approximation
  maps.
  Let $\mathfrak{m}(\ud y,\ud z) = \ud y\,\Xi(\ud z)$ and define
  \[
    A_f(y,z)=f(y+z)f(y),\qquad B_f(\xi)=\widehat{f^2}(\xi).
  \]
  The measure $\mathfrak{m}$ is positive and sigma-finite. The decomposition
  \[
    A_f-A_h=(f-h)(y+z)f(y)+h(y+z)(f-h)(y)
  \]
  and the bound for $P$ imply
  \[
    \|A_f-A_h\|_{L^2(\mathfrak{m})}
    \le D\bigl(N(f)+N(h)\bigr)N(f-h).
  \]
  Since $f^2-h^2 = (f-h)(f+h)$, the bound for $\mathscr{E}$ gives
  \[
    \|B_f-B_h\|_{L^2(\mu_\Xi)}
    \le D N(f-h)N(f+h)
    \le D\bigl(N(f)+N(h)\bigr)N(f-h).
  \]


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We identify their strong limits for a Sobolev
  profile.
  Choose real Schwartz $f_n \to g$ in $W^{1,2}$. These estimates make both
  $A_{f_n}$ and $B_{f_n}$ Cauchy in their respective $L^2$ spaces. Pass to a
  subsequence with $f_n \to g$ Lebesgue-almost everywhere. Translation
  invariance of Lebesgue measure and sigma-finite Fubini give
  \[
    f_n(y+z)f_n(y)\longrightarrow g(y+z)g(y)
    \quad\text{for }\mathfrak{m}\text{-almost every }(y,z).
  \]
  Thus, the strong $L^2(\mathfrak{m})$ limit is $A_g$. The same Fubini argument
  shows that changing the Borel representative of $g$ does not change this
  limit.

  Also
  \[
    \|f_n^2-g^2\|_1
    \le\|f_n-g\|_2(\|f_n\|_2+\|g\|_2)\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  Hence, $\widehat{f_n^2} \to \widehat{g^2}$ uniformly on all frequencies. A
  subsequence of its strong $L^2(\mu_\Xi)$ limit converges $\mu_\Xi$-almost
  everywhere, and must agree with this uniform limit. Consequently, that strong
  limit is $B_g$. For every $n$, $\|A_{f_n}\|_{L^2(\mathfrak{m})}^2 = Q(f_n) =
  \|B_{f_n}\|_{L^2(\mu_\Xi)}^2$. Passing to the strong limits proves
  \eqref{E:physical-spectral-energy}, including finiteness. Passing the Schwartz
  quartic bound to the same limit proves \eqref{E:energy-quartic-bound}. This
  proves Lemma~\ref{L:physical-spectral-energy}.
\end{proof}

In particular, for $\|g\|_2 = 1$ the energy is at most $\|\nabla g\|_2^2/2+C_0$.
Therefore, the supremum of $\mathcal{Q}_\Xi(g)^{1/2}-\|\nabla g\|_2^2$ is
finite. Applied to $\Xi = \Gamma_{\rm st}$, the profile-by-profile identity
justifies the measure-form functional in~\eqref{E:three-static-variational}
without assigning a density to the covariance or borrowing a disputed
special-case constant.

\[

    \beta(a)=4-\alpha-a,\\
    \kappa(a)=\frac{\beta(a)}\nu,\qquad
    \kappa_{\rm st}=\frac{4-\alpha}\nu.
  \end{gathered}

covariance $|s-r|^{-a}\Gamma$, for $0 < a < a_{\max}$. At $a = 0$ define
$u^{(0)} = U$ to be the static canonical solution with the same spatial

From Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{Theorem~1.2, (1.9), p.~2147}, the
static first-mean rate $L_0$ exists and
\begin{equation}\label{E:static-ratio-input}
  0<L_0:=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-\kappa_{\rm st}}\log\E U(t,x)<\infty,
  \qquad
  \lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-\kappa_{\rm st}}\log\E U(t,x)^2

\end{equation}
We use only the common $p^{\kappa_{\rm st}}$ factor for $p = 1,2$; no numerical
value of the static variational constant or its white-noise specialization is
adopted. Strict positivity in that formula follows by using a nonzero Gaussian
competitor: its interaction and gradient terms scale as $A c^{\alpha/2}$ and $B
c^2$, with $A,B > 0$, so their difference is positive for small $c$. Finiteness
and the moment law are the imported source result. The static kernel and

source's quantitative (4.3). The generalized-covariance convention is explicit

  \int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{1+|\xi|^2}
  \le\mu(B_1)+C_\alpha\mu(B_1)\sum_{j\ge0}2^{j(\alpha-2)}<\infty.
\]
Thus, the static Dalang condition holds in every admissible dimension. Measure
homogeneity of degree $d-\alpha$ is equivalent to distributional homogeneity of
degree $-\alpha$, so the source's (1.8) applies in its explicit
generalized-covariance convention. The static object is identified by its
canonical heat-limit expansion; no uniqueness assertion for other mild solutions
is used.

For $a > 0$ write $X_n^{(a)}$ for our fixed-order terms; at zero use the
corresponding static terms. Set
\[
  m_k(a)=\E X_{2k}^{(a)}(1,0),\qquad m_0(a)=1.

\begin{lemma}[Mean coefficients for the power covariance]\label{L:mean-coefficient-holder}
  For every $0 \le a < a_{\max}$, the coefficients $m_k(a)$ are nonnegative, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:mean-positive-series}
    \E u^{(a)}(t,x)=\sum_{k\ge0}m_k(a)t^{\beta(a)k}.
  \end{equation}
  The series $F_a(z) = \sum_{k\ge0}m_k(a)z^k$ is finite for every $z \ge 0$. If
  $0 < a < b < a_{\max}$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:mean-coefficient-holder}
    m_k(a)\le m_k(0)^{1-a/b}m_k(b)^{a/b},\qquad k\ge1.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We construct the static endpoint series using a regularized positive
  comparison. We then interpolate single-chain coefficients on one common
  spatial measure, remove the spatial width, and recover the mean series by
  homogeneity.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We construct the static fixed-order limits and
  summable series.
  We first justify the static endpoint and its norm series. At positive spatial
  widths its ordered terms are the bounded-variance Gaussian Bochner integrals
  of Lemma~\ref{L:terminal-wick}, with a time-constant field.
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures}, applied with temporal multiplier one to
  the diagonal and mixed-width second moments, gives a common finite diagram
  limit. The same Hilbert Cauchy argument as in Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}
  therefore gives the static fixed-order limits $X_n^{(0)}$, with the source's
  heat-limit convention. This uses no infinite solution series.

  Fix any $b_0 \in (0,a_{\max})$ and $\delta_0 > 0$, and put $c =
  \gamma_{\delta_0,b_0}^0(1) > 0$. Let $U_N^\epsilon(r;1,x)$ be the static
  partial series at horizon one and noise amplitude $r > 1$. Compare its finite
  Wick sum with that of the fractional-time partial series at widths
  $\epsilon,\delta_0$, exponent $b_0$ and amplitude $r/\sqrt c$. Since
  $\gamma_{\delta_0,b_0}^0(s-r') \ge c$ for $s,r' \in [0,1]$, every pair factor
  on the second side dominates the static pair factor. Positivity, then the full
  regularized bound \eqref{E:regularized-scaled-moment}, gives
  \[
    \E|U_N^\epsilon(r;1,x)|^2
    \le \E|u^{\epsilon,\delta_0}_{b_0}(r/\sqrt c;1,x)|^2
    \le B_{b_0}(1;r/\sqrt c)<\infty,
  \]
  uniformly in $N,\epsilon$. The notation $B_{b_0}$ means the bound
  in~\eqref{E:regularized-scaled-moment} at temporal exponent $b_0$. Both
  positive-width full series exist by Lemma~\ref{L:terminal-wick}. Letting $N
  \to \infty$ at fixed $\epsilon$ preserves this bound.
  Lemma~\ref{L:uniform-series-tail} now applies to the static family (with only
  the spatial width varying). It proves absolute $L^2$ convergence of its
  limiting series at horizon one, with geometric coefficient bounds for every $r
  > 1$. Static fixed-order homogeneity gives
  \[
    \|X_n^{(0)}(t,x)\|_2
    =t^{(4-\alpha)n/2}\|X_n^{(0)}(1,0)\|_2.
  \]
  Choosing $r > \max\{1,t^{(4-\alpha)/2}\}$ in those bounds proves norm
  summability at every fixed $t > 0$. The resulting series has precisely the
  source's canonical static kernels and fixed-order limits.



  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We interpolate the temporal powers at fixed
  spatial smoothing.
  For a single chain of length $2k$, use the initial-chain variables of
  Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}. At common positive spatial width $\epsilon$, let
  $\omega_{\epsilon,D}$ be its finite positive spatial Green--Wick measure on
  the unit time simplex, with covariance $\gamma_{2\epsilon}$ on every pair. It
  is finite because that covariance is bounded and the Green masses are the time
  gaps. Define
  \[
    m_k^\epsilon(a)=\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}\int
    \prod_{\{j,h\}\in D}|s_j-s_h|^{-a}\ud\omega_{\epsilon,D},
    \qquad 0\le a<a_{\max}.
  \]
  These time integrals for the power covariance are finite. At fixed $\epsilon$
  the spatial factors and Green masses are bounded on the finite horizon; the
  disjoint pair endpoints give integrable powers on the full time cube since $a
  < 1$. Diagonals are null there. On the disjoint union of the pairing measures,
  the time multiplier at $a$ is exactly the $a/b$ power of its multiplier at
  $b$. H\"older's inequality therefore gives
  \[
    m_k^\epsilon(a)\le
    m_k^\epsilon(0)^{1-a/b}m_k^\epsilon(b)^{a/b}.
  \]
  This is an identity for the power covariance; no exponent-dependent Laplace
  cutoff or Euler Gamma normalization is being interpolated.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We remove the common spatial width.
  We justify removal of the common spatial width. For each positive exponent,
  the proof of Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order} gives this limit with the power
  covariance as well. Indeed, writing $F_{\epsilon,\eta,D}$ for the diagram with
  common temporal cutoff $\eta$, \eqref{E:joint-temporal-tail} gives
  \[
    F_{\epsilon,\eta,D}\le F_{\epsilon,0,D}
    \le F_{\epsilon,\eta,D}+r_a(\eta),\qquad r_a(\eta)\longrightarrow0,
  \]
  uniformly in $\epsilon$. First pass to the spatial limit at fixed $\eta$, then
  send $\eta$ to zero. The resulting limit is exactly the joint diagram value
  from that lemma. At zero exponent the multiplier is one, so
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures} gives the spatial limit directly; the
  positive-width Wick calculation and the imported static fixed-order
  convergence identify it with the static expectation. Thus, $m_k^\epsilon(a)
  \to m_k(a)$ at all three fixed exponents $0,a,b$,
  proving~\eqref{E:mean-coefficient-holder} and nonnegativity. No convergence
  uniform in $a$ is claimed.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We identify the homogeneous positive mean series.
  Scaling all times and spatial displacements by $t$ in a regularized one-chain
  diagram gives the factor $t^{2(2k)-\alpha k-ak} = t^{\beta(a)k}$ and changes
  the spatial width to $\epsilon/t^2$. Passing to the just established limits
  proves $\E X_{2k}^{(a)}(t,x) = t^{\beta(a)k}m_k(a)$. Odd expectations vanish
  by Wick's formula. For positive $a$ the independently proved norm summability
  in Lemma~\ref{L:vertex-crude} justifies the mean-series interchange. At zero
  the static norm series just proved does so, with nonnegative even means. This
  proves \eqref{E:mean-positive-series}. Since $\beta(a) > 0$, every $z \ge 0$
  equals $t^{\beta(a)}$ for some $t \ge 0$, and the series is finite. This
  proves Lemma~\ref{L:mean-coefficient-holder}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:positive-series-type}
\begin{lemma}[Type of a positive coefficient series]\label{L:positive-series-type}
  Let $\nu \ge 1$, $c_k \ge 0$, $c_0 = 1$, and suppose $F(z) =
  \sum_{k\ge0}c_kz^k < \infty$ for every $z \ge 0$. If
  \[
    \tau=\limsup_{z\to\infty}z^{-1/\nu}\log F(z)<\infty,
  \]
  then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:positive-series-type}
    K:=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\big((k!)^\nu c_k\big)^{1/k}<\infty,
    \qquad \tau=\nu K^{1/\nu}.
  \end{equation}
  Conversely, finite $K$ implies the same finite $\tau$ and equality.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We first bound each coefficient by evaluating the positive series at an
  order-dependent argument. Conversely, a finite coefficient type gives an
  exponential series bound after keeping finitely many low orders separately.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We pass from growth type to coefficient type.
  If $\tau < \infty$, choose $L > \tau$. Since $c_0 = 1$, $\tau \ge 0$, and
  continuity of the power series on compact positive intervals gives $F(z) \le
  C_L e^{Lz^{1/\nu}}$ for all $z \ge 0$. For $k \ge 1$, the inequality $c_kz^k
  \le F(z)$ at $z = (\nu k/L)^\nu$ yields
  \[
    c_k\le C_L e^{\nu k}\left(\frac L{\nu k}\right)^{\nu k}.
  \]
  Monotonicity of $\log x$ and integration on $[1,k]$ give
  \[
    k\log k-k+1\le\log(k!)
    \le k\log k-k+1+\log k.
  \]
  Consequently, $K \le (L/\nu)^\nu$. Letting $L\downarrow\tau$ proves $K \le
  (\tau/\nu)^\nu$, including $K = 0$ when $\tau = 0$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We recover growth type from the coefficient bound.
  Conversely, for any $A > K$ all sufficiently large coefficients obey $c_k \le
  A^k/(k!)^\nu$. Hence, for a polynomial $P$ with nonnegative coefficients,
  \[
    F(z)\le P(z)+\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(Az)^k}{(k!)^\nu}
    \le P(z)+\exp\{\nu(Az)^{1/\nu}\}.
  \]
  The last inequality is the elementary $\ell^\nu$ bound $\sum a_k^\nu \le (\sum
  a_k)^\nu$, applied to $a_k = (Az)^{k/\nu}/k!$. The polynomial contributes zero
  to the normalized logarithmic growth. Therefore, $\tau \le \nu A^{1/\nu}$; let
  $A\downarrow K$. Combining both inequalities proves the equality when either
  quantity is finite. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:positive-series-type}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Static temporal-minimum comparison]\label{P:static-minorant-lower}
  For every $0 < a < a_{\max}$, $T > 0$ and $p \in \{1,2\}$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-minorant-moment}
    \E[u^{(a)}(T,x)^p]
    \ge\E\left[U\left(T^{1-a/(4-\alpha)},x\right)^p\right].
  \end{equation}
  In particular,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-minorant-rate}
    \liminf_{T\to\infty}T^{-\kappa(a)}\log\E[u^{(a)}(T,x)^p]
    \ge p^{\kappa_{\rm st}}L_0,\qquad p=1,2.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We compare finite regularized moments with a static model using the minimum
  temporal covariance on the horizon. We remove regularizations at fixed
  order, sum, and then identify the static effective time.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We compare the finite expected powers at positive
  widths.
  Fix positive common spatial and temporal noise widths $\epsilon,\delta$. On
  $[0,T]$, the source's Laplace-prefix temporal covariance satisfies
  \[
    \gamma_{\delta,a}^0(s-r)\ge c_{\delta,T}:=
    \gamma_{\delta,a}^0(T)>0,\qquad
    c_{\delta,T}\longrightarrow T^{-a}\quad(\delta\downarrow0).
  \]
  Compare the order-$N$ partial series of $u^{(a)}$ with the same partial series
  of a static model having covariance $c_{\delta,T}\gamma_{2\epsilon}$. At
  positive widths, the mean or squared moment is a finite Wick sum of the same
  terminal Green kernels and pairings. Every spatial factor is the same
  nonnegative $\gamma_{2\epsilon}$; every time factor on the first side is at
  least $c_{\delta,T}$. Thus, the finite expected powers are ordered for $p =
  1,2$. For $d = 3$, remove Green smoothing first at these fixed positive noise
  widths using the bounded covariance calculation preceding
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures}.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We pass fixed-order limits and the convergent
  series.
  Now send $(\epsilon,\delta)$ jointly to zero at fixed $N$.
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order} gives the fractional-time $L^2$ limits. Static
  fixed-order $L^2$ convergence gives all static split products in $L^1$, by
  Cauchy--Schwarz. Dependence on $c_{\delta,T}$ is a finite polynomial at fixed
  order, so its limit is the static model with noise amplitude $\sigma =
  T^{-a/2}$. This requires no uniform approximation in $T$ or in a varying
  infinite-order series. At every fixed $\sigma,T > 0$, static norm homogeneity
  gives
  \[
    \sigma^n\|X_n^{(0)}(T,x)\|_2
    =\|X_n^{(0)}(\sigma^{2/(4-\alpha)}T,x)\|_2.
  \]
  Thus, the amplitude-scaled static norm series is summable at the effective
  time already covered by Lemma~\ref{L:mean-coefficient-holder}. Finally, let $N
  \to \infty$ using the two $L^2$ expansions, with the static one supplied in
  Lemma~\ref{L:mean-coefficient-holder}. Means and squared moments converge,
  preserving the finite-order inequality. No covariance-order coupling of the
  two Gaussian fields is asserted.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We identify the static amplitude clock and its
  rate.
  Write $U_\sigma$ for the static solution with noise amplitude $\sigma$. In its
  $p$th moment, a total order $2k$ has amplitude factor $\sigma^{2k}$ and, by
  static diagram homogeneity, time factor $T^{(4-\alpha)k}$. For $p = 1,2$, the
  finite sums and then the convergent nonnegative moment series therefore give
  the exact identity
  \[
    \E[U_\sigma(T,x)^p]
    =\E[U(\sigma^{2/(4-\alpha)}T,x)^p].
  \]
  Only moments are identified; no spatially indexed random fields are coupled.
  At $\sigma = T^{-a/2}$ this proves \eqref{E:static-minorant-moment}. The
  effective static time tends to infinity and
  \[
    \left(T^{1-a/(4-\alpha)}\right)^{\kappa_{\rm st}}
    =T^{\kappa(a)}.
  \]
  The imported static rates~\eqref{E:static-ratio-input} now prove
  \eqref{E:static-minorant-rate}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:static-minorant-lower}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Near-static strict gap from the mean rate]
  \label{P:near-static-mean-gap}
  Define
  \[
    M(a)=\limsup_{T\to\infty}T^{-\kappa(a)}\log\E u^{(a)}(T,x),\qquad
    K(a)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\big((k!)^\nu m_k(a)\big)^{1/k}.
  \]
  Then $K(0) = (L_0/\nu)^\nu > 0$, and $K(b) < \infty$ for every $0 < b <
  a_{\max}$. Fix such a $b$ and choose any finite $B_b \ge \max\{2K(0),K(b)\}$.
  For $0 < a < b$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:near-static-mean-upper}
    M(a)\le L_0\left(\frac{B_b}{K(0)}\right)^{a/(b\nu)}.
  \end{equation}
  In particular, with
  \begin{equation}\label{E:near-static-window}
    a_*:=\frac{b\log2}{\log(B_b/K(0))}\in(0,b],
  \end{equation}
  every $0 < a < a_*$ satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:near-static-mean-gap}
    \liminf_{T\to\infty}T^{-\kappa(a)}\log\E[u^{(a)}(T,x)^2]
    >2M(a).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We convert mean growth rates into coefficient types. Coefficient
  interpolation then bounds the positive-exponent mean rate, and the static
  lower comparison gives the strict gap on the stated window.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We identify finite endpoint coefficient types.
  By Lemma~\ref{L:mean-coefficient-holder}, the substitution $z = T^{\beta(a)}$
  identifies the normalized logarithmic type of $F_a(z)$ with the mean limsup
  $M(a)$; at zero it equals $L_0$. The imported static law and
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-series-type} give $K(0) = (L_0/\nu)^\nu$. For positive
  $b$, Cauchy--Schwarz and Theorem~\ref{T:full-upper}, applied at temporal
  exponent $b$, give $M(b) < \infty$. The same type lemma therefore gives $K(b)
  < \infty$. There is no use of a printed first-moment rate constant.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We interpolate the coefficient types and apply the
  static lower rate.
  Multiplying~\eqref{E:mean-coefficient-holder} by $(k!)^\nu$, taking $k$th
  roots and then limsups yields
  \[
    K(a)\le K(0)^{1-a/b}B_b^{a/b}.
  \]
  To justify this even if coefficients vanish, for any $\eta > 0$ both endpoint
  root sequences are eventually bounded by $K(0)+\eta$ and $B_b+\eta$; take
  their weighted product and then let $\eta\downarrow0$.
  Lemma~\ref{L:positive-series-type} now gives
  \[
    M(a)=\nu K(a)^{1/\nu}
    \le L_0\left(\frac{B_b}{K(0)}\right)^{a/(b\nu)},
  \]
  proving~\eqref{E:near-static-mean-upper}. Since $B_b/K(0) \ge 2$, the window
  in~\eqref{E:near-static-window} is positive and at most $b$. For $0 < a <
  a_*$,
  \[
    2M(a)<2L_0\cdot2^{1/\nu}
    =2^{\kappa_{\rm st}}L_0,
  \]
  because $\kappa_{\rm st} = 1+1/\nu$. Combine this with
  Proposition~\ref{P:static-minorant-lower} at $p = 2$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:near-static-mean-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope and calibrations]\label{R:near-static-scope}
  The static lower comparison holds at every admissible positive temporal
  exponent; the strict gap from twice the first-moment limsup is asserted only
  in the nonempty window~\eqref{E:near-static-window}. Its endpoint depends on
  the fixed covariance and the chosen reference exponent $b$; no uniformity over
  covariance shapes is claimed. As $a\downarrow0$, the comparison clock has
  exponent tending to one, the growth exponent tends to $\kappa_{\rm st}$, and
  the mean upper bound tends to $L_0$. The static second-moment constant is
  exactly $2^{\kappa_{\rm st}}L_0$.

  The gap concerns $\E u$, which is positive by its pairing series; no theorem
  for $\E|u|$ or all moment orders is asserted. If an intended source first-mean
  lower rate $L_*(a)$ is valid, then $L_*(a) \le M(a)$,
  so~\eqref{E:near-static-mean-gap} also improves the corresponding Jensen rate
  $2L_*(a)$ in this window. This does not establish the original lower target
  $2^{\kappa_{\rm st}}L_*(a)$, or either sharp bound for arbitrary $a$. The
  intrinsic formula in Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment} does not
  identify either printed source endpoint. The proof here uses the ordinary
  Stratonovich pairing expansion, including self-contractions; no Skorohod
  result or sibling-project development is made.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Temporal interpolation of the full moment}
\label{S:temporal-type-continuity}

The constant at a fixed positive temporal exponent is identified  in
Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.

Retain the fixed nonzero covariance and the notation of this section. For
\(p=1,2\), set \(c_{p,0}(a)=1\) and, for \(k\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\label{E:full-moment-coefficients}
  c_{1,k}(a)=m_k(a),\qquad
  c_{2,k}(a)=\sum_{m=0}^{2k}
  \E[X_m^{(a)}(1,0)X_{2k-m}^{(a)}(1,0)].
\end{equation}
The zero-exponent terms are the static limits constructed in
Lemma~\ref{L:mean-coefficient-holder}.

\begin{lemma}[Interpolation of the full moment coefficients]
  \label{L:full-moment-coefficient-holder}
  For \(0\le a<a_{\max}\) and \(p=1,2\), the coefficients are nonnegative and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:full-moment-positive-series}
    \E[u^{(a)}(t,x)^p]
    =\sum_{k\ge0}c_{p,k}(a)t^{\beta(a)k}<\infty,\qquad t>0.
  \end{equation}
  If \(0\le a_1<a<a_2<a_{\max}\) and \(\theta=(a-a_1)/(a_2-a_1)\), then
  \begin{equation}\label{E:full-moment-coefficient-holder}
    c_{p,k}(a)\le
    c_{p,k}(a_1)^{1-\theta}c_{p,k}(a_2)^\theta,\qquad k\ge0.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We interpolate the full coefficient sum at common spatial smoothing,
  retaining all splits. We then identify each endpoint limit and use diagram
  homogeneity to obtain the full positive moment series.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We place the mean and second-moment diagrams on
  their common measures.
  Work first at a common positive spatial noise width \(\epsilon\) and at unit
  horizon. For each split \(m+n=2k\) and pairing \(D\), let
  \(\omega_{\epsilon,m,n,D}\) be the finite positive spatial Green--Wick measure
  in the initial-chain convention of Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, with covariance
  \(\gamma_{2\epsilon}\). For \(p=1\) take the disjoint union over the single
  specified split \((m,n)=(0,2k)\) and all pairings. For \(p=2\), take the
  disjoint union over every \(m=0,\ldots,2k\) and all pairings. In particular,
  the two empty-chain orientations are distinct terms for \(p=2\), whereas
  \(p=1\) has just one. Wick's formula has coefficient one for each pairing on
  each ordered domain.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We interpolate the temporal power factors.
  With \(\omega_{\epsilon,p,k}\) denoting this disjoint-union measure, define
  its time integral for the power covariance by
  \[
    c_{p,k}^{\epsilon}(a)=
    \int \prod_{\{j,h\}\in D}|s_j-s_h|^{-a}
    \ud\omega_{\epsilon,p,k}.
  \]
  The pairing label in the multiplier is that of its component. These integrals
  are finite: the spatial covariances and Green masses are bounded on the unit
  horizons, and the disjoint pair endpoints give integrable factors on the full
  time cube since \(a<1\). The multiplier at \(a\) is exactly the product of the
  endpoint multipliers to powers \(1-\theta,\theta\), off null diagonals.
  Hölder's inequality on the disjoint union gives
  \[
    c_{p,k}^{\epsilon}(a)\le
    c_{p,k}^{\epsilon}(a_1)^{1-\theta}
    c_{p,k}^{\epsilon}(a_2)^\theta.
  \]


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We identify all fixed-order limits.
  For each fixed positive endpoint, the all-chain bound
  \eqref{E:joint-temporal-tail} and the common temporal-cutoff sandwich used in
  Lemma~\ref{L:mean-coefficient-holder} identify the time-domain spatial limit
  with the joint diagram value. This applies to every unequal or empty split,
  not only to a mean diagram. At exponent zero, the multiplier is one and
  Lemma~\ref{L:spatial-wick-measures} gives the same limit. The static diagonal
  and mixed-width Gaussian limits identify those diagram values with the split
  expectations of the constructed static terms: products converge in \(L^1\) by
  Cauchy--Schwarz, and the terminal-to-initial chain reversal is the one in
  Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, applied before removing widths. For positive
  exponents this identification is Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}. The union is
  finite at fixed \(k\); passing to the spatial limit proves
  \eqref{E:full-moment-coefficient-holder} and nonnegativity. No interpolation
  of exponent-dependent Laplace prefixes is used.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We use homogeneity and the convergent moment
  series.
  Every diagram has \(2k\) time Jacobians, \(2k\) Green scaling factors, \(k\)
  spatial covariance factors and \(k\) temporal factors. Thus, its homogeneous
  time degree is \(4k-\alpha k-ak=\beta(a)k\), including both empty-chain cases.
  For positive \(a\), the full second-moment series follows from
  Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}; the mean series is \eqref{E:mean-positive-series}.
  At zero, the static norm series gives convergence of the finite partial means
  and squared moments. Each finite moment expansion has nonnegative mixed terms,
  so its increasing limit may be regrouped by total order \(2k\). The
  homogeneity just proved then gives~\eqref{E:full-moment-positive-series}. This
  proves Lemma~\ref{L:full-moment-coefficient-holder}.
\end{proof}

For \(p=1,2\) define the normalized time growth rates
\begin{equation}\label{E:moment-growth-types}
  \begin{split}
    \overline{\Lambda}_p(a)&=\limsup_{T\to\infty}
    T^{-\kappa(a)}\log\E[u^{(a)}(T,x)^p],\\
    \underline{\Lambda}_p(a)&=\liminf_{T\to\infty}
    T^{-\kappa(a)}\log\E[u^{(a)}(T,x)^p].
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here \(p=1\) concerns \(\E u\), not \(\E|u|\).

\LeanMarker{P:moment-growth-logconvex}
\begin{proposition}[Log-convexity of the upper growth type]
  \label{P:moment-growth-logconvex}
  The functions \(\overline{\Lambda}_p(a)\), \(p=1,2\), are positive and finite
  on \(0\le a<a_{\max}\). For the endpoints and \(\theta\) in
  Lemma~\ref{L:full-moment-coefficient-holder},
  \begin{equation}\label{E:moment-growth-logconvex}
    \overline{\Lambda}_p(a)\le
    \overline{\Lambda}_p(a_1)^{1-\theta}
    \overline{\Lambda}_p(a_2)^\theta .
  \end{equation}
  In particular, \(\log\overline{\Lambda}_p\) is convex and
  \(\overline{\Lambda}_p\) is continuous on \((0,a_{\max})\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We establish that all rates are positive and finite, convert coefficient
  interpolation into growth-type interpolation, and then use convex secant
  slopes to obtain interior continuity.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We verify positivity and finiteness of the rates.
  Put \(C_p=p^{\kappa_{\rm st}}L_0>0\). The imported static law gives
  \(\overline{\Lambda}_p(0)=\underline{\Lambda}_p(0)=C_p\).
  Proposition~\ref{P:static-minorant-lower} gives \(\underline{\Lambda}_p(a)\ge
  C_p\) for positive \(a\). The full upper bound gives finiteness for \(p=2\).
  Cauchy--Schwarz gives finiteness for \(p=1\), whose mean is at least one by
  its positive series. Hence, all logarithms and rates used here are well
  defined, positive and finite.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We transfer coefficient interpolation to the
  logarithmic type.
  Let \(F_{p,a}(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}c_{p,k}(a)z^k\). It is finite for every
  \(z\ge0\), by Lemma~\ref{L:full-moment-coefficient-holder}. Its normalized
  logarithmic type equals \(\overline{\Lambda}_p(a)\): spatial stationarity
  makes the moment independent of \(x\), while \(z=T^{\beta(a)}\) and
  \(z^{1/\nu}=T^{\kappa(a)}\). Lemma~\ref{L:positive-series-type} therefore
  gives
  \[
    K_p(a):=\limsup_{k\to\infty}
    \big((k!)^\nu c_{p,k}(a)\big)^{1/k}
    =\left(\frac{\overline{\Lambda}_p(a)}{\nu}\right)^\nu.
  \]
  Multiplying the coefficient interpolation by \((k!)^\nu\), taking \(k\)th
  roots and then limsups gives
  \[
    K_p(a)\le K_p(a_1)^{1-\theta}K_p(a_2)^\theta.
  \]
  More explicitly, the two endpoint root sequences are eventually bounded by
  \(K_p(a_i)+\eta\); let \(\eta\downarrow0\) after taking their weighted
  product. This argument permits zero or oscillating coefficient subsequences.
  Taking the \(1/\nu\) power and multiplying by \(\nu\)
  proves~\eqref{E:moment-growth-logconvex}.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We obtain continuity from the convex slope bounds.
  For completeness, continuity follows directly from secant slopes. Write
  \(f=\log\overline{\Lambda}_p\). If \(L<l\le x<y\le r<R\) are in
  \((0,a_{\max})\), convexity gives
  \[
    \frac{f(l)-f(L)}{l-L}\le
    \frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\le
    \frac{f(R)-f(r)}{R-r}.
  \]
  Both outer slopes are finite. They bound the absolute difference quotient on
  each compact interior interval, proving continuity of \(f\), and hence of its
  exponential. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:moment-growth-logconvex}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{C:static-moment-constant-continuity}
\begin{corollary}[Recovery of the static moment constant]
  \label{C:static-moment-constant-continuity}
  Put \(C_p=p^{\kappa_{\rm st}}L_0\), \(p=1,2\). For every fixed
  \(0<b<a_{\max}\) and \(0<a<b\),
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-constant-envelope}
    C_p\le\underline{\Lambda}_p(a)\le\overline{\Lambda}_p(a)
    \le C_p\left(\frac{\overline{\Lambda}_p(b)}{C_p}\right)^{a/b}.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-constant-continuity}
    \lim_{a\downarrow0}\underline{\Lambda}_p(a)
    =\lim_{a\downarrow0}\overline{\Lambda}_p(a)=C_p,\qquad p=1,2,
  \end{equation}
  and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-moment-ratio-continuity}
    \lim_{a\downarrow0}
    \frac{\underline{\Lambda}_2(a)}{\overline{\Lambda}_1(a)}
    =2^{\kappa_{\rm st}}.
  \end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The lower estimate is Proposition~\ref{P:static-minorant-lower}. For the upper
  estimate, apply Proposition~\ref{P:moment-growth-logconvex} with endpoints
  \(0,b\), using \(\overline{\Lambda}_p(0)=C_p\). This
  proves~\eqref{E:static-constant-envelope}. The finite ratio
  \(\overline{\Lambda}_p(b)/C_p\) is at least one. With \(b\) and the covariance
  fixed, its \(a/b\) power tends to one. Squeezing both rates
  proves~\eqref{E:static-constant-continuity}. The numerator and denominator
  in~\eqref{E:static-moment-ratio-continuity} tend to \(2^{\kappa_{\rm st}}L_0\)
  and \(L_0>0\), respectively. Division gives the asserted ratio. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:static-moment-constant-continuity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Order of limits and comparison with the intrinsic constant]
  \label{R:temporal-type-scope}
  The static calibration now concerns the constant, not only the time exponent.
  It is an iterated statement: first take a time liminf or limsup at each fixed
  \(a\), with its own normalization \(T^{-\kappa(a)}\), and then send
  \(a\downarrow0\). No uniformity over covariances or \(T\), no simultaneous
  limit, and no existence of the time limit at a fixed positive \(a\) follows.
  Only the upper rate is asserted to be log-convex on the interior.

  Neither \(L_*(a)\) nor \(L^*(a)\) enters this argument. The constant \(L_0\)
  is the static first-mean rate. Parameter continuity alone does not identify
  the conjectured curve. The separate intrinsic second-moment value is proved in
  Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}. No all-\(p\), absolute-first-moment,
  Skorohod, novelty or circulation result is claimed.
\end{remark}

\section{A small-order test for direct Brownian clock copying}
\label{S:two-clock-screen}

The result concerns a specified gap-transform substitution. It does not rule out
ordering corrections, a diagonal-only representation, one-sided/asymptotic use,
or the original sharp conjecture.

Theorem~6.1 of~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{pp.~37--42} represents
the time Laplace transform of a \emph{fixed-order} mean by a spatial Brownian
path and an independent scalar Brownian clock. The proof uses nonnegative cut
sums \(c_l\), obtained from one-chain time order on p.~38, before the
wave-to-heat substitution on p.~39. Remark~6.3, p.~42, singles out that order as
the obstruction for more copies. We test the direct replacement by two spatial
Brownian paths and two scalar Brownian clocks, without an additional ordering
weight.

All transforms below are at fixed total order. We begin with the original
admissible case \(d=\alpha=1\), \(\Gamma=\delta_0\), \(0<a<1\), and
\(G_s(y)=\frac12\mathbf1_{\{|y|<s\}}\). This is a falsification case for an
intended general representation, not a restriction of Theorem~\ref{T:intro-second-moment}.

\LeanMarker{L:white-clock-laplace}
\begin{lemma}[White-space low-order Laplace transforms]
  \label{L:white-clock-laplace}
  The \((1,1)\) cross-pair gap transform is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:white-cross-clock-transform}
    \begin{split}
      K_\times(\theta_1,\theta_2)
      &:=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
      e^{-\theta_1s-\theta_2t}|s-t|^{-a}
      \frac{\min(s,t)}2\ud s\ud t\\
      &=\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)}
      {2(\theta_1+\theta_2)^2}
      \big(\theta_1^{a-1}+\theta_2^{a-1}\big),
      \qquad \theta_1,\theta_2>0.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  In particular, \(K_\times(\theta,\theta)=
  \Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)/(4\theta^{3-a})\). For the empty-plus-length-two
  self-pair, independent gap damping gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:white-self-clock-transform}
    K_{\rm self}(\eta_1,\eta_2)
    :=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\eta_1r_1-\eta_2r_2}r_2^{-a}\frac{r_1}2
    \ud r_1\ud r_2
    =\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)}
    {2\eta_1^2\eta_2^{1-a}}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We compute the cross-pair transform by splitting its time domain into two
  cones, then evaluate the independent gap integrals for the self-pair.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We compute the cross-pair Laplace transform.
  The spatial cross factor is
  \[
    \int G_s(y)G_t(z)\delta_0(y-z)\ud y\ud z
    =\frac14\int\mathbf1_{\{|y|<s\wedge t\}}\ud y
    =\frac{\min(s,t)}2.
  \]
  Split its time integral into \(s=t+v\) and \(t=s+v\), with \(v>0\). Each part
  is one half the product of
  \[
    \int_0^\infty t e^{-(\theta_1+\theta_2)t}\ud t
    =(\theta_1+\theta_2)^{-2}
  \]
  and \(\int_0^\infty e^{-\theta_i v}v^{-a}\ud v
  =\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a)\theta_i^{a-1}\). All integrands are nonnegative and
  finite since \(a<1\), so Tonelli justifies the separation. This proves
  \eqref{E:white-cross-clock-transform}.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We compute the self-pair gap transform.
  For one length-two chain, its delta covariance forces the two positions equal.
  The second Green factor is \(G_{r_2}(0)=1/2\), and integration of the first
  gives mass \(r_1\). The time difference is \(r_2\). The two elementary Laplace
  integrals therefore give \eqref{E:white-self-clock-transform}. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:white-clock-laplace}.
\end{proof}

The source's analytic time covariance is
\[
  \gamma_a^{\rm an}(z)
  =\big(z\,\operatorname{sgn}\Re z\big)^{-a},\qquad \Re z\ne0,
\]
with the power taken on the right half-plane. For \(r>0,v\ge0\), put
\begin{equation}\label{E:averaged-clock-kernel}
  \Psi_a(r,v)=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
  \int_0^\infty \lambda^{a-1}e^{-r\lambda-v\lambda^2/2}\ud\lambda.
\end{equation}
From the source's complex Laplace identity
\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(6.3), p.~41}, and the Gaussian
characteristic function, \(\Psi_a(r,v)=\E(r+iZ)^{-a}\) when \(Z\) is centered
normal with variance \(v\). Fubini at fixed \(r>0\) is justified by
\(\int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}e^{-r\lambda}\ud\lambda<\infty\). In particular,
\(\Psi_a\) is nonnegative, decreases with \(v\), and \(\Psi_a(r,v)\le r^{-a}\).

Return to an arbitrary nonzero covariance in Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}, with
temporal exponent \(a\), so \(0<a<1\), \(0<\alpha<2\) and \(a+\alpha<2\). Define
\[
  h_\Gamma(w)=\int e^{-w|\xi|^2/2}\mu(\ud\xi)
  =(p_w*\Gamma)(0),\qquad
  \mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)=\int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{q^2+|\xi|^2}.
\]
Both are positive and finite for \(w,q>0\). Spectral homogeneity gives
\(h_\Gamma(w)=w^{-\alpha/2}h_\Gamma(1)\) and
\(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)=q^{\alpha-2}\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)\). The cross-pair gap
transform at common damping is
\[
  K_\Gamma(\theta)=N_2(\theta)^2
\]
with temporal exponent \(a\) in~\eqref{E:damped-norm-definitions}. This is the
spatial-regularization limit of the \((1,1)\) diagram.

Let \(B_1,B_2\) be independent spatial standard Brownian motions. For auxiliary
standard Brownian clocks with \(\E[\beta_1(u)\beta_2(v)]=\rho\min(u,v)\),
\(-1\le\rho\le1\), independent of the spatial paths, the clock difference has
variance
\[
  v_\rho(u,v)=u+v-2\rho\min(u,v).
\]
At common damping \(\theta>0\), the direct unweighted, unrestricted two-clock
covariance substitution yields the averaged candidate
\begin{equation}\label{E:naive-clock-candidate}
  J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)
  :=\frac14\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
  e^{-\theta^2(u+v)/2}h_\Gamma(u+v)
  \Psi_a(\theta|u-v|,v_\rho(u,v))\ud u\ud v .
\end{equation}
Values on \(u=v\) do not affect the integral. The factor \(1/4\) is one factor
\(1/2\) for each wave-to-heat gap transform; \(h_\Gamma(u+v)\) is the spatial
average of \(\Gamma(B_1(u)-B_2(v))\). This average is interpreted by spatial
heat regularization. For the white covariance it is \((2\pi(u+v))^{-1/2}\). The
analytic covariance, not its absolute-value bound, is being copied.

\LeanMarker{P:clock-copy-obstruction}
\begin{proposition}[Obstruction to unweighted constant-correlation clock copying]
  \label{P:clock-copy-obstruction}
  For every covariance and parameters just specified, put
  \begin{equation}\label{E:cross-clock-lambda-weight}
    w_\theta(\lambda)
    =\frac{\lambda^{a-2}}
    {2\theta\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
    \big(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta)
    -\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda)\big),\qquad\lambda>0.
  \end{equation}
  This is a positive integrable density, and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:clock-copy-obstruction}
    \begin{split}
      K_\Gamma(\theta)&=\int_0^\infty w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda,\\
      0<J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)
      &\le J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)
      =\int_0^\infty
      \frac{2\theta}{2\theta+\lambda}w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda
      <K_\Gamma(\theta).
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  When \(\alpha=1\),
  \begin{equation}\label{E:degree-one-clock-loss}
    J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)=(2-2^a)K_\Gamma(\theta).
  \end{equation}
  Thus, this direct analytic clock-copying family, including independent clocks
  and a single shared clock, fails to reproduce the cross-pair transform
  throughout the original nonzero covariance class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We first compute the exact original cross transform. We bound the clock
  candidates and evaluate the largest one, corresponding to a shared clock.
  Comparing the two positive densities gives strict loss, and degree-one
  homogeneity then gives its exact factor.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We compute the density of the original cross
  transform.
  First evaluate the cross transform. Temporarily smooth the spatial covariance,
  so Fourier and time Fubini are absolute. For \(r=|\xi|\), direct integration
  over \(s=t+v\) and \(t=s+v\) gives
  \[
    \int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\theta(s+t)-\lambda|s-t|}
    \widehat{G}_s(\xi)\overline{\widehat{G}_t(\xi)}\ud s\ud t
    =\frac{2\theta+\lambda}
    {2\theta(\theta^2+r^2)((\theta+\lambda)^2+r^2)} .
  \]
  For example, on the first cone the \(v\)-integral uses
  \[
    \int_0^\infty e^{-(\theta+\lambda)v}
    \sin(r(t+v))\ud v
    =\frac{(\theta+\lambda)\sin(rt)+r\cos(rt)}
    {(\theta+\lambda)^2+r^2}.
  \]
  Integrating the remaining sine-square and sine-cosine terms against
  \(e^{-2\theta t}\), and adding the second cone, gives the displayed identity.
  The value at \(r=0\) is its continuous limit.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We remove spatial smoothing in the nonnegative
  spectral expression.
  The resulting spectral integrand is nonnegative. Removing the spatial Gaussian
  multiplier and using the Laplace formula for the power covariance therefore
  gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      K_\Gamma(\theta)
      &=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}
      \frac{2\theta+\lambda}{2\theta}
      \int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}
      {(\theta^2+|\xi|^2)((\theta+\lambda)^2+|\xi|^2)}
      \ud\lambda\\
      &=\int_0^\infty w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda.
    \end{split}
  \]
  The second equality is partial fractions, with
  \((\theta+\lambda)^2-\theta^2=\lambda(2\theta+\lambda)\). The smoothing
  passage is justified by monotone convergence in this nonnegative spectral
  expression and the finite quadratic form in
  Lemma~\ref{L:damped-homogeneous-norms}. Alternatively, homogeneity directly
  bounds the density near zero by a constant times \(\lambda^{a-1}\), and at
  infinity by a constant times \(\lambda^{a-2}\). Its strict positivity follows
  from \(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta) -\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda)>0\),
  since \(\mu\ne0\).


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We prove integrability and compare the clock
  correlations.
  The candidate integrals are well defined and positive. The bound
  \(\Psi_a(\theta|u-v|,v_\rho)\le (\theta|u-v|)^{-a}\), together with
  \(h_\Gamma(w)=h_\Gamma(1)w^{-\alpha/2}\), supplies an absolute majorant. With
  \(w=u+v\), \(q=u-v\), the Jacobian is \(1/2\) and \(-w<q<w\). It is a constant
  times
  \[
    \int_0^\infty e^{-\theta^2w/2}w^{-\alpha/2}
    \int_{-w}^w|q|^{-a}\ud q\ud w
    =\frac2{1-a}\int_0^\infty
    e^{-\theta^2w/2}w^{1-\alpha/2-a}\ud w<\infty .
  \]
  Here \(a<1\) and \(a+\alpha/2<2\). The same bound dominates the spatial
  heat-regularized averages, whose spatial factor is \(h_\Gamma(w+2\epsilon)\le
  h_\Gamma(w)\). This is an averaged spectral bound, not pointwise covariance
  monotonicity. Since \(v_\rho(u,v)\ge|u-v|=v_1(u,v)\), monotonicity of
  \(\Psi_a\) gives \(J_{\Gamma,\rho}\le J_{\Gamma,1}\).


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We evaluate the shared-clock candidate and compare
  its density.
  We compute the latter by nonnegative Tonelli. Write
  \(A=\theta\lambda+\lambda^2/2\). Integration in \(q\) gives
  \(2(1-e^{-Aw})/A\). Also
  \[
    \mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)
    =\frac12\int_0^\infty e^{-q^2w/2}h_\Gamma(w)\ud w,
  \]
  by the elementary resolvent integral and spectral Tonelli. Consequently,
  \[
    \begin{split}
      J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)
      &=\frac1{4\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\frac{\lambda^{a-1}}A
      \int_0^\infty h_\Gamma(w)
      \left(e^{-\theta^2w/2}
      -e^{-(\theta+\lambda)^2w/2}\right)\ud w\ud\lambda\\
      &=\frac1{\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty
      \frac{\lambda^{a-2}
      (\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta)-\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\theta+\lambda))}
      {2\theta+\lambda}\ud\lambda\\
      &=\int_0^\infty
      \frac{2\theta}{2\theta+\lambda}w_\theta(\lambda)\ud\lambda.
    \end{split}
  \]
  The \(q\)-integral's factor two cancels its coordinate Jacobian \(1/2\); the
  resolvent identity supplies the other factor two. Since \(w_\theta>0\) and
  \(2\theta/(2\theta+\lambda)<1\) for every \(\lambda>0\), the strict inequality
  follows.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 5.~}We compute the exact loss at spatial degree one.
  Finally, if \(\alpha=1\), homogeneity gives
  \(\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(q)=\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)/q\). Substitution in the two
  densities gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      K_\Gamma(\theta)
      &=\frac{\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)}
      {2\theta^2\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\frac{\lambda^{a-1}}{\theta+\lambda}\ud\lambda,\\
      J_{\Gamma,1}(\theta)
      &=\frac{\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(1)}
      {\theta^2\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)}
      \int_0^\infty\lambda^{a-1}
      \left(\frac1{\theta+\lambda}
      -\frac1{2\theta+\lambda}\right)\ud\lambda .
    \end{split}
  \]
  The real Beta identity
  \[
    \int_0^\infty\frac{x^{a-1}}{x+c}\ud x
    =c^{a-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(a)\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}(1-a),
    \qquad c>0,
  \]
  follows by scaling and \((r,s)\mapsto(r+s,r/s)\) in the positive product of
  the two Gamma integrals. Their finiteness uses \(0<a<1\). Evaluation of these
  two integrals gives \(J_{\Gamma,1}/K_\Gamma=2(1-2^{a-1})=2-2^a\), proving
  \eqref{E:degree-one-clock-loss}. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:clock-copy-obstruction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Normalization and scope of the obstruction]
  \label{R:clock-copy-scope}
  At the static endpoint define the time covariance directly as one. The
  candidate with \(\Psi_0=1\) gives
  \[
    J_{\Gamma,\rho}(\theta)
    =\int\frac{\mu(\ud\xi)}{(\theta^2+|\xi|^2)^2}
    =K_\Gamma(\theta).
  \]
  This follows by integrating \(w e^{-\theta^2w/2}h_\Gamma(w)/4\) spectrally. In
  the white case it equals \(1/(4\theta^3)\). Thus, static and
  homogeneous-degree checks alone miss the positive-\(a\) loss. Relative to the
  shared-clock value \(\rho=1\), the omitted cross-pair multiplier is exactly
  \(1+\lambda/(2\theta)\). For smaller correlations the deficit can be larger.
  This does not assert a correction rule at higher orders.

  The transforms \(K_\times\) use gap damping. For the auxiliary cutoff function
  \[
    F(T_1,T_2)=\int_0^{T_1}\int_0^{T_2}
    |s-t|^{-a}\frac{\min(s,t)}2\ud s\ud t,
  \]
  nonnegative Fubini adds \(1/(\theta_1\theta_2)\) to its double
  external-horizon transform. This function is not an unequal-time solution
  correlation. Reversing chains ending at different physical times instead
  shifts the cross covariance to \(|T_1-T_2-s+t|^{-a}\). Setting the two damping
  parameters equal is also not the single Laplace transform of a
  diagonal-horizon moment. For a self-pair at common gap damping, one external
  horizon instead adds \(1/\theta\), recovering the known first-mean
  coefficient.

  The proposition rules out exactly the analytic covariance substitution
  in~\eqref{E:naive-clock-candidate}, with product heat-chain measure,
  unrestricted operational-time rectangle and constant Brownian correlation.
  Additional relative-order weights,other derived clock couplings,matrix
  representations,modulus inequalities and their possible asymptotics are not
  covered. In particular, the inequality at this first cross pair leaves open a
  shared-clock lower comparison or asymptotic equivalence at higher orders. This
  finite clock calculation does not identify the intrinsic value proved  in
  Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment} with the source's all-moment
  conjecture. This is not a Skorohod result or a no-go for every two-clock
  method.
\end{remark}

No-go~\ref{P:nogo-18} records a different degree-four obstruction: erasing
an additional shift makes one upper envelope too large to close its
comparison, without refuting the comparison itself.
