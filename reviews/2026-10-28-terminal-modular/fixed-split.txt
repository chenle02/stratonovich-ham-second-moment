\begin{lemma}[A fixed degree-split estimate]\label{L:fixed-split}
  Retain the finite interval fields above, with $R_\alpha>0$ and
  $\sigma_i>0$. Fix integers $r_i,q_i,k_i\ge0$ with
  $r_i+q_i=2k_i$, and put $k=\sum_i k_i\ge1$, $\lambda=k/T$.
  Represent $W_i^\epsilon(x)=\sum_jg_{ij}e_{ij}(x)$ as in
  Lemma~\ref{L:interval-contractions}. Retain $m_i$ Gaussian modes,
  a spatial Fourier band $S_b$, and Hermite cutoff $N_i=2k_i$ on
  color $i$. Write $P=\bigotimes_iP_{N_i}$
  and set
  \[
    B_i=P S_b\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m_i}g_{ij}M_{e_{ij}}\right)S_bP,
    \qquad V_{\boldsymbol z}(t)
    =\sum_i z_i\sigma_i\mathbf1_{I_i}(t)B_i.
  \]
  Let $\mathcal M^{\rm cut}_{\boldsymbol z}(T)$ be the phase evolution
  with this bounded potential and $A_0=-\Delta$ on the band. Define
  $U_{\boldsymbol r}v=[\boldsymbol z^{\boldsymbol r}]
  \mathcal M^{\rm cut}_{\boldsymbol z}(T)v$, with the projected
  deterministic initial phase in the Gaussian vacuum. Put
  \[
    \begin{gathered}
      \mathcal I=\{i:k_i>0\},\qquad
      \mathcal C=\{i:k_i=1,\ r_i=q_i=1\},\\
      \mathcal B=\mathcal I\setminus\mathcal C,\qquad
      \ell_i=\mathbf1_{\{r_i>0\}}+\mathbf1_{\{q_i>0\}}.
    \end{gathered}
  \]
  There is a finite $C_{\rm split}=C_{\rm split}(\alpha)$, uniform
  in all these cutoffs and in the finite inventory, such that
  \begin{equation}\label{E:fixed-split-bound}
    \begin{split}
      |\langle U_{\boldsymbol r}v,U_{\boldsymbol q}v
      \rangle_{L^2(\Omega;X_{1/T})}|
      \le{}&k^2 C_{\rm split}^k R_\alpha^k\|v\|_{1/T}^2
      \prod_{i\in\mathcal C}
      (\sigma_i^2h_i^2\lambda^{\alpha-2})\\
      &\quad\times\prod_{i\in\mathcal B}
      \frac{(\sigma_i^2h_i^{4-\alpha})^{k_i}}{(k_i!)^\nu}
      (1+k_i/(\lambda h_i))^{\ell_i}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  In particular, a singleton used twice on one copy has $\ell_i=1$;
  a higher color has at most two metric factors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  All cutoff generators are bounded and piecewise constant in time.
  Their evolution is a finite product of bounded block exponentials,
  hence is entire in every complex amplitude. Cauchy extraction of its
  vector coefficients is therefore legitimate. Write
  $\mathcal H_\rho=L^2(\Omega;X_\rho)$ and
  $Q_{\boldsymbol r,\boldsymbol q}
  =\langle U_{\boldsymbol r}v,U_{\boldsymbol q}v\rangle_{\mathcal H_{1/T}}$.
  Both copies use the same Gaussian fields.
  Coordinates outside $\mathcal I$ have degree zero on both copies and
  are evaluated at zero. Thus, the argument concerns at most $k$ fields,
  without changing their original labels in the final inventory sum.

  \emph{The split determines which norm costs are present.}
  Put $\lambda=k/T$. All fields are centered; the following names describe
  their appearances in the coefficient, not their Gaussian means:
  \[
    \begin{array}{c|c|c}
      k_i & (r_i,q_i)&\text{method}\\ \hline
      1 &(2,0)\text{ or }(0,2)&\text{mean term on one copy}\\
      1 &(1,1)&\text{one insertion on each copy}\\
      \ge2 &r_i+q_i=2k_i&\text{Cauchy on each copy where used}.
    \end{array}
  \]
  Call the second class $\mathcal C$. For the other active colors set
  \[
    \omega_i=k_i/h_i,\qquad
    d_i=2\sqrt{2k_i+1}\sqrt{R_\alpha},\qquad
    \varrho_i=\frac{\delta_a\omega_i^{2-a}}{\sigma_i d_i},
    \qquad a=\alpha/2.
  \]
  Put $\mathcal A_r=\{i\notin\mathcal C:r_i>0\}$ and
  $\mathcal A_q=\{i\notin\mathcal C:q_i>0\}$, restricting to active colors.
  On either copy write $\mathcal A$ for its corresponding set.
  Its unused amplitudes are evaluated at zero.
  In particular, a split $(2,0)$ supplies no field or cost on the second
  copy. Use the Cauchy circles $|z_i|=\varrho_i$ for $i\in\mathcal A$.

  With the colors in $\mathcal C$ set to zero, write $P_z(t,s)$ for this
  background evolution. Its phase weight is
  \[
    \Lambda(t)=\lambda+\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}
    \omega_i\mathbf1_{I_i}(t).
  \]
  This is a bounded deterministic operator on the Hilbert space containing
  all the retained Gaussian factors. Its input may depend on the very
  fields present in the background. No conditional independence between
  successive background segments is assumed.
  Lemma~\ref{L:interval-contractions}, tensored with all other color
  factors, gives for every $\rho>0$ the sandwich coefficient
  $D(t)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}|z_i|\sigma_i d_i
  \mathbf1_{I_i}(t)$.
  On every constant segment,
  \[
    D(t)\le\delta_a\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}
    \omega_i^{2-a}\mathbf1_{I_i}(t)
    \le\delta_a\Lambda(t)^{2-a}.
  \]
  The last inequality uses $2-a>1$.
  Lemma~\ref{L:interval-complex} bounds each segment by
  $100e^{2\Lambda(t)\Delta t}$ in its own phase norm.
  At most $k$ colors have at most $2k$ endpoints, so there are at most
  $2k+1$ such segments. Also
  \[
    \int_0^T\Lambda(t)\ud t
    \le\lambda T+\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\omega_i h_i\le2k.
  \]
  The norm changes, including the initial and final ones, cost at most
  \begin{equation}\label{E:interval-copy-metric}
    D_{\mathcal A}:=\prod_{i\in\mathcal A}
    (1+k_i/(\lambda h_i)).
  \end{equation}
  Only an ending interval can cause a loss. Its single ending is counted
  once, even if other intervals overlap it or end simultaneously.

  \emph{The simultaneous single-insertion coefficient.}
  Let $m=|\mathcal C|$ and let $J_i(u,w)=(0,\sigma_iW_i^\epsilon u)$,
  with the stated finite cutoffs. Regard the background amplitudes as
  fixed and give colors in $\mathcal C$ amplitudes $y_i$.
  Dyson expansion relative to $P_z$ shows that the coefficient of
  $\prod_{i\in\mathcal C}y_i$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{E:interval-cross-cube}
    \sum_{\pi}\int_{D_\pi}
    P_z(T,t_{\pi_m})J_{\pi_m}
    P_z(t_{\pi_m},t_{\pi_{m-1}})\cdots
    J_{\pi_1}P_z(t_{\pi_1},0)v\,\ud\boldsymbol t,
  \end{equation}
  where $\pi$ runs over permutations of $\mathcal C$ and
  \[
    D_\pi=\{\boldsymbol t\in
    \prod_{i\in\mathcal C}(I_i\cap[0,T]):
    t_{\pi_1}<\cdots<t_{\pi_m}\}.
  \]
  Indeed, a degree-one appearance of every $y_i$ uses exactly $m$
  insertions and a permutation of the $m$ distinct colors. Each ordered
  Dyson integral has coefficient one. The $D_\pi$ partition the cube up
  to null diagonals. Thus the sum in~\eqref{E:interval-cross-cube} is a
  single integral of the time-sorted word over that cube, without a
  multiplier $m!$ or $1/m!$. If $m=0$, it means $P_z(T,0)v$.

  Before $J_i$ occurs, color $i$ is in its vacuum: the background and
  all other insertions act identically on its Gaussian factor.
  Lemma~\ref{L:interval-contractions} therefore bounds this insertion
  from the other color factors into the enlarged phase space by
  \[
    \sigma_i\sqrt{R_\alpha}\Lambda(t_i)^{(\alpha-2)/2}
    \le\sigma_i\sqrt{R_\alpha}\lambda^{(\alpha-2)/2}.
  \]
  Previously created colors are part of the remaining tensor factor in
  that lemma. No estimate for annihilation on an occupied color is used
  at a single-insertion step.

  Apply these bounds sequentially along a word. Splitting background
  segments at the $m\le k$ insertion times gives at most $3k+1$ pieces.
  Their exponential costs still multiply to at most $e^{4k}$ and their
  fixed constants to at most $100^{3k+1}$. Each metric change still occurs
  just once along the word, so its entire metric budget is
  $D_{\mathcal A}$, not a new power of that budget at each insertion.
  Minkowski over the cube consequently bounds its vector coefficient by
  \begin{equation}\label{E:interval-cross-vector}
    C^kD_{\mathcal A}\|v\|_\lambda
    \prod_{i\in\mathcal C}
    \sigma_i\sqrt{R_\alpha}\lambda^{(\alpha-2)/2}h_i .
  \end{equation}
  This holds uniformly on all the background Cauchy circles.

  \emph{Vector Cauchy and the fixed split.}
  The vector in~\eqref{E:interval-cross-vector} is holomorphic in the
  remaining amplitudes. For the first copy Cauchy's vector formula gives
  the additional factor $\prod_{i\in\mathcal A_r}\varrho_i^{-r_i}$;
  on the second copy it gives $\prod_{i\in\mathcal A_q}\varrho_i^{-q_i}$.
  This is Cauchy extraction of two vector coefficients, followed by
  Cauchy--Schwarz between them, not Cauchy applied to a squared norm.
  Since $\|w\|_{1/T}\le k\|w\|_\lambda$ and
  $\|v\|_\lambda\le\|v\|_{1/T}$, the bound for
  $|Q_{\boldsymbol r,\boldsymbol q}|$ has a further factor $k^2$.
  Keep $k^2$ explicit. The segment constants on both copies are bounded
  by a constant to the power $k$.
  The combined metric factor is $D_{\mathcal A_r}D_{\mathcal A_q}$.
  Its exponent for color $i$ is the number of positive entries in
  $(r_i,q_i)$: one for a mean singleton, at most two for a higher color.

  For any color estimated on circles, the two exponents add to $2k_i$.
  Since $d_i^2\le12k_iR_\alpha$,
  \[
    \varrho_i^{-2k_i}
    \le(CR_\alpha\sigma_i^2h_i^{4-\alpha})^{k_i}
    k_i^{-(3-\alpha)k_i}
    \le\frac{(CR_\alpha\sigma_i^2h_i^{4-\alpha})^{k_i}}
    {(k_i!)^\nu}.
  \]
  The cross colors contribute one factor
  $R_\alpha\sigma_i^2h_i^2\lambda^{\alpha-2}$ each.
  The circle colors contribute the displayed radius bound and exactly
  $\ell_i$ metric factors. Since
  $|\mathcal C|+\sum_{i\in\mathcal B}k_i=k$,
  their covariance factors multiply to $R_\alpha^k$.
  The fixed segment constants and powers of $\delta_{\alpha/2}^{-1}$
  are absorbed into $C_{\rm split}^k$. This gives
  \eqref{E:fixed-split-bound}. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:fixed-split}.
\end{proof}
