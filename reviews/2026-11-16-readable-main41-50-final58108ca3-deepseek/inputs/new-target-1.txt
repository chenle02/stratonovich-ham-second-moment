\section{Riesz bounds by interpolation with static noise}
\label{S:static-holder}

This alternative argument concerns isotropic Riesz covariance in dimensions two
and three. It does not use the preceding conditional proposition, settle general
anisotropic covariance, or identify the sharp constant. The full upper and
intrinsic formula use separate arguments.

We use the static Stratonovich model of Chen--Hu as one input and the
space-degree-one estimate of Section~\ref{S:d1-upper} as the other. The static
source allows nonnegative generalized covariances, not only Riesz functions: see
its standing assumptions (1.2)--(1.3), p.~2145, and its explicit
generalized-covariance convention (2.18), p.~2152,
in~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}. In the latter convention every covariance
factor is heat regularized before its defining limit. A nonnegative, even,
positive-definite tempered Radon measure is such a generalized covariance. In
each application below we separately verify its homogeneity range and static
Dalang condition before using the source's quantitative coefficient estimates
(5.1) and (5.15). Positive definiteness of a weighted endpoint is proved in
Lemma~\ref{L:planar-endpoints}, or is an explicit assumption in
Hypothesis~\ref{H:three-endpoints}; weighting a measure alone is not being used
to assert that property. Chen's variational finiteness theorem supplies only
finiteness of the static constant, not a coefficient-rate theorem.

For $d \in \{2,3\}$, $c > 0$, and $0 < \lambda < \min\{2,d\}$, write
\[
  \gamma_\lambda(x)=c|x|^{-\lambda},\qquad
  \gamma_{\lambda,\epsilon}=p_\epsilon*\gamma_\lambda.
\]
All these covariances use the same amplitude $c$ and Gaussian convention. They
are nonnegative, locally integrable, and homogeneous. Their nonnegative
definiteness follows from
\[
  \gamma_\lambda(x)=\frac{c}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)}
  \int_0^\infty s^{\lambda/2-1}e^{-s|x|^2}\ud s.
\]
Indeed, each Gaussian is positive definite. For a Schwartz autocorrelation
$\psi$, the integral $\int |x|^{-\lambda}|\psi(x)|\ud x$ is finite by $\lambda <
d$ and rapid decay. Tonelli with $|\psi|$, followed by Fubini, therefore
justifies the positive Gaussian mixture as a tempered distribution. The source
hypotheses for the Riesz covariances below are thus satisfied.

\begin{lemma}[Static coefficient input]\label{L:static-holder-input}
  Let $U_n(t,x)$ be the ordered Stratonovich integrals for static Gaussian noise
  with covariance $\gamma_\chi$, where $d \in \{2,3\}$ and $1 < \chi < 2$. Put
  $U_0 = 1$ and
  \[
    B_k(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\E[U_m(t,x)U_{2k-m}(t,x)].
  \]
  Then $B_0 = 1$, and there is a finite constant $C_{\rm st} = C_{\rm
  st}(\chi,c,d) > 0$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-holder-input}
    0\le B_k(t)\le
    \frac{C_{\rm st}^{\,k}t^{(4-\chi)k}}{(k!)^{3-\chi}},
    \qquad k\ge1,\quad t>0.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We identify the full static coefficient sum using the imported fixed-order
  inputs. The imported upper coefficient rate controls all sufficiently large
  orders; the finitely many remaining orders are absorbed into one constant.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We verify the static input and pairing
  interpretation.
  The static solution has data $(1,0)$ and wave operator $\Delta$, as here. The
  degree satisfies $\chi < \min\{2,d\}$, so the Riesz covariance meets the
  source's Dalang condition. From
  Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{Theorem~3.8(i), p.~2165}, the
  fixed-order integrals exist in $L^2$. Their product rule and Wick expectation
  formula in that source's (2.17) and (2.19), pp.~2151--2152, identify $B_k$
  with the complete nonnegative sum over the $2k+1$ splits and every pairing of
  the $2k$ vertices. In particular, it includes within-chain pairs; no
  orthogonality of different orders is asserted. Empty chains contribute the
  factor one.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We convert the upper coefficient rate into an
  all-order bound.
  From~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{(5.1), p.~2173, and (5.15), p.~2180},
  specialized to $p = 2$, we have
  \[
    B_k(t)=t^{(4-\chi)k}B_k(1),\qquad
    \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac1k
    \log\left((k!)^{3-\chi}B_k(1)\right)<\infty.
  \]
  Only the finite upper coefficient rate from the latter formula is used. Choose
  $R > 0$ larger than this upper limit. For some $k_0 \ge 1$,
  $(k!)^{3-\chi}B_k(1) \le e^{Rk}$ for all $k \ge k_0$. Each of the remaining
  coefficients is finite by the fixed-order $L^2$ property. Increase $e^R$, if
  necessary, to a finite positive $C_{\rm st}$ that also dominates
  $\left((k!)^{3-\chi}B_k(1)\right)^{1/k}$ for $1 \le k < k_0$. Homogeneity
  gives~\eqref{E:static-holder-input}. The source results are imported inputs;
  this finite-order enlargement is the only deduction about their constant
  needed here. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:static-holder-input}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Interpolation bound for Riesz covariance]
  \label{P:static-holder-upper}
  Suppose that $d \in \{2,3\}$, $c > 0$, $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$, and $1 < \alpha <
  2-\alpha_0$. Let the spatial covariance be $\gamma_\alpha(x) =
  c|x|^{-\alpha}$, and let $A_k(t)$ denote the full total-order-$2k$ coefficient
  of the second moment, as in Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}. Choose and fix
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-holder-parameters}
    \alpha_0<\vartheta<2-\alpha,\qquad
    b_0=\frac{\alpha_0}{\vartheta},\qquad
    \chi=\frac{\alpha-\vartheta}{1-\vartheta}.
  \end{equation}
  Let $V_1$ be the spectral mass of the unit ball for $\gamma_1$, in the
  convention of Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution}, and define
  \[
    Z_{b_0}=\frac2{(1-b_0)(2-b_0)},\qquad
    C_{\rm low}=2Z_{b_0}\pi V_1,\qquad
    C=C_{\rm low}^{\,\vartheta}C_{\rm st}^{\,1-\vartheta},
    \qquad q=3-\alpha,
  \]
  where $C_{\rm st}$ is supplied by Lemma~\ref{L:static-holder-input} for the
  displayed $\chi$. Then, for every $k \ge 1$, $t > 0$, and $x \in \R^d$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-holder-upper}
    A_k(t)\le\frac{C^kt^{(4-\alpha-\alpha_0)k}}{(k!)^{3-\alpha}},
    \qquad
    \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp\left(qC^{1/q}t^\kappa\right).
  \end{equation}
  The finite constants may depend on $\alpha,\alpha_0,c,d$ and the fixed choice
  of $\vartheta$, but not on $k,t,x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We interpolate the regularized covariance factors on one common positive
  measure containing all pairings and splits. We bound the low endpoint
  directly, pass fixed-order limits for the target and static endpoint, and
  only then use the static coefficient estimate.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We verify the endpoint parameters.
  The interval for $\vartheta$ is nonempty by $\alpha+\alpha_0 < 2$. Since
  $\alpha > 1$, we have $0 < \vartheta < 1$, $0 < b_0 < 1$, and
  \[
    \chi-1=\frac{\alpha-1}{1-\vartheta}>0,\qquad
    2-\chi=\frac{2-\alpha-\vartheta}{1-\vartheta}>0.
  \]
  Thus, both endpoint covariances are admissible in dimensions two and three.
  Moreover,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-holder-affine}
    \alpha=\vartheta+(1-\vartheta)\chi,
    \qquad \alpha_0=\vartheta b_0.
  \end{equation}


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We place all three covariance profiles on the same
  measure.
  Fix $k \ge 1$ and $t > 0$. Use a common spatial noise width $\epsilon > 0$ and
  the target's temporal cutoff width $\delta > 0$. The covariance on each
  pairing edge has spatial width $2\epsilon$. In dimension three first use
  $G_{h,\eta} = p_\eta*G_h$ with $\eta > 0$; in dimension two take $\eta = 0$.
  For each split $m+n = 2k$ and pairing $D \in \Pi_k$, use the two separate
  roots, increments $h_l = s_l-s_{p(l)}$, and positions $x_l$ of
  Section~\ref{S:d1-upper}. Define a common positive measure $\nu_{k,t,\eta}$ by
  \[
    \int\Phi\ud\nu_{k,t,\eta}
    :=\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}
    \int_{[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^{2k-m}_<}\ud s
    \int\Phi(m,D,s,y)\prod_{l=1}^{2k}G_{h_l,\eta}(\ud y_l).
  \]
  Here $y_l$ are vector increments and each $x_l$ is their cumulative sum within
  its own chain. Empty blocks have empty products and their own root
  conventions. This measure is finite: the spatial mass at fixed times is
  $\prod_lh_l$, the time domains are bounded, and both discrete sums are finite.
  It is the same measure for all three covariance profiles.

  For $\lambda \in \{1,\alpha,\chi\}$ and $b > 0$, put
  \[
    H_\lambda=\prod_{(i,j)\in D}
    \gamma_{\lambda,2\epsilon}(x_i-x_j),\qquad
    T_b=\prod_{(i,j)\in D}|s_i-s_j|^{-b}.
  \]
  The pointwise identity $\gamma_\alpha =
  \gamma_1^{\vartheta}\gamma_\chi^{1-\vartheta}$ retains the amplitude $c$,
  since $c^\vartheta c^{1-\vartheta} = c$. H\"older's inequality on the Gaussian
  probability measure gives
  \[
    \gamma_{\alpha,2\epsilon}(z)
    \le\gamma_{1,2\epsilon}(z)^\vartheta
    \gamma_{\chi,2\epsilon}(z)^{1-\vartheta},
    \qquad z\in\R^d.
  \]
  These convolutions are finite because the degrees are below $d$. Let
  $Q_\delta$ denote the product of the target's truncated temporal covariances.
  From the regularization in Chen~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{(2.9),
  (2.11)},
  \[
    0\le Q_\delta\le T_{\alpha_0}=T_{b_0}^{\,\vartheta}.
  \]
  This uses only domination by the original temporal power; no interpolation
  property of distinct temporal cutoffs is required.

  Let $A_k^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta}(t) = \int Q_\delta H_\alpha \ud\nu_{k,t,\eta}$
  be the target regularized coefficient. The Gaussian moment formula and the
  separate chain reversals in Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} identify it with the
  finite sum of regularized mixed moments. Also set
  \[
    L_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)=\int T_{b_0}H_1\ud\nu_{k,t,\eta},
    \qquad
    B_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)=\int H_\chi\ud\nu_{k,t,\eta}.
  \]
  The second expression is the regularized static coefficient, by the Gaussian
  moment formula with all pairings and splits. The first is a deterministic
  integral with original temporal covariance. We do not identify it with a
  stochastic integral having mixed regularizations. H\"older's inequality on the
  full measure, including its two discrete variables, yields
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-holder-regularized}
    A_k^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta}(t)
    \le\left(L_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)\right)^\vartheta
    \left(B_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)\right)^{1-\vartheta}.
  \end{equation}


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We bound the low endpoint before passing limits.
  The deterministic estimates in the proof of
  Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}, at spatial degree one and temporal degree
  $b_0$, apply to $L_k^{\epsilon,\eta}$. In detail,
  Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution} gives the closing bound $\pi V_1/2$,
  uniformly in both smoothing widths. Descending integration and the opening
  arithmetic--geometric mean give the common spatial bound $(\pi V_1)^kt^k/k^k$.
  The temporal pairing sum is symmetric on the full time cube. Its integral,
  after summing every split as in~\eqref{E:d1-time-symmetry}, is
  $2^k(Z_{b_0}t^{2-b_0})^k/k!$. Hence,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:static-holder-low}
    L_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)
    \le\frac{C_{\rm low}^{\,k}t^{(3-b_0)k}}{k!\,k^k}
    \le\frac{C_{\rm low}^{\,k}t^{(3-b_0)k}}{(k!)^2}.
  \end{equation}
  The original temporal integral is finite since $b_0 < 1$. The static
  regularized coefficient is finite because its covariance is bounded at fixed
  $\epsilon$. Thus, the H\"older step has finite right side.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We remove Green and noise regularization in order.
  We pass to fixed-order limits before using the static coefficient bound. In
  dimension three fix $\epsilon,\delta$ and let $\eta\downarrow0$. The target
  and static smoothed covariance factors are bounded continuous functions of the
  spatial positions. Products of the finite Green measures converge weakly with
  masses $\prod_lh_l$. A constant times this product dominates each time
  integrand at fixed noise smoothing, so dominated convergence applies on the
  bounded time simplexes. The same argument with two Green widths gives a common
  mixed covariance limit, and hence fixed-order $L^2$ convergence. This
  identifies the static limit with the measure convention of
  Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{(2.22), pp.~2152--2153}. The target
  uses the corresponding inner limit in
  Chen~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{pp.~13--14}. We need no limit
  identification for $L_k^{\epsilon,\eta}$: its uniform upper bound
  in~\eqref{E:static-holder-low} suffices.

  Next let the noise-smoothing parameters tend to zero. From
  Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{Theorem~3.8(i), p.~2165}, each fixed
  static order converges in $L^2$ to $U_n$. For the target, the fixed-order
  $L^2$ convergence follows from Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}. If $Y,Y',Z,Z'$
  are the corresponding approximations and limits, then
  \[
    \|YZ-Y'Z'\|_1
    \le\|Y-Y'\|_2\|Z\|_2+\|Y'\|_2\|Z-Z'\|_2.
  \]
  Therefore, every fixed split product converges in $L^1$. The finite sums over
  the $2k+1$ splits converge as well; a zero-order factor stays equal to one.
  This argument applies directly in dimension two, where no Green smoothing is
  needed. Passing to these limits in~\eqref{E:static-holder-regularized},
  using~\eqref{E:static-holder-low}, gives
  \[
    A_k(t)\le
    \left(\frac{C_{\rm low}^{\,k}t^{(3-b_0)k}}{(k!)^2}\right)^\vartheta
    B_k(t)^{1-\vartheta}.
  \]
  Only now apply Lemma~\ref{L:static-holder-input}. No monotonicity in
  $\epsilon$ and no uniform static large-time bound in that parameter have been
  assumed.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 5.~}We combine the endpoint powers and sum.
  The powers combine without a counting loss. Indeed,
  by~\eqref{E:static-holder-affine},
  \[
    \begin{split}
      2\vartheta+(1-\vartheta)(3-\chi)&=3-\alpha,\\
      \vartheta(3-b_0)+(1-\vartheta)(4-\chi)
      &=4-\alpha-\alpha_0.
    \end{split}
  \]
  This proves the coefficient bound in~\eqref{E:static-holder-upper}. The
  constant term is $A_0 = 1$. Since $q = 3-\alpha > 1$, the scalar inequality
  $\sum_{k\ge0}z^k/(k!)^q \le e^{qz^{1/q}}$ proved in
  Proposition~\ref{P:d1-upper} applies with $z = Ct^{4-\alpha-\alpha_0}$.
  Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} identifies the nonnegative full coefficient series
  with $\E u^2(t,x)$. Here $(4-\alpha-\alpha_0)/q = \kappa$. This proves
  Proposition~\ref{P:static-holder-upper}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Calibrations and remaining scope]\label{R:static-holder-scope}
  The time exponent in~\eqref{E:static-holder-upper} tends to
  $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$ as $\alpha_0\downarrow0$, in agreement with the
  Chen--Hu static exponent. This does not interchange the static and large-time
  limits or assert uniformity of the constants. Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\E u(t,x)
  \le \exp(qC^{1/q}t^\kappa/2)$. If the first-moment lower rate is at least
  $L_*$, this upper bound implies $2L_* \le qC^{1/q}$. Thus, the first-moment
  and Jensen calibrations are retained, without identifying a sharp constant.
  The Skorohod comparison remains only the comparison recorded in
  Remark~\ref{R:higher-calibrations}; its exponent agrees, but its solution
  concept and covariance normalization differ. No Skorohod estimate is used in
  this proof. The argument covers isotropic Riesz covariance throughout the
  stated higher-degree range in dimensions two and three, including the
  previously uncovered two-dimensional interval $3/2 \le \alpha < 2-\alpha_0$.
  This argument does not cover arbitrary homogeneous covariances: anisotropic
  profiles need not factor into the admissible endpoints. The intrinsic
  second-moment formula is given separately in
  Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}. No novelty is asserted here.
\end{remark}

\section{General homogeneous covariance in dimension two}
\label{S:planar-holder}

This section gives planar endpoint constructions and a transfer under its stated
hypotheses. It does not use the preceding Riesz interpolation argument as an
input. The full upper and intrinsic formula use separate methods.

Throughout this section $d = 2$. Write $\Gamma$ for the spatial covariance as a
nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon measure. For a locally
integrable covariance function, this means $\Gamma(\ud x) = \gamma(x)\ud x$. The
measure interpretation uses the Gaussian approximation framework of
Chen~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{pp.~2--3, 13} and
Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{pp.~2145--2147, 2151--2153}. All products
of covariance factors below are taken after Gaussian smoothing; no product or
fractional power of distributions is used. Homogeneity means
\begin{equation}\label{E:planar-measure-scaling}
  \Gamma(rA)=r^{2-\alpha}\Gamma(A),\qquad r>0,
\end{equation}
for Borel sets $A$. This is equivalent to the covariance homogeneity in
Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog}. If $\Gamma = 0$, the noise vanishes and $u = 1$. We
first treat $\Gamma \ne 0$ and $1 < \alpha < 2$.

\begin{lemma}[Planar endpoint covariances]\label{L:planar-endpoints}
  Suppose that $\Gamma$ satisfies the preceding assumptions, and let $\alpha <
  \chi < 2$. Define the finite angular measure
  \[
    \sigma(E)=(2-\alpha)\Gamma
    \{x:0<|x|\le1,\ x/|x|\in E\},\qquad
    S=\sigma(\mathbb{S}^1).
  \]
  On $\R^2\setminus\{0\}$ define the weighted measures
  \begin{equation}\label{E:planar-endpoints}
    \Gamma_{\rm low}(\ud x)=|x|^{\alpha-1}\Gamma(\ud x),\qquad
    \Gamma_{\rm st}(\ud x)=|x|^{\alpha-\chi}\Gamma(\ud x),
  \end{equation}
  with zero mass at the origin. Both are nonnegative, even, positive-definite
  tempered Radon measures. Their measure scaling exponents are $1$ and $2-\chi$,
  respectively. For every $h > 0$, $\epsilon > 0$, $\eta \ge 0$, and $z \in
  \R^2$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:planar-line-convolution}
    \int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)(p_\epsilon*\Gamma_{\rm low})(y+z)
    \le\frac S4.
  \end{equation}
  If $\vartheta = (\chi-\alpha)/(\chi-1)$, then $0 < \vartheta < 1$ and
  \begin{equation}\label{E:planar-gaussian-holder}
    (p_\epsilon*\Gamma)(z)
    \le(p_\epsilon*\Gamma_{\rm low})(z)^\vartheta
    (p_\epsilon*\Gamma_{\rm st})(z)^{1-\vartheta}.
  \end{equation}
  No angular density or isotropy is assumed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We identify the physical polar measure and its two weighted endpoints. We
  prove their positive definiteness separately, estimate the low endpoint
  through line integrals, and apply Hölder on the original heat-weighted
  measure.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We obtain the polar measure and endpoint
  integrability.
  Since $2-\alpha > 0$, scaling and local finiteness force $\Gamma(\{0\}) = 0$.
  The unit-ball mass is finite, so $S < \infty$. It is positive: otherwise
  scaling would make every ball have zero mass, contradicting $\Gamma \ne 0$.
  Evenness of $\Gamma$ implies evenness of $\sigma$. For $0 < a < b$ and Borel
  $E \subset \mathbb{S}^1$,
  \[
    \Gamma\{x:a<|x|\le b,\ x/|x|\in E\}
    =\frac{b^{2-\alpha}-a^{2-\alpha}}{2-\alpha}\sigma(E)
    =\int_a^b r^{1-\alpha}\ud r\,\sigma(E).
  \]
  These polar rectangles form a generating family. Uniqueness of measures, first
  on finite annuli and then on their increasing union, identifies the polar
  pushforward with $r^{1-\alpha}\ud r\,\sigma(\ud\omega)$. Hence, for every
  nonnegative Borel function $f$, Tonelli gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:planar-polar}
    \int f(x)\Gamma(\ud x)
    =\int_{\mathbb{S}^1}\int_0^\infty
    f(r\omega)r^{1-\alpha}\ud r\,\sigma(\ud\omega).
  \end{equation}
  Extended values are permitted. In particular, the formula applies to the
  nonnegative wave density used below.

  The endpoint polar measures are therefore $\ud r\,\sigma(\ud\omega)$ and
  $r^{1-\chi}\ud r\,\sigma(\ud\omega)$. Their radial integrals are finite near
  zero because $\chi < 2$. Rapid decay of a Schwartz function controls their
  integrals at infinity, uniformly in direction. They are thus locally finite
  and tempered, and their stated scaling and evenness follow directly. If
  $\Gamma$ has a density, the two weighted measures also have locally integrable
  densities.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We prove positive definiteness of the low
  endpoint.
  To prove positive definiteness of the low endpoint, evenness gives the
  full-sphere line representation
  \begin{equation}\label{E:planar-line-mixture}
    \int f(x)\Gamma_{\rm low}(\ud x)
    =\frac12\int_{\mathbb{S}^1}\int_\R f(r\omega)\ud r
    \,\sigma(\ud\omega).
  \end{equation}
  It holds for nonnegative Borel $f$, and for Schwartz $f$ by absolute
  integrability. For a complex Schwartz function $\varphi$, put
  $\widetilde{\varphi}(x) = \overline{\varphi(-x)}$ and $\psi =
  \varphi*\widetilde{\varphi}$. Fubini in line and perpendicular coordinates
  gives
  \[
    \int_\R\psi(r\omega)\ud r
    =\int_{\omega^\perp}
    \left|\int_\R\varphi(s\omega+v)\ud s\right|^2\ud v\ge0.
  \]
  Schwartz decay justifies all these integrals, uniformly for unit $\omega$.
  Integrating against the finite measure $\sigma$
  in~\eqref{E:planar-line-mixture} proves $\Gamma_{\rm low}(\psi) \ge 0$. The
  factor $1/2$ is essential because both orientations of each line occur on the
  full sphere.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We prove positive definiteness of the static
  endpoint.
  For the static endpoint put $\lambda = \chi-\alpha > 0$. For $x \ne 0$,
  \[
    |x|^{-\lambda}=\frac1{\Gamma(\lambda/2)}
    \int_0^\infty s^{\lambda/2-1}e^{-s|x|^2}\ud s.
  \]
  For each $s > 0$, the measure $e^{-s|x|^2}\Gamma(\ud x)$ is positive definite.
  Indeed, the Fourier transform of $e^{-s|x|^2}\psi(x)$ is a positive Gaussian
  convolution of $|\widehat{\varphi}|^2$. Its pairing with the original positive
  spectral measure is nonnegative; Schwartz decay makes the pairing finite.
  Moreover,
  \[
    \int |\psi(x)||x|^{-\lambda}\Gamma(\ud x)
    =\int |\psi(x)|\Gamma_{\rm st}(\ud x)<\infty
  \]
  by the endpoint polar integrability just proved. Tonelli with $|\psi|$,
  followed by Fubini, justifies the Gaussian mixture against $\psi$. Thus,
  $\Gamma_{\rm st}(\psi) \ge 0$. This uses smooth Gaussian multiplication of the
  original covariance and a convergent mixture, not a fractional power of that
  covariance.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We bound the planar wave convolution.
  We next prove the convolution estimate directly. Use the planar wave density
  \[
    G_h(y)=\frac{\mathbf1_{\{|y|<h\}}}{2\pi\sqrt{h^2-|y|^2}},
  \]
  with value zero on the circle. Write $z = a\omega+b\omega^\perp$ for a unit
  perpendicular vector. If $|b| < h$, put $A = \sqrt{h^2-b^2}$. Translation
  along the line gives
  \[
    \int_\R G_h(z-r\omega)\ud r
    =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-A}^A\frac{\ud s}{\sqrt{A^2-s^2}}
    =\frac12.
  \]
  If $|b| \ge h$, the open support is empty or tangent and the line integral is
  zero. The tangency point has zero line Lebesgue measure.
  Formula~\eqref{E:planar-line-mixture} and nonnegative Tonelli yield
  \[
    (G_h*\Gamma_{\rm low})(z)
    =\frac14\int_{\mathbb{S}^1}
    \mathbf1_{\{|z\cdot\omega^\perp|<h\}}\sigma(\ud\omega)
    \le\frac S4.
  \]
  Evenness, Gaussian convolution, and Tonelli now give
  \[
    \int G_{h,\eta}(\ud y)(p_\epsilon*\Gamma_{\rm low})(y+z)
    =\left[p_{\epsilon+\eta}*(G_h*\Gamma_{\rm low})\right](z)
    \le\frac S4.
  \]
  For $\eta = 0$, use the unsmoothed Green measure. The Gaussian average has
  mass one, so the same constant works for all widths.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 5.~}We compare the regularized endpoints by Hölder.
  Finally, the measure $p_\epsilon(z-x)\Gamma(\ud x)$ is finite. Gaussian decay
  and polynomial radial growth prove this directly; the endpoint convolutions
  are finite for the same reason. The identity
  \[
    \vartheta(\alpha-1)+(1-\vartheta)(\alpha-\chi)=0
  \]
  and absence of an origin atom permit H\"older's inequality on this
  unnormalized positive measure:
  \[
    \begin{split}
      (p_\epsilon*\Gamma)(z)
      &=\int |x|^{\vartheta(\alpha-1)}
      |x|^{(1-\vartheta)(\alpha-\chi)}
      p_\epsilon(z-x)\Gamma(\ud x)\\
      &\le(p_\epsilon*\Gamma_{\rm low})(z)^\vartheta
      (p_\epsilon*\Gamma_{\rm st})(z)^{1-\vartheta}.
    \end{split}
  \]
  This proves Lemma~\ref{L:planar-endpoints}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[General planar static coefficient input]
  \label{L:planar-static-input}
  Let $\Xi$ be a nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon measure on
  $\R^2$ with $\Xi(rA) = r^{2-\chi}\Xi(A)$, where $1 < \chi < 2$. For static
  Stratonovich noise with covariance $\Xi$, data $(1,0)$, and wave operator
  $\Delta$, let $U_n(t,x)$ be its ordered integrals, with $U_0 = 1$. There is a
  finite $C_{\rm st} = C_{\rm st}(\Xi) > 0$ such that, for $k \ge 1$ and $t >
  0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:planar-static-input}
    0\le B_k(t):=\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\E[U_m(t,x)U_{2k-m}(t,x)]
    \le\frac{C_{\rm st}^{\,k}t^{(4-\chi)k}}{(k!)^{3-\chi}}.
  \end{equation}
  Also $B_0 = 1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We treat zero covariance directly. For nonzero covariance, homogeneity
  verifies the static spectral condition, and the cited finite coefficient
  rate is converted to a bound valid at every order.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We verify the static covariance hypotheses.
  If $\Xi = 0$, then $U_n = 0$ for $n \ge 1$, and hence $B_k = 0$ for $k \ge 1$.
  The choices $C_{\rm st} = 1$ and $B_0 = 1$ give the conclusion. We may
  therefore assume $\Xi \ne 0$.

  Fourier uniqueness transfers covariance homogeneity to its positive spectral
  measure $\mu_\Xi$: $\mu_\Xi(rA) = r^\chi\mu_\Xi(A)$. Its unit-ball mass is
  finite because the spectral measure is Radon. On dyadic annuli this gives
  \[
    \int_{\R^2}(1+|\xi|^2)^{-1}\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi)
    \le\mu_\Xi(B_1)
    +2^\chi\mu_\Xi(B_1)\sum_{j\ge0}2^{j(\chi-2)}<\infty.
  \]
  This verifies the static Dalang condition without an angular density. The
  present $\Xi$ also satisfies the source's covariance assumptions: it is a
  nonnegative positive-definite tempered distribution, and $1 < \chi < 2 = d$.
  The source expressly includes generalized covariances and uses their
  heat-regularized interpretation \cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{p.~2152,
  (2.18)}. Thus, the coefficient estimate invoked below is a direct application
  within the source's stated class, not an extension from density kernels. From
  Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{Theorem~3.8(i), p.~2165}, every fixed
  order exists in $L^2$. The product and Wick formulas (2.17) and (2.19),
  pp.~2151--2152, identify $B_k$ with all pairings and all splits of the total
  order, including self-pairs and empty chains. Thus, every coefficient is
  finite and nonnegative.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We state the imported finiteness input used by the
  coefficient rate.
  We make explicit the finiteness input in the source's coefficient upper rate.
  The variational constant $\mathcal{M}$ in
  Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{p.~2147} is finite under the displayed
  Dalang condition. The source's invocation on p.~2175 identifies
  $2^{\chi/(4-\chi)}\mathcal{M}$ with the square-root variational quantity
  covered by Chen~\cite{chen:23:exponential}*{Theorem~1.1, p.~2}. That theorem
  explicitly states finiteness under Dalang's condition and permits generalized
  nonnegative positive-definite covariances. We import that finiteness, not a
  sharp value or its proof.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We turn the rate into an all-order constant.
  From Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{(5.1), p.~2173, and (5.15),
  p.~2180}, with $p = 2$, it follows that
  \[
    B_k(t)=t^{(4-\chi)k}B_k(1),\qquad
    \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac1k
    \log\left((k!)^{3-\chi}B_k(1)\right)<\infty.
  \]
  Choose a finite $R > 0$ larger than this upper limit. For all sufficiently
  large $k$, $(k!)^{3-\chi}B_k(1) \le e^{Rk}$. Increase $e^R$ to a finite
  positive constant dominating the $k$th roots of the finitely many remaining
  quantities. The resulting $C_{\rm st}$ proves~\eqref{E:planar-static-input} by
  homogeneity. These are the source's general homogeneous-covariance statements,
  not a Riesz specialization. This proves Lemma~\ref{L:planar-static-input}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Planar homogeneous-covariance upper bound]
  \label{P:planar-upper}
  Let $d = 2$, $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$, and $1 < \alpha < 2-\alpha_0$. Suppose that
  the nonzero spatial covariance $\Gamma$ is nonnegative, even, positive
  definite and tempered, with~\eqref{E:planar-measure-scaling}. Choose and fix
  \[
    \alpha_0<\vartheta<2-\alpha,\qquad
    b_0=\alpha_0/\vartheta,\qquad
    \chi=(\alpha-\vartheta)/(1-\vartheta).
  \]
  Use the endpoint measures and physical angular mass $S$ of
  Lemma~\ref{L:planar-endpoints}. Put
  \[
    Z_{b_0}=\frac2{(1-b_0)(2-b_0)},\qquad
    C_{\rm low}=Z_{b_0}S,\qquad
    C=C_{\rm low}^{\,\vartheta}C_{\rm st}^{\,1-\vartheta},
    \qquad q=3-\alpha,
  \]
  where $C_{\rm st}$ is from Lemma~\ref{L:planar-static-input} for $\Xi =
  \Gamma_{\rm st}$. For the Stratonovich solution with data $(1,0)$, every $k
  \ge 1$, $t > 0$, and $x \in \R^2$ satisfy
  \begin{equation}\label{E:planar-upper}
    A_k(t)\le\frac{C^kt^{(4-\alpha-\alpha_0)k}}{(k!)^{3-\alpha}},
    \qquad
    \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp\left(qC^{1/q}t^\kappa\right).
  \end{equation}
  Constants may depend on the covariance, $\alpha_0$, and the fixed choice of
  $\vartheta$, but not on $k,t,x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We verify the planar endpoint parameters and use a common positive measure
  for all diagrams. We interpolate the regularized coefficients, bound the low
  endpoint by its line-convolution estimate, and then pass to the fixed-order
  limits before summing.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We verify the original and endpoint spectral
  conditions.
  The parameter interval is nonempty. We have $0 < \vartheta < 1$, $0 < b_0 <
  1$, and
  \[
    \chi-\alpha=\frac{\vartheta(\alpha-1)}{1-\vartheta}>0,
    \qquad
    2-\chi=\frac{2-\alpha-\vartheta}{1-\vartheta}>0.
  \]
  Thus, Lemma~\ref{L:planar-endpoints} applies, with its interpolation weight
  equal to $\vartheta$. The original spectral measure has ball growth of
  exponent $\alpha$, by the same Fourier homogeneity argument used in
  Lemma~\ref{L:planar-static-input}. A dyadic sum gives the time-dependent
  spectral condition (1.5) of
  Chen~\cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{p.~3}, since $\alpha <
  2-\alpha_0$. The low endpoint has spectral ball exponent one and $b_0 < 1$;
  the static endpoint has exponent $\chi < 2$. Thus,
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order} applies to the target, and the imported static
  fixed-order theorem is used within its stated range.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We form the common measure and apply Hölder.
  Fix $k \ge 1$, $t > 0$, and common noise widths $\epsilon,\delta > 0$. The
  spatial covariance on each edge has width $2\epsilon$. No Green smoothing is
  needed in dimension two. On the disjoint union over $m = 0,\ldots,2k$ and $D
  \in \Pi_k$, define the finite positive measure $\nu_{k,t}$ by
  \[
    \int\Phi\ud\nu_{k,t}
    :=\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}
    \int_{[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^{2k-m}_<}\ud s
    \int\Phi(m,D,s,y)\prod_{l=1}^{2k}G_{h_l}(\ud y_l).
  \]
  Here each chain has its own root, $h_l = s_l-s_{p(l)}$, the $y_l$ are its
  increments, and $x_l$ is its cumulative position. A zero-length chain has
  empty products. At fixed times the spatial mass is $\prod_lh_l$; bounded time
  simplexes and finite discrete sums therefore make $\nu_{k,t}$ finite.

  For $J \in \{\Gamma,\Gamma_{\rm low},\Gamma_{\rm st}\}$ write
  \[
    H_J=\prod_{(i,j)\in D}(p_{2\epsilon}*J)(x_i-x_j),\qquad
    T_b=\prod_{(i,j)\in D}|s_i-s_j|^{-b}.
  \]
  The Gaussian covariance functions are bounded and continuous at each fixed
  width: their positive spectral measures are tempered, so Gaussian weighting
  makes them finite. Let $Q_\delta$ be the product of the target's truncated
  temporal covariances. The source regularization gives $0 \le Q_\delta \le
  T_{\alpha_0} = T_{b_0}^{\,\vartheta}$. Lemma~\ref{L:planar-endpoints}, at the
  common edge width, gives $H_\Gamma \le H_{\Gamma_{\rm low}}^\vartheta
  H_{\Gamma_{\rm st}}^{1-\vartheta}$.

  The Gaussian moment formula and the two separate chain reversals in
  Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} identify the target regularized coefficient with
  $\int Q_\delta H_\Gamma\ud\nu_{k,t}$. Define
  \[
    L_k^\epsilon(t)=\int T_{b_0}H_{\Gamma_{\rm low}}\ud\nu_{k,t},
    \qquad
    B_k^\epsilon(t)=\int H_{\Gamma_{\rm st}}\ud\nu_{k,t}.
  \]
  The latter is the regularized static coefficient, including every split and
  pairing. The former is only a deterministic integral with original temporal
  power. H\"older's inequality on the full common measure, including its
  discrete variables, yields
  \begin{equation}\label{E:planar-coefficient-holder}
    A_k^{\epsilon,\delta}(t)
    \le\left(L_k^\epsilon(t)\right)^\vartheta
    \left(B_k^\epsilon(t)\right)^{1-\vartheta}.
  \end{equation}
  No interpolation of different temporal cutoff kernels is asserted.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We estimate the low endpoint with the full
  temporal pairing sum.
  We bound $L_k^\epsilon$ directly. Orient each pair by increasing block label.
  Descending integration of increments, as in Lemma~\ref{L:d1-spatial}, removes
  at a closing label its only remaining covariance factor.
  Lemma~\ref{L:planar-endpoints} bounds that integral by $S/4$. At an opening
  label its paired covariance has already been removed, so the Green integral is
  its mass $h_l$. The $k$ opening increments sum to at most $2t$. Thus, the
  common spatial bound is
  \[
    (S/4)^k\prod_{i\in O_D}h_i
    \le(S/2)^k\frac{t^k}{k^k}.
  \]
  The temporal pairing sum is invariant under every permutation of its $2k$
  labels. Each pairing integrates on the full time cube to
  $(Z_{b_0}t^{2-b_0})^k$. Partitioning by the two within-block orders, and then
  summing all splits, gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      \sum_{m=0}^{2k}\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}
      \int_{[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^{2k-m}_<}T_{b_0}\ud s
      &=\frac{(2k)!}{2^kk!}(Z_{b_0}t^{2-b_0})^k
      \sum_{m=0}^{2k}\frac1{m!(2k-m)!}\\
      &=\frac{2^k}{k!}(Z_{b_0}t^{2-b_0})^k.
    \end{split}
  \]
  Symmetry is used for the sum, not for an individual pairing. The integral is
  finite because $b_0 < 1$. Multiplication gives
  \begin{equation}\label{E:planar-low-coefficient}
    L_k^\epsilon(t)
    \le\frac{(Z_{b_0}S)^kt^{(3-b_0)k}}{k!\,k^k}
    \le\frac{C_{\rm low}^{\,k}t^{(3-b_0)k}}{(k!)^2}.
  \end{equation}
  At fixed $\epsilon$, bounded covariance and the finite common measure make
  $B_k^\epsilon$ finite as well. These observations also justify finiteness in
  the H\"older estimate.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We remove noise smoothing at fixed order.
  We next take fixed-order limits. The target orders converge in $L^2$ by
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}. For the static orders the corresponding input
  is Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{Theorem~3.8(i), p.~2165}. For
  products of two fixed orders, the inequality
  \[
    \|YZ-Y'Z'\|_1
    \le\|Y-Y'\|_2\|Z\|_2+\|Y'\|_2\|Z-Z'\|_2
  \]
  gives $L^1$ convergence. There are only $2k+1$ split products, and a
  zero-order factor stays equal to one. Hence, $A_k^{\epsilon,\delta}(t) \to
  A_k(t)$ and $B_k^\epsilon(t) \to B_k(t)$ as the noise widths vanish. The
  uniform deterministic estimate~\eqref{E:planar-low-coefficient} needs no
  stochastic limit interpretation. Passing to limits
  in~\eqref{E:planar-coefficient-holder} gives
  \[
    A_k(t)\le
    \left(\frac{C_{\rm low}^{\,k}t^{(3-b_0)k}}{(k!)^2}\right)^\vartheta
    B_k(t)^{1-\vartheta}.
  \]
  Only now apply Lemma~\ref{L:planar-static-input}. No monotonicity in the
  spatial width or uniform static smoothed asymptotic was used.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 5.~}We combine powers and sum the full moment series.
  The parameter identities $\alpha = \vartheta+(1-\vartheta)\chi$ and $\alpha_0
  = \vartheta b_0$ imply
  \[
    2\vartheta+(1-\vartheta)(3-\chi)=3-\alpha,\qquad
    \vartheta(3-b_0)+(1-\vartheta)(4-\chi)=4-\alpha-\alpha_0.
  \]
  This yields the coefficient bound in~\eqref{E:planar-upper} with $C = C_{\rm
  low}^\vartheta C_{\rm st}^{1-\vartheta}$. The constant coefficient is $A_0 =
  1$. For $q = 3-\alpha > 1$ and $z \ge 0$,
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^k}{(k!)^q}
    =\sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    \le\left(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    =e^{qz^{1/q}}.
  \]
  Proposition~\ref{P:mixed} supplies the nonnegative full series for the target
  solution. Take $z = Ct^{4-\alpha-\alpha_0}$ and use $(4-\alpha-\alpha_0)/q =
  \kappa$. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:planar-upper}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Full planar parameter range]\label{C:planar-range}
  In dimension two, under Hypothesis~\ref{H:homog} and the standing covariance
  positivity assumptions, the upper bound $\E u^2(t,x) \le \exp(C_{\rm dir}t^\kappa)$
  holds throughout $0 < \alpha < 2-\alpha_0$, with a finite constant $C_{\rm dir}$
  depending on $\alpha_0$ and the spatial covariance. A nonnegative generalized
  covariance is interpreted as the homogeneous measure and its Gaussian
  approximations described above.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  For $0 < \alpha \le 1$, apply Proposition~\ref{P:low-alpha-upper}. For $1 <
  \alpha < 2-\alpha_0$, apply Proposition~\ref{P:planar-upper}. If the
  covariance vanishes, $u = 1$ and $C_{\rm dir} = 0$ suffices. This proves
  Corollary~\ref{C:planar-range}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Planar calibrations and remaining goals]\label{R:planar-scope}
  The exponent tends to $(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)$ as $\alpha_0\downarrow0$,
  consistently with the static source, without an interchange of limits or
  uniformity claim for the constants. For the nonzero higher-exponent case,
  Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\E u(t,x) \le \exp(qC^{1/q}t^\kappa/2)$. If the
  first-moment lower rate is at least $L_*$, this implies $2L_* \le qC^{1/q}$.
  No sharp constant is identified. The existing comparisons with Skorohod
  exponents, in their stated Riesz subclasses, concern different solution
  concepts and are not inputs here. The planar endpoint construction is
  dimension-specific; no general higher-exponent result in dimension three
  follows from it. Section~\ref{S:interval-upper} treats the full
  homogeneous-covariance class by a separate argument. The intrinsic constant is
  identified separately in Theorem~\ref{T:intrinsic-second-moment}.
\end{remark}

\section{Three-dimensional bounds from covariance endpoints}
\label{S:three-endpoints}

The endpoint constructions and conditional moment transfer are retained as an
alternative route. No general three-dimensional factorization is asserted. The
full upper and intrinsic formula do not use these factorizations.

We first construct the product endpoints. Zero coordinate exponents below mean
constant factors, not one-dimensional white noise.

\LeanMarker{L:product-endpoints}
\begin{lemma}[Strict product endpoints]\label{L:product-endpoints}
  Let $0 \le a_j < 1$ for $j = 1,2,3$, let $\alpha = a_1+a_2+a_3 \in (1,2)$, and
  choose $0 < \vartheta < 2-\alpha$. Put $\chi =
  (\alpha-\vartheta)/(1-\vartheta)$ and define
  \begin{equation}\label{E:product-endpoint-vectors}
    \begin{split}
      L_j&=\max\{0,(a_j-1+\vartheta)/\vartheta\},\qquad
      U_j=\min\{1,a_j/\vartheta\},\\
      \Lambda&=\frac{1-\sum_jL_j}{\sum_jU_j-\sum_jL_j},\qquad
      b_j=L_j+\Lambda(U_j-L_j),\qquad
      v_j=\frac{a_j-\vartheta b_j}{1-\vartheta}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation}
  Then $0 < \Lambda < 1$, $\sum_j b_j = 1$, $\sum_jv_j = \chi$, and
  \[
    a_j=\vartheta b_j+(1-\vartheta)v_j,\qquad
    a_j>0\Longrightarrow 0<b_j,v_j<1,\qquad
    a_j=0\Longrightarrow b_j=v_j=0.
  \]
  For any $c > 0$, the ordinary covariance densities
  \[
    \gamma_a(x)=c\prod_{j=1}^3|x_j|^{-a_j},\qquad
    \gamma_b(x)=c\prod_{j=1}^3|x_j|^{-b_j},\qquad
    \gamma_v(x)=c\prod_{j=1}^3|x_j|^{-v_j}
  \]
  are nonnegative, even, positive definite, locally integrable and tempered.
  Their covariance homogeneity exponents are $\alpha,1,\chi$, respectively, with
  $\alpha < \chi < 2$. For every $\epsilon > 0$ and $z \in \R^3$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:product-gaussian-comparison}
    (p_\epsilon*\gamma_a)(z)
    \le(p_\epsilon*\gamma_b)(z)^\vartheta
    (p_\epsilon*\gamma_v)(z)^{1-\vartheta}.
  \end{equation}
  A factor with exponent zero is defined to be one everywhere.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We first show that the coordinate endpoint intervals admit a strict interior
  choice with the required sums. We then verify the covariance properties of
  the resulting product densities and apply Gaussian Hölder.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We choose strict coordinate endpoints and verify
  their sums.
  We have $\vartheta < 1$. There are at least two positive coordinates, since
  every $a_j < 1$ and $\sum_j a_j > 1$. At a zero coordinate, $L_j = U_j = 0$.
  At a positive coordinate, $L_j < U_j$: if $a_j \ge \vartheta$, then $U_j = 1$
  and $L_j < 1$; if $a_j < \vartheta$, then $U_j = a_j/\vartheta > 0$, and
  either $L_j = 0$ or $U_j-L_j = (1-\vartheta)/\vartheta > 0$.

  Let $I = \{j:L_j>0\}$ and $r = |I|$. For $r = 0$ the sum of the lower
  endpoints is zero; for $r = 1$ it is less than one. For $r \ge 2$,
  \[
    \sum_{j\in I}a_j\le\alpha<2-\vartheta
    \le r-(r-1)\vartheta,
  \]
  because the last inequality is equivalent to $(r-2)(1-\vartheta) \ge 0$.
  Therefore,
  \[
    \sum_jL_j
    =\frac{\sum_{j\in I}a_j-r+r\vartheta}{\vartheta}<1.
  \]
  If some $a_j \ge \vartheta$, its upper endpoint equals one and a different
  positive coordinate has positive upper endpoint. Otherwise, $\sum_jU_j =
  \alpha/\vartheta > 1$. Hence, $\sum_jL_j < 1 < \sum_jU_j$, which proves $0 <
  \Lambda < 1$ and $\sum_jb_j = 1$. At every positive coordinate, $L_j < b_j <
  U_j$, so $0 < b_j < 1$. The inequalities $b_j < U_j \le a_j/\vartheta$ and
  $b_j > L_j \ge (a_j-1+\vartheta)/\vartheta$ give $0 < v_j < 1$. At a zero
  coordinate $b_j = v_j = 0$. The reconstruction identity is immediate, and
  summing it gives $\sum_jv_j = \chi$. Finally,
  \[
    \chi-\alpha=\frac{\vartheta(\alpha-1)}{1-\vartheta}>0,
    \qquad 2-\chi=\frac{2-\alpha-\vartheta}{1-\vartheta}>0.
  \]


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We verify local integrability and positive
  definiteness.
  For any of the three exponent vectors $e$, local integrability is a product of
  one-dimensional integrals with exponents below one. For a Schwartz function
  $\psi$, choose $N > 1$ and use $|\psi(x)| \le C_N\prod_j(1+|x_j|)^{-N}$. This
  proves absolute integrability against $\gamma_e$ and also temperedness. For
  each active coordinate, $0 < e_j < 1$, use
  \[
    |u|^{-e_j}=\frac1{\Gamma(e_j/2)}
    \int_0^\infty s^{e_j/2-1}e^{-su^2}\ud s.
  \]
  The product over the active coordinates is a positive mixture of Gaussian
  functions in those coordinates, constant in all other coordinates. Each such
  Gaussian is positive definite. For a complex Schwartz $\varphi$, set $\psi =
  \varphi*\widetilde{\varphi}$, where $\widetilde{\varphi}(x) =
  \overline{\varphi(-x)}$. The absolute integrability just proved permits
  Tonelli with $|\psi|$ and then Fubini for the mixture against $\psi$. Its
  quadratic form is nonnegative. No Euler Gamma factor at exponent zero is
  introduced. Evenness and homogeneity follow from the displayed densities.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We compare the smoothed densities at common
  amplitude.
  The reconstruction of each exponent and the common amplitude imply $\gamma_a =
  \gamma_b^\vartheta\gamma_v^{1-\vartheta}$ almost everywhere; values on
  singular coordinate planes do not change these locally integrable covariances.
  H\"older's inequality on the Gaussian probability measure
  gives~\eqref{E:product-gaussian-comparison}. Its endpoint convolutions are
  finite by the product integrability and Gaussian decay. The amplitude remains
  $c^\vartheta c^{1-\vartheta} = c$. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:product-endpoints}.
\end{proof}

We now take $d = 3$, $0 < \alpha_0 < 1$, and $1 < \alpha < 2-\alpha_0$. Let
$\Gamma$ be a nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered spatial covariance,
with homogeneity exponent $\alpha$. As a positive distribution it is a Radon
measure satisfying $\Gamma(rA) = r^{3-\alpha}\Gamma(A)$; a covariance function
is identified with its density measure. Gaussian convolution is used for all
covariance products. Choose and fix
\begin{equation}\label{E:three-parameters}
  \alpha_0<\vartheta<2-\alpha,\qquad
  b_0=\alpha_0/\vartheta,\qquad
  \chi=(\alpha-\vartheta)/(1-\vartheta).
\end{equation}
Then $0 < b_0 < 1$ and $\alpha < \chi < 2$, by the identities in the preceding
proof. The zero target covariance is trivial, since then $u = 1$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Gaussian covariance comparison]\label{H:three-endpoints}
  There are nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered endpoint covariances
  $\Gamma_{\rm low}$ and $\Gamma_{\rm st}$ on $\R^3$ with measure scaling
  \[
    \Gamma_{\rm low}(rA)=r^2\Gamma_{\rm low}(A),\qquad
    \Gamma_{\rm st}(rA)=r^{3-\chi}\Gamma_{\rm st}(A),
  \]
  such that, for every $\epsilon > 0$ and $z \in \R^3$,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:three-gaussian-comparison}
    (p_\epsilon*\Gamma)(z)
    \le(p_\epsilon*\Gamma_{\rm low})(z)^\vartheta
    (p_\epsilon*\Gamma_{\rm st})(z)^{1-\vartheta}.
  \end{equation}
\end{hypothesis}

This is a condition on deterministic covariance measures and their Gaussian
convolutions. It assumes no moment estimate. We do not assert that every
homogeneous covariance in dimension three admits these endpoints.

\LeanMarker{L:plane-endpoints}
\begin{lemma}[Plane-supported endpoints]\label{L:plane-endpoints}
  Retain~\eqref{E:three-parameters}, let $c > 0$, and let $a_1,a_2 \ge 0$
  satisfy $s = a_1+a_2 = \alpha-1$. In coordinates $(y,z) \in \R^2\times\R$, put
  \[
    m(\ud y,\ud z)=\ud y\,\delta_0(\ud z),\qquad
    v_j=\frac{a_j}{1-\vartheta},\qquad
    \Gamma_{\rm pl}=c\prod_{j=1}^2|y_j|^{-a_j}m.
  \]
  Zero coordinate powers mean one. Then
  \[
    \Gamma_{\rm low}=cm,\qquad
    \Gamma_{\rm st}=c\prod_{j=1}^2|y_j|^{-v_j}m
  \]
  satisfy Hypothesis~\ref{H:three-endpoints} for $\Gamma_{\rm pl}$. In the
  spectral convention of Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution}, this low endpoint
  has $V_{\rm low} = c/\pi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We verify the exponents and covariance properties of the plane-supported
  measures. We then apply Hölder to their ordinary densities on the same plane
  measure and compute the low endpoint spectral mass.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We verify the endpoint parameters and measure
  scaling.
  Since $0 < s < 1-\alpha_0$ and $\vartheta < 2-\alpha = 1-s$,
  \[
    0\le a_j<1,\qquad 0\le v_j<1,\qquad
    v_1+v_2=\frac{s}{1-\vartheta}=\chi-1<1.
  \]
  A measure $f_e(y)\ud y\,\delta_0(\ud z)$, with $f_e(y) = \prod_j|y_j|^{-e_j}$
  and $0 \le e_j < 1$, is locally finite. For a Schwartz test function,
  product-weighted rapid decay proves absolute integrability and temperedness.
  Its scaling exponent as a measure is $2-e_1-e_2$. Thus, the three measures in
  the statement have exponents $3-\alpha$, $2$, and $3-\chi$. They are even and
  nonnegative.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We include the normal Dirac measure in the
  positivity argument.
  We verify positive definiteness, including the normal Dirac measure. Each
  positive coordinate power has the Gaussian mixture
  \[
    |u|^{-e}=\frac1{\Gamma(e/2)}
    \int_0^\infty r^{e/2-1}e^{-ru^2}\ud r,\qquad 0<e<1;
  \]
  a zero coordinate contributes the positive-definite constant one.
  Consequently, $f_e$ is a positive mixture of positive-definite tangential
  Gaussian functions. For $\varphi \in \mathcal{S}(\R^3)$, its quadratic form
  against the plane measure is
  \[
    \int_\R\ud z\int_{\R^2}\int_{\R^2}
    f_e(y-y')\varphi(y,z)\overline{\varphi(y',z)}
    \ud y\ud y'.
  \]
  Absolute Fubini follows by setting
  \[
    A(u)=\int_\R\int_{\R^2}
    |\varphi(y+u,z)\varphi(y,z)|\ud y\ud z.
  \]
  Rapid decay gives $A(u) \le C_N\prod_j(1+|u_j|)^{-N}$. Hence, $\int
  f_e(u)A(u)\ud u < \infty$ for $N > 1$, by one-dimensional integrability.
  Integrating the nonnegative Gaussian quadratic forms proves positive
  definiteness. This argument includes the low endpoint, where both coordinate
  powers are zero.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We compare the Gaussian convolutions on the plane.
  For $\epsilon > 0$ and $w \in \R^3$, apply H\"older on the finite positive
  measure $p_\epsilon(w-(y,z))m(\ud y,\ud z)$. Its mass is
  $p_\epsilon^{(1)}(w_3)$. If $f(y) = \prod_j|y_j|^{-a_j}$, the identity between
  ordinary densities
  \[
    cf=c^\vartheta
    \left(cf^{1/(1-\vartheta)}\right)^{1-\vartheta}
  \]
  holds $m$-almost everywhere. Both endpoint convolutions are finite, since $v_j
  < 1$ and the Gaussian decays at infinity. H\"older gives
  exactly~\eqref{E:three-gaussian-comparison}; no power of a distribution is
  taken.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We compute the spectral normalization of the low
  endpoint.
  Finally, the low spectral measure is
  \[
    \mu_{\rm low}(\ud\xi)
    =\frac{c}{2\pi}\delta_0(\ud\xi_1)\delta_0(\ud\xi_2)\ud\xi_3.
  \]
  This follows from the unit spectral atom of a constant and the one-dimensional
  Fourier identity for $\delta_0$ in the stated convention. Its unit-ball mass
  is $c/\pi$, so the uniform closing bound of
  Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution} is $c/2$. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:plane-endpoints}.
\end{proof}

\LeanMarker{L:endpoint-closure}
\begin{lemma}[Linear images and finite positive sums]\label{L:endpoint-closure}
  Fix the parameters in~\eqref{E:three-parameters}. The covariance comparison of
  Hypothesis~\ref{H:three-endpoints} is preserved by simultaneously pushing the
  target and its two endpoints forward under any invertible linear map $T:\R^3
  \to \R^3$. It is also preserved by finite sums with nonnegative weights, when
  all component comparisons use the same $\alpha,\vartheta,\chi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We first extend the comparison from isotropic to general nondegenerate
  Gaussian kernels. We then track the common determinant factor under linear
  maps and use discrete Hölder for finite positive sums.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We extend the Gaussian convolution comparison.
  First the comparison extends to every centered nondegenerate Gaussian
  probability density $q_\Sigma$ with covariance matrix $\Sigma$. Choose $a > 0$
  below the smallest eigenvalue of $\Sigma$. The Gaussian convolution identity
  gives $q_\Sigma = p_a*q_{\Sigma-aI}$. Put $F_J = p_a*J$ for each $J \in
  \{\Gamma,\Gamma_{\rm low},\Gamma_{\rm st}\}$. These functions are nonnegative,
  bounded, and continuous: Gaussian weighting makes their positive tempered
  spectral measures finite, and the covariance bound is $|F_J(x)| \le F_J(0) <
  \infty$. Tonelli and H\"older on the Gaussian probability measure yield
  \[
    q_\Sigma*\Gamma
    \le q_{\Sigma-aI}*
    \left(F_{\Gamma_{\rm low}}^\vartheta
    F_{\Gamma_{\rm st}}^{1-\vartheta}\right)
    \le(q_\Sigma*\Gamma_{\rm low})^\vartheta
    (q_\Sigma*\Gamma_{\rm st})^{1-\vartheta}.
  \]
  Every convolution here is finite.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We push all three measures through the same linear
  map.
  Write $J_T = T_\#J$ and $d_T = |\det T|$. Pushforward preserves nonnegativity,
  evenness, local finiteness, temperedness, and homogeneous scaling, since $T$
  is invertible and commutes with scalar dilation. For positive definiteness,
  take a complex Schwartz function $\varphi$ and put $\psi(y) = \varphi(Ty)$.
  Then
  \[
    (\varphi*\widetilde{\varphi})(Ty)
    =d_T(\psi*\widetilde{\psi})(y).
  \]
  The quadratic form of $T_\#J$ is therefore $d_T$ times a nonnegative quadratic
  form of $J$. With $\Sigma = \epsilon T^{-1}(T^{-1})^\top$, direct Gaussian
  change of variables gives, for every $\epsilon > 0$ and $x$,
  \[
    (p_\epsilon*J_T)(x)
    =d_T^{-1}(q_\Sigma*J)(T^{-1}x).
  \]
  Apply the preceding comparison. The two endpoint factors contribute
  $d_T^{-\vartheta}d_T^{-(1-\vartheta)} = d_T^{-1}$, which is the target factor.
  This proves the comparison for the three pushed-forward measures.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We take finite weighted sums of endpoint triples.
  For the finite-sum assertion, let $\Gamma_i,L_i,S_i$, $1 \le i \le N$, be
  component triples, and let $w_i \ge 0$. At a fixed width and translation put
  $l_i = (p_\epsilon*L_i)(x)$ and $s_i = (p_\epsilon*S_i)(x)$. Finite sums
  preserve all required covariance properties and scalings. Discrete H\"older
  gives
  \[
    \begin{split}
      p_\epsilon*\left(\sum_iw_i\Gamma_i\right)
      &\le\sum_i(w_il_i)^\vartheta(w_is_i)^{1-\vartheta}\\
      &\le\left(p_\epsilon*\sum_iw_iL_i\right)^\vartheta
      \left(p_\epsilon*\sum_iw_iS_i\right)^{1-\vartheta}.
    \end{split}
  \]
  Zero weights cause no ambiguity, since both powers are positive. The all-zero
  target also satisfies the comparison. This proves
  Lemma~\ref{L:endpoint-closure}.
\end{proof}

The linear maps in Lemma~\ref{L:endpoint-closure} act on the noise covariances.
All moment estimates below still use the original isotropic wave kernel.
Endpoint spectral masses and variational quantities are computed for the
transformed covariances; their values need not be unchanged by a nonorthogonal
map.

For the low endpoint let $V_{\rm low} = \mu_{\rm low}(B_1)$ in the spectral
convention of Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution}, and put
\[
  Z_{b_0}=\frac2{(1-b_0)(2-b_0)},\qquad
  C_{\rm low}=2Z_{b_0}\pi V_{\rm low}.
\]
For the static endpoint, write
\[
  \mathcal{Q}_{\rm st}(g)
  =\int_{\R^3}\int_{\R^3}g(y+z)^2g(y)^2\ud y
  \,\Gamma_{\rm st}(\ud z),\qquad
  \mathcal{F}_3=\{g\in W^{1,2}(\R^3):\|g\|_2=1\},
\]
using real Sobolev functions, and define
\begin{equation}\label{E:three-static-variational}
  \begin{split}
    \mathcal{M}_{\rm st}
    &=\sup_{g\in\mathcal{F}_3}
    \left\{\mathcal{Q}_{\rm st}(g)^{1/2}-\|\nabla g\|_2^2\right\},\\
    Q&=3-\chi,\qquad \nu_{\rm st}=4-\chi,\qquad
    \Lambda_{\rm st}
    =2\left(\frac{2\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{\rm st}}}{4-\chi}\right)^{4-\chi}.
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
The functional is precisely Chen--Hu's (1.11), p.~2147, for the endpoint
covariance, with unit gradient penalty. The positive-measure energy above agrees
with the spectral form on every real Sobolev profile by
Lemma~\ref{L:physical-spectral-energy}; thus no density is assigned to a
singular covariance. The lemma also supplies finiteness, since $\chi < 2$ gives
Dalang's condition as checked below. Nonnegativity follows by dilating a
unit-$L^2$ Schwartz function: its energy term is nonnegative and its gradient
penalty tends to zero.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional three-dimensional transfer]
  \label{P:three-transfer}
  Under the preceding assumptions and Hypothesis~\ref{H:three-endpoints}, with
  $\Gamma \ne 0$, there is a finite constant $C > 0$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{E:three-transfer}
    A_k(t)\le\frac{C^kt^{(4-\alpha-\alpha_0)k}}{(k!)^{3-\alpha}}
    \quad(k\ge1),\qquad
    \E u^2(t,x)\le\exp\left(qC^{1/q}t^\kappa\right),
    \quad q=3-\alpha.
  \end{equation}
  The estimates hold for all $t > 0$ and $x \in \R^3$, with data $(1,0)$. The
  constant may depend on the fixed covariances, $\alpha_0$, and $\vartheta$, but
  not on $k,t,x$. In addition, the explicit variational upper constant satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{E:three-variational-upper}
    \limsup_{t\to\infty}t^{-\kappa}\log\E u^2(t,x)
    \le q\left(C_{\rm low}^{\,\vartheta}
    \Lambda_{\rm st}^{\,1-\vartheta}\right)^{1/q}.
  \end{equation}
  This constant is not asserted sharp and is not substituted into the pure
  all-time exponential bound in~\eqref{E:three-transfer}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We verify the static input and establish a regularized coefficient
  comparison on one common measure. We identify the static and target Green
  limits separately before removing noise widths. We then sum the all-time
  coefficient bound and retain the finite low orders when deriving the sharper
  asymptotic constant.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 1.~}We state and verify the imported static
  coefficient input.
  We first record the general static input needed here. For a static endpoint
  covariance $\Xi$ of exponent $\chi$, Fourier uniqueness gives $\mu_\Xi(rA) =
  r^\chi\mu_\Xi(A)$ for its positive spectral measure. Its unit-ball mass is
  finite, and
  \[
    \int_{\R^3}(1+|\xi|^2)^{-1}\mu_\Xi(\ud\xi)
    \le\mu_\Xi(B_1)
    +2^\chi\mu_\Xi(B_1)\sum_{j\ge0}2^{j(\chi-2)}<\infty.
  \]
  Thus, the static Dalang condition holds. Let $U_n$ be the source's ordered
  integrals for this covariance. Its nonnegative positive-definite
  tempered-distribution class is explicitly included by the generalized
  covariance convention in~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{p.~2152, (2.18)}; its
  homogeneity degree satisfies $0 < \chi < 2 < 3$. No density or angular
  regularity is added here. Let $U_0 = 1$, and set $B_k(t) =
  \sum_{m=0}^{2k}\E[U_m(t,x)U_{2k-m}(t,x)]$. If $\Xi = 0$, then $B_k = 0$ for $k
  \ge 1$, and $C_{\rm st} = 1$ suffices in the bound below. For the remaining
  static calculation, assume $\Xi \ne 0$. From
  Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{Theorem~3.8(i), p.~2165, and (2.17),
  (2.19)--(2.22), pp.~2151--2153}, the expectation and product rules give finite
  nonnegative coefficients with every split and pairing included; the latter
  formulas also specify the two-chain and measure conventions. Finiteness of the
  variational input follows from Chen~\cite{chen:23:exponential}*{Theorem~1.1,
  p.~2}, which gives a finite square-root variational supremum under Dalang's
  condition. Chen--Hu identify that supremum with $2^{\chi/(4-\chi)}\mathcal{M}$
  on p.~2175. Hence, their coefficient-rate constant involving $\mathcal{M}$ is
  finite. For $\Xi = \Gamma_{\rm st}$ this functional is $\mathcal{M}_{\rm st}$
  defined above. Only finiteness, not a sharp value, is needed for the all-time
  coefficient bound.

  From Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{(5.1), p.~2173, and (5.15),
  p.~2180}, at $p = 2$,
  \[
    B_k(t)=t^{(4-\chi)k}B_k(1),\qquad
    \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac1k
    \log\left((k!)^{3-\chi}B_k(1)\right)<\infty.
  \]
  For $\Xi \ne 0$, choose $R > 0$ above this upper limit, then enlarge $e^R$ to
  cover the $k$th roots of the finitely many remaining quantities
  $(k!)^{3-\chi}B_k(1)$. We obtain a finite $C_{\rm st} = C_{\rm st}(\Xi) > 0$
  such that
  \begin{equation}\label{E:three-static-coefficient}
    B_k(t)\le\frac{C_{\rm st}^{\,k}t^{(4-\chi)k}}{(k!)^{3-\chi}},
    \qquad k\ge1,\quad t>0.
  \end{equation}
  Also $B_0 = 1$. We apply this source bound only after taking the fixed-order
  noise limits, with $\Xi = \Gamma_{\rm st}$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 2.~}We verify the target hypotheses and form the
  common regularized measure.
  For the target, the same spectral scaling calculation has exponent $\alpha$.
  Its dyadic sum for the weight $(1+|\xi|^2)^{-(2-\alpha_0)/2}$ is finite
  because $\alpha < 2-\alpha_0$. The target fixed-order limit is supplied by
  Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}. The endpoint $\Gamma_{\rm low}$ has
  covariance exponent one, with $V_{\rm low},C_{\rm low}$ defined above.
  Finiteness of $V_{\rm low}$ follows from the Radon spectral measure. It is
  positive: otherwise homogeneity gives $\mu_{\rm low} = 0$,
  contradicting~\eqref{E:three-gaussian-comparison} and $\Gamma \ne 0$.

  Fix $k \ge 1$, $t > 0$, and positive widths $\epsilon,\delta,\eta$. Noise
  width $\epsilon$ means edge covariance width $2\epsilon$; $\delta$ is the
  target temporal cutoff and $\eta$ is Green smoothing. Write
  $X_n^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta}(t,x)$ for the source's ordered integral against
  its smoothed field $W_{\epsilon,\delta}$, with every Green kernel replaced by
  $G_{h,\eta}$, and set $X_0^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta} = 1$. Explicitly, the target
  coefficient is
  \[
    A_k^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta}(t)
    =\sum_{m=0}^{2k}
    \E\left[X_m^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta}(t,x)
    X_{2k-m}^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta}(t,x)\right].
  \]
  On the full disjoint union over all splits $m+n = 2k$ and all $D \in \Pi_k$,
  define a common finite positive measure by
  \[
    \int\Phi\ud\nu_{k,t,\eta}
    :=\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\sum_{D\in\Pi_k}
    \int_{[0,t]^m_<\times[0,t]^{2k-m}_<}\ud s
    \int\Phi(m,D,s,y)\prod_{l=1}^{2k}G_{h_l,\eta}(\ud y_l).
  \]
  Each chain has its own root; $h_l = s_l-s_{p(l)}$, $y_l$ is an increment, and
  $x_l$ is the cumulative position in its chain. Empty chains use empty
  products. The spatial mass is $\prod_lh_l$, so bounded time simplexes and
  finite discrete sums prove finiteness. This measure does not depend on which
  covariance is inserted.

  For $J \in \{\Gamma,\Gamma_{\rm low},\Gamma_{\rm st}\}$ set
  \[
    H_J=\prod_{(i,j)\in D}(p_{2\epsilon}*J)(x_i-x_j),\qquad
    T_b=\prod_{(i,j)\in D}|s_i-s_j|^{-b}.
  \]
  If $Q_\delta$ is the product of the target temporal covariances, the source
  cutoff gives $0 \le Q_\delta \le T_{\alpha_0} = T_{b_0}^{\,\vartheta}$. The
  covariance hypothesis gives $H_\Gamma \le H_{\Gamma_{\rm low}}^\vartheta
  H_{\Gamma_{\rm st}}^{1-\vartheta}$ at the same edge width. Define the
  deterministic low endpoint and the regularized static coefficient by
  \[
    L_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)
    =\int T_{b_0}H_{\Gamma_{\rm low}}\ud\nu_{k,t,\eta},
    \qquad
    B_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)
    =\int H_{\Gamma_{\rm st}}\ud\nu_{k,t,\eta}.
  \]
  Gaussian moments and separate chain reversal identify the target regularized
  coefficient with $\int Q_\delta H_\Gamma \ud\nu_{k,t,\eta}$. The static
  identity has temporal covariance one and includes self-pairings. H\"older on
  the entire common measure yields
  \begin{equation}\label{E:three-regularized-holder}
    A_k^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta}(t)
    \le\left(L_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)\right)^\vartheta
    \left(B_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)\right)^{1-\vartheta}.
  \end{equation}
  No stochastic identity is assumed for $L_k^{\epsilon,\eta}$ with its original
  temporal power, and no relation between different temporal cutoff kernels is
  used.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 3.~}We estimate the deterministic low endpoint.
  Lemma~\ref{L:alpha-one-convolution} applies to the low endpoint with no
  angular-density assumption. Its uniform closing bound is $\pi V_{\rm low}/2$.
  Descending increment integration removes the only remaining covariance at each
  closing label; at each opening the paired covariance is already removed and
  the Green mass is $h_l$. The $k$ opening increments sum to at most $2t$.
  Consequently, the common spatial bound is $(\pi V_{\rm low})^kt^k/k^k$,
  uniformly in $\epsilon,\eta$. After summing pairings, the temporal integrand
  is symmetric in all $2k$ labels. Its full cube integral is
  $P_k(Z_{b_0}t^{2-b_0})^k$, where $P_k = (2k)!/(2^kk!)$. The block-order
  factors and the binomial split sum give
  \[
    P_k\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\frac1{m!(2k-m)!}=\frac{2^k}{k!}.
  \]
  Therefore,
  \begin{equation}\label{E:three-low-coefficient}
    L_k^{\epsilon,\eta}(t)
    \le\frac{C_{\rm low}^{\,k}t^{(3-b_0)k}}{k!\,k^k}
    \le\frac{C_{\rm low}^{\,k}t^{(3-b_0)k}}{(k!)^2}.
  \end{equation}
  Here $b_0 < 1$ makes the original temporal cube integral finite. At fixed
  spatial width, the static covariance is bounded, so its regularized
  coefficient is finite too. This justifies the finite right side
  in~\eqref{E:three-regularized-holder}.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 4.~}We identify the static source and its
  zero-Green-width integral.
  We now identify the static inner Green limit with the source's prescribed
  zero-Green-width approximant. Take the source's static isonormal noise $W^{\rm
  st}$ with covariance $\Gamma_{\rm st}$ and set $W_\epsilon(x) = W^{\rm
  st}(p_\epsilon(x-\cdot))$. All widths are therefore on the source's Gaussian
  space, and the covariance is $p_{2\epsilon}*\Gamma_{\rm st}$. This covariance
  is bounded and continuous, since Gaussian weighting makes its positive
  tempered spectral measure finite. It has a continuous modification. Indeed,
  with $\mu_{\rm st}$ the spectral measure,
  \[
    \E|W_\epsilon(x+h)-W_\epsilon(x)|^2
    \le |h|^2\int_{\R^3}|\xi|^2e^{-\epsilon|\xi|^2}
    \mu_{\rm st}(\ud\xi).
  \]
  The integral is finite by temperedness. Gaussian eighth moments are therefore
  bounded by a constant times $|h|^8$. Kolmogorov's continuity criterion in
  dimension three, applied on compact sets and then on a countable exhaustion,
  gives the claimed modification. We choose this version. For $n \ge 1$ define
  $\Phi_n(\mathbf{x}) = \prod_{j=1}^n W_\epsilon(x_j)$ as an $L^2$ random
  variable. Gaussian moments show that $\Phi_n$ is continuous as a map from
  $(\R^3)^n$ to $L^2$ and uniformly bounded there: if $v_\epsilon =
  (p_{2\epsilon}*\Gamma_{\rm st})(0)$, then
  \[
    \|\Phi_n(\mathbf{x})\|_2^2\le P_n v_\epsilon^n.
  \]
  The continuity follows by applying the same finite Gaussian moment formula to
  $\E|\Phi_n(\mathbf{x})-\Phi_n(\mathbf{x}')|^2$. Its separable continuous image
  is strongly measurable.

  Let $\mathfrak{g}_{n,t,x}^{\eta}$ be the finite positive ordered Green-chain
  measure, including its time variables, from the source's kernel (2.13), with
  each Green factor smoothed by $p_\eta$. For $\eta = 0$, its spatial marginal
  is exactly the measure $\mu_n^{t,x}$ specified immediately before (2.22),
  pp.~2152--2153. Its mass is finite because each spatial factor has mass its
  time gap. Thus,
  \[
    U_n^{\epsilon,0}(t,x)
    :=\int\Phi_n(\mathbf{x})
    \mathfrak{g}_{n,t,x}^{0}(\ud\mathbf{s},\ud\mathbf{x})
  \]
  exists as a Bochner integral in $L^2$. We verify its agreement with the
  source's pathwise integral, rather than identifying it only by notation. Put
  $\rho = \mathfrak{g}_{n,t,x}^{0}$ and $M_n = P_n^{1/2}v_\epsilon^{n/2}$, and
  denote the pathwise integral by $J_n = \int\Phi_n\ud\rho$. The continuous
  field version makes the integrand jointly measurable. Since
  \[
    \int\E|\Phi_n|\ud\rho\le M_n\rho(1)<\infty,
    \qquad
    \|J_n\|_2
    \le\int\|\Phi_n\|_2\ud\rho\le M_n\rho(1),
  \]
  the pathwise integral exists almost surely and belongs to $L^2$. For every $Y
  \in L^2$, Cauchy--Schwarz and Fubini give
  \[
    \E[YJ_n]
    =\int\E[Y\Phi_n]\ud\rho.
  \]
  Bochner duality gives the same identity for the $L^2$ integral. Taking $Y =
  J_n-U_n^{\epsilon,0}$ identifies the two random variables almost surely.
  Changing to another jointly measurable field modification leaves the integral
  unchanged by Fubini. Since the static integrand is independent of the time
  variables, this is the integral against $\mu_n^{t,x}$ in the source's
  convention and hence its smoothed approximant $S_{n,\epsilon}$ with common
  width, from Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{(3.1), p.~2153, and the
  convention preceding (2.22)}. Define $U_n^{\epsilon,\eta}$ by the same formula
  with $\mathfrak{g}_{n,t,x}^{\eta}$, and retain $U_0^{\epsilon,\eta} = 1$.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 5.~}We identify both inner Green limits in mean
  square.
  In the second and mixed moments of these integrals, the Gaussian moment
  factors are bounded continuous functions of all spatial positions. For each
  pair of time vectors, weak convergence of the products of Green measures,
  including mixed products with one width already zero, applies to these
  functions. The masses are unchanged. A constant depending only on $n,\epsilon$
  times the product of all time gaps dominates the resulting time integrals.
  This is integrable on the two bounded time simplexes. Hence, as
  $\eta\downarrow0$,
  \[
    \E\left[(U_n^{\epsilon,\eta})^2\right]
    \longrightarrow\E\left[(U_n^{\epsilon,0})^2\right],
    \qquad
    \E\left[U_n^{\epsilon,\eta}U_n^{\epsilon,0}\right]
    \longrightarrow\E\left[(U_n^{\epsilon,0})^2\right].
  \]
  Subtracting twice the second limit from the two square terms proves
  $U_n^{\epsilon,\eta} \to U_n^{\epsilon,0}$ in $L^2$. This identifies the limit
  with the source approximant, rather than only showing that positive-width
  approximants are Cauchy. For the target we specify the zero-Green-width object
  as well. At fixed $\epsilon,\delta$, its field covariance is
  $\gamma_\delta^0(s-r)(p_{2\epsilon}*\Gamma)(y-z)$, where
  \[
    \gamma_\delta^0(u)
    =\Gamma(\alpha_0)^{-1}\int_0^{1/\delta}
    e^{-\lambda|u|}\lambda^{\alpha_0-1}\ud\lambda,\qquad
    \gamma_\delta^0(0)
    =\frac{\delta^{-\alpha_0}}{\alpha_0\Gamma(\alpha_0)}.
  \]
  Here $\Gamma(\alpha_0)$ is Euler's gamma function. The variance
  $v_{\epsilon,\delta} = \gamma_\delta^0(0)(p_{2\epsilon}*\Gamma)(0)$ is finite.
  This field has a jointly continuous modification. The temporal covariance
  satisfies $\gamma_\delta^0(0)-\gamma_\delta^0(h) \le C_\delta|h|$, by
  $1-e^{-\lambda|h|} \le \lambda|h|$. The spatial increment bound just proved
  applies to $p_{2\epsilon}*\Gamma$. The joint increment variance is therefore
  bounded by $C_{\epsilon,\delta}(|h_0|+|h'|^2)$. Gaussian twelfth moments are
  bounded by $C_{\epsilon,\delta}|h|^6$ for $|h| \le 1$. Kolmogorov's criterion
  in dimension four supplies the version.

  Let $\mathfrak{f}_{n,t,x}^{\eta}$ be the ordered Green measure of the target's
  kernel (2.16), with $s_{n+1} = t$, $x_{n+1} = x$: its factors are
  $G_{s_{j+1}-s_j,\eta}$. Define
  \[
    \begin{split}
      \Psi_n(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{x})
      &=\prod_{j=1}^nW_{\epsilon,\delta}(s_j,x_j),\\
      X_n^{\epsilon,\delta,0}(t,x)
      &=\int\Psi_n(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{x})
      \mathfrak{f}_{n,t,x}^{0}(\ud\mathbf{s},\ud\mathbf{x}).
    \end{split}
  \]
  The Gaussian moment bound is $\|\Psi_n\|_2^2 \le P_nv_{\epsilon,\delta}^n$.
  Joint measurability, finite measure mass, and the preceding Fubini argument
  show that this Bochner integral equals the pathwise integral in the target's
  prescribed convention \cite{chen:author2026:time-dependency}*{pp.~13--14}. The
  same definition at positive $\eta$ gives $X_n^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta}$ above.
  Its second and mixed $\eta/0$ moments converge by weak Green convergence
  against the bounded continuous Gaussian moment functions, with dominating
  bound a constant times the product of the time gaps. Thus,
  \[
    \|X_n^{\epsilon,\delta,\eta}
    -X_n^{\epsilon,\delta,0}\|_2\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  This proves the inner-limit identification with the target approximant. Both
  order-zero objects remain one.


  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 6.~}We remove noise widths and sum the all-time
  coefficient bound.
  First take this Green limit in~\eqref{E:three-regularized-holder}, using the
  uniform bound~\eqref{E:three-low-coefficient}. Next send the noise widths to
  zero. The static fixed-order $L^2$ limit is from
  Chen--Hu~\cite{chen.hu:25:hyperbolic}*{Theorem~3.8(i), p.~2165}; the target
  fixed-order limit is from Lemma~\ref{L:joint-fixed-order}. Products of two
  fixed orders converge in $L^1$, since
  \[
    \|YZ-Y'Z'\|_1
    \le\|Y-Y'\|_2\|Z\|_2+\|Y'\|_2\|Z-Z'\|_2.
  \]
  The split sums are finite, and zero-order factors remain one. We obtain
  \[
    A_k(t)\le
    \left(\frac{C_{\rm low}^{\,k}t^{(3-b_0)k}}{(k!)^2}\right)^\vartheta
    B_k(t)^{1-\vartheta}.
  \]
  Only now use~\eqref{E:three-static-coefficient}. Neither monotonicity of
  spatial smoothing nor a uniform smoothed static asymptotic was needed. The
  identities
  \[
    \begin{split}
      2\vartheta+(1-\vartheta)(3-\chi)&=3-\alpha,\\
      \vartheta(3-b_0)+(1-\vartheta)(4-\chi)&=4-\alpha-\alpha_0
    \end{split}
  \]
  give the coefficient estimate with $C = C_{\rm low}^{\,\vartheta}C_{\rm
  st}^{\,1-\vartheta}$. Finally, $A_0 = 1$, $q = 3-\alpha > 1$, and
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^k}{(k!)^q}
    \le\left(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{k/q}}{k!}\right)^q
    =e^{qz^{1/q}},\qquad z\ge0.
  \]
  The nonnegative full series of Proposition~\ref{P:mixed}, with $z =
  Ct^{4-\alpha-\alpha_0}$ and $(4-\alpha-\alpha_0)/q = \kappa$, yields the
  moment estimate. It remains to prove~\eqref{E:three-variational-upper}. The
  precise upper coefficient rate in Chen--Hu's (5.15), p.~2180, at $p = 2$ is
  \[
    \begin{split}
      \limsup_{k\to\infty}
      \left((k!)^Q B_k(1)\right)^{1/k}
      &\le 2^{-Q}2^{\nu_{\rm st}}
      \left(\frac{2\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{\rm st}}}{\nu_{\rm st}}\right)^{\nu_{\rm st}}\\
      &=\Lambda_{\rm st}.
    \end{split}
  \]

  \medskip
  \noindent\textit{Step 7.~}We retain the precise asymptotic coefficient rate
  and its low-order remainder.
  The factor two follows from $\nu_{\rm st}-Q = 1$; the logarithm in the source
  applies to the entire coefficient-rate product. Fix $r > 0$. There is $N_r \ge
  1$, depending on the fixed endpoint and $r$ but not on $t$, such that
  \[
    B_k(1)\le\frac{(\Lambda_{\rm st}+r)^k}{(k!)^Q},
    \qquad k\ge N_r.
  \]
  Static coefficient homogeneity and the already established coefficient
  H\"older inequality therefore give, simultaneously for every $t > 0$ and $k
  \ge N_r$,
  \[
    A_k(t)\le\frac{D_r^k t^{\beta k}}{(k!)^q},\qquad
    D_r=C_{\rm low}^{\,\vartheta}(\Lambda_{\rm st}+r)^{1-\vartheta},
    \qquad \beta=4-\alpha-\alpha_0.
  \]
  For each $1 \le k < N_r$ the same inequality with the exact static coefficient
  gives $A_k(t) \le d_kt^{\beta k}$, where
  \[
    d_k=\frac{C_{\rm low}^{\,\vartheta k}
    B_k(1)^{1-\vartheta}}{(k!)^{2\vartheta}}<\infty.
  \]
  Adding $A_0 = 1$ and the nonnegative series yields
  \[
    \E u^2(t,x)\le P_r(t)+e^{qD_r^{1/q}t^\kappa},\qquad
    P_r(t)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{N_r-1}d_kt^{\beta k}.
  \]
  This finite power sum has $\log P_r(t)/t^\kappa \to 0$; its exponents need not
  be integers. The logarithm of a sum of two positive terms is at most the
  larger logarithm plus $\log2$. Consequently, the required limsup is at most
  $qD_r^{1/q}$. Let $r\downarrow0$ to obtain~\eqref{E:three-variational-upper}.
  This also covers a zero static rate, without division by that rate. The exact
  static homogeneity makes $N_r$ independent of $t$; no target coefficient
  homogeneity or smoothed static asymptotic was invoked. The finitely many low
  orders were kept, so this argument does not replace the all-time constant by
  the asymptotic one. This proves Proposition~\ref{P:three-transfer}.
\end{proof}
