# Durable lessons from the direct-event calibration

- Rescale the actual model before choosing a stability norm. The original
  large-time problem has noise sqrt(delta) AND wave coefficient delta^2;
  a fixed-speed small-noise theorem does not establish its growth estimate.
- Global C0 smallness can hide order-one perturbations on the microscopic
  time scale. The periodic example even has b_delta/sqrt(delta)->0 in C0,
  but its limiting growth rate differs. This is a uniform-topology no-go,
  not failure of Gaussian convergence in probability.
- A local transfer bound must reach the full product and final fractional
  block. Positive entrywise comparisons avoid unjustified commutation of
  different transfer matrices. Initial-data and phase-space hypotheses matter.
- Preserve the negative square in the exact velocity gauge. Its presence is
  harmless for scalar continuous paths but exposes a genuinely missing
  product/cutoff estimate for spatially rough fields.
- Wellposedness is not a spectral lower estimate. The held pathwise source
  has stricter parameters, global Sobolev data and an explicit non-patching
  proof. Do not infer a restartable transfer map from its theorem title.
- Kernel-check actual real-power normalizations and matrix consumers rather
  than only an exponent screen. Keep Gaussian/Green/series and ODE analysis
  in the independent application review.
- Zero perturbation is a required calibration. State transfer-error bounds
  nonstrictly so eta0 is included; equality there must not be ruled out.
- Distinguish covariance normalization from process normalization: raw time
  covariance needs H(2H-1), while the Gaussian primitive needs its square root.
