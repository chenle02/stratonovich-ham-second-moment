I have read the manuscript, its bibliography, and the frozen cited-source texts (in particular the current 64-page author version of Chen's source, the Chen–Hu 2025 paper, Crouzeix–Palencia v1, and Balan–Conus 2016), and I checked the key pinpoints the paper relies on. Below are the concerns that caught my attention at referee pace, in free-form prose. I am not grading the paper; these are the points that would make me hesitate.

---

**1. The manuscript presents itself as "Working notes," not a paper.** The title block (line 68) and abstract (lines 70ff) both say "Working notes." The document is ~6300 lines with 16 sections, and the text repeatedly tells the reader which parts are "retained to record their separate hypotheses and limitations" rather than used (e.g., Section 1, "Main result and dependencies," lines 143–210, and the abstract). Only Section 5 ("Interval fields and the full upper bound," line 5042) is an actual input to the main theorem (Theorem T:full-upper, line 6041). Large swaths — the higher-α convolution bound (line 2037), the Riesz-by-interpolation bound (line 2422), the general planar covariance section (line 2728), the three-dimensional endpoint construction (line 3193), the "energy fidelity" section (line 4083), and the two static-phase sections (lines 4228, 4470) — are either superseded, conditional, or explicitly not inputs. For a journal submission this is a genuine acceptance concern independent of correctness: a reader cannot tell what the paper's final claimed content is without reconstructing a dependency graph.

**2. The sharp-constant target is stated in terms of quantities the paper itself says are not well-defined.** Goal G:sharp (line 6305) is phrased in terms of $L_*$ and $L^*$, and the abstract (lines 76–78) and Theorem T:first (line 283, with the `\myCmts` warning at lines 275–280) say the source's printed variational normalization "requires clarification; no correction is adopted." So the paper's second goal — the sharp constant, repeatedly called "the conditional target" — is stated in terms of constants whose intended meaning is unresolved. A referee will find it hard to evaluate a "goal" that is not actually a well-posed statement. The honest framing is fine for notes, but it means the only unconditional content of the paper is the nonsharp upper bound (Goal G:upper), and everything else (the numerical calibrations in Corollary C:d1-first-calibration, Remarks R:d1-comparisons, R:static-holder-scope, R:planar-scope, R:three-endpoint-scope, all the "$2L_*\le\dots$" remarks) is conditional on a correction that is not specified.

**4. The hypothesis of the main theorem is not self-contained.** Theorem T:full-upper is stated "under Hypothesis H:homog," but H:homog (line 125) only says $\gamma\ge0$ is nonnegative definite with spectral measure $\mu$ and $\gamma(cx)=c^{-\alpha}\gamma(x)$. The proof in Section S:interval-upper (line 5042) actually uses a richer standing object: "a nonnegative, even, positive-definite tempered Radon measure $\Gamma$" with $\Gamma(rA)=r^{d-\alpha}\Gamma(A)$ and a spectral convention $\Gamma=\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu(\ud x)$. The words "even" and "tempered Radon measure," the spectral ball mass $\mu(B_1)$, and the physical nonnegativity used in the resolvent comparison (Lemma L:interval-contractions) are not all part of H:homog. So the theorem as stated is not self-contained; the reader must hunt for the standing assumptions.

**5. Proposition P:Jensen (line 330) is used before the convergence it needs is established.** The proof writes $\mathbb E u(t,x)=\sum_{n\ge0}\mathbb E S_{2n}(g_{2n})\ge1$, which requires $\mathbb E u = \lim \mathbb E U_N$, i.e., $L^1$-convergence of the chaos series (equivalently $L^2$-convergence). That convergence is only established later in the document (Lemma L:crude-corrected, line 668, or via the imported Theorem 4.1). At the point P:Jensen appears (Section "What is known"), the interchange is unjustified. This is a minor logical-ordering gap, but a referee will notice it because P:Jensen is then invoked in several "calibration" remarks as if it were unconditional (which, once the later convergence is granted, it is).

**6. Notation overload, acknowledged but genuinely impairing.** Section 1's "Notation" paragraph (lines 153–168) concedes that $\Gamma$ is both the covariance measure and Euler's gamma function, $\delta$ is both the exponent $\alpha-1$ and a temporal cutoff width, $p$ is both the inventory exponent $2-\alpha_0$ and a moment order, $b$ is both $2-\alpha$ and a coordinate power, and $\chi$ vs $\alpha$ denote different spatial degrees. The paper says these are "distinguished by their arguments," but in several formulas both senses occur in the same display — e.g., the definition of $\gamma_\delta^0$ in the proof of T:full-upper (near line 6150) contains both $\Gamma(\alpha_0)$ (Euler) and the measure $\Gamma$. This is a real readability cost for a document already burdened by heavy hedging.

**7. The claim that a single "parameter-only" constant works for the entire covariance class deserves explicit scrutiny.** Theorem T:full-upper asserts $C=C(\alpha,\alpha_0)$, with covariance entering only through $R_\alpha=\alpha\pi\mu(B_1)/(2\sin(\pi\alpha/2))$. This is a very strong uniformity statement — one constant for *every* homogeneous covariance in $d=1,2,3$, including anisotropic ones and $\delta_0$ in $d=1$ — and it is exactly the kind of place where a hidden covariance- or cutoff-dependence could be silently absorbed into a "parameter-only" constant. The paper is careful to list its cutoff-removal steps ("Cutoff dependence and the order of limits," near line 6220), and I did not find a specific error, but I would flag this as the point of highest verification load for the referee: the uniformity in the finite interval family $J$, in $\epsilon$, in the mode/band cutoffs, and in the localization $f_T$ must all be checked to carry no covariance constant.

**8. The first-moment/mild-solution identification is explicitly non-unique.** Proposition P:mild-construction (line 823) proves the expansion series satisfies the mild equation but states "No uniqueness assertion is made" (and this is repeated in the abstract and Section 1). Consequently, the title's claim about "the second moment of the hyperbolic Anderson model" is, strictly, a bound on the second moment of one particular (the canonical expansion) solution. Given that the source's Theorem 1.1 part (2) only gives necessity of the spectral condition, not uniqueness, the paper's own hedging is appropriate — but a referee will want the statement of the main result to say exactly which object is being bounded, rather than leaving it to a remark.

**9. Specific technical spots I could not fully verify but would want checked.** (a) Lemma L:plane-endpoints (Section 3D endpoints) asserts the plane-supported low endpoint has spectral ball mass $V_{\rm low}=c/\pi$; this is the sort of normalization where a factor of $2$ or a missing antipodal accounting easily slips in (the same remark explicitly worries about a "$1/2$ essential" factor elsewhere), and it feeds Corollary C:three-finite-images — though that material is not an input to the main theorem. (b) Lemma L:complex-phase (line ~4550) claims its bound is "independent of $H$, $\|A_0\|$ and $\|B\|$," which is load-bearing for cutoff removal; the proof is elaborate (inverse square roots, numerical-range parabola, Crouzeix–Palencia), and I verified the Crouzeix–Palencia citation ((3) on p. 1, Theorem 6 on pp. 5–6 of arXiv:1702.00668v1) matches how it is invoked, but the full operator estimate itself I did not re-derive. (c) The finite-order calibration tables in Sections S:G1/S:d1-upper (e.g., the order-four white table, Proposition P:white-four, and the static table, Lemma L:static-four) are presented as "computed" with no closed forms for the residual integrals; I checked a few of the aggregate numbers (e.g., the static order-four total $11/540$ and the order-two total $t^3/3$) and they are internally consistent, but the individual entries are unchecked.

**10. Repeated comparison to Balan–Conus is labeled "comparison only," but its scope keeps shifting.** The exponent claim $(2H+2-a)/(3-a)\to\kappa$ with $H=1-\alpha_0/2$, $a=\alpha$ checks out against Balan–Conus (their $\rho_w=(2H+2-a)/(3-a)$, line 499 of the supplied source, and $\alpha_H=H(2H-1)$). The paper is careful to say these are Skorohod-model comparisons with different solution concepts and are "not inputs." But the same comparison is restated in at least four places (Remarks R:d1-comparisons, R:higher-calibrations, R:static-holder-scope, R:three-endpoint-scope) with slightly different scoping caveats each time, and each carefully avoids identifying constants. The cumulative effect is that the reader repeatedly encounters a comparison that is declared irrelevant to the proof; this contributes to the "notes, not paper" impression.

---

The one thing I would put on the positive side, because it is a genuine and — as far as I checked — correct contribution: the diagnosis in Remark R:source-uniformity (line 638) is right. The source's Remark 5.1 cites (4.4), which is a smoothing-limit statement, while the actual bound behind (5.8) is (5.4); and (5.4)'s claimed time-uniform constant is falsified by the explicit $n=1$ white-noise computation in Lemma L:white-test. The paper's repaired route (Lemma L:crude-corrected and Lemma L:vertex-crude) is internally coherent. My hesitation to accept stems not from that part, but from the items above: the document's form, the undefined sharp-constant target, and the fact that the one unconditional theorem rests on a fixed-order construction imported from a preprint the paper itself finds defective, with the hypothesis of that theorem not fully stated at the point of use.
