# Durable lessons from the physical phase test

- Test the velocity as well as the position. A known position criterion
  cannot be differentiated by deleting its sine denominator. Combining both
  phase components first exposes the actual positive temporal norm.
- A single-field estimate is not an operator estimate. Sum over the input
  phase basis, retain all four Gram factors and check both frequency groups,
  localization weights and low modes. Here this yields the genuine d1
  localized Gaussian Hilbert--Schmidt result.
- The energy phase can be too strong even when the point solution exists.
  Choose the phase from the actual trace budget, keeping critical logarithms
  and the balanced endpoint rather than forcing the familiar norm.
- Point evaluation need not be the right observable for a point-moment lower
  bound. A normalized spatial average transfers back by stationarity and L2
  triangle. Prove that transfer and its Bochner meaning; otherwise the
  averaged problem is a different goal.
- A spatial scale change must preserve the L1 mass of the test. This prevents
  an unnecessary R^d loss in the original second-moment target.
- Fixed-positive-a frequency asymptotics are not uniform at a=0. The exact
  static identity and vanishing leading coefficient explain the different
  phase budget without changing the actual moment exponent.
- Separate spatial Fourier prefactors from temporal trigonometric factors.
  The operator trace has1/(2pi), while the Gram product has its own half.
- Kernel-check finite phase/Gram/real-power consumers, then review their
  Gaussian/measure/operator application. A read receipt is not reviewer
  execution and a first response is not an all-order random flow.
- Stationarity plus amplified positive mixed coefficients gives uniform-in-x
  canonical norm tails. Do not substitute a stronger unproved uniform-
  regularization premise merely to cite a convenient tail lemma.
