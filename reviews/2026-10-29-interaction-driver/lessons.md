# Durable lessons from the interaction-driver wave

1. A fixed-time response estimate cannot supply a time-uniform increment
   estimate by renaming its constant. Rescale the temporal covariance and
   treat balanced endpoint logarithms explicitly.
2. At the white endpoint, keep the positive temporal norm and pass through
   integrable Gaussian mixture components. An unconditional Fourier swap
   over the non-integrable balanced convolution is not justified. Check the
   mixture prefactor and exponent with actual kernel deductions.
3. Localizing an interaction increment requires finite propagation and an
   enlarged observation cutoff. Multiplication by chi does not commute with
   the wave group, and stationary noise does not give a global HS operator.
4. A source cutoff lives on the source's common Gaussian construction,
   possibly larger than the minimal raw-field span. Establish both filtered
   and DEFECT variance bounds, then use deterministic-test convergence.
   This linear contraction does not imply nonlinear mean/solution order.
5. Pointwise L2 cutoff convergence is insufficient for convergence in a
   Holder driver norm. Use uniform stronger Holder moments plus fixed-grid
   interpolation, and preserve the requested-moment/internal-moment order.
6. Finite local driver maxima and their polynomial box budget are not a
   global or uniformly-local flow estimate. Keep data1, causality, growing
   cone and all-order solution identification as explicit missing consumers.
7. Formalize actual square-root/rpow/normalization and parameter consumers,
   not formulas assumed as the target. Semantic controls must test the exact
   claim boundary: a failed sufficient union-bound budget is not a theorem
   that Gaussian maxima fail, and the midpoint selector is not necessary
   for every possible Holder proof.
8. Inspect an existing service by its live/terminal handle before creating
   a successor. A refused duplicate setup is not permission to overwrite
   raw evidence. Retain failures and verify committed archive members before
   removing only owned terminal raw directories.

9. Follow wiki digests to the closest held primary theorem. Here the book
   already covers Banach Young integration and linear estimates; its separate
   analytic-semigroup theorem is not a wave-group theorem. The next value
   lies in the actual spatial/local interface, not rebuilding generic calculus.

These are project lessons, not an upstream skill edit or a novelty claim.
